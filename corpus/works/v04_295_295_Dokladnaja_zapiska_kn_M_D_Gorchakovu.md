## IV. \* [MEMORANDUM TO PRINCE M. D. GORCHAKOV?]

Your Excellency.

The matter about which I wish to speak to you is too great and important for me, in speaking of it, to fear speaking the truth. Sevastopol is moving with rapid steps toward its fall, the garrison toward its destruction, Russia toward its disgrace. The fall of Sevastopol is important as the destruction of all the good that Russia acquired with blood, as a silent eternal acknowledgment not of the superiority but of the dominion of the Anglo-French; it is important as a renunciation of Russia's centuries-old glory and the destruction forever of the honor and hopes of the Russians. — And we are moving toward it with rapid steps. — I am speaking not about the number of troops, powder, [not] about the facts of the war and siege,—I am speaking about the spirit of the troops. The spirit of the troops at the present moment is a sorrowful doubt in the possibility of defending Sevastopol, submission to the will of Providence, but not the enthusiasm of heroes, which alone can save us. — The germ of the heroic feeling of readiness to die for the cause of Christianity and the honor of the fatherland lies in all of us. Give it scope and manifestation, and this spirit will express itself in deeds worthy of a man and a Russian. —

Let an appeal, which arose from within the garrison and has already been deeply realized by many of us, be read to each of us from the highest to the lowest.

[L]et each of us in Church before the face of [God] take the sworn promise which, in the form of a duty, lies hidden in the soul of each; let each [one of] us in the name of St George put upon himself the renunciation of life and the complete dedication of himself [to the] common cause. — And the Garrison of Sevastopol will be [not a sile]nt victim of inevitable fall but a great indestructible community of heroes, and Sevas[topol will b]e not an instrument of our disgrace but an instrume[nt] [of] its defenders and of the glory of our gre[at] father[land], and we will cru[sh] *avim* our own.
