## \*XII. WHY DO PEOPLE WRITE?

1) Why do people write? In order to acquire—some money, some fame, and some both; while some say they write in order to teach people virtue. Why do people read, why do they give money and fame for books? People want to be happy; that is the common cause of all actions. The only way to be happy is virtue; therefore it is reasonable only to read and to give fame and money [^1]

But are books not useful when, by portraying virtue beautifully, they act through example? Almost everyone agrees that virtue means the subordination of the passions to reason. Yet poets and novelists, historians and natural scientists, instead of developing reason and thereby inclining people toward reasonable actions, develop the passions and thereby incline them toward unreasonable actions. It will be said that the natural sciences are necessary for the comforts of private life. But do the comforts of private life contribute to the development of virtue? Not at all; on the contrary, they subject us still more to the passions.—

Of course, everything has its use. It has been proved that the existence of the flea that bites him is useful to man; but there is objective usefulness, which would be better called necessary influence, and subjective usefulness. It is the latter that I was speaking about.—

## Notes

[^1]: The words *and to give fame and money* were inserted later above the line and disrupted the construction of the sentence.
