## VI. \* [SOLDIERS' CONVERSATIONS.][^1]

Spiritual-poetic conversation — about the dead — about the 24th — about politics — ethnography and geography — joking with Vasin. —

*Volkov, a young pink-cheeked little soldier with Alexander I side-whiskers.* — I had a dream today, as if my mother was feeding me porridge.

*Kuzmin, a man with side-whiskers, No. I.* The things one dreams! Sometimes you fly.

*Volkov.* And it feels so good, above the huts; once soldier Melnikov caught me by the leg, and another time an officer wanted to do something to me, so I just up and flew away from him. —

*Abrosimov.* And what does it mean, my brothers, when you fly?

*3rd.* The soul flies. —

*4th.* Yes, that's true.

*Silence.*

*A young and handsome soldier with a somewhat Jewish physiognomy.*[^2] Where does it fly, then? —

*3rd.* To the tavern, of course. Where else. —

*Old soldier.* The thing is, our bodies are all one thing, but the soul is quite separate; whatever nonsense there is, you dream it all, you see, now that we expect death every hour, and if some girl were to come here. —

*Volkov.* I'd drag her into the hut.

*Old soldier.* No. I wouldn't now, not for anything.

*Young soldier.* Well, you'd still have a little fun. —

*Volkov.* What do you think, brothers, we'll all soon serve out a month for a year here. Why, Melnikov [?] has only 2 months to stand and then leave, once peace comes.

<!-- vol. 4, p. 298 --> *Old soldier.* When peace comes, I reckon they'll let everyone go.

*Vasin.* Peace won't come till '56; the new Tsar too, I reckon, will have a look. —

*A rifle bullet whistles and strikes* <ins>striking</ins> *1/2 arshin from the young man.*

*Old man.* There, another clean one will come out before evening. —

*Laughter. Whistle and impact of a cannonball.*

Why, it'll tear you in half.

*1*) I saw them putting [one] on a whole cart.

*2*) The convicts make ugly sport of it like that, head against head, and it knocks. The other day we watched; a lot of people had gathered, the stench knocks the convict over, the people are downwind, he drives them off; he grabbed a mangled chunk and flung it into the crowd, and splattered them with pus. I'd like to pile all of you in here, he says. Then the priest started on him for those words.

*D.* Well, he's in despair.

*Political.* Don't they have an oath of allegiance? Why, he's a rebel?

*V.* No, he himself is the state, that means.

*About the 24th.* So, he sees, it has run into his boot,—I see I have to fall, so I just threw myself to the ground. —

*Ethnography. In Wallachia there are* pristavki *all around.*
