## V. \*\* PLAN OF *YOUTH*.

# Plan of *Youth*.—

*1st ch. The window frames are taken out.* Dissatisfaction with myself, desire for something, self-improvement, doing gymnastics. 2nd chapter. Choir. Good Friday. Bustle, failure to sustain the impression, vanity, fear of sin.[^1] 2nd chapter. *Dinner on Good Friday.* A view of the whole family from the standpoint of virtue and happiness, plans. 3rd chapter. *Confession.* Devoutness, fear of God, repentance and <hope> conviction in future eternal perfection. 4 <*Communion*> *Examinations* <disillusionment>, vanity, bustle, and too many desires. I do worse in examinations because I desire too much that is good.—5) Injustice. 6) The Nekhlyudov family. Independent friendship. 7) Walks. Independence in life, money.—8) Journey to the country.—<Poor> Feeling of compassion for the peasants. Plans. 9) Family relations, observations. Freedom, merriment, and carelessness. 10) Stepmother. We are ashamed of her in youth; the feeling of vanity is strong and harmful. <11)> Neighbors, pleasures. Volodya's cruelty; toward him I myself do not know what I am doing. 12) Legal affairs, father falls.—12) Haymaking, plans, music, Franklin's diary, journal. 13) University. Judgments about Mathematics and the sciences. 14) Concert. Dmitry too is vain, and I diverge somewhat from him. 15) Katenka marries an artist [*1 illegible*]. 16) The Kornakovs <money, debts, gambling.> Father's gambling. 17) <Prince Ivan Ivanovich> Passing into the second year, worldliness. 18) Feigned love. At Iv. Iv.'s. 19) Failed the examination. 20) Revels, gambling, money. 21) Father gives freedom. 22) A concurrence of misfortunes. 23) I come to my senses and go to the country with plans for constructing a philosophy and for life as a landowner, which in themselves contain nothing but melancholy. I am thrown off course; I become a junker.[^2]
