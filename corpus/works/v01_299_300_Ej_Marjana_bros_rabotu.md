<!-- vol. 1, p. 299 -->

III.

Hey, Maryana, drop your work!  
Hear them firing beyond the hill:  
Surely our Cossacks from the campaign  
Are coming home.  
Come out onto the little bridge  
To meet them with bread and salt.  
<!-- vol. 1, p. 300 --> Now your lover will  
Walk with you the whole night through.  
Tie your fair braid  
With a red silk kerchief,  
Put on trimmed slippers  
And stockings with arrow patterns,  
Put on your neckpiece and coin necklace  
Of silver coins,  
Set your head neatly  
And put on your red beshmet.  
Quickly now, young woman:  
There, to meet the Cossacks,  
The whole village has already gathered  
At the gates with chikhir wine.  
With hands shading their eyes, toward the road  
They gaze intently. In the dust  
Now one can see, thank God,  
The pennons have appeared;  
One can hear them starting a song:  
“Not over the mountains, the mountains,”  
They fire bullets into the air,  
They lash the horses with whips.  
Ahead of the hundred rides smartly  
In a thin white zipun  
Sotnik Sekhin; and to the right,  
On a flaxen-chestnut horse,  
Vanka Grunin, there’s Stepanka,  
There is Rodion Malashkin.  
— Where is your lover, Maryanka?  
Why is he riding so far behind?  
Is his horse limping?  
Or is the poor fellow already drunk?  
Tell me then, whoever knows,  
Where is Daddy Kupriyan?  
— “Kupriyashka has given his soul  
To God,” the sotnik told her  
And sadly toward the road  
Pointed with his head.  
Maryana’s heart clenched,  
She looked around:  
Behind the hundred there was moving  
At a walk a horse-drawn cart.  
On the cart, covered with a burka cloak,  
The corpse of the slain man lay,  
Kupriyashka’s rifle,  
His shashka saber and dagger. —

(Disgusting). April 16, 1853 <br> Chervlennaya.
