## THREE DEATHS

## \* No. 1

Fedka had been ill for the third month—“the insides,” as he called it. What this illness consisted in was known neither by his brother, nor his nephew, nor the medical assistant to whom they showed the sick man, nor by the sick man himself. The sick man, however, did not wish to know. He knew and said that his death had come, and nothing more. ~~Not long before he had still been a strong, cheerful peasant, had driven courier runs, sung songs, and liked to drink. Now for the second month he had not come down from the stove, occasionally groaned, asked for a drink, and repeated disconnected prayers.~~ The day before yesterday he received communion.

## \* No. 2

The husband sat alone in his room and was silent; the cousin sat beside him and was silent. They sought and could not find what to say to one another, and yet felt a need to say something. One candle was burning.

“Forgive me, my friend,” said the cousin, “when she was still well” (the cousin sighed, the husband did too: he had begun to think over a new way of life without his wife, and had been interrupted), “it is hard for you, but what can be done? She said that she wished to rest with her mother at Shcherbinki. Won't you take her there and fulfill her last wish? The expense, of course, will not stop you in such an important matter.”

“Why did she say ‘last wish’ when she herself says it is an old one, and surely she invented it herself,” thought the husband. “The expense, though, will not be very great, about 200 rubles,” he thought further.

“Ah, my friend, I am happy to do everything I can for her memory,” he said, “only truly I cannot manage any of this myself. I am so upset.”

“Ah, that is understandable, my friend.”

## \* No. 3

Over the lady's grave stands a brick chapel, and there a priest in vestments swings incense and sings eternal memory to the servant of God. Over Uncle Khvedor's grave thick grass grows from beneath the stone and seems to want to move it. The same grass grows around the chapel; both kinds dry up every year, are covered with snow, and are renewed; every day the sun rises and shines ~~on the grave~~ and on the chapel and on Uncle Khvedor's stone. And no one knows what became of the lady from Shirkino and of Uncle Fyodor. ~~Once Uncle Khvedor's wife came to eat a flat cake on his grave, and the cake was tasty, and crumbs fell on the thick dark-green dewy grass, and the sun shone brightly, and the bell boomed loudly, and the people came cheerfully out of church, and God could [not] rejoice enough as he looked at his world, while beneath the earth God knows what remained of Uncle Khvedor.~~[^1]
