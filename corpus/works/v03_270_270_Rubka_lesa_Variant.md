# \* VARIANT OF “FELLING THE FOREST.”

~~Felling the Forest. Diary of a Caucasian Officer.~~

In the Caucasus there are three kinds of war: raids, sieges of fortresses or, more correctly, fortified *auls*, and the construction of fortresses in enemy territory. — Raids are undertaken with the aim either of ravaging, burning, and destroying the enemy's dwellings, or of seizing in them the enemy's weapons, or their principal leaders, and they are undertaken only in those parts of the Caucasus where the enemy, according to accepted custom and the arrangement of their *auls*, does not defend himself in them. — This kind of war is especially brilliant, if one may put it that way, but rarely brings the expected benefit, since the enemy, through his scouts, almost always knows the movements of the Russians beforehand and takes his measures. The siege and capture of fortified *auls* are prolonged and are often connected with hard labor and losses; but, when crowned with success, they bring a clear, definite benefit by removing obstacles to further conquests. The third kind of war—the construction of fortresses—is the essence of our operations in the Caucasus; by moving the lines of our fortresses, from various sides, closer and closer toward the center of the enemy's territory, we advance toward our goal, though slowly, with a firm and confident step. But since the enemy's terrain is unknown and inaccessible because of the forests covering it, in order to begin building a fortress it is necessary to learn the terrain, that is, to carry out a *reconnaissance*, and to clear the terrain, that is, to carry out a *felling of the forest*. *Reconnaissances* are carried out quickly and for the most part easily; but *fellings of the forest*, at least in the region of the principal theater of war, make up the most prolonged, difficult, and useful occupation of the troops here. —
