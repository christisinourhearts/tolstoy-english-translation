## \* I. [ON MILITARY-CRIMINAL LEGISLATION]

## [A][^1]

I want to examine the existing Russian military-criminal legislation. — Military society cannot be regarded as civil society. The aim of civil society, that is, of an association, lies in itself—the realization of the ideals of eternal truth, good, and general happiness; the aim of military society is external. Military society is one of the instruments by which contemporary truth is realized; its aim is killing. It is abnormal: what is a crime in civil society is not so in military society, and vice versa.[^2] [^3] <ins>reasonably</ins> was <!-- vol. 5, p. 238 --> habit and the immovability of the criminal law (not fear, for fear of death is greater than fear of the rods, but the 1st may be, the 2nd is certain).[^4]

Russian military-criminal] legislation permits the following kinds of punishment for a crime: arrest, removal from service, transfers to the army, and so on, reduction to the ranks, transfers to convict companies, extension of the term of service, being run to death through the gauntlet of spitzrutens, a specified number of spitzruten strokes, and punishment with rods. Sentencing criminals is carried out differently.[^5] [^6]

Let us examine some of these punishments and the way they are applied.

First I consider it necessary to enter into some considerations about the peculiarities of military society, its difference from civil society, and the aim and influence of criminal laws arising from that difference. The aim of civil criminal laws is general justice; the aim of military society is discipline. Besides this, the spirit of the army.

1) Transfer to the army. Influence on discipline and spirit. Example of the aristocratic corruption of the army.

2) Reduction to the ranks—about the soldier, who chiefly constitutes the spirit of the army. View of him, and in the extension of the term.[^7]

3) Running the gauntlet. a) Impossibility, recognized by law.[^8]

4) Punishment with rods. Arbitrariness. Contrary to discipline. Failure to attain the aim. Executioners judges. Judge and defendant, commander of the unit. Neither correction nor threat. What can replace it, they will say? First prove the necessity of the barbarous custom. — Example: the French, the best army. There are examples in ours. —

## [B]

Among the kinds of punishment existing in Russian military-criminal legislation are the following:

1) *Transfers*, *from the Guards to the army and from the army to line battalions and* *garrisons*.

<!-- vol. 5, p. 239 -->

2) reduction to the ranks.

3) extension of the term of service.

4) ~~removal from the rolls~~.

4) Running the gauntlet. and 5) punishment with rods.

The assignment of these punishments, depending on whether the crime was committed in peacetime or wartime, depending on the importance of the crime and on the rank and status of the offender, depends on a court or on the arbitrary will of the commander.

Wishing to examine the justice and expediency of these five kinds of punishment, among others now existing, we see the necessity first of clarifying the difference between the criminal laws of the army and the general criminal laws of the state. —

Military society cannot be regarded as a part of one whole—the state—and cannot be brought under the general laws, because the rights and duties of the members of this society are completely different from and often opposite to the rights and duties of members of civil society.

Killing, according to the laws of general justice, is a crime for the citizen; for the warrior in many cases it is a duty. The cause of this difference lies in the relation of the two societies. — The aim of civil society is general justice and the good of all citizens and lies in itself.

The more the laws of this society approach the general and eternal ideal, the more perfect they are. The aim of military society is killing, violence, in a word, force, and therefore lies outside it.[^9]

(I consider it unnecessary to speak of the legitimacy of the existence of military society despite its injustice. No society fully and directly realizes the general aims of eternal justice; by way of contemporary injustice all move toward general and eternal truth.)
