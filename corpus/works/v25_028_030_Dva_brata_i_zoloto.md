# TWO BROTHERS AND GOLD

Long ago, not far from Jerusalem, there lived two brothers, the elder, Athanasius, and the younger, John. They lived on a hill near the city and lived on what people gave them. The brothers spent all their days working. They did not work for themselves, but did the work of the poor. Wherever there were people worn out by work, wherever there were sick people, orphans, or widows, the brothers went there, worked, and left without taking payment. In this way the brothers spent the whole week apart and came together only on Saturday evening at their dwelling. Only on Sunday did they stay home, pray, and talk together. And the angel of the Lord came down to them and blessed them. On Monday they each went their own way. The brothers lived like this for many years, and every week the angel of the Lord came down to them and blessed them.

One Monday, when the brothers had gone out to work and had already parted in different directions, the elder brother, Athanasius, was sorry to part from his beloved brother, and he stopped and looked back. John was walking with his head lowered in his own direction and did not look back. But suddenly John too stopped and, as though he had seen something, shaded his eyes with his hand and stared at it. Then he went closer to what he was looking at, suddenly leapt aside, and, without looking back, ran down one hill and up another, away from that place, as though a fierce beast were chasing him. Athanasius was astonished and went back to the place to find out what had frightened his brother so much. As he approached, he saw something shining in the sun. He came closer: on the grass lay a heap of gold, as though poured out from a measure, enough for two loads. Athanasius was more astonished still, both at the gold and at his brother's leap.

“What was he frightened of, and why did he run away?” Athanasius thought. “There is no sin in gold; the sin is in the person. Gold can be used to do evil, and it can be used to do good. How many orphans and widows could be fed, how many naked people clothed, how many crippled and sick people healed with this gold! We serve people now, but our service is small because our strength is small, while with this gold we can serve people more.” Athanasius thought this and wanted to say it all to his brother; but John was already out of earshot and could be seen only like a little bug on the next hill.

Athanasius took off his garment, scooped into it as much gold as he could carry, hoisted it onto his shoulder, and carried it to the city. He came to an inn, left the gold with the innkeeper, and went back for the rest. When he had brought all the gold, he went to the merchants, bought land in the city, bought stone and timber, hired workers, and began building three houses. Athanasius stayed in the city for three months and built three houses there: one house, a shelter for widows and orphans; another, a hospital for the sick and infirm; and the third, a house for travelers and beggars. Athanasius found three devout elders and put one in charge of the shelter, another in charge of the hospital, and the third in charge of the house for travelers. Athanasius still had three thousand gold coins left. He gave each elder a thousand to distribute directly to the poor. All three houses began to fill with people, and people began praising Athanasius for everything he had done. Athanasius rejoiced at this so much that he did not want to leave the city. But Athanasius loved his brother and, after saying goodbye to the people and keeping not a single coin for himself, set off back to his dwelling in the same old clothes in which he had come.

As Athanasius approached his hill, he thought: “My brother judged wrongly when he leapt away from the gold and ran from it. Did I not do better?”

No sooner had Athanasius thought this than he suddenly saw the angel who used to bless them standing in his path and looking sternly at him. Athanasius was struck numb and could only say:

“Why, Lord?”

The angel opened his mouth and said:

“Go away from here. You are not worthy to live with your brother. One leap of your brother's is worth more than all those deeds of yours that you did with your gold.”

Athanasius began to tell how many poor people and travelers he had fed and how many orphans he had cared for. And the angel said to him:

“The same devil who put down this gold to tempt you also taught you these words.”

Then Athanasius's conscience convicted him, and he understood that he had not done his deeds for God, and he wept and began to repent.

Then the angel stepped aside from the road and opened the way for him, and John was already standing there waiting for his brother. From then on Athanasius did not give in to the temptation of the devil who had scattered the gold, and he understood that one can serve God and people not with gold, but only with labor. And the brothers began to live as before.
