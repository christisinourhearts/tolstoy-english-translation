# \* VARIANTS FROM THE MANUSCRIPT OF “THE DEMOTED OFFICER.”

Here, leaving aside minor variant readings, changes in punctuation and paragraphing, the most important variants are given from the manuscript from which the story was typeset at the printing house. The manuscript is abbreviated as *ms.* Passages which can with certainty or with a high degree of probability be regarded as having suffered from censorship are marked with an asterisk.

Page 75, line 17. *The word:* earthen — *is absent from the ms.*

\* Page 76, line 26. *After the words:* in a sheepskin coat — *in the ms.:* on which a soldier's St George Cross dangled,

Page 76, line 29. *After the word:* stopped. — *in the ms.:* I noticed that my gaze embarrassed him still more.

Page 77, line 26. *Instead of:* for a man no longer young — *in the ms.:* especially for a man no longer young

Page 78, line 21. *After the word:* petty — *in the ms.:* worthless

Page 78, lines 36—39. *Instead of:* No... why then?... *through:* under his breath, — *in the ms.:*

— No.... Oh no.... How could one, — the lower rank began speaking in fright, but suddenly fell silent and, evidently resolving to take offense and assuming a pensive-sad expression,

\* Page 79, lines 5—6. *Instead of:* discussed the authorities, — *in the ms.:* cursed the authorities,

\* Page 79, lines 16—18. *Instead of:* — Serves him right, *through:* now himself — *in the ms.:*

— What, is this that same Adjutant Alferov of yours whom it was impossible to play with? — Lieutenant O. asked, — he cleaned everyone out.

— Him, him, my dear fellow, he used to clean everyone out, and now he himself

\* Page 80, line 5. *After the words:* turned away from him. — *in the ms.:* and Lieutenant O..., whom this conversation, as a passionate gambler, interested especially, turned to me to change the subject:

— No, there is something not right here, I with him

<!-- vol. 3, p. 275 --> \* Page 80, line 10. *Instead of:* — Pavel Dmitrievich, *through:* have known him a long time, — *in the ms.:*

— I know him, — I said, — he plays a rock-solid game.

\* Page 80, line 20—23. *Instead of:* I *was angry* *through:* has to play. — *in the ms.:* I wavered between the thoughts: is he a cardsharp, or simply a gambler cleverer than all those with whom he has to play, and I always believed the latter supposition more.

Page 80, lines 30—31. *The words from:* what it means *through:* for an officer. — *are absent from the ms.*

\* Page 80, lines 32—33. *Instead of:* — He is devilishly *through:* not to play with him. — *in the ms.:*

— No, say what you like, — Lieutenant O. continued, — but I will never believe that luck alone is involved here.

— Do you really think he plays dishonestly? — Sh. said. — Why has he lost now, then?

— The scythe struck a stone, that's all.

During this conversation I looked at Guskov. He was evidently offended, or tried by the expression of his face, unnaturally knitting his brows, to show that he was offended by O.'s suspicion concerning the Adjutant, with whom he had lived and whom he said was his friend. He even whispered something that no one could hear.

Page 80, line 37. *The words:* the lieutenant said angrily. — *are absent from the ms.*

\* Page 81, line 1. *Instead of:* our regimental cardsharp, — *in the ms.:* plays with marked cards.

\* Page 81, lines 1—2. *The words from:* Sh. said, *through:* his invention. — *are absent from the ms.*

\* Page 82, line 11. *After the words:* did not appeal to me... — *in the ms.:* In her there was developed to the highest degree that Russian, especially Petersburg, aristocratism which expresses itself only in servility before a certain society and a certain *comme il faut*,[^1] through which they look crookedly, askance, and immorally at the whole world of God, and an aristocratism which no misfortunes, no influence are capable of knocking out of a person if by a proper upbringing and, still worse, by success in society it has been grafted onto him.

\* Page 82, lines 15—17. *Instead of:* who was *through:* with prejudice. — *in the ms.:* — who had made for himself in the service a very considerable fortune, and knowing the sister's tendencies, I expected nothing good from young Guskov.

<!-- vol. 3, p. 276 --> \* Page 83, line 30. *Instead of:* under arrest, — *in the ms.:* in the fortress,

\* Page 83, line 33\* *Instead of:* society — *in the ms.:* terrible society

Page 84, line 2. *After the word:* Adjutant, — *in the ms.:* Alferov,

\*Page 84, line 27. *Instead of: —* I don't know, — *in the ms.: —* The devil knows them,

Page 85, line 25. *Instead of: —* Strange jokes, — *in the ms.: —* I am old now,

Page 87, lines 18—19. *After the words:* *jargon du monde*[^2] and — *in the ms.:* I confess I did not expect it and was always surprised that people found me

\* Page 87, line 23. *After the words:* these advantages, — *in the ms.:* I took everything bad to heart; dishonesty, injustice, vice were repugnant to me, and I stated my opinion directly, and spoke incautiously, too heatedly and boldly.

\* Page 87, line 28. *Instead of:* under arrest, — *in the ms.:* in the fortress,

\* Page 88, line 15. *Instead of:* N. regiment. — *in the ms.:* S— regiment. *The same below.*

\* Page 88, line 26. *Instead of:* bad — *in the ms.:* filthy

\* Page 88, lines 27—33. *The words from:* It disgusted me, *through:* bound me. — *are absent from the ms.*

\* Page 89, line 2. *After the words:* It is simply terrible! — *in the ms.:* This regiment is rarely in action, and so drunkenness, cards, and debauchery flourish there to the highest degree, more than here.

\* Page 89, line 7. *After the word:* petty — *in the ms.:* vile

\* Page 90, line 19. *Instead of:* they promised me, — *in the ms.:* Alferov promised me,

\* Page 90, line 22. *Instead of:* carousing, — *in the ms.:* drunkenness,

\* Page 90, line 27. *After the words:* make you feel it, — *in the ms.:* The junkers, this is an impossible society, the soldiers, the sergeant majors........

Page 95, line 2. *After the word:* anteroom, — *in the ms.:* and at once she runs to meet me, and I hear her voice.

\* Page 96, line 24. *Instead of:* at our place — *in the ms.:* at Alferov's

Page 96, line 35. *After the words:* took an interest in him. — *in the ms.:* praised me to my face, said that he had taken a great liking to me back in Moscow, and here offered me his friendship.
