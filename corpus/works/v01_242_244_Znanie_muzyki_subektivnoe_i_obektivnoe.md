## [*FRAGMENT II.]

Knowledge of music is subjective and objective. Objective knowledge is knowledge of the theory of music, that is, of its fundamental principles. Subjective knowledge is divided into knowledge of the rules of music and knowledge of reproducing music. The beginning of music is the ability to express some musical thought.—

A musical thought is a combination of sounds constituting one whole, having a beginning and an end.—

These thoughts are expressed on some instrument. For convenience, the expression of musical thoughts on paper was invented; it does not itself constitute music, but is an intermediate expression of it.

The ability to compose a musical thought is called fantasy; the ability to understand this thought lies in the proper construction of the ear and in the presence of what is called musical feeling.

Musical feeling is the ability to perceive a musical thought so as not to disturb its unity in the soul.—

The ability to transmit a musical thought depends 1) on musical memory, 2) on exercise or the study of music, that is, according as the thought is transmitted directly in music or in writing.

And so the subjective study of music [consists] 1) in developing the faculty of fantasy, 2) in developing musical intelligence and feeling, 3) in developing: a) musical memory and b) the ability to play some instrument, sing, or write music.

And from writing to an instrument and vice versa.

4) Exercises. - - - - -

The relation of sounds with respect to force - - -

This relation can be infinite. With each sound a different degree of force is used. - - -

It may be divided into five degrees: *evenly*, *strongly*, *very strongly*, *weakly*, *very weakly.* Just as for determining simple [pitch of a sound one uses] a *tuning fork*, for time a *metronome*, for force a special instrument that strikes the keys *evenly*. - - - - - - - -

The study of composition—[unclear: into] three. - - - -

1) General rules concerning relation in space and the combination of sounds.—Thoroughbass.

2) General rules concerning relation in time. Rhythm.

3) General rules concerning relation in force. *Accentuation*. - - - - - - - - - -

Relations of all kinds can be innumerable; conventionally, however, 7 are accepted in space, in time [*1 illegible*], in force 5.—

With respect to time generally (fast and slow movement) and particularly, that is, each note relative to another, it can be subdivided infinitely, but conventionally down to 1/64.—For calculation, division into measures is adopted, that is, equal parts in time.
