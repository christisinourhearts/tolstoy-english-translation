<!-- vol. 41, p. 28 -->

10 January

The basis of education is the establishment of a relation to the source of everything and of guidance for conduct flowing from that relation.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 29 -->

1

> But whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to stumble, it would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck and he were drowned in the depth of the sea.
> Woe to the world because of temptations, for temptations must come; but woe to the person through whom temptation comes.

> *Matthew, ch. 18, vv. 6–7.*

2

> In educating children, we must remember that we are educating them not for life in the present, but in the future, better condition of the human race, that is, for life under other, better conditions of life. Parents usually educate children only so that they will be fit for the present world, though it is corrupt. In educating children for the future, better arrangement of the world, we thereby improve the future arrangement of the world.

> *After Kant.*

3

> In order to educate a person fit for the future, one must educate him with a fully perfect human being in view — only then will the pupil be a worthy member of the generation in which he will have to live.

4

> To bring a child to awareness of the divine nature within himself seems to me the chief duty of parents and educators.

> *Channing.*

5

> The aim of true education is not only to make people do good deeds, but to find joy in them; not only to be pure, but to love purity; not only to be just, but to hunger and thirst for justice.

> *John Ruskin.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 30 -->

Religious teaching is the basis of education, and yet in our Christian world people teach what no one believes. Children are perceptive and see this, and believe neither what they are taught nor those who teach them.
