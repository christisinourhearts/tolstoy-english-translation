<!-- vol. 41, p. 13 -->

2 January

One of the crudest superstitions is the belief held by most of the so-called scholars of our time that a person can live without faith.

1

> Always, in every age, people have thirsted to know, or at least to have some idea of, the beginning or final purpose of their earthly existence, and religion appeared to satisfy <!-- vol. 41, p. 14 --> this need of theirs and to illuminate the bond that connects all people as brothers having one common source of origin, one common task in life, and one common final goal.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

2

True religion is a relation established by a person toward the infinite life surrounding him, a relation that joins his life to that infinity and guides his actions.

3

The essence of every religion consists solely in the answer to the question: why do I live, and what is my relation to the infinite world surrounding me? There is no religion, from the most exalted to the crudest, that does not have at its foundation this establishment of a person's relation to the world surrounding him.

4

Religion is the highest and noblest agent in the education of man, the greatest force of enlightenment, while outward manifestations of faith and self-interested political activity are the chief obstacles to humanity's movement forward. The activity of both the clergy and the state is opposed to religion. The essence of religion, eternal and divine, equally fills the human heart wherever it feels and beats. All our investigations point us to one foundation of all great religions, to one teaching developing from the beginning of human life to the present day.

Deep beneath all faiths flows the stream of one eternal truth.

Let the Parsis wear their taovids, the Jews their phylacteries, Christians their cross, Muslims their crescent; but let them all remember that these are only outward signs, while the fundamental essence of all religions—love of one's neighbor—is equally demanded by Manu, Zoroaster, Buddha, Moses, Socrates, Hillel, Jesus, Paul, and Mohammed.

*Maurice Flügel.* <!-- vol. 41, p. 15 -->

5

> *Not the content of certain teachings as divine revelations (for that is called theology), but the content of all our duties in general,* *as God's commandments,* *constitutes the essence of every religion.*

> *After Kant.*

A person's life without faith is the life of an animal.
