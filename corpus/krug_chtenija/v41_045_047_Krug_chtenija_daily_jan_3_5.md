<!-- vol. 41, p. 45 -->

19 January

Social life can be improved only by self-renunciation.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 46 -->

1

People say: one swallow does not make spring. But because one swallow does not make spring, must the swallow that already feels spring not fly, but wait? If every bud and blade of grass waited like that, spring would never come. In the same way, for us to establish the kingdom of God, we need not think about whether I am the first or the thousandth swallow.

2

> Heaven and earth are eternal. They are eternal because they do not exist for themselves. Therefore they are eternal.
>
> In the same way, the holy man renounces himself and by this becomes eternal. And becoming eternal, he becomes powerful and accomplishes everything he needs.
>
> *Lao-Tzu.*

3

*In private and common life there is one law: if you want to improve life, be ready to give it.*

4

> In times when peoples await only a sign to begin the greatest battle between good and evil that has ever taken place on earth—when dull rolls of thunder can already be heard in all parts of the world, when people feel that the time is near when two armies will clash—the army of God and the army of Satan—and that the future fate of humanity—its liberation or enslavement—depends on this clash, in these solemn times one must remember above all that the name of a soldier in God's army can be earned only by following the example of its leader, who became like the poor in order to save people: to renounce everything, to have no place to lay one's head, so as not to grow soft, so as to have the full possibility of being here today and there tomorrow, of being everywhere there is danger, where the struggle is taking place, and to leave the dead to bury their dead. The dead are those who, immersed in cares about what is passing and in preoccupations with material things, do not even know that there is in them a soul demanding liberation, that to live means to struggle, means to die, that only by this path can the great liberation be accomplished.
>
> *Lamennais.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 47 -->

5

A person's perfection is measured by the degree of his liberation from personality. The more a person frees himself from his personality, the more perfect he is.

All attempts to improve life without sacrifice are vain. Such attempts only put off the possibility of improvement.
