<!-- vol. 41, p. 39 -->

15 January

The fundamental meaning of Christ's teaching lies in establishing direct communion between man — the son of God — and God the Father.

1

> You ask what the chief essence of Christ's character is. I answer that it is his confidence in the greatness of the human soul. He saw in man the reflection and image of the Deity and therefore loved man, whoever he was, whatever the conditions of his life and character. Jesus looked at people with a gaze that penetrated the material shell — the body disappeared before him.
> He looked through the rich man's fine clothes and the beggar's rags into the human soul; and there, amid the darkness of ignorance and the stains of sin, he found the beginnings of strength and perfection capable of infinite development, a spiritual, immortal nature. In the most fallen, depraved person he saw a being who could turn into an angel of light. More than that: he felt that there was nothing in himself that every person could not attain.

> *Channing.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 40 -->

2

> For nations, as for individuals, liberation from prejudices does not lessen moral barriers, but only replaces cruder guides to life with higher ones. Many poor souls lose their support in this change. But there is nothing bad or dangerous in this. It is only growth. The child must learn to walk alone. At first a person deprived of familiar superstition feels lost, homeless. But the taking away of external supports drives him inward and thereby strengthens him.
> He feels himself face to face with God: he reads the meaning of the teaching not in a book but in his own soul, and his little chapel expands into the majestic temple of the vault of heaven.

> *Emerson.*

3

> Knowledge of God may be either intellectual or moral, founded on faith. Intellectual knowledge is unreliable and subject to dangerous errors; moral knowledge ascribes to God only those qualities that demand moral actions. Such faith is natural and supernatural.

> *After Kant.*

4

> Seek not only a moral life, but strive for that which is above morality.

> *Thoreau.*

Beware of everything that comes between you and God — the spirit living in your soul.
