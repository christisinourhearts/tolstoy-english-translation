<!-- vol. 41, p. 15 -->

3 January

“My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work,” Christ said. Each of us has this work of His. We cannot know the whole work that God does through us, but we cannot help knowing what our part in it should be.

1

> Not everyone who says to me: Lord! Lord!—will enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of my heavenly Father.

> *Matthew 7:21.*

2

If you do not have the strength to burn and spread light, then at least do not block it.

3

> The one who knows the principles of sound reason is below the one who loves them. The one who loves them is below the one who practices them.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

4

The main question of our life is only whether, during this short span of life given to us, we are doing what the one who sent us into life wants from us.

Are we doing it?

5

*It is hard for me,* *I ask God to help me.* *But my task is to serve God,* *not for him to serve me. One only has to remember this, and the burden grows lighter.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 16 -->

6

> There is no chasm between earth and heaven, and it would be blasphemous to think that the dwelling God has given us must remain forever in the power of evil, self-interest, and oppression. Earth is not a place of atonement, but our home, in which we must work to achieve truth and justice, the striving for which lies hidden in every human soul.

> *Mazzini.*

7

> We must carry out the work appointed to us honestly and faultlessly, whether we hope eventually to become angels or believe that we were once slugs.

> *John Ruskin.*

Imagine that the purpose of life is your happiness—and life is a cruel absurdity. Accept what human wisdom, your reason, and your heart tell you: that life is service to the one who sent you into the world, and life becomes a constant joy.
