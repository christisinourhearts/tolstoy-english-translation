<!-- vol. 41, p. 183 -->

23 March

Land, like air and sunlight, belongs to everyone and cannot be an object of property.

1

> You are all wanderers in this world. Go north, south, west, or east—wherever you stop, there will be someone who drives you away, saying: “This is my land.” And, having gone around all <!-- vol. 41, p. 184 --> the countries of the world, you will return having learned that nowhere is there a miserable piece of land where your wife could give birth, where you could stop to cultivate the land, and where your children could bury your bones.
>
> *Lamennais.*

2

> To throw a person into the middle of the Atlantic Ocean, telling him that he is free to go to shore, would be no greater mockery of him than placing him on land wholly taken into private property, while telling him that he is a free man, free to work for himself and make use of his earnings.
>
> *Henry George.*

3

> The unlawful right of property in land has deprived more than half the people of every nation of their natural inheritance.
>
> *Thomas Paine.*

4

> Place a hundred people on an island from which there is no exit, and whether you make one of these people absolute master over the other ninety-nine, or absolute master of all the land on the island, there will be no difference.
>
> *Henry George.*

5

> Is it not slavery that although in England there is enough land to support ten times more people than now, still many must beg alms from their brothers, or labor hard for day wages, or die of hunger, steal, or be hanged as people unworthy to live on the earth.
>
> *Gerrard Winstanley.*

6

The right of property in land means hunger, thirst, lack of clothing, labor spent in vain, seizure of the products of completed labor, destruction of homes, poverty, sickness, death <!-- vol. 41, p. 185 --> of parents, children, wives, despair and brutalization in the hearts of the poor when legal force strikes them in the most sensitive and vital rights of humanity. All this is contained in the right of landed property.

*Cardinal Manning.*

The person who owns landed property in greater measure than is needed to support himself and his family is not only a participant in, but a cause of, the want, the calamities, and the corruption from which the masses of the people suffer.
