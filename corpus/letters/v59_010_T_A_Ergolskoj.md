*10. To T. A. Yergolskaya. December? 1848. Moscow.*

The letter I am writing to Nikolenka [^1] is very unclear, and there is no time to copy it out; write to him yourselves about this and tell Andrei to put the draft power of attorney into the letter to him.

My letters, dear Aunt, should be doubly agreeable to you: first because they prove to you that I am safe and sound, and second because they prove to you that I am becoming sensible.—On arriving in Moscow I was very eager to give you news of myself because I had good news to give you about my way of life, and then I stopped giving it because I was dissatisfied with myself. I have completely let myself go in this life of society; now all of it bores me horribly and I dream again of my country life, which I intend to resume soon. Valerian asks me to be godfather to his child, which would give me great pleasure if his break with Aunt Pauline [^2] did not cause me 3 too much pain; so tell him from me that I will come to him only with my aunt.

<!-- vol. 59, p. 26 --> Please tell Andrei that I am no longer angry with him and that he did very badly to send the flour, but that it would be better to send oats if there are still carts; in any case I need 200 silver rubles, and have him arrange to buy horses.

### Editorial notes

1. *The letter I am writing to Nikolenka [^3] is very unclear, and there is no time to copy it out; write to him yourselves about this and tell Andrei to put the draft power of attorney into the letter to him.*

2. My letters, dear auntie, should be doubly agreeable to you: first, they assure you that I am safe and sound, and second, that I am becoming sensible. On arriving in Moscow, I hurried to give you news of myself because I could report good things about my way of life; afterward I stopped writing because I was dissatisfied with myself. I let myself go, giving myself up to society life. Now all of it has become terribly tiresome to me, I dream again of my country life and intend to return to it soon. Valerian asks me to be godfather to his child, which I would gladly agree to if I were not distressed by his break with Aunt Pauline. [^4] So tell him that I will come to him only together with my aunt.—

3. Please tell Andrei that I have stopped being angry with him, that he should not have sent the flour, that it would have been better to send oats if there are still *carts*, that in any case I need 200 silver rubles, and that he should arrange to buy horses.

4. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. Published here for the first time. The letter is dated approximately from its contents: he has already been living in Moscow for a comparatively long time.
