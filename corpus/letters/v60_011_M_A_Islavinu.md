\* 11. To M. A. Islavin. 1856. May 29. Yasnaya Polyana.

What has happened to you, dear Mikhail Alexandrovich? — All your family in Moscow are in the same ignorance and perplexity as I am. For me, besides the perplexity, there is the important question of the apartment in which my furniture remains and for which — the apartment — I have not yet paid for May. Are you still willing to keep it (you will deduct May) and take the furniture except that from the study? — I left Mr Gorbunov[^1] there, who will hand everything over to you. Best of all would be if you stopped by to see me on your way back. Please give my heartfelt regards to Alexander Mikhailovich and Sofya Alexandrovna,[^2] and until we meet. —

Devoted to you,

Count L. Tolstoy.

29 May.

On the fourth page:

To His High Nobility Mikhail Alexandrovich Islavin.

### Editorial notes

1. The year is determined from the contents.

2. On the fourth page of the letter, which was folded and sealed with wax, an unknown hand wrote: “I should very much like to see you — I am expecting Vera here one of these days and shall go myself to accompany her as far as Tula — perhaps somehow I shall see you too. — I am sending the letter back to you because Mikhail Alexandrovich was not here and is not here, and I have absolutely no idea where he is to be found.”

3. Mikhail Alexandrovich Islavin (1819–1905) — second son of Alexander Mikhailovich Islenyev, a friend of Tolstoy's father, and an official of the Ministry of <!-- vol. 60, p. 62 --> State Domains. Tolstoy later married his niece Sofya Andreevna Behrs.

4. On 10 June 1856 Islavin wrote Tolstoy a letter refusing to keep Tolstoy's Petersburg apartment for himself.

## Notes

[^1]: Ivan Fyodorovich Gorbunov. See the note to letter no. 15.
[^2]: Alexander Mikhailovich Islenyev (1794–1882) (on him see vol. 59, p. 671) and his second wife Sofya Alexandrovna, née Zhdanova (1812–1880), daughter of the Odoev district police chief. She is portrayed in *Childhood* as the “belle Flamande.”
