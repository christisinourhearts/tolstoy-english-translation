63. To N. A. Nekrasov. 1852. July 3. Starogladkovskaya stanitsa.

July 3, 1852.

Dear Sir!

My request will cost you so little trouble that I am sure you will not refuse to carry it out. Look over this manuscript[^1] and, if it is not fit for printing, return it to me. If, on the other hand, it is, then value it, send me what it is worth in your opinion, and print it in your journal. I agree beforehand to all the cuts that you find it necessary to make in it, but I wish it to be printed without additions or alterations.

In substance this manuscript constitutes the first part of a novel—*Four Epochs of Development*; whether the following parts appear in print will depend on the success of the first. If, because of its length, it cannot be printed in one issue, I ask you to divide it into three parts: from the beginning to chapter 17, from chapter 17 to 26, and from chapter 26 to the end.

If it were possible to find a good copyist where I live, the manuscript would have been copied better, and I would not fear the additional prejudice against it which you will now inevitably acquire.

I am convinced that an experienced and conscientious editor—especially in Russia—by virtue of his position as a constant intermediary between writers and readers, can always determine beforehand the success of a work and the public's opinion of it. I therefore await your verdict impatiently.

<!-- vol. 59, p. 194 --> It will either encourage me to continue my favorite pursuits or make me burn everything I have begun.

With feelings of perfect respect,

I have the honor to be,

Dear Sir, your obedient servant,

L. N.

My address: through the town of Kizlyar to Starogladkovskaya stanitsa, to Artillery Lieutenant Count Nikolai Nikolaevich Tolstoy, to be handed to L. N.—Money for the return postage is enclosed in the letter.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the LB. The letter is not written in Tolstoy's hand. First printed by N. S. Ashukin in AK, pp. 185—186. At the top of the letter Nekrasov later made the notation: “With this letter was sent the story *Childhood*. The author is Count Lev Tolstoy.”

2. Nikolai Alekseevich Nekrasov (b. November 22, 1821, d. December 27, 1877), poet. Having come to Petersburg in 1838 from Yaroslavl province, Nekrasov was a non-matriculated student in the philological faculty there in 1839—1841, but devoted little time to his studies, spending much of it earning the means to live. In 1840, under the initials “N. N.,” he published the collection of poems *Dreams and Sounds*, which Belinsky received unfavorably. In 1843—1846 Nekrasov published a number of collections, and at the end of 1846, together with I. I. Panaev, bought from P. A. Pletnev the journal *Sovremennik*, founded by Pushkin in 1836; it soon became the best Russian journal and continued until 1866. Tolstoy's relations with Nekrasov, begun by this letter, continued until 1859, when they went their separate ways, though without any quarrel. Of their correspondence, twenty letters from Tolstoy to Nekrasov and twenty-eight letters from Nekrasov to Tolstoy are known in all.
