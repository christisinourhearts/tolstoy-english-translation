\* 10. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 1856. May 10. Petersburg.

10 May.

Dear Aunt!

I think you are already worried by my silence and by the fact that I have not arrived yet, having promised to come in the second week after Easter. Imagine that it is now more than two months since I submitted my report, and for a month they have been assuring me that in a few days I would have my leave, and I still cannot get it. The day before yesterday I submitted a second report, and I suppose that in any case I shall be able to leave in a week at the latest; which means that in two weeks I shall be with you at Yasnaya, for I intend to spend a few days in Moscow and go to the Trinity.[^1] I cannot tell you how unpleasant this delay is to me, now that the finest season of summer is beginning and I am obliged to spend it here. I have finished my hussars (a story) and have begun nothing new, and Turgenev has gone away too; I feel now that I have grown very fond of him, despite the fact that we were always quarreling.[^2] So I am terribly bored. In any case, until we meet, dear Aunt, and write me a little word: where are you? What are Marie and the brothers doing?

Léon.

On the envelope:

To Her High Nobility Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya. City of Tula.

10 May.

Dear Auntie!

You are surely worried that I have not written for a long time and have not come, after promising to be there in the second week after Easter. Imagine, it is already more than two months since I submitted my report, and for a whole month they have been assuring me that I shall receive leave in a few days, and still there is none. The day before yesterday I submitted a second report and think that I shall be able to leave no later than a week from now; which means that in two weeks I shall be with you at Yasnaya; I intend to spend a few days in Moscow and go to the Trinity.[^3] I cannot express how unpleasant this delay is to me; the finest season of summer is beginning, and I am forced to spend it here. I have finished my hussars (a story) and have begun nothing new, and Turgenev has gone away too; I feel now that I have grown very fond of him, <!-- vol. 60, p. 61 --> despite the fact that we were always quarreling.[^4] So I am terribly bored. In any case, until we meet, dear Auntie, and write me a few words: where are you? How are Mashenka and the brothers? —

Lev.
