*17. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. April 1...30, 1849. Petersburg.*

Serge, I am very busy [^1]—with what? I will write to you later. That is why I am writing you only a few words. Go to Yasnoye and speak with the merchants yourself.—I notice from Andrei's far too eloquent letters that there is something shady in the whole affair. To put you in possession of the facts I will tell you only this: Kopylov is offering 250 rubles [^2] and gave me money in advance; the other merchants were offering 310 and still more money in advance; so since that is more advantageous, one can take them and pay Kopylov back. It is true I wrote to him that I would let him have it for 275, but since they are offering more he must do the same or withdraw. In any case, if you think it is better to give it to Kopylov even for 270, do so.—The only thing I insist on is that all this be finished, and without that rogue Andrei. [^3]—

<!-- vol. 59, p. 42 --> I am sorry, I learned nothing about Shcherbachevka. [^4] Obolensky [^5] said it would be finished soon, but I did not question him in detail. Mitenka [^6] wrote him another letter and he wanted to stop by today to get an explanation from me. Goodbye, kiss Aunt T. A.'s [^7] hand for me.—Very busy. What do you think of the borzois I sent you? Lovely! You might at least observe the courtesies and thank me.. [^8]

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Published here for the first time. The letter is dated from its contents: the apology that he “learned nothing about Shcherbachevka.” Count S. N. Tolstoy had asked him to find out about Shcherbachevka in a letter of 31 March, to which this letter is therefore a reply. The letter concerns the sale of the Savin birch wood.

## Notes

[^1]: Tolstoy was occupied with preparing for examinations.
[^2]: Deleted: *arg*.
[^3]: Seryozha, I am very busy; with what? I will write to you later. Therefore I am writing you only a few words. Go to Yasnoye and speak with the merchants yourself. From Andrei's letters, which are too eloquent, I notice that the business is not clean. To keep you informed, I will say only that Kopylov is offering 250 rubles and gave me money in advance; the other merchants are offering 310 and still more advance money. Consequently, since this is more advantageous, one can take those people and pay Kopylov. It is true I wrote to him that I would let him have it for 275, but since they are offering more, he must do the same or clear out. However, if you think it is better to give it to Kopylov even for 270, do so. I insist only that all this be finished as soon as possible and without the participation of that swindler Andrei.
[^4]: Shcherbachevka was an estate in Sudzha district of Kursk province that fell to Count Dmitry Nikolaevich Tolstoy in the division among the Tolstoy children in 1847. At the division Shcherbachevka had 331 souls, 1,000 desyatinas of land, forest tracts, and a flour mill.
[^5]: Prince Dmitry Alexandrovich Obolensky. On him, see note 7 to letter no. 12.
[^6]: Count Dmitry Nikolaevich Tolstoy. On him, see note 5 to letter no. 84.
[^7]: Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya.
[^8]: The phrase beginning with “You might at least” was added at the side of the page.
