15. To N. A. Nekrasov and I. F. Gorbunov. 1856. June 12. Tula.

Nikolai Alexeyevich!

This letter was intended for Gorbunov, but it turned out to be more convenient to address it to you. This explains what follows. —

I am terribly grateful to you and ashamed before you, dear Gorbunchik, for the trouble I accidentally heaped upon you. Do this: if Islavin does not appear, give up the apartment, pay for the time it remained mine, and move the furniture somewhere, even pawn it for the time being, or put it somewhere with an acquaintance. I am writing to you not from the country but from Tula, where I received your letter[^1] and where I have no money, but by the next post I shall send 50 rubles. \* Send the boots. I shall not go abroad before October.[^2] It is delightful in the country. Come and see me.

Yours, Count L. Tolstoy.

12 June.

On the reverse:

Pass the second half-sheet to Nekrasov.

Second sheet:

First of all, be sure to answer me at least a line, because the chief purpose of my letter is to know about you. How is your illness, <!-- vol. 60, p. 69 --> and are you going away, and when?[^3] Are you living in town or at the dacha? Do you go on playing and, stretching yourself, chuckling and finding that “what more could you wish for, there is enough medicine...,” and have you been writing, and are you writing? — After leaving you I spent about 10 days in Moscow very pleasantly,[^4] then went almost straight to my sister and to Turgenev.[^5] — One has to see him in the country. There he is quite different, closer to me, a good man. I stayed with them about a week and, if it had not been for my affairs with the peasants, would never have left. My affairs with the peasants have gone so badly that I am not going abroad before October. You can imagine that the Emperor’s words about emancipation,[^6] with various additions and embellishments, reached [them], and together with their vague notion of whom the landowner’s land belongs to, this has caused them not to accept my most advantageous proposals, on the pretext that their old men gave no written undertakings and they do not want to either. I shall have a talk with the Slavophiles about the greatness and sanctity of the communal assembly. The most absurd nonsense. I shall show you sometime the minutes of the assemblies th[at I recorded?].[^7] Well, for that reason I am not altogether well at home. The affair failed, and it put me off writing, so that I have not yet begun anything. To make matters worse, four days ago I set up gymnastics at home, jumped rather awkwardly, and wrenched my lower back, so that I can barely walk and came to Tula today to consult doctors. A terrible muddle has occurred with my apartment and furniture. Please help me; your Vasily[^8] will arrange everything, and lend me about 50 rubles. I do not want to muddle accounts with you and shall send them by the next post. Goodbye. I embrace you from my heart.

Count L. Tolstoy.

\* or I shall ask Nekrasov to lend it to me.

### Editorial notes

1. First published in *Sovremennik*, 1913, no. 8, pp. 267–268. The year is determined from the contents.

2. Ivan Fyodorovich Gorbunov (1831–1893) — storyteller, dramatic actor, and writer.

## Notes

[^1]: I. F. Gorbunov’s letter is unknown.
[^2]: Tolstoy left Moscow for abroad on 29 January 1857.
[^3]: N. A. Nekrasov left to seek medical treatment abroad on 11 August 1856.
[^4]: Tolstoy was in Moscow from 18 to 27 May.
[^5]: On Tolstoy’s visit to I. S. Turgenev and M. N. Tolstaya, see note 9 to letter no. 13.
[^6]: See note 9 to letter no. 14.
[^7]: By the “minutes of the assemblies” Tolstoy means the records of negotiations with the peasants of the village of Yasnaya Polyana about freeing them from serf dependence, entitled *A Landowner’s Diary* (see vol. 5).
[^8]: Vasily Matveevich — N. A. Nekrasov’s servant.
