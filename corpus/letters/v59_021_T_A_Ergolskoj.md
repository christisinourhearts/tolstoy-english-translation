*21. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 20–26 May 1849. Petersburg.*

This letter is dated 20 May; as you see, it has been lying on my table for six days and I cannot bring myself to send it, always waiting for a letter from you. I assure you it is cruel of you. Andrei writes to me [^1] that you look grieved. Forgive me, dear Aunt, forgive me, darling, I am a wretch, a bad man, to make you suffer on my account; I know that I am the cause of your grief, and from this moment I resolve to return to you as soon as possible and never leave you again except sometimes for a few weeks. [^2] I wrote to you that I want to enter the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; but I give it up, I will enroll in something, prepare for the examination in Tula, and spend the whole winter at Yasnaya in order to economize.

My debts are unpayable, but God willing this is the last time.—The point now is that I cannot leave, not because they are keeping me, but because I promised to pay before leaving, and I need 100 silver rubles for the journey. Altogether I now need 400 silver rubles.—Goodbye, I kiss your hands and hope to see you soon.—

Do not show this letter to anyone, once again, and do not speak of my coming soon; [^3] I would like to arrive unexpectedly. Tell Serge that I embrace him and thank him for the trouble he is taking.—

### Editorial notes

1. This letter, as you see, is dated 20 May; it has been lying on my table for six days and I still cannot bring myself to send it, while waiting for a letter from you. Truly, this is cruelty on your part. Andrei writes [^4] that you look sad. Forgive me, dear auntie, *forgive me, darling,* I am contemptible, I am <!-- vol. 59, p. 51 --> bad, causing you suffering; I know that the cause of your grief is me, and I resolve to return to you as soon as possible and not part from you any more except occasionally, for a few weeks. [^5] I wrote you that I want to enter the Ministry of Foreign Affairs; I abandon that intention, *I will enroll in something, prepare for the examination in Tula, and spend the whole winter at Yasnaya in order to economize.*

2. *My debts are unpayable, but God willing this is the last time.—The point now is that I cannot leave, not because they are keeping me, but because I promised to pay before leaving, and I need 100 silver rubles for the journey. Altogether I now need 400 silver rubles.—Goodbye, I kiss your hands and hope to see you soon.—*

3. Do not show this letter to anyone, once again, and do not tell anyone about my coming soon. I want to appear unexpectedly. Tell Seryozha that I kiss him and thank him for his efforts.—

4. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. Published here for the first time. The beginning of the letter has not survived. The year of the letter is determined from its contents: the need to pay a debt keeps Tolstoy in Petersburg. See letter no. 19.
