\* 76. To Count S. N. Tolstoy and T. A. Yergolskaya. 1853. First half of June. Starogladkovskaya Stanitsa.

I am writing in haste, so forgive me for the letter being short and muddled. *Childhood* was spoiled, and *The Raid* was simply ruined by the Censorship. Everything that was good was either thrown out or mutilated.[^1] I have submitted my resignation[^2] and one of these days, that is, in about 1 1 / <sub>2</sub> months, I hope to go to Pyatigorsk as a free man, and from there to Russia. If my recommendation goes through, I shall receive my rank even after retiring, so I lose nothing. You, as they write to me, are attending to my affairs and, it seems, willingly. You would not believe how pleased I was in many respects to learn this. — I do not remember to whom I wrote (Valerian or Eg. Dm.)[^3] about sending me 100 rubles, which I have not yet received. Find out about it and have them sent to me and, if possible, another 200 silver rubles. Rest easy: I do not play cards, but since I have decided to leave, I should like to stock up here on many things that are almost necessities. — The contract for the mill seems advantageous.[^4] According to Aunt T. A.'s last letter, she should be with you, so I shall add a note to her:

Dear Aunt!

If I write to you rarely, it is because I am accustoming myself to the idea of the happiness of seeing you again soon, and I expect to express to you better in person than in writing all the gratitude I feel for your love and kindness. 5

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. Extracts from the letter were first printed by P. I. Biryukov in B, I, 1906, p. 218; somewhat longer extracts were given by P. A. Sergeenko in PTS, I, p. 32. The letter is dated by its contents: the mention of *The Raid* and the statement that he “submitted his resignation,” which occurred in May.

## Notes

[^1]: On receiving the March issue of *Sovremennik* for 1853, in which *The Raid* was printed, Tolstoy wrote in his diary under 28 April 1853: “Received the issue with my story reduced to the most pitiful condition. This upset me.”
[^2]: On this see note to letter no. 75.
[^3]: Yegor Dmitr. Khrustalev, manager of Yasnaya Polyana.
[^4]: On this see Count S. N. Tolstoy's letter to Lev Nikolaevich of 18 May 1853 in the note to letter no. 74.
