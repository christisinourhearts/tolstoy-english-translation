*20. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 11 May 1849. Petersburg.*

11 May.

I answer your four points as follows: 1) I took one examination and passed it, [^1] but fell ill and could not continue. I do not know whether they will allow me to take the remaining ones in August or not—I am attending to this and hope they will allow it; in that case I will also have to write a dissertation.—2) I will come to the country this summer only if they do not allow me to finish the examinations (and even then I do not think so, because in that case I will enter military service, as I already wrote to you) and when I have paid all my debts.—3) I already wrote to you about Shcherbachevka and wrote to Mitenka; I am just going to dine with Obolensky and will find out more exactly. 4) I received your letters and always with great pleasure, which is why I ask you to write more often.—

In my last letter I wrote you various foolish things, chief among them that I intended to enter the Horse Guards; now I am abandoning that plan unless I fail the examination and the war becomes serious.—I am very grateful to you for selling the forest; the only thing I do not like is that I would like the 400 silver rubles I received in Petersburg to be counted under the former contract, and to receive the full 1,000 silver rubles. Tell Andrei or Kopylov about this.—I hope you will also finish the sale of Vorotynka as quickly and well; though it is a pity, there is nothing to be done.—Because of my foolishness I am now in the most unpleasant position; you experienced it in Moscow, and so you will surely try to get me out of it. Tell Aunt Toinette that I ask her pardon for my thoughtlessness, and tell Andrei to <!-- vol. 59, p. 49 --> hand Aunt T. the 150 silver rubles. I do not write to Aunt because I feel too guilty toward her. I began three letters to her and tore them up. Ask her to write to me. I kiss her hands. [^2]

About the dogs, I wrote you not about live ones but about the plaster ones I sent you. Please tell A. I. [^3] that instead of the things I needed they sent me things I did not need, such as various fur coats and so on, while they did not send the summer and winter frock coats, trousers, summer neckerchiefs, foulard scarves, silver spoons, socks, sheets, towels, napkins, seals. Have them sent, and find out whether Fyodor [^4] drank these things away or they forgot them. Answer quickly and in more detail. Goodbye.

Have the money Andrei receives for the forest, grain, and horses sent to me. I intend to satisfy the Board of Guardians with the money from the sale of Vorotynka, [^5] which, I repeat once more, must be sold; it is a pity! But there is nothing to be done; it is not enough to regret, one must also repair the evil that has already been done.—

You could do me a great kindness if you would let Alexei Petukhov [^6] come to me. I know you will not refuse; all the more because in that case I will support his family and send you in exchange the old Isleniev pantryman and huntsman who now serves me, a good man though he likes to drink; besides, he has no family. But the point is whether Petukhov will agree. Tell him that I offer him 10 silver rubles a month in wages with his food provided, and that I will not abandon his family.—I feel the need here for a good servant very strongly.—So if Petukhov agrees, have Andrei give him money for the road and let him go; the sooner the better.

I am writing to you and not Andrei because he has driven me out of patience with his arguments. Tell him that I am very dissatisfied with his letters, advice, and change of village elders, but that nevertheless he should not stop writing with every post.

On the envelope:

To Tula. To Andrei Ilyich Sobolev. To be delivered to Count Sergei Nikolaevich Tolstoy without delay.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. An excerpt from the letter was first published by P. I. Biryukov in B, I, 1906, p. 155. The year of the letter is determined from the postmarks: “St Petersburg, 13 May 1849” and <!-- vol. 59, p. 50 --> “received 17 May 1849.” The letter is a reply to Count S. N. Tolstoy's letter from the second half of April 1849.

## Notes

[^1]: On the examinations, see the preceding letter.
[^2]: Tell Auntie that I ask pardon for my flightiness, and ask Andrei to hand her 150 silver rubles. I do not write to Auntie because I feel too guilty before her. I began three letters to her and tore them up. Ask her to write to me. I kiss her hands.
[^3]: Andrei Ilyich Sobolev.
[^4]: Tolstoy's servant.
[^5]: Malaya Vorotynka.
[^6]: We have no information about Alexei Petukhov.
