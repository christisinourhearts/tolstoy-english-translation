80. To N. A. Nekrasov. 17 September 1853. Pyatigorsk.

Dear Sir,

Nikolai Alexeyevich,

I am sending a short piece [^1] for publication in your journal. I value it more than *Childhood* and *The Raid*, and therefore for the third time I repeat the condition I make for its publication—that it be left exactly as it is. In your last letter you promised me that you would act in accordance with my wishes in this respect. [^2]—If the Censorship should again make cuts, then, for God's sake, return the piece to me, or at least write to me before printing it.—Whether to print this piece under the title given at the beginning of the notebook or as *A Suicide. A Marker’s Story* I leave entirely to your discretion. [^3]

(N. L.), placed above a line, means a new line.—Awaiting your reply and your judgment of the piece I am sending, I have the honor to remain, with perfect respect, your humble servant.

Count L. Tolstoy.

17 September 1853. [^4] Pyatigorsk—

On page 4:

To His High Nobility Nikolai Alexeyevich Nekrasov

In St Petersburg, Editorial Office of the journal *Sovremennik*.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the LB. First printed by N. S. Ashukin in *AK*, pp. 200–201.

## Notes

[^1]: “A Marker’s Notes” was written in four days, 13–16 September.
[^2]: Tolstoy is referring to Nekrasov's letter of 6 April 1853: “Dear Sir, Lev Nikolayevich. You are probably dissatisfied with the appearance of your story in print. I confess, I thought for a long time over its badly marked-up proofs—and finally decided to print it, in the conviction that, although it had been badly spoiled, much that was good still remained in it. Others recognize this as well. In any case, this gives you a measure of the degree to which such things are permissible, and in future I shall act according to what you tell me after rereading your story in its printed form.”
[^3]: “A Marker’s Notes,” spoiled by censorship, was printed in *Sovremennik*, 1855, no. 1.
[^4]: The autograph mistakenly has: 1883.
