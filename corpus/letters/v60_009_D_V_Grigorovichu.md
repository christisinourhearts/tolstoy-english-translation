\* 9. To D. V. Grigorovich. 1856. May 5. Petersburg.

For a long, long time I have been meaning to write to you, first about the exceedingly favorable impression made by your *The Ploughman*,[^1] and about what I know of that impression, and second about the excellent impression made on me by the April part of your *The Emigrants*.[^2] Now I shall write nothing except that I love you terribly and want to see you as soon as possible. Druzhinin and I are planning to go to Moscow on the 8th and spend about a week with Botkin.[^3] Come, dear soul, please. We shall have an excellent time, and perhaps it will be possible to carry you off to our parts for a week — that is, to me, to my sister, and to Turgenev.

Yours, Count L. Tolstoy.

### Editorial notes

1. Tolstoy's letter is a postscript to a collective letter sent from Petersburg to D. V. Grigorovich on 5 May 1856 by Druzhinin, Panaev, Nekrasov, and Goncharov. At the bottom is Grigorovich's notation: “Letter received 15 May 1856 from Petersburg. L. N. Tolstoy (*Childhood and Boyhood*). Druzhinin, Panaev, Nekrasov, Goncharov.” Apparently this letter was written at a farewell dinner at Turgenev's before his departure for his estate Spasskoye.

2. Dmitry Vasilyevich Grigorovich (1822–1899). On his acquaintance with Tolstoy see D. V. Grigorovich, *Literary Recollections*, Academia, Leningrad, 1928, pp. 249–254. Of the correspondence between the two writers only one letter from Tolstoy and one from Grigorovich have survived. Their acquaintance continued later as well: in the 1880s–1890s Grigorovich visited Tolstoy at his house in Khamovniki.

## Notes

[^1]: *The Ploughman* — a novel by Grigorovich, printed in no. 3 of *The Contemporary* for 1856.
[^2]: *The Emigrants* — a novel by Grigorovich, serialized in *Notes of the Fatherland* in 1855, nos. 11 and 12, and in 1856, nos. 4–8.
[^3]: Tolstoy managed to leave for Moscow only on 17 May. He visited V. P. Botkin, who at that time was living at a dacha in Kuntsevo near Moscow, twice: on the day of his arrival (spending the night with him) and on 23 May. After spending 10 days in Moscow, Tolstoy left after dinner on 27 May for Yasnaya Polyana.
