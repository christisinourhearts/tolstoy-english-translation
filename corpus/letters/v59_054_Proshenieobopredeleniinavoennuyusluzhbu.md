54. Petition for Appointment to Military Service. 1851. December 31. Tiflis.

Most Serene, Most Sovereign, Great Sovereign Emperor Nicholas Pavlovich, Autocrat of All Russia, Most Gracious Sovereign.

Collegiate Registrar Count Lev Nikolaevich son Tolstoy petitions as follows:

Having been released from service in the Tula Assembly of Noble Deputies, I have an earnest desire to enter Your Imperial Majesty's military service; therefore, submitting herewith the following documents: 1st, a certificate of birth and baptism, no. 252; 2nd, a medical certificate of my sound constitution and fitness for military service, no. 12; 3rd, a certificate of studies given to me by the Board of Kazan University, no. 999; 4th, a certificate from the acting Tula Provincial Marshal of the Nobility that I am in a position to maintain myself in foot or horse artillery and cavalry, no. 599; 5th, two official communications of the Tula Assembly of Noble Deputies, nos. 552 and 556, concerning my release from civil service, as well as the decree concerning my retirement, no. 93; 6th, a copy of the protocol concerning my noble and comital origin, no. 267; and 7th, a written declaration that I do not belong to secret societies—I most humbly petition as your subject:

That it be ordered that this petition of mine be accepted and that I, the person named above, be appointed to service in the 20th Field Artillery Brigade. — To be submitted to the Chief of Artillery of the Separate Caucasian Corps. — Tiflis, December 31, 1851.

<!-- vol. 59, p. 144 --> This petition was composed by the petitioner himself and fair-copied by Grigory Vasilyev, son Domnitsky 2nd, Fireworker 2nd Class of Mountain Battery No. 4 of the 21st Artillery Brigade. —

To this petition the Collegiate Registrar, Count Lev Nikolaevich son Tolstoy, has set his hand.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the original in the “file” of the Military-Historical Archive held in Moscow in the Central Archive (F. M. I., file no. 348—1852). Only the words beginning “To this petition the Collegiate Registrar...” are in Tolstoy's hand. Published here for the first time. On the cover, which is not of the official pattern and into which the documents, including Tolstoy's petition, are sewn, is written: “Concerning the communication of the Chief of Artillery of the Caucasian Corps on the origin of Count Lev Tolstoy, appointed to Battery No. 4,” fol. 3. From the file it may be concluded that Tolstoy's petition reached the Inspectorate Department of the War Ministry with report no. 431 of the Chief of Artillery of the Separate Caucasian Corps, dated February 15, 1852.

2. This report states that Tolstoy, in accordance with his petitions and after passing an examination before the Caucasian Grenadier Artillery Brigade, “was appointed by me to service, pending consideration by the Inspectorate Department of the War Ministry of the documents concerning his origin, with the rights of a volunteer, as a Fireworker 4th Class in Battery No. 4.” After listing the documents submitted and requesting permission for Tolstoy to serve, the author of the report certifies in conclusion: “the declaration mentioned in the petition, concerning non-membership in any Masonic lodges and other secret societies... has been retained for preservation among the files of my Administration.”

3. On fol. 28 is the office copy of a communication from the Inspectorate Department dated October 31, 1853, no. 28669, addressed to the Commander of Battery 4 of the 20th Artillery Brigade, stating that “Fireworker... Tolstoy is to be considered of the nobility and styled Count, in accordance with the decree of the Governing Senate of October 11 of this year, no. 7727, and the service of the said Count Tolstoy is to be reckoned on the basis of point 1 of article 503 of volume 5 of the Code of Military Regulations.”
