3. To N. A. Nekrasov. Late February 1856. St Petersburg.

I revised a few things, copied it out, and shortened it, but I confess I would not like to copy it out yet again. If it is not written too badly, especially the last sheet, then send it to the printer.—I will try to call on you in the morning. Goodbye.

Count L. Tolstoy.

On the fourth page:

To Nikolai Alexeyevich Nekrasov.

### Editorial notes

1. First published in *Sovremennik*, 1913, no. 8, p. 266. Dated from the content.

2. Nikolai Alexeyevich Nekrasov (1821–1877)—see vol. 59, p. 194.

3. The letter concerns Tolstoy's story *The Snowstorm*.

4. On 7 February 1856 Nekrasov wrote to V. P. Botkin about *The Snowstorm*: “Tolstoy has returned and made me happy: he has already written a story and is giving it to me for the 3rd <!-- vol. 60, p. 53 --> issue. It is so nice of him that I did not expect it” (*Pechat i Revolyutsiya*, 1928, bk. 1, p. 51). On 25 February Tolstoy read the story at Prince P. A. Vyazemsky's—at that time assistant minister of public education. Besides Turgenev, A. V. Nikitenko was also present at the reading. On 27 February Turgenev informed S. T. Aksakov that Tolstoy “has written an excellent story entitled *The Snowstorm*.”
