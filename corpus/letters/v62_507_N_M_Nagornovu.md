*507. To N. M. Nagornov. 1879. Mid-May. Yasnaya Polyana.*

Don't be angry with me, dear Nikolai Mikhailovich, for not sending you the horse. It was ill, is thin, and limps. It will get better here, and then you'll take it. — Thank you for sending the promissory notes. Please arrange it and discount it. And if you have a chance to collect some money, collect it. There are periods of wealth, and now I am in a period of poverty. I wish you success <!-- vol. 62, p. 487 --> on your trip. [^1] It would be good if you stopped by to see us. I kiss Varya and the children and shake your hand in friendship.

Yours, L. Tolstoy.

### Editorial notes

1. First published in the *Collection of the State Tolstoy Museum*, Moscow, 1937, p. 221. The date is determined by the mention of (1) Solovyov's promissory notes, (2) Nagornov's trip (see note 1).

## Notes

[^1]: On 26 May 1879 I. N. Nikolev wrote to Tolstoy: “Nikolai Mikhailovich Nagornov has left, they say, for the Don, and when he will return is unknown.”
