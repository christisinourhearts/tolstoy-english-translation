15. To the Rector of Petersburg University, P. A. Pletnev. 30 March 1849. Petersburg.

To His Excellency Mr Rector of the Imperial St Petersburg University, Actual State <!-- vol. 59, p. 39 --> Councillor and Knight Pyotr Alexandrovich Pletnev

from former Student of Kazan University Count Lev Tolstoy

Petition.

Wishing to take the examination for the scholarly degree of Candidate, I most humbly ask Your Excellency to admit me to examination together with the Students of this University in the Faculty and Division of Law.

I enclose herewith the following documents:

1) The Certificate issued to me by Kazan University. [^1]

2) The Metrical Certificate of birth and baptism. [^2]

Count Lev Nikolaevich Tolstoy.

30 March 1849.

In the left margin, in Tolstoy's hand: The Certificate from Kazan University was received back by Count Lev Tolstoy on 26 May.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the archive of Leningrad University in “File” No. 21 for 1848: “On admitting various outsiders and University auditors to examinations together with University students for the scholarly degree of candidate or the title of actual student. Begun 20 Jan. 1848. Finished 28 May 1849.” First published by I. A. Shlyapkin in his book *In Memory of Count L. N. Tolstoy*, St Petersburg, 1911, pp. 27–28. After Tolstoy's text, in an unknown hand: “Ref. From the certificate of Kazan Univ. it is evident that Count Lev Tolstoy was in the Faculty of Law and was released from the 2nd year on 19 April 1847.” Tolstoy told his biographer Löwenfeld the following about his intention to take examinations at Petersburg University: “It was very pleasant for me to live in the country with Aunt Yergolskaya, but an undefined thirst for knowledge again drew me away; this was in 1848 [sic!]. I still did not know what I should undertake. In Petersburg two roads opened before me: I could enter the army in order to take part in the Hungarian campaign, or I could complete my university studies in order afterward to obtain a post as an official. But my thirst for knowledge overcame my ambition, and I again took up my studies. I even passed two examinations in criminal law, but then all my good intentions collapsed. Spring came, and the charm of country life drew me back to the estate.” In the above-mentioned “file” <!-- vol. 59, p. 40 --> of Petersburg University there is no information on which subjects or with which professors Tolstoy took examinations.

2. Pyotr Alexandrovich Pletnev (1792–1865), literary critic and friend of Pushkin, handled his publishing affairs. After Pushkin's death in 1837 he took a close part in publishing the poet's “posthumous” works, which appeared in the journal *Sovremennik*, founded by Pushkin; Pletnev edited and published it from 1838 to 1846 and sold it to I. I. Panaev and Nekrasov. In 1832 Pletnev took the chair of Russian literature at Petersburg University, and from 1840 to 1861 was rector of Petersburg University. He was an academician (from 1841) and chairman of the Department of Russian Language and Literature of the Academy of Sciences from 1859 to 1865.

3. Tolstoy's personal acquaintance with Pletnev began in November 1855, when Tolstoy, arriving in Petersburg from Sevastopol after the Crimean campaign, became close to the literary circle of *Sovremennik* and emerged as a writer of great promise. L. B. Modzalevsky published Tolstoy's correspondence with Pletnev in 1862: one letter by Tolstoy and two letters by Pletnev concerning the journal *Yasnaya Polyana*, the *Books for Reading* published by Tolstoy, and Turgenev's new novel *Fathers and Sons*. See *Tolstoy (1850–1860): Materials and Articles*, edited by V. I. Sreznevsky, Leningrad, 1927, pp. 22–28. In 1910, recalling Pletnev, Tolstoy said that the latter “was very kind” to him (A. B. Goldenweiser, *Near Tolstoy*, vol. II, p. 89).
