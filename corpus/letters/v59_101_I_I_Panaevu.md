101. To I. I. Panaev. 1855. June 14. Position on the Belbek River.

Most Gracious Sir Ivan Ivanovich!

Will you believe that I have to overcome the shame I feel in order to take up the pen and write to you? I am ashamed <!-- vol. 59, p. 316 --> that I am alive and well, since only the opposite, if it were so, could serve as an excuse for my silence in answer to your 3 letters,[^1] letters so kind, stirring me and sometimes, in moments of self-important delusion, making me believe in my talent and in my significance in literature, which, I confess, I should very much like to have. I answer your questions in the previous letter point by point. 1) I received the money for *Boyhood*, 2) send me both money and books at the old address, at Headquarters, 3) and for each article separately. — Unfortunately, I promised you too much — my contributors were lazy while I was in Sevastopol; now it has been a month since I left, and I know nothing about their articles.[^2] I myself was ill, but despite that I hope that in about 3 days I shall send you “A Junker's Story”[^3] — a fairly long article, not a Sevastopol one but a Caucasian one, which will be in time for the VII issue. Believe me, the thought of military articles occupies me now just as much as before, and one of these days I shall go to Sevastopol in order to hurry Rostovtsev and Bakunin and take their articles, if they are ready;[^4] but reality is too rich in events for anyone to have time left for thought. For myself I still answer to you — one article every month — for the others, not with certainty. My address is still the same, and still through the couriers, if you have arranged that. — If Turgenev is in Petersburg, ask his permission to put on the article *A Junker's Story*: dedicated to I. Turgenev. This thought came to me because, when I reread the article, I found in it much involuntary imitation of his stories.

With perfect respect and devotion I have the honor to be your obedient servant.

Belbek, 1855, June 14.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the IRLI. First published by P. A. Sergeenko in PTSO, pp. 1–2.

2. Ivan Ivanovich Panaev (born 15 March 1812, died 12 February 1862), writer. After graduating in 1830 from the Noble Boarding School attached to Petersburg University, he served in the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Public Education, where until 1845 he was attached to the editorial office of the *Journal of the Ministry of Public Education*. After retiring, Panaev devoted himself entirely to literature. From 1839 he was an active contributor to *Notes of the Fatherland*, publishing novellas and stories there. At this time he became very close to V. G. Belinsky. At the end of 1846, with his own money, he and N. A. Nekrasov bought <!-- vol. 59, p. 317 --> from P. A. Pletnev the journal *The Contemporary*. After A. V. Nikitenko gave up the editorship, Panaev became the responsible editor of *The Contemporary*. Almost every issue of the journal contained articles by him, often unsigned or under the pseudonym “The New Poet.” Panaev's *Literary Memoirs* are especially well known (republished in 1928 under the editorship of Ivanov-Razumnik). In their day the “Notes of the New Poet” were very popular, and Panaev's name was placed alongside Belinsky's. Tolstoy, who became acquainted with Panaev after arriving in Petersburg in November 1855, met him often, but there was no particular intimacy between them. Fifteen letters from their correspondence have survived — six letters by Tolstoy and nine by I. I. Panaev.

3. The letter is a reply to three letters from I. I. Panaev: of 19 April, 3 May, and 19 May 1856.
