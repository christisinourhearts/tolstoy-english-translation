1852. October 29. Starogladkovskaya stanitsa.

29 October.

1852

Starogladovskaia.

Dear aunt!

I cannot conceive of or explain your long silence. Have I had the misfortune to deserve it? In that case forgive me, but do not deprive me of your letters. You cannot imagine how precious they are to me. They give me tranquillity, gaiety, courage, and a pleasure that I cannot convey to you; I read and reread them a hundred times; they mark epochs for me. — Write to me often, dear aunt. — I have a small reproach to make to you: why do you show other people the letters I write you? I have told you and written to you that there is no one I love as much as you and that I am convinced I am truly loved only by you: that is why the letters I write you are different from those I write to others, and I would not want everyone to read them. The demon who has undertaken to make all my enterprises fail continues his work. Yesterday I received a paper according to which I cannot be promoted for two years from today. This news grieved me. The happy moment of seeing you again, which I thought had come, is postponed for two years. Yes, the plans I made in one of my letters are too beautiful to be realized so soon. Everything that has happened to me and appeared unfortunate for me has been for my good; I hope that God will not abandon me and that the same will be true of the rest. The eighteen months I have spent in the Caucasus have made me less bad; I shall try to use profitably the two years I still have to spend, to become better, worthy of you and of the happiness I promise myself beside you. My health is good, my occupations are the same, hunting still gives me much pleasure. Last week I killed a wild boar, which gave me a moment of joy such as I had never yet experienced. — From a letter from the steward[^1] I presume that you are at Pokrovskoye; in that case embrace Marie, the children, and Valerian for me. Tell the latter that I have not yet received the 60 silver rubles of which I have <!-- vol. 59, p. 209 --> been told; I ask him to send me, as soon as possible, 300 silver rubles (a sum which, I hope, will be enough for me until the new harvest), that I am perfectly satisfied with his arrangements, that I thank him for his care and ask him to continue what he has so well begun! Goodbye. I kiss your hands.

29 October 1852. Starogladovskaya.

Dear auntie!

I can find no explanation for the cause of your long silence. Can it be that I deserved it? Then forgive me and do not deprive me of your letters. You cannot imagine how much I value them. They calm me; I become cheerful and spirited, and I cannot express what pleasure they give me; I read and reread them up to a hundred times; they constitute an epoch in my life. Write to me more often, dear auntie. — I want to reproach you: why do you let others read my letters? I have told you and written to you that I love you more than anyone else in the world and am sure that I am truly loved by you alone; therefore I do not write to you as I do to others, and I would not like everyone to read them. The little demon who has undertaken to ruin all my undertakings continues to operate. Yesterday I received a paper stating that I will not be promoted until two years from today. This news grieved me very much. The joy of seeing you, which I already regarded as quite near, is postponed for two years. Evidently the plans I made in one of my letters are so beautiful that they are not destined to be realized soon. Everything that has happened to me and seemed a misfortune to me has been for my good; I hope, with God's help, that it will be so in the future as well. In the eighteen months I have spent in the Caucasus I have become better; I shall try to spend the remaining two years usefully, to improve myself, to become worthy of you and of a happy life beside you. My health is good, my occupations are still the same, and I enjoy hunting as before. Last week I killed a wild boar, and I have never yet experienced a stronger joy. — Judging by the steward's letter,[^2] you must be at Pokrovskoye; kiss Mashenka, the children, and Valerian for me. Tell him that I have not yet received the 60 silver rubles they wrote me about, and ask him to send me, as soon as possible, 300 silver rubles (I hope they will last until the new harvest). I am completely satisfied with his arrangements, thank him for his trouble, and ask him to continue what he has begun so well! Goodbye, I kiss your hands.
