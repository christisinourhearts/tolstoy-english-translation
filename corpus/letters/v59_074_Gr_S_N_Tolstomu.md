\* 74. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 1853. April 17. Starogladkovskaya Stanitsa.

17 April 1853.

Again our correspondence has broken off, and not through my fault; you did not answer my last letter. — It seems to me that all of you there have agreed not to write to me: every post I wait, I don't know how, and every post—nothing.

You surely know that Nikolenka has retired and will be going to you in the next few days. I had wanted to do the same, because 1) I have already been here almost 2 years (the term I set myself), 2) with the sale of Mostovaya all my debts should be paid, 3) the disorderly campaign life and bad company have become pretty tiresome to me, and 4) because somehow my service has gone especially unfortunately; but I do not want to leave now and almost cannot until I receive the rank for which, they say, I have been recommended for the present campaign. The point is that even if this recommendation goes through, I will still have to travel to Petersburg for an examination, which I very much do not want to do, and I want to arrange matters so that I am promoted on the spot. This is the principal thing I want to ask you about. I know for certain that one can be promoted on the spot and that it is done. One need only ask someone of importance in Petersburg, in the Feldzeugmeister's Staff, where my recommendation ought to be. Could you not find a way to one of these gentlemen through the Gorchakovs, A. I.[^1] or S. D.,[^2] or through the Tolstoys, Prask. Vas.?[^3] You will understand that, <!-- vol. 59, p. 232 --> intending to retire as soon as I am promoted, it would be very disagreeable for me to set off for Petersburg in a gray greatcoat, live there for two months and cram old lessons; therefore to be promoted, even into the infantry (the Kurinsky or Kabardinsky regiment), without an examination, or into the Artillery if that is possible, is very important to me. Do everything you can to settle this matter, then, and in any case answer me. Please also find out through someone in Petersburg (I have written myself, but as I say, everyone seems to have agreed not to answer me) at the Inspectorate Department why my papers have been held up there for more than a year. — This delay has meant that the rank I ought to have received after 6 months I am now receiving after 2 years for distinction, while to this day I have still not been recognized as a Junker but remain an Unter-Officer.

The other day I received a wonderful letter from Andrei Ilyin, in which, among various foolish things, he writes to me that K. Yenikeyev[^4] very much wants to buy Mostovaya and offers much more than the others, but that no one has told him about it. Tell Yenikeyev if you see him.

My literary affairs are going badly. I sent a story to *Sovremennik* a long time ago, and for 3 months now there has been neither word nor sign of it.[^5] — As for writing anything new, I have written nothing because I have been on campaign all this time; and somehow I had no inclination. Now I am taking it up again. Goodbye, write about yourself. You spent the winter in Moscow. I am very interested to know what sort of life you led there. Are you not thinking of getting married? What about Masha the Gypsy? Please tell Valerian that the 240 rubles I asked him to send[^6] (he knows) are very much needed; therefore, if possible, let him send them.

## Notes

[^1]: Prince Andrei Ivanovich Gorchakov. On him, see note 1 to letter no. 33.
[^2]: Prince Sergei Dmitrievich Gorchakov. On him, see note 1 to letter no. 7.
[^3]: The Tolstoys — Countess Praskovya Vasilyevna Tolstaya, née Barykova (1796—1879), wife of Tolstoy's grandfather's brother, mother of Countess Alexandra Andreyevna Tolstaya, lady-in-waiting from 1846 to 1866 to Nicholas I's daughter Grand Duchess Maria Nikolaevna.
[^4]: K. Yenikeyev — Prince Yenikeyev. This is, of course, the same man mentioned in letters nos. 32 and 34 and in the notes to them.
[^5]: This refers to the story “The Raid.”
[^6]: To K. Begichev. On Begichev, see the note to Count S. N. Tolstoy's letter quoted in the note to letter no. 73.
