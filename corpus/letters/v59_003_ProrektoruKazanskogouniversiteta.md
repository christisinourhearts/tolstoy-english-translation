*3. To the Prorector of Kazan University, K. K. Voigt. 25 August 1845. Kazan.*

To His High Nobility Mr Prorector of the Imperial Kazan University and Knight of Orders, Karl Karlovich Voigt.

From Count Lev Tolstoy, first-year Student in the Division of Oriental Literature

Petition.

By my own desire and on the advice of relatives, I intend to change from the Faculty of Oriental Literature to the Faculty of Law; therefore I most humbly ask Your High Nobility to admit me to attendance at the lectures of the Faculty of Law.

To this petition Count Lev Nikolayev son Tolstoy, first-year Student of the Division of Oriental Literature of the Imperial Kazan University, has set his hand.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Published here for the first time. It is dated on the basis of an annotation in ink on the autograph in an unknown hand: “Rec. [that is, Received] 25 Aug. 1845.” The petition also bears an annotation in pencil in an unknown hand: “retained for poor progress.”

2. On the basis of unsatisfactory half-year examinations in January 1845, Tolstoy was not admitted to the second year of the Faculty of Oriental Literature and was thus left for a second year in the first year of that faculty. This apparently prompted him to change faculties. In response to Tolstoy's petition to the prorector of Kazan University for transfer from the Faculty of Oriental Literature to the Faculty of Law, the prorector, in submission No. 357 of 10 September 1845, asked the administrator of the Kazan educational district about the matter. The administrator of the Kazan educational district, who was also rector of the university, N. I. Lobachevsky, expressed his agreement on 13 September to Tolstoy's transfer to the Faculty of Law. On 10 October 1845 Prorector Voigt addressed the board of Kazan University with proposal No. 416, in which he informed <!-- vol. 59, p. 10 --> the board of Lobachevsky's opinion and asked that student Tolstoy be admitted to lectures at the Faculty of Law, which was done. All this official correspondence is held in the State Tolstoy Museum.

3. Karl Karlovich Voigt (1808–1873), professor of Russian literature and the history of world literature at Kazan University, was the son of Kazan University professor K. T. Voigt (1760–1811). He received his secondary education at the Kazan Gymnasium and was a student of Kazan University from 1823 to 1826, graduating as a candidate of the literary division. From 1826 to 1833 he taught languages at the university; in 1834 he became adjunct professor, and from 1837 to 1850 librarian. From 1839 he was extraordinary professor in the chair of Russian literature and literary history, and from 1842 ordinary professor. In 1845, because university rector N. I. Lobachevsky had been charged with administration of the district, K. K. Voigt served by election as prorector for seven months. From 1849 to 1852 he was dean of the literary faculty. In 1852 he was transferred to the post of rector of Kharkov University, later became trustee of the Kharkov educational district, and from 1868 was chairman of the scholarly committee of the Ministry of Public Education. K. K. Voigt published a number of works on Russian literature and literary history.
