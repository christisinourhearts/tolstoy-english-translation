*95. To N. A. Nekrasov. 1857. November 11. Moscow.*

I am writing to you, dear Nikolay Alexeyevich, to say that if only I don't die, by the 26th I will send you *The Lost Man*, a story of about three printer's sheets, which I am beginning to be pleased with. [^1] —

Yours, Count L. Tolstoy.

Please give me a definite answer about Fet's poems that I passed on to you. [^2] He is with me now and sends you his warm regards.

On Pyatnitskaya Street. Vargin's house.

### Editorial notes

1. First published in AK, p. 196. Dated from the contents. Judging from the Diary entries, the story (*The Lost Man*) was sent to Nekrasov on 26 November, and before that date Fet had been at Tolstoy's only on 11 November. See vol. 47, p. 163.

## Notes

[^1]: As soon as Nekrasov received it, the story was set in type, but on 30 November Tolstoy sent a telegram to suspend printing.
[^2]: Béranger's “Deathbed Poem,” translated by A. A. Fet. See note 5 to letter no. 96.
