94. To Prince G. V. Lvov. 15 January 1855. Simferopol.

Dear Lvov,

This note will be delivered to you by Bauer, [^1] a music teacher from Simferopol, a very good man and teacher and a decent pianist. Help him in Moscow—he is no worse than the Moscow teachers, and is an extraordinarily gentle and pleasant man.—Goodbye, dear friend; if you wrote me a word or two, I would answer with a long and interesting letter. Interesting if only because my address in Sevastopol is—to a Second Lieutenant of the 12th Artillery Brigade. Please do not entirely forget one who sincerely [^2] loves you. Count L. Tolstoy.

15 January 1855

Simferopol.

On page 4:

On Malaya Dmitrovka, in Khomyakov's house, To His Serene Highness

Prince Georgy Vladimirovich Lvov.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from a photograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. The whereabouts of the autograph are unknown. First printed by M. A. Tsyavlovsky in *L. N. Tolstoy. Jubilee Collection*, GIZ, Moscow–Leningrad, 1928, p. 56.

2. Prince Georgy Vladimirovich Lvov (1821–1873), to whom the letter is addressed, was on friendly terms with Lev Nikolayevich. Tolstoy frequently mentions G. V. Lvov in his diary of the 1850s. After graduating from the School of Jurisprudence in 1842, Prince G. V. Lvov served in the Senate and then was attached to Senator Count Tolstoy, who had been sent to Eastern <!-- vol. 59, p. 299 --> Siberia to conduct an inspection. In 1855 Lvov transferred to the naval administration, where reforms were then being carried out at the initiative of the liberal Grand Duke Konstantin Nikolayevich. On the basis of a memorandum prepared by Lvov on the life of military and naval cantonists, this peculiar estate was abolished. Later Georgy Vladimirovich served in Moscow and belonged to a circle of officials whose aim in government service was to put liberal principles into practice. Lvov spent the last years of his life in Paris, where he died.
