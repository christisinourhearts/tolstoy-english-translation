*25. To T. A. Yergolskaya and Countess M. N. Tolstaya. 10 January 1850. Tula?*

Dear Aunt,

Your letter caused me great emotion. As I unsealed it, I thought I would find in it the news of Marie's delivery, but unfortunately there is still nothing, and we must wait some more. I congratulate you on the New Year, which I am spending in Tula, and a little more sensibly than the year 1849. [^1]—Yesterday I received a letter from Serge, who tells me that Pauline [^2] has safely given birth to a boy whom they are naming Theodore.

Serge writes that on 7 January he intends to leave for Petersburg for two weeks.

As for the money I owe you, as well as Aunt Pauline's, [^3] I do not have it at present, but I expect to bring it to you. I beg your pardon for that. Goodbye, I kiss your hands, as well as Aunt Lise's. [^4]—

Give birth soon, my dear Mashenka; you would not believe how tiresome it is for the future uncle to wait. Goodbye, I kiss you very tenderly and ask you also to kiss Valerian for me.

10 January 1850.

On page 3:

To His [sic] High Born Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya.

### Editorial notes

1. Dear auntie, I was very agitated on receiving your letter; as I opened it, I thought you were telling me that Mashenka had given birth. Unfortunately—not yet, and we must wait some more. I congratulate you on the New Year, which I welcomed in Tula and more sensibly than 1849. [^5]—Yesterday I received a letter from Seryozha, <!-- vol. 59, p. 55 --> who reports that Pauline [^6] safely gave birth to a boy who was named Fyodor. Seryozha writes that on 7 January he is going to Petersburg for two weeks.

2. As for my debt to you and Aunt Pauline, I have no money at present, but I expect to bring it to you. Forgive me. Goodbye, I kiss your hands and Aunt Lise's.—

3. *Give birth soon, my dear Mashenka; you would not believe how tiresome it is for the future uncle to wait. Goodbye, I kiss you very tenderly and ask you also to kiss Valerian for me.*

4. *10 January 1850.*

5. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. An excerpt (the Russian text beginning “Give birth soon, my dear Mashenka...”) was published by N. N. Gusev in his book *Tolstoy in His Youth*. Moscow, 1927, p. 255.

[^2]: Praskovya Fyodorovna Perfilieva. On her, see note 4 to letter no. 6.
[^3]: Pelageya Ilyinichna Yushkova.
[^4]: Countess Elizaveta Alexandrovna Tolstaya.
