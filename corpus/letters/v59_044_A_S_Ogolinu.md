44. To A. S. Ogolin. <br> 1851. May 30...June 2. Starogladkovskaya stanitsa.

Mr.

Ogolin!

Make haste,

Write

About all of you

To the Caucasus,

And is

Molostvova[^1] well?

Oblige

Lev Tolstov.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the text communicated by Aleksandr Petrovich Mertvago, nephew of Zin. Mod. Thiele, née Molostvova, in his article “Tolstoy's First Love” (*Utro Rossii*, issue of June 12, 1911). From a text communicated by Baron Meyendorff, the note was printed by P. I. Biryukov in the second edition of B, I, 1911, p. 175. Finally, M. Vatatzi's family chronicle “A Tale of the Past” (*I. V.*, 1913, no. 5, p. 431) also gives the text of the note. The first and third texts coincide; in the text printed by P. I. Biryukov the first line is absent, while instead of the line “And is she well” it reads “Is she well.”

2. We determine the time of writing on the basis of A. P. Mertvago's statement in his article that “having arrived in the Caucasus toward autumn [this is incorrect. M. Ts.], Lev Nikolaevich writes A. S. Ogolin in a joking verse form: ‘Mr. Ogolin!..’” The Tolstoys arrived at Starogladkovskaya on May 30 (see Tolstoy's diary, <!-- vol. 59, p. 102 --> vol. 46), and therefore we tentatively date it May 30...June 2.

3. Alexander Stepanovich Ogolin (1821—1911) graduated from the School of Jurisprudence; he was Kazan provincial prosecutor, chairman of the Tiflis Judicial Chamber, and from 1873 a senator. In 1851 Alexander Stepanovich was engaged to the daughter of the Kazan landowner Modest Porfiryevich Molostvov, Elizaveta Modestovna Molostvova (b. December 20, 1831, d. April 5, 1852). Tolstoy travelled with him to V. I. Yushkov's village and later told P. I. Biryukov: “When Ogolin and I arrived and approached the house, opposite which stood a group of young birches, I proposed to Ogolin, while the servant was announcing our arrival, that we wager who could climb these birches better and higher. When Vladimir Ivanovich came out and saw the prosecutor climbing a tree, for a long time he could not recover from his astonishment.” (B, II, 1911, p. 175). A. S. Ogolin was married to Sofya Nikolaevna Zagoskina, daughter of Ekaterina Dmitrievna. He died in Geneva (?).

## Notes

[^1]: Zinaida Modestovna Molostvova (b. November 18, 1828, d. February 10, 1897), sister of Elizaveta Modestovna, daughter of Modest Porfiryevich Molostvov (1799—1854), marshal of the nobility of Spassky district, and Varvara Ivanovna Mergasova (1803—1881), granddaughter of the Kazan provincial marshal of the nobility and niece of the trustee of the Kazan educational district. She studied at the Kazan Rodionov Institute, where she was a friend of Lev Nikolaevich's sister, Countess Maria Nikolaevna Tolstaya. While still a student, Tolstoy knew her, meeting her in Kazan drawing rooms. Though not beautiful, Zinaida Modestovna was witty, strongly inclined to humor, and distinguished among her contemporaries by an uncommon cast of soul. During his week-long stay in Kazan in May 1851, Tolstoy, as an entry in his diary of July 8, 1851 testifies, was completely enchanted by Z. M. Molostvova. In July 1852 Zinaida Modestovna married Nik. Vas. Thiele (d. 1893), an official for special assignments under the Kazan governor and later a businessman. Tolstoy never met her again, but fifty years later, not without emotion, asked her nephew A. P. Mertvago about Zinaida Modestovna (on which see the latter's article “L. N. Tolstoy's First Love” in *Utro Rossii*, 1911, no. 134, June 12). On Z. M. Thiele see K. S. Shokhor-Trotsky's article “Tolstoy's Kazan Acquaintances” in the collection *To the Great Memory of L. N. Tolstoy: Kazan University, 1828—1928*, Kazan, 1928, pp. 105—123 (ch. V—The Molostvovs; ch. VI—Z. M. Molostvova).
