\* 34. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 1850. December 15. Moscow.

Seryozha!

1) The money taken for subsistence and sowing has been added to the capital.[^1] —

2) I paid the interest for the silver and gave Petr Evstratich the certificate to pass on to you.[^2]

3) I have just been to Kryukov and finally found him at home. — He asked me to tell you the following: 1) he no longer intends to renew the debt obligation that remains from the payment for the grove, saying, why did you not do it before? The principal reason why he no longer wants to do it is that a new regulation on surety has now come out, under which a guarantor is scarcely liable for the person for whom he stood surety. — If, however, you want to remortgage, he agrees to give the paper in which he declared his consent to the remortgage, <!-- vol. 59, p. 77 --> on condition that this money be deposited with the guardianship office. — He asks you to send the interest. What a vile man! I intend, however, to go to him once more and say: I. P.,[^3] because of your restriction on our estate all our hands are tied, and until it is removed we shall suffer losses for this reason and that, and you will not get your money. — Therefore, on my own behalf and on behalf of all the brothers, I give you my word that if you do not renew the promissory notes after the last payment for the forest, you will not get your money even in ten years; but if you remove the restriction, I give you my word that the sum will be paid in full within a year. Do you agree to this? That is, to offer him peace or war. — In any case I would advise you to occupy yourself with submitting a petition to the Tula Civil Chamber of the following contents: that we assume the debts, and ask that the division be approved. 4 — 4) About Kapnist,[^4] I have not seen Lvov,[^5] whom I wanted to ask to find out about this; and I have not gone to the office. I will inform you by the next post. — 5) About the additional souls. I do not understand. — 6) Can one remortgage under the Eighth Revision? One can. The Board will issue the money on the basis of the Certificate, whatever it may be, and will not make inquiries, because all the inquiries should already be contained in the Certificate. — 7) I am sending *Odontine* and *elexir* with Petr; 1 ruble 50 kopeks has been spent beyond the amount, which you should give to Petr. — My money is becoming scarce, so send me some as soon as possible. — I am living neither well nor badly—modestly; I do not play cards, only for 2 kopeks at *Yeralash* with Andrei Ivanovich Gorchakov, *to pay court to him*. 8 — Goodbye. If what I write to you about the petition I advise submitting seems obscure, Petr will explain to you what the matter is; it is necessary, of course, to give the secretary some money, asking him to come to the apartment, and to submit the petition as soon as possible, signing in our names, of course. Kazarinov[^6] is in Moscow, consequently Elagin[^7] is acting in his place and will do everything regarding the petition. —

December 15.

1850.

Petr insists that signing in our names is dangerous, and it seems to me he is right; better send for Mitenka and Valerian. I shall be there by the first days of January.[^8]

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Published here for the first time. The letter is a reply to Count S. N. Tolstoy's letter of December 11, 1850:

2. “Lyova. I received your esteemed letter of November 7, to which I reply as follows: Please be so kind as to write me everything in greater detail and answer all the points of my first two letters, omitting nothing: —

3. 1) about the money taken for sowing and subsistence.

4. 2) about the silver. —

5. 3) About Kryukov. —

6. 4) About Kapnist. —

7. 5) About the additional souls.

8. 6) About remortgaging under the Eighth or Ninth Revision.

9. 7) About *Odontine* and *elixir Peletier*.

10. Petr Evstratov lies about everything; find out properly yourself whether the Board will accept it under the Eighth Revision and whether it will make inquiries. If it will accept it and will not make inquiries, then it will be possible to do this, because in the Civil Chamber they tell me that a certificate under the Eighth Revision can be obtained. — If you have already written me a second, more detailed letter and have not properly found out about remortgaging under the Eighth Revision, then do not be lazy: find out properly and write again. I am waiting for your answer so that I can begin to act, and there will be no time to lose; the certificate must be obtained before Christmas. This is already my second letter to you; I do not know whether you received the first, in which I write about my fulfillment of all your errands. This letter went to you with the wagon train, with which I ask you to send me *odontine*. If the wagon train has already been sent, then send it by post. I have not yet seen auntie, so I do not know the contents of your letter to her. Write me more fully about yourself, how everything is—*shukr* or! *pashukr*? *trebitsy* or *na lovy*? I am very glad that the apartment is not *frizovaya*. Answer everything quickly. S. Pirogovo. Count S. Tolstoy.” (Letter unpublished; autograph in the ATB.)

11. Count S. N. Tolstoy has an obvious mistake: in November Tolstoy was still in Tula. “This is already my second letter... whether you received the first”—that is, the one of December 5, 1850. In the Romani words *shukr, pashukr, trebitsy* or *na lovy*, Count S. N. Tolstoy mixed something up, and it proved impossible to translate them.

## Notes

[^1]: On this, see Count S. N. Tolstoy's reply of December 21, 1850, quoted below.
[^2]: Count S. N. Tolstoy wrote about this on December 5: “Do not delay in paying the interest on the silver and send the certificate back to me.”
[^3]: I. P. Kryukov. See note 2 to letter no. 32.
[^4]: Ivan Vasilyevich Kapnist (1794—1860), son of the well-known writer, governor of Smolensk in 1842—1844 and civil governor of Moscow in 1844—1856, in whose office Count S. N. Tolstoy served, or rather was nominally listed.
[^5]: Prince Georgy Vladimirovich Lvov. On him, see the introductory note to letter no. 94. In Tolstoy's diary under December 21 it is written: “Find out from Lvov about Seryozha's service.”
[^6]: Nikolai Pavlovich Kazarinov (b. 1808), chairman of the Tula Chamber of Civil Court in 1844—1861.
[^7]: Nikolai Pavlovich Elagin (1801—1863), deputy chairman of the Tula Chamber of Civil Court in 1844—1849.
[^8]: The edge of the sheet, with several words, has been torn away.
