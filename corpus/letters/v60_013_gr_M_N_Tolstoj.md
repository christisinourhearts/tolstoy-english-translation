13. To Countess M. N. Tolstaya. 1856. June 5. Yasnaya Polyana.

I am sending you the pianoforte; send me the grand piano and *Don Giovanni*,[^1] as you promised, and something else by Beethoven or Mozart that you perhaps do not need. And I also remember that, passing through from the Caucasus[^2] or from the Crimea,[^3] I left some of my old writing with you; if it really is with you, send it to me. — I shall not change my word and after St Peter's Day[^4] I shall come, but would it not be better for you to take it into your heads and come to me now for a few days, and then you can come again later. Yasnaya is so beautiful that it is impossible to leave. The bathing in the river is excellent; I have only just come back from there. I would arrange a bathing place for you there too. Think about it. Perhaps Ivan Sergeyevich[^5] would come with you too? Auntie[^6] and I kiss all of you and do not despair of your coming. —

Tell Varinka from me, Uaaaa! And tell Nikolinka, Uiiii! and tell the little criminal, Uuuu — ah![^7]

Please, until very soon. My business with the peasants is going badly,[^8] all the more reason I cannot leave. I arrived home at 4 in the morning,[^9] everyone was asleep, and sitting down on the balcony I read Pushkin's *Don Juan*[^10] and was so delighted that I wanted immediately to write Turgenev about my impression.

I kiss you. Your brother,

Count L. Tolstoy.

5 June.[^11]

<!-- vol. 60, p. 64 --> On the fourth page:

To Her Excellency Countess Maria Nikolaevna Tolstaya.

### Editorial notes

1. First published in the book *Lev Nikolaevich Tolstoy. Jubilee Collection*, State Publishing House, 1929, pp. 40–41. The year is determined from the contents and the Diary entry under 3 June 1856 (vol. 47, p. 78).

2. Countess Maria Nikolaevna Tolstaya (1830–1912) — Tolstoy's sister. She was married to her third cousin Count Valerian Petrovich Tolstoy (1813–1865). See vol. 59, pp. 97–98.

## Notes

[^1]: *Don Giovanni* — Mozart's opera.
[^2]: In the last days of February–first days of March 1854 Tolstoy was at Pokrovskoye, from where he went to Bucharest.
[^3]: That Tolstoy stopped at Yasnaya Polyana while traveling from the Crimea to Petersburg in November 1855 is attested by the Diary entry under 21 November 1855.
[^4]: Tolstoy arrived at Pokrovskoye on 3 July.
[^5]: I. S. Turgenev did not come to Yasnaya Polyana.
[^6]: T. A. Yergolskaya.
[^7]: The young children of M. N. Tolstaya.
[^8]: This concerns the emancipation of the peasants. On 29 May, the very first day after his arrival at Yasnaya Polyana from Petersburg, Tolstoy called a village assembly and made a speech announcing the conditions on which he proposed that the peasants go free. A draft of this speech had been written while he was still in Petersburg. The speech did not produce on the peasants the effect Tolstoy expected. See the Diary entries under 3 June (vol. 47, p. 78) and 9 June (vol. 47, p. 80), and also letter no. 26.
[^9]: On 31 May 1856 at 5 in the morning Tolstoy rode to Turgenev at Spasskoye-Lutovinovo (Mtsensk district, Orel province). That same evening Tolstoy and Turgenev went together to Pokrovskoye. On 3 June “in the fifth hour” of the morning Tolstoy returned to Yasnaya Polyana (Diary, 31 May–3 June 1856, vol. 47, pp. 77–78).
[^10]: *Don Juan* — Pushkin's “The Stone Guest,” whose text Tolstoy read in volume IV of Pushkin's collected works edited by P. V. Annenkov, 1855.
[^11]: The numeral 5 was corrected from 4.
