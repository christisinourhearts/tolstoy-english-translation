\* 43. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 1851. May 27. Astrakhan.

27 May, 1851.

Dear aunt!

We are in Astrakhan and about to leave for *Kizlyar*, which means that we have a journey of 40 v. [?] to make over one of the most dreadful roads. — I spent a most pleasant week in Kazan; my journey as far as Saratov was unpleasant, but, by way of compensation, from there the passage in a little boat to Astrakhan was very poetic and full of charm, both from the novelty of the places and from the very manner of travelling, for me. Yesterday I wrote a long letter to Marie,[^1] in which I tell her about my stay in Kazan; I say nothing about it to you for fear of repeating myself, although I am sure that you will not compare the two letters. So far I am very pleased with my journey; I see many things that make me think, and then the very change of places is pleasant. Passing through Moscow I took out subscriptions, so that I have plenty to read, which I do even in the *tarantas*. Then, <!-- vol. 59, p. 100 --> as you can well imagine, Nicolas's company contributes greatly to my contentment. I never cease thinking of you and of all my family; I even reproach myself sometimes for having left that life which your affection made so sweet for me; but it is only an interruption, and I shall have all the more pleasure in seeing you again and taking it up again. — If I were not pressed for time I would have written to Serge;[^2] but I put that off until I am settled and more at ease. Kiss him for me and tell him that I greatly repent of the coldness that existed between us before my departure and for which I blame only myself. Goodbye, dear aunt, I kiss your hands a thousand times.

In Count N. N. Tolstoy's hand:

I kiss your hands very tenderly, my good, excellent aunt; forgive me if I write you only a few words, but we are hurrying to send our letters to the post and then to leave; our journey has already been going on a very long time like this, but we cannot complain too much about it. Goodbye, my good aunt, believe that I think of you very often and love you very tenderly. — I embrace Serge.

On the fourth page:

To Her High Nobility Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya.

In the town of Tula.

27 May 1851.

Dear auntie!

We are in Astrakhan and are setting out for Kizlyar, with 400 versts (?) of the most dreadful road ahead of us. — I spent a very pleasant week in Kazan; the journey to Saratov was unpleasant, but from there to Astrakhan we travelled in a little boat—it was both poetic and charming; everything was new to me, both the country and the very manner of travelling. Yesterday I sent a long letter to Mashenka,[^3] in which I describe my stay in Kazan; I do not write about it to you so as not to repeat myself, although I am sure that you will not compare the letters. So far I am very pleased with my journey; I see much that gives rise to thoughts, and the very change of place is very pleasant. Passing through Moscow I took out subscriptions, so I have plenty of books, and I read even in the *tarantas*. Then, as you understand perfectly well, Nikolenka's company contributes greatly to my pleasure. I never cease thinking of you and all our people; sometimes I even reproach myself for having left the life that was dear to me through your love; but I have only interrupted it, and the greater will be my joy in seeing you again and returning to it. — I would have written to Seryozha[^4] if I were not in a hurry; I put it off until I am settled and calmer. Kiss him for me and tell him that I repent of the coolness that arose between us before my departure and for which I blame only myself. Goodbye, dear auntie, I kiss your hands a thousand times.

<!-- vol. 59, p. 101 --> In Count N. N. Tolstoy's hand:

I tenderly kiss your hands, good and wonderful auntie; forgive me for writing you only a few words, but we are hurrying to send the letters to the post and to leave. Our journey has already dragged on quite long, although there is nothing to complain about. Goodbye, good auntie; believe that I often think of you and love you tenderly. — I kiss Seryozha.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. Almost the whole letter (without the last phrase and Count N. N. Tolstoy's postscript) was published in French and in translation by P. I. Biryukov in B, I, 1906, p. 168; it was first published in full (only in translation), without Count N. N. Tolstoy's postscript, by P. A. Sergeenko in PTS, I, pp. 8—9.

2. On the envelope in T. A. Yergolskaya's hand: “Received on June 10.” Postmarks: “Astrakhan, 27 May 1851” and “Received June 8.”
