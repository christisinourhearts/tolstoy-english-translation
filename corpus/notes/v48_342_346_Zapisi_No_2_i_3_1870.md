## No. 2. 1870

The tsaritsa's stolniks—10 years. Husband's nurse Anna Ivanovna, an orphan girl, a boyar's daughter.

In 1695 the tsaritsa is 27 years old.

Carts arrived from the Ryazan village.

Ioann Alexeyevich with Praskovya Fyodorovna.

Andrei was born

1660

82

Felt the belly, found one illness. You do not love your wife—you love another woman.

<!-- vol. 48, p. 343 -->

She panted—the trace is unpleasant.

The tsaritsa's outings at night.

Washerwomen.

Nests of arrows.

Kardim brother.

Cries when he falls into the water, and it is easier.

Little by little he remembers greatness and everything burdensome. Remembers things too.

Most repellent of all are the tsarevna and his own lie.

## No. 3. 1870

1870. 2 February.

I hear the critics: “Sleighing at Christmastime, Bagration's attack, the hunt, dinner, dancing—that is good; but his historical theory, philosophy—bad, no taste, no joy.”

A cook was preparing dinner. [^1] He threw and poured refuse, bones, and blood into the [^2] yard. Dogs stood at the kitchen door and rushed at whatever the cook threw out. When he killed a chicken, a calf, and threw out [^3] blood and guts, when he threw out bones, the dogs were pleased and said: he cooked dinner well. He is a good cook. But when the cook began peeling [^4] eggs, chestnuts, artichokes and throwing the shells into the [^5] yard, the dogs rushed over, sniffed, turned up their noses, and said: [^6] Before, he prepared dinner well, but now he has gone bad; he is a bad cook. But the cook went on preparing the dinner, and the dinner was eaten by those for whom it had been prepared.

<!-- vol. 48, p. 344 -->

However much people may say that in drama action should predominate over talk, if drama is not to be ballet, the characters must express themselves in speeches.

Yet whoever speaks well acts badly, and therefore the hero cannot express himself in words. The more he speaks, the less he will be believed. If others speak instead of him, attention will be directed toward them rather than toward him. —

Comedy—a hero of the comic—is possible, but tragedy, [^7] given the [^8] psychological development of our time, is terribly difficult. [^9] That is why only in textbooks can one speak of *Iphigenia*, *Egmont*, *Henry IV*, *Coriolanus*, and so on. But it is impossible either to read or to stage them. — That is why talentless imitators can imitate Pushkin's (weak) imitation, *Boris Godunov*. That is why blank verse—about which what Pushkin said of his future self will remain an unquestionable truth:

Listen, grandpa, every time I  
Look upon this Castle Retler,  
It comes into my head: what if this is prose,  
And bad prose at that. —

Vladimir's capture of Korsun—epic.

Menshikov has Peter II marry his daughter; his exile and death—drama.

3 February.

— 1870.

A system, a philosophical system, besides errors of thought, carries within it errors of system. —

Whatever form you fit your thoughts into, for the person who truly understands them those thoughts will be only the expression of the philosopher's new worldview.

To say clearly what you have to say, speak sincerely; and to speak sincerely, speak as the thought came to you.

<!-- vol. 48, p. 345 -->

Even with great thinkers who left systems behind, the reader, in order to assimilate what is essential in the writer, laboriously tears the system apart and takes the torn pieces for himself by relating them back to the person. —

Such are Plato, Descartes, Spinoza, Kant. — Schopenhauer says that his system is a circle (he says a vault), and that to understand it one must go around it several times.

With weak thinkers—Hegel, Cousin—once you tear the system apart, you come into direct contact with an empty man from whom there is nothing to take.

The crowd, however, loves a system. The crowd wants to catch the whole truth, and because it cannot understand it, it readily believes.

Goethe says: truth is repellent, error attractive, because truth presents us to ourselves as limited, while error presents us as omnipotent. — Besides this, truth is repellent because it is fragmentary, incomprehensible, while error is coherent and consistent. —

Russian dramatic literature has two examples of one among the many, many kinds of drama: [^10] one, the most minor and weakest kind, the satirical—*Woe from Wit* and *The Government Inspector*. The rest of the enormous field—not satire, but poetry—has not yet been touched.

1870, 14 February.

One of the best examples of how the causes of human actions that are obvious to us (and seem to be causes to the people acting) are not causes, because they do not coincide with the consequences, but have other causes that do coincide with the consequences, is fashion. The reason for the perpetual changing of clothes among people is the desire of the richest to distinguish themselves from the poorer. But it is clear that the aim is senseless, because there are many distinctions besides fashion, and the aim is not achieved, because everyone changes at once. But an aim invisible to the people acting is achieved: discarded clothes clothe [^11] the entire poor <!-- vol. 48, p. 346 --> population of London, Paris—large cities. — And this is no accident, as is clear from the fact that where there is a large concentration of people, a proletariat, there are rapid changes of fashion, which make still-unworn material available cheaply to the poor. And where there is less proletariat, fashion moves more slowly; where there is more, faster. [^12]
