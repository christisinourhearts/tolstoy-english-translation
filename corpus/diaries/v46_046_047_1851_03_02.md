2 March.

I have begun to weaken a little, chiefly because it was beginning to seem to me that however much I work on myself, nothing will come of me. — This same thought came to me because I occupied myself exclusively with exerting the will, without caring about the form in which it manifested itself. — —

I shall try to correct this error. — Now I want to prepare for the candidate examination; consequently, here is the form in which the will must manifest itself; but[^1] it is not enough to take a notebook and read, I must prepare myself for this, I must study systematically; I must obtain[^2] questions on all the subjects and from them make outlines. — I must find a student who could[^3] give guidance and explanations. — Tomorrow morning read the Encyclopedia from the beginning, consulting Nevolin,[^4] from 8 o’clock until 12; at 12 go and find a student; at 2 to gymnastics; from 6 until night[^5] study the Encyclopedia or something else, and an hour for <!-- vol. 46, p. 47 --> music. — Rule. Remember in every undertaking that the first and only condition on which success depends is patience, and that what hinders every undertaking most of all and what has especially harmed me greatly is haste. —
