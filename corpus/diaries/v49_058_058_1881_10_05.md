1881, 5 October. <ins>Moscow.</ins>

A month has passed—the most tormenting in my life. The move to Moscow.—Everyone is getting settled. When will they begin to live? Everything is done not in order to live, but because that's how people do it. Unhappy people! And there is no life.—

Stench, stones, luxury, poverty. Vice. Villains who have robbed the people have gathered, recruited soldiers and judges to protect their orgy, and are feasting. The people have nothing else to do but, taking advantage of these people's passions, wheedle back from them what was stolen. The peasant men are more adept at this. The women stay at home; the men scrub floors and bodies in bathhouses, drive cabs.—

Nikolai Fedorych—a saint. A tiny room. To put it into practice!—That goes without saying.—Doesn't want a salary. No linen, no bed.—

Poor Solovyov, without understanding Christianity, condemned it and wants to invent something better. Chatter, chatter without end.

Was in Torzhok at Syutaev's—a consolation.
