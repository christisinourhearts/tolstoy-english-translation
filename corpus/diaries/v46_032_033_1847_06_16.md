<!-- vol. 46, p. 32 -->

16 June.

Will I ever reach the point of depending on no external circumstances? In my opinion, this is a tremendous perfection; for in a person who does not depend on any outside influence, the spirit will necessarily [^1], according to its need, surpass matter, and then the person will attain his purpose. I am beginning to get used to the first rule I set myself, and today I set myself another, namely: regard the company of women as a necessary unpleasantness of social life and, as far as possible, keep away from them. — Indeed, from whom do we get sensuality, effeminacy, frivolity <!-- vol. 46, p. 33 --> in everything, and many other vices, if not from women? Who is to blame for our losing the feelings innate in us—courage, firmness, prudence, justice, and so on—if not women? Woman is more receptive than man; therefore, in ages of virtue women were better than us, while in the present corrupt, vicious age they are worse [^2] than us. —
