<ins>7 March.</ins>

3, 4, 5, 6 — did not write the diary.

7 March.[^1] Besides determining future actions, I find a useful purpose for the diary — an account of each day from the point of view of those weaknesses of which one wants to rid oneself. — Today. In the morning I did not get up for a long time, fidgeted about, somehow deceived myself. — Read novels when there was other work to do; told myself: I have to drink my coffee, as though one cannot do anything while drinking coffee. — With Koloshin I do not call things by their names, although we both feel that preparation for the examination is a sham; I did not tell him this plainly. — Received Poiret too familiarly and let myself be influenced by unfamiliarity, Koloshin’s presence, and inappropriate grand-seigneur airs.[^2] — Did gymnastics in a hurry. — Could not bring myself to knock at the Gorchakovs’ because of fausse honte.[^3] — At the Koloshins’ I left the drawing room badly, hurried too much, and wanted to say something very agreeable — it did not come off. At the riding school I gave in to mauvaise humeur,[^4] and because of a lady forgot about the matter at hand. At Begichev’s I wanted to show myself off and, shamefully, wanted to imitate Gorchakov. Fausse honte. — Did not remind Ukhtomsky about the money. — At home I rushed from the piano to a book, and from the book to the pipe and food. — Did not think over the peasants. — I do not remember whether I lied. Probably. — Did not go to the Perfilievs and Panin through thoughtlessness. — All the mistakes of today can be attributed to the following tendencies:

<!-- vol. 46, p. 48 --> 1) Indecis[ion], lack of energy. 2) Deceiving oneself, i.e. foreseeing something bad in a thing and not thinking it over. — 3) Haste. 4) Fausse honte, i.e. fear of doing something improper, arising from a one-sided view of things. — 5) Bad spirits, arising for the most part 1) from haste, 2) from a superficial view of things. 6) Confusion, i.e. a tendency to forget immediate and useful aims in order to appear to be something. — 7) Imitation. 8) Inconstancy. 9) Thoughtlessness. — —

Occupations for tomorrow: — From 8 to 9 letters to Auntie, to Nikolenka about the sale, to Zagryazhsky, and to the office about money and the division.[^5] From 9 to 10 gymnastics, from 10 to 11 music — scales, — waltz and adagio. — From 11 to 1 Poiret. — From 1 to 2 <sup>1</sup> / <sub>2</sub> to the horse-breeding office, to Volkonsky, Lvov, and Gorchakov.[^6] Gymnastics, dinner, novel, guests, and diary. — —
