[17 March 1847. Kazan]. 17 Mar.

It is already six days since I entered the clinic, and six days that I have been almost satisfied with myself. *Les petites causes produisent de grands effets.* [^1]—I got gonorrhea, naturally from what it is usually got from; and this trivial circumstance gave me a push from which I reached the step on which I had long ago set my foot, but could in no way get my body over (probably because, without thinking, I put my left foot instead of my right). Here I am completely alone, no one interferes with me, here I have no service, no one helps me—consequently nothing external has any influence on my reason and memory, and my activity must necessarily develop. The chief benefit, however, consists in my having seen clearly that the disorderly life which most people in society take to be a consequence of youth is nothing other than a consequence of the early corruption of the soul. [^2]—Solitude is equally [^3] useful for a person living in [^4] society ([^5]) as [^6] sociability is for a person not living in it. Let a man separate himself from society, let him enter into himself, and as soon as reason takes from him the spectacles <!-- vol. 46, p. 4 --> that showed him everything in a distorted form, and his view of things becomes clear, it will even be incomprehensible [^7] to him how [^8] he failed to see [^9] all this before. Let reason act; it will point out your purpose to you, it will give you rules with which you can boldly go into society. Everything consonant with man's chief faculty—reason—will be equally consonant with everything that exists; the reason [^10] of an individual person is a part of all that exists, and a part cannot disorder the order of the whole. The whole, however, can kill the part.—For this, form your reason so that it is consonant with the whole, with the source of everything, and not with a part, with human society; then your reason [^11] will merge into one with this whole, and then society, as a part, will have no influence upon you. [^12]—It is easier to write 10 volumes of Philosophy than to apply any one principle to practice.—
