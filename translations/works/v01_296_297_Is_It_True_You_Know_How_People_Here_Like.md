## *XVI. [FRAGMENT OF A CONVERSATION BETWEEN TWO LADIES.]

## CHAPTER I

—“Is it true? You know how people here like to spread false Petersburg rumors. And whom did you hear it from, my dear?”

—“Good heavens, Marya Ivanovna, I am telling you, I was at Princess Polyanskaya’s; the young princess only arrived from Petersburg yesterday and said that they talk of nothing else but this wedding. *(Slowly and distinctly.)* La comtesse Lise Bersenieff, la jeune personne la plus recherchée, la plus aimable, le parti le plus brillant de Pétersbourg épouse un *some* Taramonoff, mauvais genre achevé.[^1]

The old lady moved the snuffbox with the portrait and wiped its little glass with one of her two cambric handkerchiefs.

“Incroyable...[^2]

Marya Ivanovna Igrenyeva, née Damydova, is a respectable lady. She was born 50 years ago in Moscow to rich and distinguished parents; there were two sisters and three brothers. M. I. married Igrenyev, a rich man and also a Muscovite. They always spent the winters in Moscow,; <!-- vol. 1, p. 297 --> where they had extensive connections not only with the Moscow nobility but with everything distinguished in Russia, the summers—at their Tula country estate, [and] lived grandly both in town and in the country. The deceased held official rank and enjoyed universal respect. In a word, the Igrenyev household was like this: a visitor whom they considered worthy was asked: “Have you already been to the Igrenyevs?” or: “How is it you haven’t yet been to the Igrenyevs?”

“Marya Ivanovna was widowed 16 years ago, with a somewhat disordered fortune and two children: a son of 15 and a daughter who was already married. M. I. is a very intelligent woman, or no, better to say, cunning, despite her kindness, and able to exert influence on others and enjoy general respect.—She must not have been good-looking; a large aquiline nose especially interfered with her beauty; but she was a fine old woman, fresh not so much physically—(She used to say: “I wonder at myself, how could I bear so many sorrows!”)—as morally. She kept up with fashion as much as she could, herself inventing how to remake a cap or mantilla *“in an old-lady way.”* She loved society and society loved her, she judged everything and everyone......
