# 1. Note on Farming

Farming with the cultivators left as owners of such plots that they would not be placed under the necessity of seeking means of subsistence. —

In a contract one side always has more rights and influence over the other, and one depends on the other. —

With regard to a contract for the cultivation of landed property, it is more just that the working side should have greater influence. The peasants should be placed in this position when they are freed. For this purpose, the necessary amount of land must be secured to them. Once it is secured, the landowner will be dependent on their labor and will be compelled—large by magnitude, small by insignificance—to give it to them. To whom will he give it? Not equally to all. To the most capable. — The result is farming with ownership. — A nobleman cannot be a cultivator, because he will be on the same level as the lower class—hostility; democracy is impossible[^1] because of inequality of education. The nobleman will indeed be the peasants' protector, because his land will be in their hands. —

The proletariat existing in the commune hidden by force.

## Notes

[^1]: *In the original:* impossible [feminine form].
