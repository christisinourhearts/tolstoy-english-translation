## II. PREFACE NOT FOR THE READER, BUT FOR THE AUTHOR

The chief fundamental feeling that will guide me throughout this whole novel is love for rural landowner life. The scenes in the capital, the provincial town, and the Caucasus must all be permeated by this feeling—a longing for that life. But the charm of rural life that I want to describe consists not in peace, not in idyllic beauties, but in the direct aim it presents—to devote one's life to *good*—and in its simplicity and *clarity*.

The main idea of the work: happiness is virtue.

Youth feels this unconsciously, but various passions stop it in its striving toward this aim. And only experience, mistakes, and misfortunes force it, once it has consciously grasped this aim, to strive toward it alone and to be happy, despising evil and calmly enduring it. On this basis the novel too should be divided into three parts. The noble but inexperienced enthusiasm of youth; mistakes and infatuation with passions. Correction and happiness. Secondary ideas: the chief springs of human activity: 1) good: a) virtue, b) friendship, c) love of the arts; 2) evil: a) vanity, b) self-interest, c) passions: a') women, b') cards, c') wine.

(Negative idea: love, which in novels constitutes the chief spring of life, is in reality the last.)

Secondary idea: how difficult it is to do good and how it must be done.

Principal characters.

1) The hero. A good, noble, and receptive man, carried away by everything and so ardent that even good principles do him harm.

2) The representative of virtue and friendship (her past), an old maid, the aunt of his fiancée (the representative of love for everything refined and his first love).

3) His fiancée.

4) Representative of vanity, a landowner *(illegible).*[^1]

5) Representative of love for the refined and of friendship, vanity—Kostinka.

6) Representative of self-interest, the landowner Tartuffe[^2] (Voeikov).

7) Representative of self-interest, a young official who wants to marry his fiancée.

8) Representative of the passions in the country (Voeikov).

9) Representative of the passions in town, Ozerov.

10) Representatives of vanity: his city aunt and her sister.

Mass on Trinity Day. Acquaintance with[^3] Voeikov.

Description of the prince, tour of the village. Arrival of[^4] K.

Acquaintance with the Marshal of the Nobility. Lawsuit.

The aunt of his fiancée does not understand the slightly mocking, though good-natured, view young people take of feelings, and fears for her niece's love. —

Thinking he will find wisdom in the advice of old peasant men, he is mistaken. Their wisdom can be felt only in constant life together with them, since it consists solely in composure and freedom from passion.
