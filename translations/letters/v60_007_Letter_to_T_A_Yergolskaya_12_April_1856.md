\* 7. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 1856. April 12? Petersburg.

Dear Aunt.

I am writing only a little word to thank you for your kind letter[^1] and tell you that I am well, that I have submitted a petition for 11 months' leave — 3 months in the country and 8 abroad — and that today I am going to try to obtain a short leave for the holidays. I am waiting only for that in order to leave. Yesterday I finished a little story[^2] which I think I shall give to *Notes of the Fatherland*,[^3] and for the moment I am in that good humor which comes from finishing a piece of work of several months. — I hope until we meet, dear Aunt. At the very latest in Thomas week I hope to cover your little hands with kisses. —

Léon.

On this same sheet I am writing a little word on business to the “steward.”[^4] Give it to him, dear Aunt, and please insist that he pay these two small debts more quickly. When I come to the country I hope not to need money.

Dear Auntie!

I am writing a few words to thank you for your kind letter,[^5] to tell you that I am well, that I have submitted a petition for 11 months' leave —

3 months in the country and 8 months abroad — and that today I shall try to obtain a short leave for the holidays. I am waiting only for that in order to leave. Yesterday I finished a short story,[^6] which I think of giving to *Notes of the Fatherland*,[^7] and at this moment I am in that good state of mind that comes after finishing work on which one has labored for several <!-- vol. 60, p. 57 --> months. — I hope that it is until we meet, dear Auntie. At the very latest in Thomas week I hope to cover your little hands with kisses. —

Lev.

On this same sheet I am writing a few words on business to the “steward.”[^8] Give it to him, dear Auntie, and please insist that he pay these two small debts as soon as possible. When I arrive in the country I hope not to need money.

### Editorial notes

1. Dated from the contents: “Two Hussars” was published with the date 11 April 1856.

## Notes

[^1]: T. A. Yergolskaya's letter to Tolstoy has not survived.
[^2]: Tolstoy means the novella “Two Hussars,” whose original title was “Father and Son.” The novella was conceived on 12 March, as recorded in the Diary. In a letter of 17 April 1856 Nekrasov wrote to Botkin: “Tolstoy has written an excellent novella, ‘Two Hussars’; I have it and it will be in no. 5 of *The Contemporary*. Dear Tolstoy! As a journalist I owe him pleasant moments lately, and he is a good man too; the folly will wear off” (*Pechat i revolyutsiya*, 1928, no. 1). On 19 April 1856 Tolstoy recorded in the Diary: “Finished even the corrections to ‘Father and Son,’ which on Nekrasov's advice I called *Two Hussars* — better.”
[^3]: *Notes of the Fatherland* — A. A. Kraevsky's journal (see vol. 59, p. 337). The story was not given to *Notes of the Fatherland*. See vol. 3.
[^4]: The letter has not survived.
