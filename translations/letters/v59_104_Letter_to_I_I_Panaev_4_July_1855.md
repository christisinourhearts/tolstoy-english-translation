104. To I. I. Panaev. 1855. July 4. Position on the Belbek River.

Most Gracious Sir

Ivan Ivanovich!

I am sending you my Sevastopol article.[^1] Although I am convinced that it is incomparably better than the first, it will not be liked, <!-- vol. 59, p. 324 --> of that I am certain. I am even afraid they may not let it through at all. As for preventing it from being mutilated, as you yourself will see, I have taken every possible precaution.[^2] In all the places that seemed dangerous to me I made variants with signs of this kind (v), or indicated in brackets what to exclude in case it does not please the censorship.

— If, beyond what I have marked, they begin to strike anything out, absolutely do not print it. Otherwise it will greatly grieve me. For the title I made a variant,[^3] because “Sevastopol in May” points too plainly to the affair of 10 May,[^4] and *The Contemporary* is not permitted to print about military operations. I replaced Napshisetsky with Gnilokishkin in case the censorship says that an officer cannot refuse duty because of a gumboil; then they are 2 different officers.[^5] The Polish phrase, if it can be included, then with a translation in a footnote; if it cannot, then use the Russian one under the sign (+).[^6] And the Russian and French curses — could they not be indicated by dots, even without the initial letters, if it cannot be otherwise? But they are necessary.[^7]

In general I hope that you will be so kind as to defend my story as much as possible — knowing the censorship's view better, you will insert some variants in advance so as not to anger it, and make any insignificant unforeseen changes in such a way that the meaning does not suffer. Very much awaiting your reply to my 2 letters[^8] and once again repeating my humble request to patronize and defend this last story,

I have the honor to be

your most obedient servant,

Count L. Tolstoy.

4 July 1855

Belbek.

Please also leave the section numbers and dashes as they are in my manuscript.

On page 1:

To His High Nobility Ivan Ivanovich Panaev.

### Editorial notes

2. <!-- vol. 59, p. 325 --> 1 “Sevastopol in May,” written from 18 to 26 June.

## Notes

[^1]: “Sevastopol in May,” written from 18 to 26 June.
[^2]: The manuscript sent by Tolstoy is now held in the LB. From this manuscript the story was first printed only in the present edition (see vol. 4, pp. 18–59 and 387–392).
[^3]: The variant was: “A Spring Night in Sevastopol.”
[^4]: On the night of 10–11 May there was a battle on the right flank of the defensive line for our counter-approaches by the cemetery and the Quarantine Bay. At the cemetery, after a five-hour battle, the enemy troops occupied part of the counter-approaches; by the bay, the trenches and lodgments changed hands three times and finally, at about one o'clock after midnight, were definitively occupied by the French. During the night we lost 2,516 men from the ranks, and the enemy 2,303 men.
[^5]: See pp. 25 and 192 of volume 4.
[^6]: See ibid., pp. 36 and 204.
[^7]: See ibid., pp. 45, 55, 57, and 214.
[^8]: The letters of 14 and 16 June.
