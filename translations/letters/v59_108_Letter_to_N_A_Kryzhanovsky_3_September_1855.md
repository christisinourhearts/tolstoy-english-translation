*108. To N. A. Kryzhanovsky (draft). 3 September 1855. Sevastopol.*

Your Excellency!

I am very much afraid that I have undertaken something that is not my business, because, despite two days of diligent work, what I have done seems to me extremely poor and inadequate.

The information about the bombardment, however, is very insufficient, especially from the left flank, while accounts of the course of the assault often contradict one another. The accounts of eyewitnesses (field artillerymen) helped me greatly, so that in describing the course of the assault specifically on Malakhov Kurgan, [^1] it seems to me that I am not mistaken. You yourself will judge that.

3 September.

With true respect and devotion I have the honor to be Your Excellency's most humble servant, Count L. Tolstoy.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. The text of the letter is written on the reverse of leaf 5 of the manuscript of “Report on the Last Bombardment and the Capture of Sevastopol by the Allied Forces” (vol. 4, p. 79). First published almost in full by V. I. Sreznevsky in a note to the “Report” in vol. 4, p. 418.

2. Nikolai Andreyevich Kryzhanovsky (born 30 October 1818, died 29 April 1888) graduated from the Artillery School in 1837. In 1842–1850 he served with the forces of the Black Sea coastal line. From the beginning of the Eastern War of 1853–1856 he served as chief of artillery of the Southern Army and took part in the siege of Silistria. With the opening of the Crimean campaign, Kryzhanovsky went to Sevastopol, where he served as chief of staff of the artillery and was severely concussed. He took an active part in the battle at the Chernaya River on 4 August 1855. In 1857–1860 he was head of the Mikhailovsky Artillery School and the Artillery Academy. In 1865–1881 Kryzhanovsky was Governor-General of Orenburg and commander of the district troops.

3. <!-- vol. 59, p. 334 --> He was dismissed from the latter posts without having requested it after a senatorial inspection uncovered abuses committed by Kryzhanovsky's subordinates in the distribution of Bashkir lands. Kryzhanovsky wrote a number of works on military affairs and military history, as well as a novel.

4. In September 1876, while in Orenburg, Tolstoy visited Kryzhanovsky.

5. The letter accompanied the “Report on the Last Bombardment and the Capture of Sevastopol by the Allied Forces,” which Tolstoy sent to N. A. Kryzhanovsky, chief of staff of the artillery of the Crimean Army.

## Notes

[^1]: One of the principal fortifications of Sevastopol, situated on the eastern side of the defensive line, between the 2nd and 3rd bastions.
