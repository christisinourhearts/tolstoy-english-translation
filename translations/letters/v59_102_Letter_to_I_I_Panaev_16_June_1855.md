102. To I. I. Panaev. 1855. June 16. Buyuk-Syuren.

Most Gracious Sir!

Ivan Ivanovich!

In order to keep my promise[^1] and send you *A Junker's Story* for the 7th issue of *The Contemporary*, I am sending it to you uncopied, in a somewhat unpresentable form. You will notice, however, that despite the corrections everything is very legible and clear.[^2] I am very afraid that you might take the carelessness of the copying for carelessness in the composition; on the contrary, no story has cost me so much labor and time, therefore [several lines cut out] if they were excluded, they would destroy the whole meaning of the story, although the fact that it cost me much time proves nothing to me about its merit; on the contrary, it seems very doubtful to me, and since I have shown it to no one, I shall be very interested to know your opinion of it, which I ask you to communicate to me very soon and with complete frankness. The next story will be a Sevastopol one.[^3] It will miss the VII issue by a little, which I greatly regret, all the more because in the note to my article you promised contemporary articles, whereas I promised only military ones.[^4] In any case, if you find this story in its present form worth printing, print it; if not, send it back to me.[^5] — Then, impatiently awaiting your answer, with perfect respect I have the honor to be [several lines cut out].

16 June 1855.

Buyuk-Syuren.[^6]

On page 4:

To His High Nobility Ivan Ivanovich Panaev.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the IRLI. First published by M. A. Tsyavlovsky in *Krasnaya nov*, 1928, no. 9, pp. 218–219.

## Notes

[^1]: In his letter to I. I. Panaev of 14 June 1855, no. 101, Tolstoy wrote that he hoped in about three days to send “A Junker's Story.”
[^2]: Tolstoy means the story “The Wood-Felling,” begun as early as June 1853. In Tolstoy's diary under 16 June 1855 it is recorded: “Finished ‘Notes of a Junker’ unclearly and badly, but it can be sent.” On 17 June: “...worked all the while on ‘Notes of a Junker’”; on 18 June: “In the morning finished ‘Notes of a Junker,’ wrote a letter and sent it.” The manuscript of the story sent to Panaev has not survived.
[^3]: The story “Sevastopol in May” is meant.
[^4]: See I. I. Panaev's letter of 19 May, quoted in the note to letter no. 101.
[^5]: “The Wood-Felling. A Junker's Story,” with a dedication to I. S. Turgenev, was printed in *The Contemporary*, 1855, no. 9, with the date “15 June 1855” and the signature “L. N. T.”
[^6]: Buyuk-Syuren or Buyuk-Syuyren — a Tatar village in the valley of the Belbek River, on the Yalta highway, 12 versts from Bakhchisarai.
