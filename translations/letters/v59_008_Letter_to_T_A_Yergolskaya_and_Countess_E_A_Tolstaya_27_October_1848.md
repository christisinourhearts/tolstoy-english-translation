\* 8. To T. A. Yergolskaya and Countess E. A. Tolstaya. <br> 27 October 1848. Moscow.

27 October.

It seems to me that we have changed roles, my dear aunts: it is I who write to you and lament not receiving any news from you, while you are the ones being lazy. It will soon be two weeks, and not a word from you. — I am fairly well and, while waiting to settle into the new apartment, am leading a rather stupid and inactive sort of life. —

<!-- vol. 59, p. 23 -->

What more can I write to you? Receiving no letters from you, all I can do in mine is ask you questions, but unfortunately I've exhausted them all; so goodbye.

In the postscript I repeat everything I did in the two previous letters.

### Editorial notes

1. It seems to me that we have changed roles, my dear aunts: I write and complain that I receive no news from you, while you are being lazy. It will soon be two weeks since I received a single word from you. — My health is fairly good; while waiting to move into my new apartment, I spend my time foolishly and idly. —

2. What more could I tell you? Receiving no letters from you, in mine I can only ask questions, but unfortunately they too have run out. And so, goodbye. In the postscript I repeat what I did in the two previous letters.

3. Printed from the autograph held in ATB. Published here for the first time. The year of the letter is determined from its contents: this is the second week in which he has received no letters from T. A. Yergolskaya; he himself has written two letters; and he is moving to a new apartment.
