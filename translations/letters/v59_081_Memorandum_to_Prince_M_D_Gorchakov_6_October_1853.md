\* 81. Memorandum to the Commander of the Forces, Prince M. D. Gorchakov. 1853. October 6. Pyatigorsk.

5 November.

Memorandum.

20th Artillery

Brigade,

Battery No. 4,

Separate Caucasian

Corps,

Fireworker 4th

Class, Count Lev

Tolstoy. 6 October

1853. Pyatigorsk.

Having left the University[^1] and entered the civil service as a Collegiate Registrar,[^2] in 1852 I retired and, in order to serve together with my brother, entered military service as a Fireworker 4th Class.[^3]

Now, since my brother has retired,[^4] and the only relatives I have in service[^5] are in the active army, I should like to be transferred to one of the horse or foot batteries of the forces under your command, and I venture to ask Your Excellency's gracious intercession in this matter. —

Fireworker 4th Class

Count Lev Tolstoy.

To the Commander of the Forces stationed in Moldavia and Wallachia, General of Artillery, Prince and Knight Mikhail Dmitrievich Gorchakov.

### Editorial notes

1. <!-- vol. 59, p. 249 --> Printed from the autograph held in the Central Archive (a photograph of the autograph is held in the GTM). Published here for the first time.

2. Above the text of the memorandum, in an unknown hand, is written: “Under Duty Office No. 5748”; “To the duty general [3 illegible] hand over this matter”; in the left margin: “Duty office in the 12th a[rtillery] br[igade?] [?].”

3. Prince Mikhail Dmitrievich Gorchakov (born 20 September 1791, died 19 May 1861) — Tolstoy's third cousin once removed, a participant in the Battle of Borodino and the campaigns of 1812–1814, the Turkish War of 1828–1829, and the Polish campaign of 1830–1831. From 1831 he was chief of staff of the army under Field Marshal Paskevich. In June 1853 Gorchakov was appointed commander of the forces of the 3rd, 4th, and 5th Infantry Corps, which were to occupy the Danubian Principalities and the southern border of Bessarabia along the lower Danube. At the end of 1854 he was appointed commander-in-chief of the Southern Army stationed on the northwestern coast of the Black Sea and on the Prut River, and in February 1855 commander-in-chief of the Crimean Army. In January 1856 Gorchakov was appointed Viceroy of the Kingdom of Poland. He held this post until his death.

## Notes

[^1]: See petition no. 5.
[^2]: In the Tula provincial administration.
[^3]: The order enrolling Tolstoy as a Fireworker 4th Class in Battery No. 4 of the 20th Artillery Brigade, with seniority from 14 January, was issued on 13 February 1852. See note 4 to letter no. 58.
[^4]: Count N. N. Tolstoy retired in February 1853 with the rank of staff captain.
[^5]: Tolstoy means his fourth cousins Dmitry and Alexander Sergeyevich Gorchakov. On them see note 4 to letter no. 84.
