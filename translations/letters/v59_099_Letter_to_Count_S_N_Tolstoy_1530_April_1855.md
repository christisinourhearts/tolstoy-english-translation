*99. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 1855. April 15...30? Sevastopol.*

God knows why I do not write to you; part of the reason is that one does not feel like writing, and it is unpleasant to write in a place where today you do not know whether you will be alive tomorrow.

Balyuzek, [^1] who will deliver this letter to you, will tell you everything about me. I am very well here; I am so calm that I write a little at a time, and I do not play cards.—What made you think of entering not military service but the Rifle Regiment? It is true that they will take you into the active army as a junker, but couldn't you transfer into the active army from the Rifle Regiment?

Goodbye, dear friend; do not be angry with me for my silence, <!-- vol. 59, p. 312 --> and sometime write me a long letter. I shall write a long one as soon as things become quieter here. Please find out whom one should correspond with at the editorial office of *Sovremennik* and write back to me.

Your friend and brother, Count L. Tolstoy.

On page 8:

To His Serene Highness Count Sergey Nikolayevich Tolstoy.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Published here for the first time. The letter is dated on the basis of the words “lieutenant in the Rifle Regiment.” Tolstoy learned that Count S. N. Tolstoy had been enrolled in the Rifle Regiment from Count V. P. Tolstoy's letter of 9 April 1855. See note 7 to letter no. 97. The words “as soon as things become quieter here” indicate that the letter was written in Sevastopol.

## Notes

[^1]: Lev Fyodorovich Balyuzek. See note 6 to letter no. 96.
