\* 40. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 1851. March 1...15. Moscow.

Dear aunt!

Nicolas's arrival was a very pleasant surprise for me, since I had almost lost hope of seeing him arrive at my place. — I was so happy to see him that I even neglected my duties a little, or rather my habits, or, as Nikolai Sergeevich[^1] says, *not to let oneself be distracted in a crowd*; I allowed myself *to be distracted*. Now I am alone again, and alone in the literal sense: I go nowhere and receive no one. — I am making plans for the spring and summer; will you approve <!-- vol. 59, p. 92 --> of them? Toward the end of May I shall come to *Yasnoye*, spend a month or two there, and try to keep Nicolas there as long as possible, and then go with him on a tour of the Caucasus (that is, in case I come back Gros-Jean, just as before. —)

Nicolas told me a little piece of gossip about you, dear aunt; he told me that you were extremely afraid that I might make a bad marriage (a marriage of calculation). — Do you know, I am very surprised to see that you, who are so good and so noble, believe every possible horror about others, even about people whom you love. — Goodbye, dear aunt, I kiss your hands. — On rereading what I have just written to you, I have just put myself into a very unpleasant alternative: either to send you this letter, which will make you angry, or to wait until tomorrow to write to you. But, at bottom, although you will be a little angry, you will nevertheless be glad to receive news of me; I decide to send it, even at the cost of receiving a reprimand.

Leon.

On the envelope:

To Her High Nobility Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya. Tula. Chern.

Dear auntie!

Nikolenka's arrival was a very pleasant surprise for me, since I had almost lost hope that he would come to me. — I was so glad to see him that I even somewhat neglected my duties, or rather departed from my habit—*not to let oneself be distracted in a crowd*, as Nikolai Sergeevich says; I allowed myself *to be distracted*. Now I am alone again, and completely alone: I go nowhere and receive no one. — I am making plans for the spring and summer; will you approve them? Toward the end of May I shall come to *Yasnoye*, spend a month or two there, trying to keep Nikolenka there as long as possible, and then go with him to the Caucasus (all this in the event that nothing works out for me here).

Nikolenka gossiped to me a little about you, dear auntie; he said that you are terribly afraid that I might marry badly (that is, for calculation). You know, I am surprised that, with your kindness and nobility, you are ready to believe every nasty thing about others and even about those whom you love. — Goodbye, dear auntie, I kiss your hands.

Having reread what I wrote, I am debating whether to send this letter, which will make you angry, or to wait and write to you tomorrow. — However, although you will be a little angry, you will still be glad to have news of me. I decide to send it, even though I shall receive a reprimand. Lev.
