72. To N. A. Nekrasov. 26 December 1852. Starogladkovskaya Stanitsa.

26 December.

Dear Sir,

I am sending a short story; if you are willing to print it on the terms offered to me, then be so kind as to grant the following requests of mine: do not omit anything, do not add anything, and above all, do not change anything in it. If there is something in it that displeases you so much that you would not venture to print it without a change, then it would be better to postpone printing it and come to an understanding first.

If, contrary to expectation, the Censorship crosses out too much in this story, then please do not print it in a mutilated form, but return it to me. On the last page I have marked with × and * two variants that I made in two places about which I am afraid in this respect; look them over and insert them if you find that useful.

I believe that the notes I made on the last sheet, or at least some of them, are necessary for Russian readers.

I should also like the divisions I marked with a dash to remain that way in print.—

<!-- vol. 59, p. 222 --> Forgive me for the ugly and untidy way the manuscript is written; even writing it like that cost me terrible effort! [^1] Awaiting your reply and your opinion of this story, I have the honor to remain, with perfect respect, your most humble servant.

Count L. Tolstoy.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the LB. First printed by N. S. Ashukin in *AK*, pp. 189–190. The year is determined by the content: the letter concerns the story “The Raid,” printed in the March 1853 issue of *Sovremennik*.
