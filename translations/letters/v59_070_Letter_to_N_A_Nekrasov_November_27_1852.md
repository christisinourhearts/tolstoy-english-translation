70. To N. A. Nekrasov. 1852. November 27. Starogladkovskaya stanitsa.

Dear Sir!

I am very sorry that I cannot immediately fulfill your wish by sending something new for publication in our <!-- vol. 59, p. 214 --> journal; all the more so because I find the terms you offer me too advantageous to myself and agree to them completely.

Although I do have some things written, I cannot send you anything now: first, because some success of my first work has developed my authorial vanity, and I should like the subsequent ones to be no worse than the first; second, the cuts made by the censorship in *Childhood* have forced me, in order to avoid similar ones, to rework much of it anew. 1 — Without mentioning petty changes, I shall note two that struck me as especially unpleasant. They are the omission of the story of Natalya Savishna's love, which to some extent depicted the way of life of the old days and her character and lent humanity to her person; and the change of the title. — The title *Childhood* and a few words of preface explained the idea of the work; the title *The Story of My Childhood*, on the contrary, contradicts it. Who cares about the story of my childhood? The latter change is especially unpleasant to me because, as I wrote to you in my first letter, I wanted *Childhood* to be the first part of a novel, whose following parts were to be *Boyhood*, *Youth*, and *Young Manhood*.

I shall ask you, Dear Sir, to give me a promise concerning my future writing, if you are willing to continue accepting it for your journal—to change absolutely nothing in it. — I hope you will not refuse me this. As for me, I repeat my promise to send you the first thing I consider worthy of printing.

I sign with my surname, but ask that it be known only to the editorial office. —

With perfect respect

I have the honor to be,

Dear Sir,

Your most obedient servant,

Count L. N. Tolstoy.

P. S. Be so kind as to send me a copy of my story, if that is possible. —

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the LB. First printed <!-- vol. 59, p. 215 --> by N. S. Ashukin in AK, pp. 187—189. Dated by its content. After receiving Nekrasov's letter of October 30, 1852, cited above (see the note to letter no. 66), Tolstoy recorded in his diary under November 26: “received a letter from Nekrasov. They are giving me 50 silver rubles per sheet, and I want, without putting it off, to write stories about the Caucasus. Began today. I am too vain to write badly, but I hardly have it in me to write another good thing.” The next day he recorded: “Wrote a letter to Nekrasov and now I am calm on this score. Without hurrying I shall set about something.”
