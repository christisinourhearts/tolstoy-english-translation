*106. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 7 August 1855. Bakhchisaray.*

Aren't you ashamed not to answer me, when I know from Stolypin, [^1] who saw you, that you are in Petersburg? In spite of that I am still writing you a few lines to reassure you about me after the battle of the 4th, in which I took part and came out unhurt;—though in fact I did nothing, because my Mountain Artillery had no occasion to fire. Isn't it a sin for you not to write me? I should so much like to know how you are and what you are doing in your regiment. It seems to me we have not seen each other for a thousand years.—

If you are still in Petersburg, then please find out whether Zubkov, whom I owe money, is there, where he lives, and how much I owe Dussot [^2] and Sharmer, [^3] and their addresses. You might even send me their bills, explaining to them that this is all I am waiting for in order to pay them. Please do this. Goodbye, dear friend, keep well, and for once write me a longer letter.

Count L. Tolstoy.

And in literary matters too, how you could help me if you were in Petersburg, and letters now travel amazingly fast.—

7 August.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Four lines from the letter were first published by P. I. Biryukov in *B*, I, 1906, p. 251. The year is determined by the content: it refers to the battle at the Chernaya River on 4 August 1855.

## Notes

[^1]: Arkady Dmitrievich Stolypin. See note 8 to letter no. 90.
[^2]: Dussot—a restaurant in Petersburg.
[^3]: Sharmer—a very well-known Petersburg tailor in his day.
