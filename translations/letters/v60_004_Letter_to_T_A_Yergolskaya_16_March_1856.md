\* 4. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 1856. March 16. Petersburg.

Dear Aunt!

Forgive me for writing only a few words; I shall write at greater length another time, but at present I am trying not to be late for the post. The parcel you will receive with this letter is a portrait of Dmitry, which I had made for you, remembering that it was something you wished to have.[^1] — My health has been bad for nearly two weeks. I had rushes to the heart which had frightened me, but now the doctor says it is hemorrhoidal; I have stopped drinking coffee and vodka, and I feel much better. I try to go out in society as little as possible and to work as much as possible. There is plenty of desire and plenty of thoughts, but I do not know what will come of it.

I am making plans to go abroad in the spring. God knows whether I shall manage it — whether they will let me go and whether I shall have the money necessary for it. — What are you doing, dear Aunt, and how is your health? Farewell, I kiss your hands. In any case I shall see you at Yasnaya in May.[^2]

Your Léon.

16 March.

Please give the enclosed little note to the steward.[^3]

On the envelope:

To Her High Nobility Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya. Tula. Village of Yasnaya Polyana.

Dear Auntie!

Forgive me for writing only a few words; next time I shall write at greater length. I am hurrying today so as to catch the post. The parcel you will receive with this letter is a portrait of Mitenka; I ordered it for you, remembering that you wished to have it.[^4] — I was ill for about two weeks. I had rushes to the heart which had frightened me, but now <!-- vol. 60, p. 54 --> the doctor has said that it is hemorrhoidal; I stopped drinking coffee and vodka, and I feel much better. I try to go into society as little as possible and to work as much as possible.

There is plenty of desire and plenty of thoughts, but I do not know what will come of it.

I am making plans to go abroad in the spring. God knows whether I shall succeed. Whether they will let me go, and whether there will be the money necessary for it. — What are you doing, dear Auntie, and how is your health? Farewell, I kiss your little hands. In any case we shall see each other at Yasnaya in May.[^5]

Yours, Lev.

16 March.

Please give the enclosed note to the steward.[^6]

### Editorial notes

1. The year is determined from the contents and the postal postmark.

2. Tatyana Alexandrovna Yergolskaya (1792–1874) — Tolstoy's third cousin once removed and his foster mother. On her see vol. 59, pp. 12–13, and Tolstoy's *Recollections*.

## Notes

[^1]: This portrait of D. N. Tolstoy is unknown.
[^2]: On 17 May Tolstoy, having received eleven months' leave, left for Moscow, and on the 28th arrived at Yasnaya Polyana (see letter no. 12).
[^3]: The steward at Yasnaya Polyana at that time was Vasily Yermilovich Zyabrev (1824–1885, set free in 1859). Tolstoy described him in the fragment “Summer in the Country” (vol. 5, p. 264) and in the entry for 28 May 1856 in *A Landowner's Diary* (ibid., p. 249).
[^4]: This portrait of D. N. Tolstoy is unknown.
[^5]: On 17 May Tolstoy, having received eleven months' leave, left for Moscow, and on the 28th arrived at Yasnaya Polyana (see letter no. 12).
