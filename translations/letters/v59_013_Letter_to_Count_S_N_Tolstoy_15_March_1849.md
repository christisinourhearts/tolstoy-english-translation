*13. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 15 March 1849. Petersburg.*

Serge!

15 March.

I wrote to Basile and Pauline [^1] concerning various arrangements I asked him to make. First, I wrote to him to send me Fyodor with the things I left in Moscow; if this has not been done, I ask you to do it as quickly as possible. Second, I asked him, on receiving 900 silver rubles from Andrei, to pay my creditors. Now I ask you to write to me by the first post whether the money has been received or not. If you want to do me a service, tell them, that is, Orlov, [^2] Chevalier, [^3] Afanasy, [^4] [illegible][^5] and Vasenka, that since my intentions have changed I have had to establish myself in Petersburg, which has cost me a great deal of money; but that within a month at most I expect to satisfy them completely. And in fact it is already a long time since I wrote to Andrei to sell the forest and send 900 silver rubles to Moscow, which you or Basile will take upon yourselves to distribute to all these gentlemen.— — — I will not be at peace until these debts are paid. Goodbye. Apart from business I would very much like to know what all of you are doing.—Is Masha and “The Little Goat” [^6] still all the rage, or is there something new? You are surely taking part in the repetition of the masquerade for the Imperial family; [^7] for you provincials, after all, this is a novelty.

Seryozha!

15 March.

I wrote to Vasenka and Pauline [^8] about various matters that I asked him to take care of. 1. I wrote to him to send me Fyodor with the things I left in Moscow; if this has not been done, then please arrange it as quickly as possible. 2. I asked him, on receiving 900 rubles from Andrei, to settle with my creditors. Now I ask you to write by the first post whether the money has been received or not. If you want to do me a service, tell them, that is, Orlov, [^9] Chevalier, [^10] Afanasy [^11] [1 illegible] and Vasenka, that I changed my intention and was forced to settle in Petersburg, which cost me a great deal of money; but that in a month at most I expect to satisfy them completely. And in fact I wrote to Andrei long ago to sell the forest and send 900 silver rubles to Moscow, which you or Vasenka <!-- vol. 59, p. 37 --> will take the trouble to distribute to all these gentlemen. I will be at peace only when these debts are paid. Apart from business I would very much like to know what all of you are doing.—*Is Masha and “The Little Goat” [^12] still all the rage, or is there something new? You are surely taking part in the repetition of the masquerade for the Imperial family; [^13] for you provincials, after all, this is a novelty.*

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Published here for the first time.

2. The year is determined from the contents: the Savin forest had not yet been sold; it was sold in April 1849.

## Notes

[^1]: Vasily Stepanovich and Praskovya Fyodorovna Perfiliev. Tolstoy's letter to them is unknown. Concerning the sending of Fyodor to Petersburg, V. S. Perfiliev wrote to Tolstoy on 31 March–3 April 1849.
[^2]: V. S. Perfiliev wrote to Lev Nikolaevich on 31 March–3 April 1849 about Orlov, to whom Tolstoy had lost money at cards: “Of your creditors Orlov is troubling me; I brought him somewhat to his senses by giving him 15 silver rubles. As a specimen I am sending you his note under No. 1227; by the next post, if you like, I will send the remaining 1226 as well, on condition that you pay the postage” (letter unpublished; held in the ATB).
[^3]: Hippolyte Chevalier's hotel was in Staro-Gazetny Lane, in Markova's house, opposite the present Moscow Art Theatre. Tolstoy stayed at this hotel in 1856 and in 1862 with Sofya Andreevna. Chevalier's hotel is depicted by Tolstoy in the novel *The Decembrists* and, according to Sofya Andreevna, in *The Cossacks*.
[^4]: We cannot say who Afanasy was.
[^5]: Vasily Stepanovich and Praskovya Fyodorovna Perfiliev. Tolstoy's letter to them is unknown. Concerning the sending of Fyodor to Petersburg, V. S. Perfiliev wrote to Tolstoy on 31 March–3 April 1849.
[^6]: “The Little Goat” is the Russian song “The Little Goat Jumped into the Garden.”
[^7]: From 28 March to 10 April 1849 Nicholas I and his family stayed in Moscow, where festivities were held on the occasion of the completion of the Great Kremlin Palace. The masquerade in the palace was on 7 April.
[^8]: Vasily Stepanovich and Praskovya Fyodorovna Perfiliev. Tolstoy's letter to them is unknown. Concerning the sending of Fyodor to Petersburg, V. S. Perfiliev wrote to Tolstoy on 31 March–3 April 1849.
