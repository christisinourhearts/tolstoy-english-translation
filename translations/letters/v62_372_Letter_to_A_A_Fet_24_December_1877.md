*372. To A. A. Fet. 1877. December 24. Yasnaya Polyana.*

I have just received your letter, [^1] dear Afanasy Afanasich, and am terribly glad at the thought of seeing you, especially on New Year's Day, about which I am superstitious. So, at the end of the coming year, 1878, I shall either be terribly disappointed by something—if for some reason you do not come—or very glad. I have the *Critique of Pure Reason* [^2]—what I need is the *Critique of Practical Reason*. I think there is one.

<!-- vol. 62, p. 363 --> Everything is well with us, thank God. Our regards to Marya Petrovna. Please come.

From the hint in your letter I see that you are having troubles with your nephews again.

Goodbye.

Yours, L. Tolstoy.

24 December.

## Notes

[^1]: Of 22 December 1877, in which Fet asked that horses be sent to fetch him at Kozlovka on 1 January for the evening train.
[^2]: Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason*. Fet wrote on 22 December: “I bought it and am bringing it to you.”
