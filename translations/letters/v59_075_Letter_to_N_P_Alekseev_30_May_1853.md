75. To N. P. Alekseev. 1853. May 30. Starogladkovskaya Stanitsa.

Having no intention of continuing in the service, I have the honor most humbly to ask Your High Nobility to make a submission to the Lord Chief of Artillery on this matter, on the basis of Article 56 of Volume 5 of the Code of Military Regulations cited in this report.

Fireworker 4th Class

Count Lev Tolstoy.

30 May 1853.

Starogladkovskaya Stanitsa.

### Editorial notes

1. The whereabouts of the autograph are unknown. Printed from the text published by I. A. Yanzhul in the article “Toward a Biography of Count L. N. Tolstoy,” published in *Russkaya Starina*, 1900, II, p. 356. Tolstoy's text was written on a “report” of the following contents:

2. ADMINISTRATION

3. of the Chief of Artillery

4. of the Separate

5. Caucasian Corps.

6. No. 816.

7. 18 May 1853.

8. Tiflis.

9. To the Commander of Battery No. 4

10. of the 20th Artillery Brigade,

11. Lieutenant Colonel and Knight

12. Alekseev

13. From the Acting

14. Duty Staff Officer.

15. REPORT.

16. In accordance with the wish of Fireworker 4th Class Count Lev Tolstoy of the battery entrusted to you, and by order of the Lord Chief of Artillery, I have the honor to report that, on the basis of Article 56 of Volume 5 of the Code of Military Regulations, he may be discharged from the service without being accorded a military rank, and therefore, if he wishes this, would Your High Nobility be so good as to make the appropriate submission through the chain of command to the Lord Chief of Artillery in the prescribed manner, so that the proper disposition may be made.

17. Captain Mooro.

18. <!-- vol. 59, p. 235 --> In accordance with this report I propose that His Excellency Fireworker 4th Class Count Tolstoy inform me hereon.

19. Lieutenant Colonel Alekseev.

20. Tolstoy's text is found at the end of the text of the “report” and N. P. Alekseev's endorsement. Above Tolstoy's date, in the text of *Russkaya Starina*, stands “No. 18,” probably added by someone other than Tolstoy.

21. Grigory Alexandrovich Mooro, captain, duty staff officer in the administration of the Chief of Artillery of the Separate Caucasian Corps; in 1848 he served with the rank of lieutenant as brigade adjutant of the 20th Artillery Brigade (Yanzhul, II, p. 38).

22. In his diary under 16 April 1853 Tolstoy wrote: “I wanted to retire, but false shame at returning to Russia as a junker decisively holds me back. I shall wait for promotion, which is scarcely likely to come.” Under 28 May: “Today Alekseev sent me a paper by which Brimmer promises to release me on leave with a civil rank. When I remember my service, I involuntarily lose my temper.” The “paper” in this entry is, of course, Mooro's “report” of 18 May. Brimmer — Eduard Vladimirovich, Chief of Artillery of the Separate Caucasian Corps. On him, see note 2 to letter no. 49. Under 29 May Tolstoy's diary records: “Resolved to look over Article 56, retire, and asked Alekseev about it.” Article 56 of Book 1, Part II of the *Code of Military Regulations* (on service in the Military Department), ed. 1838, states: “If anyone wishing to enter military service does not agree to remain in it for the term that will be assigned by the Inspectorate Department after examination of his documents, he may obtain discharge from military service with the permission of division commanders without being accorded a military rank.” In the *Continuations of the Code of Military Regulations* issued through 1853, this article underwent no changes.

23. Mooro's report was probably an answer to a letter from Tolstoy to Mooro or Brimmer that has not come down to us. In response to Mooro's report N. P. Alekseev replied with a report, of which we learn from Mooro's letter to Tolstoy of 15 June 1853: “Most Gracious Sir Lev Nikolaevich! In consequence of your letter to me, received on the 11th of this June, I have the honor to inform you that concerning your discharge, owing to necessary domestic circumstances, from the service without being accorded a military rank, on the basis of Article 56 of Volume 5 of the Code of Military Regulations, a report under No. 665 was received on that same date from the battery commander, Lieutenant Colonel Alekseev, and as no obstacles to such a discharge are perceived, a submission will shortly follow from the Lord Chief of Artillery to the Lord Inspector of all Artillery, and from him to the Inspectorate Department of the War Ministry; the petition enclosed with your aforementioned letter, being unnecessary, I hereby return, and ask you to accept the assurance of my respect for you. G. Mooro. 15 June 1853. Tiflis.” (Letter unpublished; original in the ATB.)

24. Tolstoy's letter to Mooro and petition for discharge from the service mentioned in this letter are unknown.
