5. To the Rector of Kazan University, I. M. Simonov. 12 April 1847. Kazan.

To His Excellency Mr Rector of the Imperial Kazan University, Actual State Councillor and Knight Ivan Mikhailovich Simonov.

Petition of Count Lev Nikolaevich Tolstoy, a Student of the 2nd year of the Faculty of Law maintained at his own expense.

Because of impaired health and domestic circumstances, and not wishing to continue the course of studies at the University any longer, I most humbly ask Your Excellency to make whatever disposition depends upon you for removing me from the number of Students and issuing me all my documents.

To this petition Student Count Lev Tolstoy has set his hand.

12 April 1847.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the archive of Kazan University. First published by N. P. Zagoskin in the article “L. N. Tolstoy's Student Years” — *Istorichesky Vestnik*, 1894, no. 1, p. 121; facsimile (incomplete) in PTSO, before p. 1. Above Tolstoy's text is an annotation in an unknown hand: “Rec. 12 April 1847,” and another: “To the board.”

2. On Tolstoy's petition the board adopted the following resolution: “Remove Tolstoy from the lists of students and draw up a certificate concerning his time at the university.” A copy of the certificate issued to Tolstoy is held in the archive of Kazan University (first published by N. P. Zagoskin in the article “L. N. Tolstoy's Student Years” in *Istorichesky Vestnik*, 1894, no. 1, pp. 121–122):

3. <!-- vol. 59, p. 16 --> “The bearer of this, Count Lev Nikolayev son Tolstoy, having first received his education at home and passed the appropriate examination in the subjects of the full gymnasium course, was admitted as a student of Kazan University in the division of Arabic-Turkish literature, in the first year; but with what success he studied in that year is unknown, because he did not appear for the annual examinations, for which reason he was left in the same year, and on the basis of permission No. 3919 of the administrator of the Kazan educational district of 13 September 1845 was transferred from the division of Arabic-Turkish literature to the first year of the Faculty of Law, in which he studied with the following results: excellent in logic and psychology; good in encyclopedia of law, history of Roman law, and Latin; satisfactory in general and Russian history, theory of eloquence, and German. He was transferred to the second year, but with what success he studied in that year is unknown, because there were no annual examinations. His conduct, Tolstoy's, during his time at the university was excellently good. Now, in accordance with the petition submitted on 12 April of the current year, he has been released from the university because of impaired health and domestic circumstances; therefore he, Mr Tolstoy, as one who has not completed the full university course of studies, cannot enjoy the rights assigned to actual students, but, on the basis of article 590 of volume III of the Code of Laws (1842 ed.), upon entering civil service is equated in privileges of promotion by rank with persons educated in secondary educational institutions and belongs to the second category of civil officials. In attestation of which this certificate has been given to him, Count Lev Tolstoy, by the board of the Imperial Kazan University, with the requisite signatures and application of the official seal, on the basis of the charter most graciously granted to Kazan University, on plain paper.”

4. Tolstoy himself later spoke as follows about leaving the university: “I was little interested in what our teachers lectured on in Kazan. At first I spent about a year studying Oriental languages, but made very little progress. I gave myself passionately to everything, read an endless number of books, but always in one and the same direction. When some question interested me, I did not turn away from it either to the right or to the left and tried to acquaint myself with everything that could shed light on that one question. So it was with me in Kazan as well. There were two reasons for my leaving the university: 1) my brother had finished his course and was leaving; 2) strange as it may be to say, my work on the *Nakaz* and *Esprit des lois* [*The Spirit of the Laws*] (I have it now) opened to me a new field of independent intellectual work, while the university, with its requirements, not only did not assist such work but hindered it.” (B, I, 1911, pp. 137–138).

5. Tolstoy spent two years and seven months at Kazan University.

6. The *Nakaz* was a work by Catherine intended to serve as a guide for the “Commission for Drafting a New Code” convened in Moscow in 1767. On Tolstoy's work on the *Nakaz*, see volume 46. *The Spirit of the Laws* is a work by the French writer Montesquieu (1689–1755).

7. Ivan Mikhailovich Simonov (1794–1855), astronomer, studied at Kazan University; from 1812 he was a master's graduate, from 1814 adjunct professor in the chair of astronomy; in 1816 extraordinary professor, and from 1822 <!-- vol. 59, p. 17 --> ordinary professor; from 1841 honored ordinary professor. In 1847 he was rector of Kazan University. A well-known astronomer in his time, Simonov, at the proposal of the Academy of Sciences, undertook a two-year scientific voyage (1819–1821) in the southern hemisphere. He was repeatedly sent abroad, was a member of sixteen learned societies, and published a number of works on astronomy in Russian and foreign journals.
