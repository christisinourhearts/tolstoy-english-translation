*14. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 1...25 March 1849. Petersburg.*

Seryozha!

First, please ask Lvov [^1] to have one of his colleagues attend to my case with the Krapivinskys concerning the demarcation of the Goncharova estate; [^2] it is before the general assembly.—Second, in my last letter I order Andrei to sell all the horses.—I think sales at auction in Moscow are good now; if you are of the same opinion, write to Andrei about it and take care of this. I think that what they will sell them for now and what was received from the earlier sale will amount to your whole debt (under the division), and on this occasion we will be quits. [^3] <!-- vol. 59, p. 38 --> Third, for God's sake write me at least two words; it is becoming ridiculous—an entire month without Fyodor, without half a word from anyone. I am studying seriously for the examination. [^4]—I was at Pushkin's. [^5] He [^6] is very favorably disposed toward me.—Goodbye.

For the first time I am worried.—I keep thinking that something has happened to someone in our family.—

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the State Tolstoy Museum. Published here for the first time. The letter is dated from the preceding letter and from Count S. N. Tolstoy's reply of 31 March 1849.

## Notes

[^1]: Prince Georgy Vladimirovich Lvov. On him, see the introductory note to letter no. 94.
[^2]: On the general-survey plan and in the economic notes of 1833 to it, among the holdings of “Lieutenant Colonel Countess M. N. Tolstaya (that is, Tolstoy's mother) and Major Ivan Ivanovich Krapivin” is listed the “Gonchurovka waste ground” (arable land 3 desyatinas 1960 sazhens; hayfields 7 des. 2000 sazhens; forest 43 des. 1671 sazhens; unusable land 4 des. 1933 sazhens; total 60 des. 364 sazhens), lying “on the right side of the Goncharovsky ravine and on both sides of the high road from Efremov to Tula.” Tolstoy writes about this waste ground (or “estate”), in connection with whose demarcation he was evidently conducting a case with the children of I. I. Krapivin, whom official documents also call “Krapivinsky.” Ivan Ivanovich and Elena Dmitrievna Krapivin had six sons—Valerian (b. 1814), Alexander (b. 1816), Orest (b. 1817), Arkady (b. 1827), and Ivan (b. 1831)—and two daughters, Anna (b. 1815) and Ekaterina (b. 1835).
[^3]: Concerning the sale of the horses, Count S. N. Tolstoy replied in a letter of 31 March 1849: “As for your horses, if you want, I will try to sell them at the fair in Bogoroditsk, which will be on 20 April; I cannot sell them at auction in Moscow, because, alas, I am in Pirogovo...” Concerning the debt, Sergei Nikolaevich wrote: “Explain to me, I ask you, what you mean when you say that when you sell the horses you will be quits with me over the debt under the division deed. I confess, I do not understand it.” (Letter unpublished; held in the ATB.)
[^4]: Tolstoy was preparing for examinations at Petersburg University for the degree of Candidate of Law. See letter no. 15.
[^5]: Mikhail Nikolaevich Musin-Pushkin. On him, see note 8 to letter no. 12.
[^6]: Deleted: *is patronizing [me]*.
