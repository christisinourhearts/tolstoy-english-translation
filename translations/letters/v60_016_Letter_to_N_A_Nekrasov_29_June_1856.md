16. To N. A. Nekrasov. 1856. June 29. Ya. P.

I am writing only so as not to miss the post in answering your letter[^1] and to ask the following. Dmitry Kolbasin[^2] needs about 800 rubles’ worth of paper for the publication of *Childhood* and *Boyhood*, as he writes.[^3] Could you not get this paper from your supplier on two months’ credit against my receipt, after which I shall send him or you the money? My teeth hurt terribly, which is why this letter is so wild. I liked your latest little poem very much.[^4] I have not seen my sister yet, but shall see her in the next few days and show it to her. I received from my brother Nikolai his *Hunting Notes* — about three printer’s sheets. In the next few days I shall show them to Turgenev, but in my opinion they are delightful. I wrote to my brother asking him to allow me to print them, and then you will have a splendid piece for the ninth issue.[^5] To my shame, I myself am writing nothing. Yesterday I began.[^6] Please do not be so downhearted, and write me even as absurdly as I do; however, on Saturday I shall write more carefully.[^7] Goodbye. God grant that you grow healthier physically and morally, and rosier.

Yours, Count L. Tolstoy.

29 June.

### Editorial notes

1. First published in *Sovremennik*, 1913, no. 8, pp. 268–269.

## Notes

[^1]: This letter of Nekrasov’s is unknown. Judging by the Diary entry, it was received on 26 June 1856.
[^2]: Dmitry Yakovlevich Kolbasin (died after 1895) — brother of the fiction writer and literary historian Elisey Yakovlevich Kolbasin (1831–1885). D. Ya. Kolbasin was close to the circle of *Sovremennik* contributors in the 1850s and carried out their publishing errands (see vol. 47, p. 322).
[^3]: In a letter of 21 June 1856 D. Ya. Kolbasin asked for about 300 rubles to be sent for the purchase of paper, so that he could settle with the supplier (not 800, as Tolstoy writes).
[^4]: It has not been established which poem Nekrasov sent in the letter to which Tolstoy is replying.
[^5]: “Hunting in the Caucasus,” sketches by Count N. N. Tolstoy, were printed in *Sovremennik*, 1857, book 1, pp. 169–232.
[^6]: Tolstoy means the revision of *Youth* that he had begun. See the Diary entry under 28 June (vol. 47, p. 84).
[^7]: Tolstoy wrote to Nekrasov on Saturday, 2 July (see letter no. 18).
