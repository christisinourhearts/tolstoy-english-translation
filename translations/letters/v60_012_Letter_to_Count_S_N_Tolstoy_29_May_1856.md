\* 12. To Count S. N. Tolstoy. 1856. May 29. Yasnaya Polyana.

I am writing a few words from Yasnaya, where I arrived yesterday. What a misfortune. Natalya Petrovna[^1] makes me regret it still more with her stories about you: as though you say, “Well, Natasha, lay out the cards: will Lyovochka come or not?” The thing is, do not be angry with me. I received my leave only in the middle of May, and then only by means of various contrivances, despite the fact that they had promised it to me every [day]. In Moscow I spent 10 days through my own fault, but they were extraordinarily pleasant — without champagne and gypsies, but a little in love — with whom, I shall tell you later.[^2] I shall remain at Yasnaya until August, and therefore shall meet you and see you off.[^3] — In August, if there is money (the leave has now come through), I shall go abroad. —

I am writing to you at Kursk, and I confess the chief object of my letter is Shcherbachovka:[^4] what do you intend to do with it? I intend without fail to sell my share, even at 10 percent less, but so as to get the money sooner. I should like and hope that you too will agree. None of us is in a position to buy, nor can we manage it from 500 versts away, and none of us has ever had money either. I particularly want this not in order to pay debts — I have almost none — nor do I want it in order to spend the money, but in order to be in an independent and unconstrained position. I wanted at least to learn from Auntie your opinion of my “Hussars,” but they say you have not read it yet. Write how many points you give me for it: 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5. Today I am going to Masha's,[^5] shall return by Trinity Day, and then for two months I shall sit at Yasnaya without going anywhere. —

Please answer. Until we meet, dear friend. —

Yours, Count L. Tolstoy.

29 May.

### Editorial notes

1. <!-- vol. 60, p. 63 --> An excerpt from the letter was first printed in B. I, 1, p. 294; a somewhat fuller text was printed in PTS, I, p. 53. The year is determined from the contents: Tolstoy arrived at Yasnaya Polyana “yesterday,” that is, on 28 May.

## Notes

[^1]: Natalya Petrovna Okhotnitskaya — an impoverished noblewoman who lived with the Tolstoys.
[^2]: Tolstoy means his infatuation with Princess Alexandra Alexeyevna Obolenskaya, née Dyakova — sister of his friend D. A. Dyakov.
[^3]: See note 6 to letter no. 17.
[^4]: Shcherbachovka — an estate of D. N. Tolstoy in Sudzha district, Kursk province, which came to him under the deed of division of 1847 and, after his death, passed to his brothers Nikolai, Sergei, and Lev.
[^5]: M. N. Tolstaya lived with her husband Count V. P. Tolstoy at Pokrovskoye, the estate of his mother in Chern district, Tula province, 90 kilometers from Yasnaya Polyana.
