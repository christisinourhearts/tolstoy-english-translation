*16. To A. I. Sobolev (draft). 1849. March 15...31? St Petersburg.*

Andrei Ilyich,

Everything you do is wrong.—Tell me, for heaven's sake, why on earth should I sell the timber to Kopylov in a lump when it is twice as profitable to sell it by the desyatina? No matter how much you swear that everything is as it should be, something or other is still wrong.—Deliver the parcel enclosed herewith to Sergey Nikolayevich.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from a rough draft written by Tolstoy across a letter from Count S. N. Tolstoy to Lev Nikolayevich dated 9 March 1849. Published here for the first time. Dated from Count S. N. Tolstoy's letter.

2. Andrei Ilyich Sobolev was probably a freed serf, since already in the 1830s official papers call him a “servant” of the Tolstoys.

3. <!-- vol. 59, p. 41 --> In a rough draft connected with *A Russian Landowner's Novel* and entitled “Characters and Persons,” Tolstoy gives a characterization of A. I. Sobolev. “He is a master at going to court,” Tolstoy writes; “it gave him great pleasure to tangle up a straightforward case, to discuss profoundly with the secretary the meaning of Article 365... But when he goes on a drinking bout—then there is trouble! He strips naked and goes—winter or summer, it makes no difference—to one particular flower bed in the garden, lies there on his stomach and weeps... In matters concerning the landowner's estate, family affairs, and public business, Andrei Ilyich is in essence a spendthrift, a despot, cruel, and generally a great scoundrel, but he is kind and intelligent.” (See vol. 4, pp. 377–378.) A. I. Sobolev is also mentioned by Tolstoy in chapter III of *Recollections of Childhood*. Sobolev remained manager of Yasnaya Polyana until July–August 1852, when he was dismissed for drunkenness.

4. On this same letter from Count S. N. Tolstoy, alongside drawings and pen trials, Tolstoy wrote:

5. “Dear Aunt, you cannot imagine how much pain your silence causes me. [Dear auntie, you have no idea how much you distress me by your silence.]

6. ~~Catiline, secondly, by all who~~ [Catiline, secondly, by all, who]

7. For the student—find out about notebooks

8. Girganzen [?] To Pushkin Kosten dispatch. to Andreyev Write a letter to Vas. Milyutin

9. To Fersen—about service Herzen Fersen Fersen Herzen Kostinka Polivanov Pushkin Sazanov Safonov Peter the First Cather. Cath. Catherine II”

10. Pushkin—probably Mikhail Nikolayevich Musin-Pushkin. Fersen—a friend of Tolstoy's, Baron G. E. Fersen. Kostinka—K. A. Islavin.
