\* 28. To an Unknown Woman. Draft. 1850. October... November. Yasnaya Polyana.

Madam,

Your kind letter, although dated September 12, did not reach me until the 25th. Presuming that my reply would take just as long, I decided to reply to you in Moscow. In no case would I have delayed going in response to your kind invitation, by which I am as touched as can be and which was entirely in keeping with my very sincere desire to see you, if my brother Dmitri had told me ~~at which estate~~ where you intend to spend these few months ~~is at~~ or in some other place. I even sent to make inquiries on the subject, but no one could tell me anything certain; it was therefore only from your letter that I learned that it was at your estate at Alexin,[^1] very near my home—that you were expecting me. I was ready to leave when, on the eve of my departure, a terrible fire in my village deprived me, over and above all the other ~~inconveniences~~ things, of the pleasure I had expected from my visit to you. But what is delayed is not lost; I shall make this visit to you in Moscow, where I expect to spend the first days of December.

Madam,

your kind letter, although it is dated September 12, reached me only on the 25th. Supposing that my reply would take just as long, I decided to reply to you in Moscow.

<!-- vol. 59, p. 58 --> I would in no case have delayed in coming at your kind invitation, which touched me as much as possible and fully answered my most sincere desire to see you, if my brother Dmitri had told me where you expected to spend these few months, or whether you would be somewhere else. I even sent someone to inquire about this, but they could tell me nothing definite. Thus it was only from your letter that I learned that you were expecting me at your Alexin estate, so close to me.

I was already supposed to leave when, on the eve of my departure, a terrible fire in my village deprived me, besides everything else, of the pleasure I had expected from my journey to you. But what has been postponed is not lost; I shall visit you in Moscow, where I expect to spend the first days of December.

### Editorial notes

1. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. Published here for the first time. The letter is dated from the handwriting and contents. The handwriting is from the late 1840s—early 1850s. From the contents of the letter it is evident that it was written after September 25 and no later than November. The year of the letter is determined by the entry about the fire at Yasnaya Polyana in the diary under December 8, 1850 (see vol. 46, p. 38).

2. It has not been possible to determine the identity of the person to whom the letter was addressed.

## Notes

[^1]: Alexin — a district town of Tula province.
