*27. To T. A. Yergolskaya. 20 April 1850. Pirogovo.*

It has been a long time since I have had any news from you, dear Aunt, and now it is my turn to worry about you. Serge and I are both well: he is at Pirogovo, where I could not join him today so that I might finish my devotions. I congratulate you, as well as Marie, Valerian, and Aunt Lise, on the Easter holidays. How is Aunt Lise? My compliments to Mlle Vergani. [^1]

The Arsenievs [^2] are extremely anxious to have her in their household and have asked me to find out her terms.—They will agree to whatever terms she wishes to propose. For my part I advise her [unclear] to enter that household; not to mention the pleasure we shall have in [1 illegible] her often, the Arsenievs are, at bottom, very good people.

Goodbye, I kiss your hands. When will you come?

C. L. Tolstoy.

20 April

18

### Editorial notes

1. It has been a long time since I have had any news of you, dear auntie, and it is my turn to worry about you. Seryozha and I are well; he is in Pirogovo; I did not go there with <!-- vol. 59, p. 57 --> him today so that I could finish my devotions. I congratulate you, Mashenka, Valerian, and Aunt Liza on the Easter holiday. How is Aunt Liza's health? My regards to Mlle Vergani. [^3]—The Arsenievs [^4] very much want her to enter their service and asked me to find out her terms.—They will agree to whatever she asks. I advise her to go to them. Quite apart from the pleasure it will give us to see her often, the Arsenievs are basically good people.

2. Goodbye, I kiss your hands. When will you come?

3. Count L. Tolstoy.

4. 20 April.

5. Printed from the autograph held in the ATB. Published here for the first time. The year is determined by the content: Tolstoy congratulates them on the Easter holiday, which in 1850 fell on 23 April.
