<!-- vol. 42, p. 356 -->

19 December

*The true good is always in our hands. It follows a good life as a shadow follows an object.*

1

> Everything that can make us better and happier God has placed right in front of us or near us.

> *Seneca.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 357 -->

2

> There is no body so strong and healthy that it would never fall ill; there is no wealth that would never be lost; there is no power so high that no one would dig beneath it. All this is perishable and fleeting, and a person who has placed his life in all this will always be anxious, afraid, grieved, and suffering. He will never attain what he desires, but will fall into the very thing he wants to avoid.

> The human soul alone is safer than any impregnable fortress. Why then do we try in every way to weaken this, our only stronghold? Why do we occupy ourselves with things that cannot bring us joy of the soul, and not care about the one thing that can give peace to our soul?

> We keep forgetting that if our conscience is clear, no one can harm us, and that it is only from our unreason and the desire to possess external trifles that all sorts of quarrels and enmities arise.

> *Epictetus.*

3

> A person who has placed his life in spiritual perfecting cannot be dissatisfied, because what he desires is always in his power.

> *Pascal.*

4

> Happiness, true happiness, is virtue itself.

> *Spinoza.*

5

The activity of people who do not understand true life is always directed toward acquiring pleasures, delivering themselves from sufferings, and keeping inevitable death away from themselves.

But the desire for pleasures increases the intensity of the struggle, increases sensitivity to sufferings, and brings death nearer. To hide from themselves the approach of death, such people know only one means: to increase their pleasures more and more.

For people who do not understand life, the chief cause of these sufferings lies in their considering as pleasure that which cannot be evenly distributed among all people and must be taken from others by force. And taking from others by force what they need destroys the possibility of that goodwill toward all, that state of love, which alone gives people the true good.

And therefore, the more intense the activity directed toward attaining such pleasures, the more impossible becomes the only good accessible to man—love.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 358 --> But pleasures have a limit, beyond which they pass into sufferings and the fear of a death that comes nearer and nearer.

6

> There are two kinds of happy state of soul: 1) tranquility of spirit (a clear conscience); 2) an always cheerful heart. — The first is created, on condition that a person is conscious of no guilt on his part, by a clear notion of the nothingness of earthly goods; the second is a gift of nature.

> *Kant.*

7

> To make every moment of life as good as possible, from whatever hand of fate, favorable or unfavorable, it falls to our lot—this is the art of living and the true advantage of a rational being.

> *Lichtenberg.*

8

> The surest and purest joys of human life are attained without turmoil of the soul and are remembered without pangs of conscience.

> *John Ruskin.*

He who says that in doing good he feels unhappy either does not believe in God, or what he does and considers good is not good.
