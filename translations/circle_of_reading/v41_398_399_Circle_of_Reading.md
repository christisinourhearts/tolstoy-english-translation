<!-- vol. 41, p. 398 -->

14 June

How little effort is needed in order not to condemn one's neighbor, and how life is made easier for the one who does not condemn others. And how few people make this effort.

1

In the lives of the saints there is a story of how an elder saw in a dream a dead monk, a man weak in his life, in the best place in paradise, and, when he asked for what this unworthy monk with many weaknesses had been granted so great a good, received the answer: for not having condemned anyone in his life.

2

> Therefore you are inexcusable, every man who judges another, for by the same judgment by which you judge another you condemn yourself, because, judging another, you do the same.

> *Romans 2:1.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 399 -->

3

> Do not condemn the actions of others. In condemning others we trouble ourselves in vain and fall into great errors. Look deeply into yourselves, and your labor will not be in vain.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

4

> *The more strictly and mercilessly you condemn yourself, the more justly and leniently you will judge others.*

> *Confucius.*

5

> Do not seek glory in the dishonor of others.

> It befits a kind person to conceal the shame of others, even of those who have done him harm.

> Do not remind one who repents of his former transgressions.

> *The Talmud.*

6

> It is easy to notice the errors of others, but hard to notice one's own; people like to pick over the mistakes of their neighbors, but hide their own, as a thief tries to hide his picklock.

> A man likes to condemn others; he looks only at the mistakes of people, and his own passions grow more and more, drawing him away from improvement.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

7

> As long as you yourself are sinful, do not say a word about the sins of others.

> *Tablets of the Babis.*

Break yourself of condemning in words, and you will feel in your soul an increase in the capacity for love, you will feel an increase of life and of the good.
