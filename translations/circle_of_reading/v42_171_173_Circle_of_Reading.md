<!-- vol. 42, p. 171 -->

25 October

A person who is conscious of his calling is by that very fact conscious of his dignity. And only a religious person is conscious of his calling.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 172 -->

1

> A king asked a holy man: do you think of me? The holy man answered: I do, when I forget God.

> *Saadi.*

2

> *We honor God each time we feel the life of our neighbors as we feel our own life.*

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

3

> Nothing so characterizes a person as his treatment of fools.

> *Amiel.*

4

One need only free oneself from the duty of respect toward one person on the ground that he is bad, stupid, incorrigible, and there will no longer be any limit to the disrespect for people that we will allow ourselves.

5

> It is time at last for a person to know his own worth. Why, really, is he some sort of illegitimate creature? Is it fitting for him to hide and look timidly about on all sides? No, let my head sit firmly and upright on my shoulders. Life is given me not for show, but so that I may live it. I am conscious of my duty to speak the truth, the pure truth, at every crossroads. I must care not about people's opinion of me, but about my true calling.

> *Emerson.*

6

> Freedom of the person is the greatest thing; on it and only on it can the freedom of a people grow. A person must respect his freedom in himself and honor it no less than in his neighbor, than in a whole people.

> *Herzen.*

Only a person who is conscious of himself as a spiritual being can be conscious of the human dignity of himself and of other people, and only such a person will not degrade either himself or his neighbor by an act or a position unworthy of a human being.
