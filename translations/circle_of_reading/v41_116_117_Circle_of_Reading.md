<!-- vol. 41, p. 116 -->

19 February

It is a sin not to work because you can live without working.

1

Nothing ennobles a person as labor does. Without labor a person cannot preserve his human dignity. That is just why idle people care so much about outward grandeur: they know that without this setting people would despise them.

2

> *It is physically impossible that a truly religious understanding and pure morality should exist in those classes of the people who do not earn their bread by the labor of their own hands*.

> *John Ruskin.*

3

You have only to accept the truth wholly and repent wholly in order to understand that in the matter of life no one has or can have rights, advantages, or special privileges, while to duties there is no end and no limit, and that the first and indubitable duty of a person is participation in the struggle with nature for his own life and the life of other people.

4

> One of the indubitable and pure joys is rest after labor.

> *Kant.*

5

> Rich and poor, strong and weak, every person who does not work is a scoundrel. Every person must learn a craft, real manual labor. Only by working can one know one of the best, pure joys. Rest after labor and this joy are the greater, the harder the labor.

> *After Rousseau.*

6

> Work constantly, do not regard work as a misfortune for yourself, and do not wish for praise for it.

> *Marcus Aurelius*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 117 -->

7

> The most outstanding gifts are ruined by idleness.

> *Montaigne.*

Justice requires that you take from people no more than you give them. But there is no possibility of weighing your own labors and the labors of others that you make use of; besides, at any hour you may lose the ability to labor and will have to make use of the labors of others. And therefore try to give more than you take, so as not to be unjust.
