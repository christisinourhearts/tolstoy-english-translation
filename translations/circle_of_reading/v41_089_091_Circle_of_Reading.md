7 February

Self-perfection is both inner work and outer. A person cannot perfect himself without association with people, and without their influence on him and his on them.

1

> Three temptations torment people: the lusts of the body, pride, and love of wealth. From this—the calamities of people. Without lusts, pride, and covetousness all people would live happily. How, then, can one be rid of these terrible diseases? It is hard to be rid of them, chiefly because their germ is in our very nature.

> To rid oneself of them there is only one means: the work of each person on himself. It is often thought that laws and governments can help, but this cannot be, because laws are written and people are ruled by people of the same kind, suffering from the same temptations of lust, pride, and covetousness. And therefore one cannot rely on laws and rulers. And therefore the one thing people can do for their good is the destruction in themselves of lust, and pride, and covetousness. No improvement is possible until each person begins this improvement with himself.

> *After Lamennais.*

2

> To learn patience one must practice as much as in studying music, and yet we, as soon as the teacher comes, as soon as a chance falls to us to learn a little patience, run away from the lesson.

> *John Ruskin.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 90 -->

3

*“Be perfect, as your heavenly father is perfect,” it is said in the Gospel. This does not mean that Christ commands a person to be the same as God, but it means that every person must try to draw near to divine perfection.*

4

> Perfection without any admixture—this is God; drawing near to God—this is the life of man. He who constantly strives for his own perfecting is reasonable and can distinguish good from evil. And when a person knows that good is good and evil is evil, he cleaves to good and withdraws from evil.

> *Confucius.*

5

> However little educated I may be, I can walk on the path of reason. The one thing I need to fear is self-conceit. The highest reason is very simple, but people do not understand it, because they think that they understand what they do not understand.

> *After Laozi.*

6

> Strange! A person is indignant at evil that comes from outside, from others—at what he cannot remove, but does not struggle with his own evil, although this is always in his power.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

7

> If the time and the energies that are now spent on attacks against the rich and on attempts to devise means for changing the existing order of life and for establishing a just division of wealth were spent on the work of self-perfection, then there would quickly come that change for the better in our state, social, and moral life which we so desire. Let humanity learn to think rightly—and our world would become as happy as it is now unhappy. But the people do not want to know the truth that liberates, because it is contrary to the state and religious errors to which they have grown accustomed.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

There is nothing more harmful for oneself and others than activity directed exclusively at improving one's animal life, and there is nothing more beneficial for oneself and others than activity directed at improving one's soul.
