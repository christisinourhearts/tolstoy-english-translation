21 May

In order to believe in good, one must begin to do it.

1

*Adorn each passing day with a good deed.*

2

> It is best of all to begin each day like this: on waking, to think whether it might not be possible to bring joy that day to at least one person.

> *Nietzsche.*

3

> Kindness is our duty. He who often fulfills it and sees how his good intentions are realized ends by actually beginning to love the one for whom he has done good. The words “Love your neighbor as yourself” do not mean that you must first come to love him and only then, as a consequence of your love, do him good. No, you must do good to your neighbor, and this activity of yours will kindle in you a love for humanity, which will be the consequence of your activity directed toward good.

> *Kant.*

4

> A good will is good not by what it accomplishes—not by its fitness for reaching some intended goal, but in itself; considered in itself, without any comparison, <!-- vol. 41, p. 339 --> it has a far higher worth than everything that could ever be accomplished by means of it for the benefit of anyone at all, even for the benefit of all people. If by some particular misfortune or because of too meager abilities such a will were completely deprived of the possibility of carrying out its intention, if with its greatest exertion nothing were nevertheless done by it, and there remained only the good will alone (not, of course, as a mere bare wish, but as the use of all the means that are within our power), then even in that case such a will would still shine for itself like a precious diamond, as something in which, in itself, the greatest worth is contained.

> *After Kant.*

5

> No one can have a notion of good until he has begun to do it. And no one can truly love it until he has done it often and with sacrifice. And no one can find peace in it until he does it always.

> *Martineau.*

6

> If you have done your neighbor evil, even a small one, regard it as great; and if you have done him great good, regard it as unimportant; but a small good shown to you by another, regard as great.

> God's blessing will come down on him who gives to the poor; a double blessing will rest on him who, in doing so, greets him and sees him off kindly.

> *The Talmud.*

7

In doing good, be grateful for it.

8

> Know firmly and feel deeply that you must devote every day of your life to the good of others, doing for them all that you can. Doing, not chattering.

> *John Ruskin.*

If you cannot train yourself to look for an occasion to do good as a hunter looks for his game, then at least do not let an occasion to do good slip by.
