<!-- vol. 42, p. 76 -->

25 September

Labor is not a virtue, but an unavoidable condition of a virtuous life.

1

> Always remember the great and unchanging truth that what you have no one else can have, and that every particle of any substance that you use or consume is a particle of human life.

> *John Ruskin.*

2

There is labor that is unnecessary, fussy, impatient, irritable, hindering others and drawing attention to itself. Such labor is much worse than idleness. Real labor is always quiet, even, unnoticeable.

3

What you can do yourself, do not lay on another. Let everyone sweep in front of his own door. And if each one does this, the whole street will be clean.

4

> There are only three ways in which a man can acquire wealth: by labor, by begging, and by stealing. And if those who labor receive so little, it is only because too much falls to the share of beggars and thieves.

> *Henry George.*

5

If a man living alone excuses himself from the duty of struggling with nature, he is at once punished in that his body perishes. But if a man excuses himself from this duty by forcing other people to fulfill it, he is at once punished in that the movement of perfecting proper to man comes to a stop.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 77 -->

6

> *It is not enough that you are industrious! What are you laboring at?*

> *Thoreau.*

7

If there is an idle man, then there is another man laboring beyond his strength. If there is a man who is surfeited, then there is another—a hungry one.

Most of the occupations of idle people that they consider labors are amusement, not only not lightening the labor of others but laying new labors on them. Such are all luxurious amusements.
