<!-- vol. 42, p. 385 -->

29 December

As long as there is violence, there will also be war. Violence can be conquered not by violence, but by nonresistance to it and nonparticipation in it. But what will that better humanity then think of the so-called refined civilization of which we are so proud? Almost the same as we think of the ancient Mexican people and its cannibalism, at once warlike, pious, and bestial.

*Letourneau.*

1

> *If my soldiers began to think, not one would remain in the army.*

> *Frederick II.*

2

The savage instinct of military killing has for thousands of years been so carefully cultivated and encouraged that it has put down deep roots in the human brain. One must hope, however, that a society better than ours will be able to free itself from this terrible crime.

3

> And I understood discipline, namely that the corporal is always right when he speaks to a soldier, the sergeant—when he speaks to a corporal, the noncommissioned officer—when he speaks to a sergeant, and so on up to the field marshal, even if they were to say that two times two is five. At first this is hard to understand, but understanding it is helped by the fact that in every barracks there hangs a placard, and it is read through in order to clarify one's thoughts. On this placard is written everything that a soldier may wish to do, such as, for example, to return to his village, to refuse to perform his service, not to submit to his superior, and so on, and for all this punishments are set down: the death penalty or five years of penal servitude.

> *Erckmann-Chatrian.*

4

> I bought a Negro, he is mine. He works like a horse; I feed him badly, dress him the same way, and beat him when he does not obey.

> *Anatole France.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 386 --> What is surprising in this? Do we treat our soldiers any better? Are they not deprived of freedom just as this Negro is? The only difference is that a soldier costs much less. A good Negro now costs at least 500 écus; a good soldier costs barely 50. Neither the one nor the other can leave the place where they are kept; both the one and the other are beaten for the slightest mistake; the pay is almost the same; but the Negro has the advantage over the soldier in that he does not expose his life to danger, but spends it with his wife and children.

War will be destroyed only when people take no part whatever in violence and are ready to bear all the persecutions to which they may be subjected for this. This is the one means of destroying war.
