<!-- vol. 42, p. 137 -->

11 October

People for the most part are proud not of what is worthy of respect, but of what is unnecessary or harmful to them: power, wealth.

1

There is no scoundrel who, having searched, would not find scoundrels worse than himself in some respect, and who therefore could not find a reason to be pleased with himself.

2

> He who does not himself know how to read and write cannot teach this to others; how then can he who does not know what he himself should do point out to people what they should do?

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> There are people who have barely listened to wise instructions before they themselves begin to instruct others. They do the same thing as a sick stomach, which at once throws up the food it has taken. Do not imitate such people. First digest well within yourself what you have heard, and do not throw it up before its time—otherwise such filth will come out as can serve no one for food.

> *Epictetus.*

4

Pride is not at all the same as the consciousness of human dignity. Pride increases with external success; the consciousness of one's human dignity, on the contrary, with external humiliation.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 138 -->

5

A proud man respects not himself but the opinion that people form of him; a man with the consciousness of his dignity respects only himself and despises people's opinion.

6

> *There is still mind in a fool who is conscious of his foolishness; but surely there is no mind in him who is firmly sure of his wisdom.*

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

7

> A fool will spend his whole life beside a wise man and will not in the least come to know the truth, just as a spoon will never understand the taste of food.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

8

> A man in love with himself has this advantage, that he has few rivals.

> *Lichtenberg.*

9

A presumptuous man is always limited. The one is the cause of the other. He is always limited because he is presumptuous. And he is presumptuous because he is limited. He is conscious in himself of the impossibility of producing anything good, and therefore assures himself that everything he produces is good.

Pride at first so bewilders people that they yield to suggestion and begin to ascribe to the proud man the very importance that he ascribes to himself; but the suggestion passes, and the proud man soon becomes ridiculous.
