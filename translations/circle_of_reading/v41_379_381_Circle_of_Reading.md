<!-- vol. 41, p. 379 -->

4 June

Owing to the perversion of Christianity, our life has become worse than pagan life.

1

Man must be a slave. The only choice for him is whose: if of his passions, then inevitably of people too; but if of his spiritual principle, then only of God.

It flatters everyone to have a higher master.

2

> Cruelty in our time develops still more as a result of the subtle encouragement of self-love by the teaching that all that is considered evil leads, after all, to good. This teaching <!-- vol. 41, p. 380 --> in practice leads to this: that we, although we use the same serious efforts to avoid everything unpleasant for ourselves, nevertheless complacently and calmly watch the working of the evil that others undergo.

> *John Ruskin.*

3

> “The poor you always have with you.” No words of scripture have been reinterpreted for such devilish ends as precisely these words. If, despite all our successes, we still have among us poor people who, through no fault of their own, cannot rise into healthy and normal conditions of life, this happens through *our* fault and to *our* shame. Anyone who looks around him will see that only injustice, which takes natural advantages away from working people and deprives them of the fruits of their labors, that only it prevents us all from being rich.

> *Henry George.*

4

> The greater part of the crimes and evil of the world is committed out of distrust of reason: “Believe, or be damned.” This is the chief cause of evil. Accepting without reasoning what he ought to have examined with his reason, a man in the end loses the habit of reasoning, and really does fall under the curse himself and leads his neighbors into sin. The salvation of people lies only in learning to think for themselves, so as to direct their thought rightly.

> *Emerson.*

5

> The system on which all the nations of the world act is founded on the grossest deception, on the deepest ignorance, or on a union of both; so that under no modifications of the foundations on which this system rests can it produce good for people; on the contrary—its practical consequences must always be evil.

> *Robert Owen.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 381 -->

6

*The greater the respect surrounding objects, customs, laws, the more attentively must their right to respect be examined.*

The correction of the existing evil of life cannot begin with anything other than the exposure of religious falsehood and the free establishment of religious truth in himself by each individual person.
