<!-- vol. 41, p. 223 -->

2 April

Real life is in becoming better, overcoming one's body by the power of the spirit, drawing near to God. This does not happen by itself. Effort is needed for it. And this effort gives joy.

1

> A habit can never be recognized as good, not even a habit of good actions. Because of it, what is good ceases to be virtue. Only that is good which is gained by effort.

> *Kant.*

2

> Carry your burden, and know that in it is your good; draw from it what is needed for your rational life, as the stomach draws from food everything needed for the flesh, or as fire, which blazes up brighter when something is thrown into it.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> The more you push away your cross, the heavier it becomes.

> *Amiel.*

<u>4</u>

*Always be attentive to what you are doing, and consider nothing unworthy of attention.*

*Confucius.*

5

> The constant fulfillment of inconspicuous duties with a simple and morally elevated feeling strengthens character to the degree at which it will act courageously and strongly in the noise of the world and on the scaffold.

> *Emerson.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 224 -->

6

> Growth is a slow process, not a convulsive explosion. It is as impossible to conquer sin by a convulsion of repentance as to know a whole science by an instantaneous impulse of thought. The real means of inner perfecting lies only in constant, patient effort guided by wise reasoning.

> *Channing.*

Moral effort and the joy of the consciousness of life alternate just as bodily labor and the joy of rest do. Without bodily labor there is no joy of rest; without moral effort there is no joy of the consciousness of life.
