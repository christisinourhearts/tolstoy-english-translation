<!-- vol. 41, p. 176 -->

18 March

Condemning another is always wrong, because no one can ever know what has gone on and is going on in the soul of that other.

1

We often judge people: we call one good, another evil, one foolish, another wise. But this must not be done. A person flows like a river. Every day he is the same and not the same: he was foolish, he became wise; he was evil, he became good, and the other way around. One cannot judge a person. You have condemned him, and he is already different.

2

> If you are so fortunate that you always say only what really is, reject what is false, doubt only what is doubtful, desire only good and benefit, then you will not be indignant at evil and senseless people.

> “Why, they are thieves and swindlers!” you say. But what is a thief and a swindler? Why, it is a person who is depraved and has gone astray. And such a person must be pitied, not be angry with. If you can, convince him that it is not good for him himself to live as he lives, and he will stop doing evil. And if he does not yet understand this, then it is not surprising that he lives badly.

> “But can it be,” you will say, “that such people should not be punished!” Do not say that. Better say: this person is mistaken about what is most important in the world. He is blind not with bodily blindness, but with spiritual. And as soon as you say this to yourself, you will understand how cruel you have been to him. If a person's eyes have fallen ill and he has lost his sight, you will not say that he must be punished for it. So why do you want to punish a person who is deprived of what is dearer than eyes, deprived of the greatest good—the ability to live reasonably? One must not be angry with such people, but only pity them.

> So pity these unfortunate people, and try not to let their errors embitter you. Remember how often you yourself have erred and sinned, and be indignant rather at yourself because malice and cruelty nest in your soul.

> *Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 177 -->

3

If you remember your own shortcomings and try to correct them, it will not even enter your mind to condemn others, and there will be no time for it either.

4

> *Do not condemn your neighbor until you have been in his place.*

> *The Talmud.*

5

> Forgive others much, yourself nothing.

> *Publilius Syrus.*

I know about myself that I do not want to do evil, and if I do it, it is because I cannot restrain myself. All others, if they do evil, also do it because they cannot restrain themselves. So why do I think ill of them and condemn them?
