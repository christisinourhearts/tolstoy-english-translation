<!-- vol. 41, p. 377 -->

3 June

*Whether they know it or do not know it, all beings are inseparably bound to one another.*

1

> Son of man, have you not deceived your brothers? No, no, for you said to them: “Come to me, and I will give you rest.” But they did not come to you, did not receive your teaching with heart and deeds, did not submit to your commands, did not love one another as children of one father. If they had truly come to you, they would love one another, would all be one, and if they were all one—where is the power that could prevent them from establishing <!-- vol. 41, p. 378 --> justice and founding the kingdom of God? But now they are powerless, because, divided, each of them is weak and stands alone against the erring oppressors. They are powerless because they have neither faith, which conquers all, nor love, which is stronger than faith itself. They are powerless because they have grown rigid in their self-love, because there is not in them that very thing by virtue of which people sacrifice themselves, by virtue of which they struggle not for one day but for all days, never growing weary, never losing hope. They are powerless because they fear people, because they do not understand what you told them, namely, that he who has saved his life will lose it, and that he who has lost it for the sake of founding the kingdom of your law will save it.

> *Lamennais.*

2

> A person who considers only his own personality truly existing, and other beings phantoms, to which he grants a certain relative existence only because they can further or oppose his aims, such a person, feeling himself separated by an immeasurably deep abyss from all other beings, cannot, recognizing himself as existing only in his personality, fail to see that with his death there perishes not only the one thing that existed, that is, he himself, but together with him the whole world as well.

> But a person who sees his own being in all others and in everything living, who through his life merges with the existence of all that lives, such a person loses at death only a small part of his existence: such a person goes on existing in all others—in those in whom he always recognized and loved his own being and himself; for such a person the deception that separated his consciousness from the consciousness of the rest disappears.

> It is in this, if not exclusively then chiefly, that there is rooted the difference in the way particularly good and particularly evil people meet their hour of death.

> *After Schopenhauer.*

3

> I will never seek and will never accept separate, personal salvation. I do not want to receive peace alone; but always and everywhere I will live and labor, striving for the universal <!-- vol. 41, p. 379 --> salvation of every being in all worlds. Until all are set free, I will not leave the world of sin, sorrow, and struggle.

> *Chinese Kwan-Hin.*

4

> Rational beings, called to labor together at one and the same work, fulfill in the common life of the world the purpose that the members serve in the human body. They are created for rational concerted action. In the consciousness that you are a member of a great spiritual brotherhood there is something encouraging and consoling.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

5

> Humanity is beginning keenly to realize that all must rise or fall together. People listen more and more to that voice which speaks unceasingly within us.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

Do not think that there could be a good of a separate being, or that the evil of a separate being would not be the evil of the whole world and would not affect you.
