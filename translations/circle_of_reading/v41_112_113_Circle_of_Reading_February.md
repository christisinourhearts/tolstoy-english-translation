## I SELF-RENUNCIATION

Even for the firmest people there are hours of despondency. You see good, strive toward it, want to bring it about—and all efforts seem vain, and you feel abandoned by those for whose sake you sacrificed yourself. You endure hatred, slander, persecution. It is just then that the cry breaks from the heart: “Father, deliver me from this hour...” Christ experienced this. Alone in the midst of a world sick, blind, and deaf, among disciples who did not understand him; among a crowd crude and indifferent, among merciless enemies, foreseeing the execution that was to be the first fruit of his work, Christ said: “Father, save me from this hour,” but at once added, foreseeing both the torments and the death on the cross: “But for this hour I have come.”

Yes, precisely for this, in order to suffer and die and conquer by suffering, conquer by death.

An eternal example for those who want to continue his work! He teaches them that it bears fruit only through self-sacrifice, that he who sows does not reap, *that if he does not die, he will remain alone; and if he dies,* he will grow, like a grain thrown into the earth, and *will bear much fruit.*

You who feel that your soul is troubled because your word is rejected, because you do not see its action, and that the future which was to come out of it will, as it seems to you, be thrown together with you into the grave in which the sons of Satan would like to bury the truth itself—believe, on the contrary, that it is precisely at this time that the work of life will begin, that *for this hour you have come.*

Disciples of Christ, you are not greater than your teacher; you must follow him on the path that he has laid for you, fulfill <!-- vol. 41, p. 113 --> duty for duty's own sake and, asking nothing on this earth, expecting nothing more, say, like Didymus: *And we too go and die with him*. Sow and sow under the scorching sun, under the icy rain; sow everywhere, in courts and in prisons, in the very places of execution; sow, the harvest will come in its time.

*Lamennais.*

In order truly, not in words, to be able to love others, one must not love oneself—also not in words, but in deed. But usually it happens like this: we think that we love others, we assure ourselves and others of this, but we love only in words, while ourselves we love in deed. Others we will forget to feed and put to bed, but ourselves never. And therefore, in order truly to love others in deed, one must learn to forget to feed oneself and put oneself to bed, just as we forget to do this for others.

The greater the sacrifice, the more love, and the more love, the more fruitful the deeds, the more benefit to people.

There are two limits: one is to give up one's life for one's friends; the other is to live without changing the conditions of one's life. Between these two limits are all people: some at the level of Christ's disciples, who left everything and went after him; others at the level of the rich young man, who at once turned away and went off when he was told about changing his life. Between these two limits are various Zacchaeuses, who change their lives only in part. But in order to be even a Zacchaeus, one must strive unceasingly toward the first limit.

*L. N. Tolstoy.*
