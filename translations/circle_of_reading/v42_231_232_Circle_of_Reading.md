<!-- vol. 42, p. 231 -->

6 November

Condemnation of one's neighbor is unreasonable: it is of no use for anything, and meanwhile it is harmful both to oneself and to others.

1

When, at the end of an evening gathering, one of the guests took his leave and went out, those who remained began to judge him and said many bad things about him. The same happened with the second one to leave. So all the guests went away, down to the last one. “Allow me to stay the night,” said this last one. “I heard how all those who left have suffered, and I am afraid for myself.”

2

A proverb says: of the dead speak good or keep silent. I think that, on the contrary, one should not speak ill of the living, because this can hurt them and spoil their relations with the living; but about the dead, of whom it is customary to speak flattering lies, nothing prevents one from speaking the whole truth.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 232 -->

3

Condemnation is especially bad in that the very judgment about a person's shortcomings that, said to his face, could be useful to him is concealed from the one who may need it and communicated to those to whom it is harmful, arousing in them a bad feeling toward the one condemned.

4

> *Be strict with yourselves and indulgent to others, and you will have no enemies.*

> *Chinese wisdom.*

5

A person stops condemning others as soon as he conquers himself.

6

I knew a little old man who on purpose drew out his words, so that several seconds passed between each word. He did this deliberately, fearing to sin with a word.

7

We are all bad, and therefore everything that we condemn in others we will always find in ourselves. Let us, then, forgive one another. The one means for us to live in peace is mutual forgiveness.

A word is the expression of thought, thought is a manifestation of divine power, and therefore a word must correspond to what it expresses. It may be indifferent, but it cannot and must not be an expression of evil.
