<!-- vol. 41, p. 489 -->

11 July

True mercy is only the mercy of one who is strong, giving his labors and efforts to one who is weak.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 490 -->

1

The giving of alms is a good deed only when what is given is the product of labor.

A proverb says: a dry hand is tight-fisted, a sweaty hand is open-handed. So too in the “Teaching of the 12 Apostles” it is said: let your alms come out of your hand with sweat.

2

> *Might is given to man not so that he may crush one who is weak, but so that he may support him and help him.*

> *John Ruskin.*

3

> Every good deed is mercy. To give water to the thirsty is mercy. To remove stones from the road is mercy. To persuade one's neighbors to be virtuous is mercy. To show a wanderer his way is also mercy. To smile, looking into one's neighbor's face, is mercy.

> *Mishkat tradition (Muhammad).*

4

> Give to everyone who asks of you, and from him who has taken from you do not ask it back. And as you want people to deal with you, so you also deal with them.

> *Luke 6:30–31.*

5

> What you have given away is yours, and what you have kept back is lost.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

6

A man who had given away all his property was being praised. “There is nothing to praise me for,” said this man, “I have not done anything yet. I have only, on coming to the river I have to swim across, undressed so that my clothes would not hinder me. The point is how I will swim.”

If a rich man is truly merciful, he will soon cease to be rich.
