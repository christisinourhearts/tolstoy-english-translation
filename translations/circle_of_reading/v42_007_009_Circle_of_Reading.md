<!-- vol. 42, p. 7 -->

1 September

Reason points out to people their departures from the law of life. But these departures are so habitual to people and seem so pleasant that people try to stifle reason, so that it will not hinder them from living as they are used to living.

1

Both the salvation and the punishment of man lie in this: that when he lives wrongly, he can befog himself so as not to see the wretchedness of his situation.

2

If life does not fit conscience, then conscience is bent by stupefaction to fit life.

3

When soldiers stand as a covering force under fire and have nothing to do, they diligently seek out occupations for themselves in order to bear the danger more easily. And all people at times seem to be such soldiers, escaping from life: some by ambition, some by cards, some by writing laws, some by women, some by games, some by horses, some by hunting, some by wine, some by affairs of state.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 8 -->

4

It is hard to imagine the beneficial change that would take place in the whole of human life if people stopped stupefying and at the same time poisoning themselves with vodka, wine, tobacco, opium.

5

It is told of the followers of a certain sect that at the end of their meetings they put out the light in order to give themselves up to debauchery.

In our society, in order to give themselves up to constant debauchery, people ceaselessly put out the light of reason with stupefying substances.

6

One of the chief conditions for improving the life of the people of our time is to free themselves from the suggestion under which they find themselves, and yet they ceaselessly and diligently keep themselves in this state with tobacco, wine, vodka.

7

The fact that the government takes upon itself the duty of supplying, at a profit to itself, alcohol, which corrupts and kills people's souls and bodies, shows in the most obvious way, even if there were no other proofs of it, that the government not only does not care, as it asserts, for the morality and the good of the people, but, on the contrary, harms the people in the most obvious way for the profit of those who make up the government.

8

*Stupefying oneself with anything whatever, though it is not yet a crime, is already a preparation of oneself for every kind of crime.*

9

The viciousness and, above all, the senselessness of the life of the people of our time come chiefly from the constant state of <!-- vol. 42, p. 9 --> intoxication into which they bring themselves. People who were not drunk could not do even a small part of what is being done now in our world.

You say that it is of no importance whether one drinks or does not drink, smokes or does not smoke. If it is of no importance, then what does it cost you to stop, when you know of the harm that you do by this both to yourself and to others by your example?
