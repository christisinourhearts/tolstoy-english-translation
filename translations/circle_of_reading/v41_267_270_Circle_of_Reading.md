<!-- vol. 41, p. 267 -->

19 April

A person who is not conscious of the beneficence of sufferings has not yet begun to live a rational, that is, a true life.

1

> All that is great is accomplished in humanity only on condition of suffering. Jesus knew that he too had to expect this, and he foresaw everything: both the hatred of those whose power he had come to destroy, and their secret plots, and their violence, and the ungrateful betrayal of that people whose sickness he healed, whom in the wilderness of the old society he fed with the heavenly bread of his word; he foresaw both the cross, and death, and abandonment <!-- vol. 41, p. 268 --> by his own, more grievous still than death itself. And this thought did not leave him, but it does not stop him for a minute. If his bodily nature pushes away “this cup,” a stronger will accepts it without hesitation. And in this he gives to all those who will continue his work—to all those who, like him, will come to labor for the salvation of people, for their liberation from the burden of error and evil—gives them an example that must always be remembered. If people want to reach the goal to which Christ leads, they too must go by the same path. Only at this price do people serve people. You want them to be truly brothers, you call them to the laws of their common nature, you fight against every oppression, every lawlessness, every hypocrisy; you call down upon the earth the kingdom of justice, duty, truth, love—how then can those whose power is founded on the opposite not rise up against you? Can they really, without a struggle, leave you to destroy their temple and build another: not like theirs—no longer the work of human hands—but an eternal temple, whose foundations God himself has laid? Give up this hope, if ever you were so light-minded as to have it. You will drink the cup to the last drop. You will be taken as thieves; false testimony will be sought against you, and at the testimony that you yourselves give about yourselves a cry will go up: he blasphemes! and the judges will say: he is worthy of death. When this happens, rejoice: this is the last sign—the sign that you are truly sent by the father.

> *Lamennais.*

2

> As the darkness of night reveals the heavenly lights, so only sufferings reveal the whole meaning of life.

> *Thoreau.*

3

Without sufferings there can be no spiritual growth, no increase of life is possible—that is why sufferings always accompany death. Suffering is a necessary and beneficent condition of life. That is why the people say that God loves the one whom calamities visit.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 269 -->

4

> Illness, the loss of a limb, a cruel disappointment, the loss of property, the loss of friends seems at first an irreparable loss. But the years bring out the deep healing power that lies in these losses.

> *Emerson.*

5

> The good news consists precisely in this, that the truth about the life of man is a door opened for the passage of people from an elementally unconscious life to a rationally conscious one; so that sufferings remain sufferings, and death remains death, but in rational consciousness they are accepted as a good—the good of the common, universal, divine, eternal, immortal life.

> *Buka.*

6

> It is undoubtedly more important how a person accepts fate than what it is in reality.

> *Humboldt.*

7

> Small sufferings lead us out of ourselves, but great ones return us to ourselves. A cracked bell gives a dull sound: break it into two parts—it again gives a clear sound.

> *Jean Paul (Richter).*

8

> The strength and grace of religion lie in this, that it explains to man the meaning of his existence and his final destination. But when (as we have all done in our age of science and intellectual freedom) we have thrown away all the foundations of the morality that flows from religion, there is no longer any means of knowing why we have appeared in this world and what we are to do in it.

> The mystery of fate embraces us on all sides with its mighty questions, and, indeed, one must not think at all in order not to feel the tormenting, terrifying senselessness <!-- vol. 41, p. 270 --> of life. Bodily sufferings, moral evil, pains of the soul, the happiness of the wicked, the humiliation of the righteous man—all this could be borne if one could understand the inner order of the world's arrangement, if one could suppose a providence in it. The believer rejoices in his wounds, he patiently bears the injustices and violence of his enemies; sin, even crimes, do not deprive him of hope. But for a person in whom all faith has been extinguished, evil and sufferings lose their meaning, and life appears only as a loathsome joke.

> *Anatole France.*

*A person living a spiritual life cannot fail to see that sufferings move him toward the desired goal of perfecting, and for such a person suffering loses all its bitterness and becomes a good.*
