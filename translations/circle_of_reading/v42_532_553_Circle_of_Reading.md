## UNFINISHED POPULAR VERSION OF “CIRCLE OF READING”

1 January

In every matter a little, but good, is better than much, but bad. So too with books.

1

One must read little, but try to understand and think over what one has read.

2

Too many books distract the mind. Read only those books which everyone acknowledges to be good.

3

Read the best books first of all, or else you will not have time to read them at all.

The difference between material and mental poisons is that most material poisons are repulsive to the taste, while mental poisons, in the form of newspapers and bad books, are often attractive, and the more attractive, the more perverted the taste.

2 January

One cannot live without faith. ~~A person without faith is the same as an animal.~~

1

Only faith truly unites people.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 533 -->

2

All people suffer alike, think alike, die alike, and over all people is one and the same God, and therefore there is also only one true faith.

3

True faith is not in words, but in deeds.

A person's life without faith is the life of an animal.

3 January

People live badly only because they believe wrongly.

1

One must not hold on to the old at any cost. One must be ready to change former ways if [^1] they are harmful. A sailor who sets the same sails in every wind will not get far.

2

The chief cause of people's calamities is that people serve either those whom they can no longer see and who no longer need their help, or the proud and wicked, who blind their eyes and whom one ought not to help.

3

One has only to accept Christ directly and simply for that terrible deception in which all of us live, and each of us lives, to become clear.

4 January

All people of all nations are bound to one another both by industry, and by trade, and by art, and by knowledge, and, above all, by this: that all of us alike think, feel, suffer, and die.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 534 -->

1

> *Good people help one another without thinking about it, but the wicked harm one another on purpose.*

> *Chinese proverb.*

2

> The world is so arranged that a thousand people working together will accomplish many times more than the same thousand people could accomplish separately. And therefore people must come together to work in common, but come together freely, and not under the power of one.

> *Henry George.*

3

> A good man is the teacher of a bad one; a bad man is the material of a good man. He errs who does not respect his teacher and does not love the material on which he works.

> *Laozi.*

At first people lived apart all their lives, not knowing one another. The longer we live, the closer nation draws to nation and man to man. Such is the law of life, and therefore every person must do what brings people closer together and not do what divides them.

5 January

One person shouts in a building filled with people: “Fire!”—and the crowd rushes, and tens, hundreds of people are killed.

Such is the obvious harm produced by a word. But this harm is no less great even when we do not see the people who have perished from our word.

1

> A wound inflicted by a firearm can still be healed, but a wound inflicted by the tongue never heals.

> *Persian saying.*

2

> For we all sin much. He who does not sin in word is a perfect man, able to bridle the whole body as well. Look, we put bits into the mouths of horses so that they obey us, and <!-- vol. 42, p. 535 --> we direct their whole body; look, ships too, however large they are and however strong the winds that drive them, are directed by a small rudder wherever the helmsman wishes; so the tongue too is a small member, but does much. See how much matter a small fire sets alight; and the tongue is a fire, an adornment of untruth. The tongue is so placed among our members that the whole body is defiled, and it inflames the circle of life, being itself inflamed by Gehenna... Is any among you wise and understanding? prove it in deed by good conduct with wise meekness.

> *James 3:2–6, 13.*

3

> When you hear slander against people, do not taste this anger.

> When you hear flattery of people, do not taste this joy. If you hear people talk about a person's viciousness, do not share their pleasure.

> Listen to talk about people's virtue; approve, imitate, and rejoice. Rejoice at the sight of a virtuous person. Rejoice at news of good deeds. Rejoice at the spread of the principles of justice.

> Rejoice at the spread of deeds of good. But when you hear of people's evil, let it be as painful to you as needles digging into your back. And when you hear of good deeds, clothe yourself in them as in garlands of flowers.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

4

> Listen to disputes, but do not interfere in disputes. God keep you from vehemence and heat, even in the slightest expression. Anger is out of place everywhere, and most of all in a just cause, because it darkens and muddies it.

> *Gogol.*

5

> I said: I will watch over my ways, so that I do not sin with my tongue; I will bridle my mouth while the wicked man is before me.

> *Psalm 39:1.*

*Be afraid of being a violator of people's unity, of arousing in them by words unkind feelings against one another.*

6 January.

Effort is needed for doing good, but still more needed for abstaining from evil, for taming passion and lust.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 536 -->

1

> For attaining holiness there is nothing more important than abstinence. And abstinence must be an early habit. If it is an early habit, it acquires many virtues. For him who has acquired many virtues there is nothing that he could not overcome.

> *Laozi.*

2

> All that people so admire, all for the sake of which they so worry and bustle, all this brings them not the slightest happiness. As long as people bustle, they think that their good lies in what they are striving for. But as soon as they get what they wanted, they again begin to worry, to grieve, and to envy what they do not yet have. And this is very understandable, because freedom is attained not by satisfying one's idle desires but, on the contrary, by ridding oneself of such desires.

> If you want to make sure that this is true, then put into freeing yourself from your empty desires even half as much labor as you have spent until now on fulfilling them, and you will soon see that in this way you will obtain much more peace and happiness.

> *Epictetus.*

3

> But he who has endured to the end will be saved.

> *Matthew 24:13.*

4

> Glory to the man who does not yield to temptation. God tests everyone: one by wealth, another by poverty; the rich man—whether he will open his hand to the needy, and the poor man—whether he will bear his sufferings without murmuring, with submission to Providence.

> *The Talmud.*

5

*A person's greatest strength lies in stopping.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 537 -->

6

> Only him will I call a true charioteer who restrains his anger, which rushes like a swift chariot; but others, powerless, only hold on to the reins.

> *Buddhist wisdom.*

> If, burdened with unpleasant affairs, you feel a fit of anger or indignation, then hasten to get away from yourself and do not give in to impressions that could deprive you of self-control. The more we practice returning by force of will to a calm disposition of soul, the more the capacity to keep calmness of spirit grows stronger.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

However many times you may have to fall without having gained victory over your passions, do not lose heart. As much as you have struggled with a passion, so much have you lessened its strength, and the easier will be the victory over it.

7 January

One must be kind to all people. Kindness [^2] is contagious. If you are kind to people, you are doing the most important work in the world: you are increasing kindness in people.

1

One must not reject any person, however bad he may be. If there is such a person, it means that he had to be. Meeting such a person, one must think: “what is to be done? there must be such people too,” and treat him with respect. But if we do not do this, we are wrong and, besides, we call such people to war against ourselves. After all, he cannot remake himself. And if we do not want to acknowledge him as he is, then nothing else is left for him to do but to fight us as his worst enemies.

And therefore, living in the world, we must patiently bear all [^3] the shortcomings of people, know how to deal in the best way with people as they are, not counting on their being able to change.

*Schopenhauer.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 538 -->

2

*Do not be severe with one who has fallen into temptation, and try to comfort him as you yourself would wish to be comforted.*

3

> 1) Do not put off until tomorrow what you can do today.

> 2) Do not make another do what you can do yourself.

> 3) Do not buy what you do not need, if it is cheap.

> 4) Pride costs more than all that is needed for food, drink, dwelling, clothing.

> 5) One rarely has to regret having eaten too little.

> 6) How many times we have tormented ourselves over what did not happen.

> 7) If you get angry, count to ten; very angry, count to a hundred.

> *Jefferson.*

Kindness adorns life, resolves all contradictions, makes the tangled clear, makes the difficult easy, makes the gloomy joyful.

8 January

The Christian teaching is so clear that infants understand it. Only people who wish to seem and to be called Christians, but not to be such, can fail to understand it.

1

The Buddha said: a person who has devoted himself to religion is like a person who carries a light into a dark house. The darkness at once disperses, and it becomes light. Only persevere in seeking wisdom, try to acquire knowledge of the truth, and complete enlightenment will take place in you.

2

> The working people, those whom the corruption that comes from the idle and the rich has not touched, the people free from what Christ calls being blinded by riches, content with daily bread, asking of the heavenly father only what he gives to the little birds, which neither sow nor reap—the working people live the true life, the life of the heart, more than other people, who are immersed in the desires and cares of this world.

> *Lamennais.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 539 --> And therefore it is to the people that Christ turns, and therefore the people recognize in him the messenger of the Father, glorify his name, proclaim his power, submitting to it, and crown him king of the future. The princes of the church, the ancient scribes, curse him and kill him, but in spite of their violence and cunning, in spite of the execution, he triumphed through the people; the people founded his kingdom in the world, and through the people it will spread in it: through the people a new era will be born, whose divine seed the powers of the past would like to stifle, already seized by fear for their near end, by a panic fear of death.

3

> One must beware of two equally pernicious superstitions: the one that the essence of the deity can be expressed in words, and the superstition of science, which supposes that divine power can be explained by scientific analysis.

> *John Ruskin.*

4

Christ's last commandment expresses his whole teaching: “Love one another, as I have loved you, and by this all will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another.” He does not say: “If you believe in this or that,” but “if you love.” Faith is bound up with time and changes along with time. Love is not temporal; it is unchanging, eternal.

5

> My religion is love for all that lives.

> *Ibrahim of Cordoba.*

*For there to be true Christianity, it is only necessary that the false be destroyed.*

9 January

A person truly knows only what he has arrived at himself.

1

*For the human mind it is less harmful not to study at all than to study too early and too much.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 540 -->

2

> The merit of the sages lies in this: that, independently of the books and traditions that existed before them, they expressed what they themselves thought, and not what the people who had lived before them and who surrounded them thought.

> In just the same way each of us must lie in wait for and catch those bright thoughts which, like sparks, now and then flash up and flare in our mind: for each of us such inner illuminations have far more significance than the study of the thoughts of others, of poets and sages.

> *Emerson.*

Read less, study less, think more. Learn, both from teachers and in books, only what you need and want to know.

10 January

In upbringing the first thing is faith.

1

> But whoever tempts one of these little ones who believe in me, it would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck and he were drowned in the depth of the sea.

> Woe to the world because of temptations, for temptations must come; but woe to that man through whom the temptation comes.

> *Matthew 18:6, 7.*

2

> *To bring a child to the consciousness of the divine nature in himself—this is the chief task of parents and educators.*

> *Channing.*

3

> The object of true upbringing is not only to make people do good deeds, but also to find joy in them; not only to be pure, but also to love purity; not only to be just, but also to hunger and thirst for justice.

> *John Ruskin.*

Teaching faith is the first thing in upbringing. But those who teach must themselves believe what they teach. Children are perceptive, and if the teachers do not believe, then they too fail to believe not only what they are taught, but also those who teach them.

11 January

Only the person who considers himself bad can perfect himself.

“Why should I perfect myself when I am good as it is?”

<!-- vol. 42, p. 541 -->

1

> My child, do your work with meekness, and then you will earn love. The higher you are, the more humble yourself. Many live on the heights and in glory, but mysteries are revealed only to the lowly. Do not seek things too difficult and beyond your strength. But what is prescribed to you, think on that with respect. Do not be curious about what you have no need of. Even as it is, more has been revealed to you than you can understand.

> Many are deceived by their own vain opinion; therefore do not boast of knowledge that you do not have.

> *Ecclesiastes (apocryphal).*

2

> But Jesus, calling them to him, said: you know that the princes of the nations lord it over them, and the great men rule over them; but it shall not be so among you; but whoever wants to be greater among you, let him be your servant; and whoever wants to be first among you, let him be your slave; for the son of man came not *for this*, that he should be served, but to serve and to give his soul for the redemption of many.

> *Matthew 20:25–28.*

3

> He who is insulted and can calmly bear the insult and does not return it has won a great victory in the work of life.

> *Genevio-Lan.*

4

> Some of your friends censure you and some praise you; draw near to those who censure you and move away from those who extol you.

> *The Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 542 -->

5

> Take a place lower than befits you.

> It is better if they say to you: come up higher, than: go down below.

> Whoever exalts himself, God humbles; and whoever humbles himself, God exalts.

> *The Talmud.*

6

Strive unremittingly to destroy in yourselves every desire to dominate, beware of vanity, do not seek glory and praise—all this can only ruin your soul. Beware of the thought that you are better than others and that you are adorned by virtues which you do not meet in those like yourselves.

7

> Although the sage is strict with himself, he demands nothing of others. He is content with his position and never complains against Heaven, does not blame others for his lot—therefore, being in a low station, he submits to fate. But the ordinary mortal, seeking earthly goods, falls into dangers.

> When an arrow does not hit the mark, the archer blames himself for it, and not another. So too does the sage act.

> *Confucius.*

8

And he who has taken hold of the plow and looks back is not fit for the kingdom of God.

9

> *Let the greater of you be your servant: for whoever exalts himself will be humbled; and whoever humbles himself will be exalted.*

> *Matthew 23:11–12.*

Remember all the bad you have done. This will help you not to do bad. But if you remember the good you have done, this will hinder you from doing good.

12 January

There are people who take upon themselves the right to decide for others their relation to God, and there are many people who give this right over to others. Both the one and the other are equally criminal.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 543 -->

1

> There are people who, having learned that all religious questions must be resolved and all religious laws established, at once give themselves over into the hands of those people who undertake such resolving and establishing.

> Why should they trouble themselves about what others so definitely acknowledge as their own unchanging business? There are pleasures, amusements, agreeable pastimes, which will fill the day and night and lull the years in pleasant dreams.

> This is just what is produced in the people: dull contentment and the cessation of efforts to know the truth.

> *Milton.*

2

> We are all like children, who first repeat the irrefutable truths of their grandmothers, then of their teachers, and, as they grow older, of other remarkable people who come our way.

> With what effort we try to learn by heart the words we have heard! But when we ourselves reach the step on which these predecessors stood, and understand the meaning of these words, our disappointment is so strong that we would gladly forget everything we heard from them.

> *Emerson.*

3

> But you, do not be called teachers, for one is your teacher—Christ, and you are all brothers.

> *Matthew 23:8.*

4

> Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly are ravening wolves; by their fruits you will know them. Do people gather grapes from a thornbush, or figs from a burdock? So every good tree bears good fruits, and a bad tree bears bad fruits: a good tree cannot bear bad fruits, nor a bad tree bear good fruits. So then, by their fruits you will know them.

> *Matthew 7:15–20.*

A person may make use of the tradition that has come down to him from the wise and holy people of the past, but it is up to him to test by his reason what is handed down, and to reject one thing and choose another.

*Every person must himself establish his relation to God.*

13 January

True faith gives peace of soul.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 544 -->

1

> One thing is necessary: to give oneself up to God. Be in order yourself, and leave it to God to untangle the skein of the world and its destinies. Let there be annihilation or immortality. What must be—will be. What will be—will be good.

> *Amiel.*

2

> Religion has higher aims than the formation of a good person. It presupposes that he already exists, and its chief aim is to raise this good person to the highest degree of understanding.

> *Lessing.*

3

> There is a twofold peace. A negative peace: this is the absence of noise, of gnawing cares. This is the calm after struggle, after storms. But there is another, more perfect peace of the soul, which this first peace only precedes—that divine calm which understands everything, whose true name is “the kingdom of God within.”

> Faith gives us this calm. It is a conscious unity with God and the universe, a union of love with all beings, love for everything that is pure and happy, the ability to sacrifice one's desires and interests, participation in the spirit and life of the universe, the complete accord of the cause with its infinite source.

> *Channing.*

4

> They say that on the last day there will be a general judgment and that the good God will be angry. But from goodness itself nothing can come but good. Do not fear: the end will be full of joy. The difference of faiths has divided mankind into seventy-two peoples—of all their dogmas I have chosen one: divine love.

> *Persian Khayyam.*

5

> *Who is a good person? Only a religious person is good. But what is goodness? First and most important of all, it is the agreement of the will with conscience.*

> *Chinese Buddhism.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 545 -->

6

If I truly say with my heart: your will be done both on earth and in heaven, that is, both in this temporal life and in the eternal one, then I need no assertions or proofs of immortality whatever. I give myself up to the will of the Infinite Being, blessing this will, I know that it is love—so what more do I need?

Christ, dying, said: “Father, into your hands I give up my spirit.” For one who can say these words, understanding them in all their meaning, nothing more is needed: faith, true faith, resolves everything.

And in order to have this faith, one must cultivate it in oneself. And in order to cultivate it, one must do the deeds of faith.

The essence of the deeds of faith is not in feats, but in deeds that are often unnoticed, insignificant, but done undoubtedly and exclusively for God.

One will have to die alone, said Pascal, and one must live alone before God, and not before people. As much as you live alone before God, and not before people, so much does faith increase. And as much as faith increases, so much does care about the future decrease.

Do not think that you can find peace of soul without faith.

14 January

Love for one's carnal “I” is a perversion of love for God.

To love in oneself him who is one in all is to love God.

1

> Teacher! which is the greatest commandment in the law? Jesus said: “Love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your understanding: this is the first and greatest commandment; and the second is like it: love your neighbor as yourself; on these two commandments rests the whole law and the prophets.”

> *Matthew 22:36–40.*

2

> One must love only God and hate only oneself.

> *Pascal.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 546 -->

3

*People live by love: love of oneself is the beginning of death, love of God and people is the beginning of life.*

4

If a person cannot forgive his brother, he does not love him. True love is infinite, and there is no number of offenses that it would not forgive, if it is true love.

5

> God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God in him. No one has ever seen God. If we love one another, then God abides in us, and his love is perfect in us. He who says: I love God, but hates his brother, is a liar; for he who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see? Brothers, let us love one another, for he who loves is born of God and knows God, because God is love. He who abides in love abides in God, and God in him.

> *1 John, from ch. 4.*

6

Love is the power of life. Love is the rule for fulfilling all rules.

7

Love is not the fundamental principle of our life. Love is a consequence, not a cause. The cause of love is the consciousness in oneself of the divine spiritual principle. This consciousness demands love, produces love.

To love one who is pleasant to us is not to love God, is not to love at all.

True love is attained by effort. Remember that the one with whom you are dealing loves himself just as you love yourself, and you will understand how you ought to treat him.

15 January

Death and birth are two limits. Beyond these limits there is the same something.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 547 -->

1

Where will the souls of the dead go? To where the souls of the unborn are.

2

When the soul is well occupied, the question of immortality does not interest it. Everything is so good now that it is sure it will be good in the future too. One must trust that if it is better for us to live, we shall live. It is much better and calmer for the soul to be convinced of this than that before us lie millions more years, centuries, millennia...

3

> *While we are alive, our souls are dead and buried in our body; and when we die, they come to life.*

> *Heraclitus of Ephesus.*

4

There is no death, but there is a series of changes, which I have already lived through, and better ones I will yet live through.

5

The human soul cannot be completely destroyed together with the body—but something of it remains that is eternal.

If there is eternity, then it is in the past just as in the future.

16 January

My food is to do the will of him who sent me and to accomplish his work. Each of us has this work of his. We may not know what this work consists in, but we cannot fail to know what our participation in it must consist in. Our participation is to love our neighbor.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 548 -->

1

> Not everyone who says to me: Lord, Lord! will enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of my heavenly father.

> *Matthew 7:21.*

2

If there is no strength to burn and shed light, then at least do not block it.

3

> He who does not know the rules of sound reason is lower than he who loves them. He who loves them is lower than he who fulfills them.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

4

> Whether you have been doing what needs to be done takes on enormous importance, because the only meaning of your life lies in this: whether you are doing, in this short term of life given to you, what he who sent you into life wants of you.

> Is that what you are doing?

> *The Talmud.*

5

*I feel burdened, I ask God to help me. But surely my business is to serve God, and not his to serve me. One need only remember this, and the burden is lightened.*

6

> The earth is not a place of atonement; it is precisely our dwelling, in which we must labor to attain truth and justice, the seed of which every person hides in his soul.

> *Mazzini.*

Imagine that the aim of life is your happiness—and life is a cruel absurdity. Acknowledge what tradition, reason, and the heart say, that life is service to him who sent you into the world, and life becomes a reasonable and joyful passage, not a state.

17 January

A person can serve the improvement of social life only to the extent that he fulfills in his own life the demands of his conscience, the law of God.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 549 -->

1

> We in the world are like a child who has come into a room where a learned man is speaking about his science. The child did not hear the beginning and goes away before the end. He has heard something, but has not understood what he heard. The great speech of God was begun many centuries before we began our learning, and it will go on even when we are dust. We have heard only part of it and have not understood the greater part of what we heard. But all the same, though little and dimly, we have understood something great, solemn, inspiring, hallowing something.

> *David Thomas.*

2

> The dreamer often determines the future correctly, but he does not want to wait for it. He wants the future to come at once, hastened by him. What nature needs thousands of years for, he wants to see accomplished in his own lifetime.

> *Lessing.*

3

> Why do you torment yourselves in vain in your wretched situation? Your desire is good, but you do not know how to attain it. Know that only he who gives up life can give it. You will attain nothing without God. You toss on your bed of suffering, and what have you found? You have abolished the laws of slavery—and they have given you new laws of slavery. Do not trust people who stand between you and God, lest their shadow hide him from you. These people have only bad intentions. Only from God comes the liberating power, only from God comes the uniting love. What can a person do for you who is guided only by his own thought and the law of his own will? If he has good intentions and wishes only good, he will still give you his will instead of law and his thought instead of a rule. Freedom is not in this one ruling rather than that one, but in no one ruling. And where God does not rule, man rules. The kingdom of God is the kingdom of justice in minds and of mercy in hearts: the foundation of this is faith in God and faith in the teaching of Christ, who proclaimed the law <!-- vol. 42, p. 550 --> of God, the law of mercy, the law of justice. The law of justice teaches that all are equal before the father God and the one teacher Christ. The law of mercy teaches love and help for one another, as sons of one father and disciples of one teacher.

> But those who say to you: “Before us no one knew what justice is; justice comes from us, believe us, and we will set up for you such a justice as will satisfy you”—these people are deceiving you or, if they sincerely promise you freedom, are themselves deceived—because they want you to acknowledge them as masters, and then your freedom will be only obedience to these new masters. Answer them that your master is one, Christ, and you do not want another, and Christ will set you free.

> *Lamennais.*

4

It would be good if wisdom were of such a nature that it could flow over from the one who is full of it into the one in whom there is none, as water flows through wool from one vessel into another until both are equal. But for receiving another's wisdom, independent work is needed first of all.

5

> *If you can teach a person good and do not do it—you lose a brother. If a person is not disposed to accept your teachings, and you nevertheless pass them on to him—you lose words.*

> A wise, enlightened person loses neither brothers nor his words.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

Do your work of life, fulfilling the will of God, and be sure that only in this way will you contribute most fruitfully to the improvement of the common life.

18 January

An enlightened person is one who knows his purpose in life and tries, as far as he is able, to fulfill it.

1

A learned person is one who knows much from books; an educated person is one who has assimilated all the knowledge most widespread in his time; an enlightened person is one who understands the meaning of *his own* life.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 551 -->

2

The only explanation of that insane life, contrary to their own consciousness, which people of our time lead lies in the fact that the young generations are taught countless, most difficult subjects: about the state of the heavenly bodies, about the state of the earth millions of years ago, about the origin of organisms, and the like. Only one thing is not taught, the one thing that is needed: what the meaning of human life is, what the wisest people of antiquity thought about this question and how they resolved it. Not only is it not taught, but instead of it the young generations are taught and have instilled in them, under the name of the law of God, the most obvious nonsense, in which neither those who teach nor those who are taught believe. Under the whole edifice of our life, instead of stone, bubbles blown up with air have been laid. How can this edifice not fall?

One thing is needful: in actions—good, and in science—knowledge of what the true good is.

This knowledge is accessible to all.

19 January

In order to know what a good life consists in, one must know what a bad one consists in; in order to know what to do, one must know what one must not do.

1

One of the errors of external religious teachings is that they prescribe particular deeds, sacrifices, sacraments, prayers, whereas the true religious teaching only points to what one must not do.

2

*Life demands its manifestation. If the paths of evil are closed to it, it manifests itself on the path of good.*

3

How often one meets people who are indignant against war, prisons, violence, and at the same time indirectly take part in those very deeds which they condemn.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 552 -->

4

A person of your time cannot examine attentively enough the aims and applications of those seemingly innocent deeds which he does, if he wants to live a moral life. A person eating a cutlet must know that this cutlet is the body of a slaughtered lamb taken from its mother; and, receiving a salary at an arms factory, a powder factory, or as an officer or an official in the collection of taxes, must know that the money he receives is the sweat and blood of tormented and killed people, or of people who are yet to be tormented and killed, and that his participation in these deeds is a deed no better, though more hidden, than the deed of Cain.

5

*In our time the greatest and most harmful crimes are not those that are committed from time to time, but those that are committed constantly and are not recognized as crimes.*

Do not console yourself with the thought that if you do not see those whom you torment and kill, and if you have many comrades doing the same, you are not a tormentor, not a murderer. It was possible for you not to be one so long as you did not understand what you were doing, but if you have understood, then there is no justification for you, not before people (before people there is justification in everything and always), but before your own conscience.

19 January

(Reserve)

Social life can be improved only by the self-renunciation of people.

1

People say: one swallow does not make a spring; but is it really so that, because one swallow does not make a spring, the swallow that already feels the spring should not fly, but wait? If every bud and blade of grass were to wait like that, there would never be a spring. So too for us, for the establishment of the kingdom of God, there is no need to think about whether I am the first or the thousandth swallow.

2

*In private and in common life there is one law: if you want to improve life, be ready to give it.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 553 -->

3

> In those times when the peoples are waiting only for a sign to begin the greatest battle that has ever been on earth between good and evil—when in all parts of the world the dull peals of thunder are already heard, when everyone feels that the time is near when both armies will clash with each other—the army of God and the army of Satan, and that the future fate of mankind—its liberation or its enslavement—depends on this clash, in these solemn times one must first of all firmly know that the title of warrior of God's army can be earned only by following the example of its leader—by renouncing everything, that for this one must, in the image of the son of man, who made himself like the poor whom he came to save, have no place to lay one's head. This is needed in order not to grow soft, in order to have full possibility of being here today, there tomorrow, of being everywhere where there is danger, where the struggle is going on; that for this one must leave the dead to bury the dead; and the dead are those who, having sunk into cares about the transient and into bustle about the material, do not even know that there is in them a soul demanding liberation, that to live means to struggle, means to die so that this great liberation may be accomplished.

> *Lamennais.*

All attempts to improve life without sacrifice are vain. These attempts only put off the possibility of improvement.
