<!-- vol. 41, p. 95 -->

11 February

*Human life is good only to the extent that it is the fulfillment of the law of life, the law of God.*

1

Evil, in the form of death and suffering, is visible to a person only when he takes the law of his fleshly, animal existence for the law of his life. Only when he, being a man, descends to the level of an animal, only then do both death and suffering become terrible to him. Death and suffering, like scarecrows, hoot at him from all sides and drive him onto the one road of human life open to him: the fulfillment of the law of God, expressed in love. Death and suffering are only man's transgressions of this law. For a person, however, who lives fully by the law of God, there is no death, there is no suffering.

2

> What is to be done when everything leaves us: health, joy, attachment, freshness of feeling, memory, the capacity for work, when it seems to us that the sun is growing cold and life seems to lose all its charms? What is one to do when there is no hope at all? Stupefy oneself or turn to stone? The answer is always one: the merging of one's will with the will of God. Come what may, if you feel peace of conscience, if you feel yourself reconciled and in your place. Be what you ought to be—the rest is God's affair. And even if there were no God of love, but only the law of everything, duty would still be the solution of the mystery.

> *Amiel.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 96 -->

3

> The fulfillment of duty has nothing in common with personal pleasure. Duty has its own special law, its own special court, and if we wanted to mix duty and personal pleasure in order to live by this mixture, duty and pleasure would at once separate from each other of themselves.

> *After Kant.*

We know the law of God both from the traditions of all religions and from our own consciousness, when it is not darkened by passions and deceptions of thought, and we can find it out from the application of this law to life: all those requirements of the law that give us an inalienable good, all these are requirements of the true law.
