<!-- vol. 42, p. 79 -->

27 September

Condemning one's neighbor is an amusement from which people who do not understand all the harm of this amusement find it hard to refrain. But when one understands all the harm of condemnation, it is a sin not to refrain from it merely for the sake of amusement.

1

> From the thoughts a person expresses one cannot judge how he would act in fact. And likewise the other way around: from a person's deeds it is very hard to judge for what reason he acts so, what thoughts he has in his head, and what motives in his soul. If I see that a person tirelessly busies himself, reads, writes, or works from morning till night, or even sits over his work whole nights through, I will still not say that this person loves to work, or works for the benefit of people, if I do not know why he does all this. After all, no one will say of a person who carouses whole nights through with dissolute women that he is useful or that he loves to work. And not only vile deeds, but seemingly fine ones too are often done for vile ends, for example, for money or for fame; and one cannot say of a person who acts so that he is industrious and useful, however tirelessly he may work and whatever resounding deeds he may accomplish. I will say of a person that he loves work and is useful to people only when I learn that he works for his soul—for God.

> But another's soul is darkness. How then shall I learn the inner motives of a person, known only to himself?

> And it turns out that a person cannot judge a person, that is, condemn or justify him, nor praise nor blame him.

> *Epictetus.*

2

> Wishing to judge me, be not with me but in me.

> *Mickiewicz.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 80 -->

3

It is as hard for the good to suppose evil in others as for the evil to suppose good.

4

*In disputes truth is forgotten. The wisest one stops the dispute.*

5

> Our chief imperfection is in our inner sight: we are terribly farsighted for seeing the bad in others, but the bad in ourselves is invisible to us.

> *Brown.*

6

> He is truly noble who easily forgives people's errors and at the same time is as afraid of doing anything bad as though he had never forgiven anyone.

> *Pliny the Younger.*

As soon as you begin to judge a person, remember not to say anything bad about him even when you know that bad thing for certain, and all the more so when you do not know it but are only repeating other people's words.
