<!-- vol. 41, p. 542 -->

1 August

Only reason frees a person—the more unreasonable a person's life, the more unfree it is.

1

> You will ask by what path freedom is to be attained? For this one must learn to distinguish good and evil oneself, and not by the direction of the crowd.

> *Seneca.*

2

> If there is in you no desire to do evil, no ambition, then there is no virtuous act of which you would not be capable.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

3

> To conquer oneself is the best means of not being conquered. To bridle oneself is the best means of not having a master over oneself.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

4

> To tie oneself to a particular idea is almost the same as tying oneself to a post dug into the ground. The measure of freedom a person will enjoy will depend wholly on the length of his tether, and the greatest freedom will be enjoyed by the one who ties himself to the idea of the good for all.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 543 -->

5

> Only he lives freely who finds joy in fulfilling his duty, who has thought out the path that he must travel in life; who submits to the moral law not out of fear, but respects and obeys it because he considers that this is how it should be; over whom there is no other power than his own desire and judgment.

> *Cicero.*

6

> Only of that person can it be said that he is free who lives as he wants. A reasonable person always lives as he wants, because he desires only what it is possible to obtain. And therefore only a reasonable person is free.

> No one wishes to be guilty; no one wants to live in error, unrighteously; no one deliberately chooses for himself a life that will make him grieve and suffer; no one will say that he wants to live vilely and dissolutely. So all people who live an unrighteous life live so not by their own wish but against their will. They want neither grief nor fear, and yet they constantly suffer and are afraid. They do what they do not want. It follows that they are not free.

> The sage Diogenes said: “Only he is truly free who is always ready to die.” He wrote to the Persian king: “You cannot make truly free people slaves, any more than you can enslave a fish. Even if you take them captive, they will not be slaves to you. And if they die in captivity with you, what profit is it to you that you took them captive?”

> These are the words of a free man: such a man knows what true freedom consists in.

> *Epictetus.*

7

We have arranged for ourselves a life contrary to both the moral and the physical nature of man, and, living such a life, we want to be free.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 544 -->

8

> Obedience is good and necessary if it is obedience to good and truth; but if it is obedience to what is bad and false, then it is, on the contrary, the highest degree of human baseness and humiliation.

> *Carlyle.*

9

> Whoever is seized by the flame of passionate cravings, whoever thirsts for pleasures, keeps increasing his lusts and himself puts himself in chains.

> Whoever thinks only of the joys of tranquility, whoever, absorbed in his thoughts, is happy in that in which people do not see happiness, will break these chains of death, will cast them off forever.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

10

*Freedom is attained not by seeking freedom, but by seeking truth. Freedom must not be a goal, but can only be a consequence.*

Freedom cannot be given to a person by a person. Each person can free himself only by himself.
