<!-- vol. 42, p. 117 -->

6 October

Illnesses are a natural phenomenon, and one must know how to regard them as a natural condition of life, proper to people.

1

Neglect of the health of the body deprives one of the possibility of serving people. Excessive concern for one's body leads to the same thing. In order to find the middle, there is one means: to care for one's body to the extent that this does not hinder service, is not opposed to it.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 118 -->

2

When a sick person stops living and concentrates his whole life on treatment, it would be far more advantageous for him, not only with an incurable illness but also with curable illnesses, to pay no attention to the illness and live an ordinary life, which, even if it were shortened by this (which is always doubtful), would still be life and not constant fear and worry about his body.

3

*There is no illness that would prevent a person from doing what he ought. If you cannot serve people by your labors, serve them by an example of loving patience.*

4

> Illnesses of thought are more ruinous and occur more often than illnesses of the body.

> *Cicero.*

5

Hippocrates' saying that the basic condition of treatment is that it should do no harm is often not applied even in relation to the body; and in relation to the soul it is never applied.

The rule of not harming the body was not observed in the bloodlettings of former times, and it is not observed now in operations, in the taking of poisonous medicines, and in much else. And the harm to the soul, which always accompanies any treatment, no one thinks of or mentions. And yet this harm consists in the justification of the crudest egoism: the demand for service to oneself instead of one's own service to people.

Do not fear illness, fear treatment, and treatment not in the sense of harmful medicines, but, chiefly, treatment in the sense of considering oneself, because of illness, freed from moral demands.
