<!-- vol. 41, p. 553 -->

6 August

Reason, both of each separate person and of people taken together, is the sole guide of human life.

1

> The lamp of the body is the eye; so, if your eye is pure, your whole body will be light; but if the eye is bad, your body will be dark; so look: is not the light that is in you darkness?

> *Luke 11:34–35.*

2

> If we look at the life of most people, it will seem to us that man is a being created in order, like a plant, to absorb various juices, to grow, to continue his kind on earth, and finally to grow old and die. In that case, of all beings man least attains the aim of his existence, because he uses his superior faculties for aims that other beings attain far more surely and better. And of all beings man would most deserve contempt, at least in the eyes of true wisdom, if he did not partly realize, and did not hope to realize fully, that life, not only animal but rational, proper to man alone, of whose possibility he is conscious.

> *After Kant.*

3

Everything lives together and everything lives separately: a man lives separately, a worm lives separately. And each separate being considers itself alone to be living and demands everything from life for itself alone, and yet every separate existence is ceaselessly approaching death, the destruction of separate existence.

This contradiction would be insoluble if there were no reason in the world. But there is reason in man, and reason destroys this contradiction.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 554 -->

4

Rational life is like a man carrying a lantern far out in front of him, lighting his way. Such a man never reaches the end of the lighted place; the lighted place goes on ahead of him. Such is rational life, and only in such a life is there no death, because the lantern shines ceaselessly to the last minute, and to go on after it is as calm as during the whole course of life.

5

All people live and act partly by their own thoughts, partly by the thoughts of other people. In how far people live by their own thoughts and how far by the thoughts of other people lies one of the chief differences among people. Some people in most cases use their thoughts as a mental game, treat their reason like a flywheel from which the drive belt has been removed, and in their acts submit to custom, tradition, law; others, considering their thoughts the chief movers of all their activity, listen to the demands of their reason, submit to it, and only occasionally, and then after critical appraisal, follow what has been decided by others.

*Every person can and must use all that the collective reason of mankind has worked out, but at the same time he can and must test with his own reason the truths worked out by people who lived before him.*
