<!-- vol. 41, p. 177 -->

19 March

How perverse must be that arrangement of the world in which rich people, living by the labors of the poor, housed, fed, and clothed by the poor, can think that they are the benefactors of the poor!

1

> A stone falls on a jug—woe to the jug, a jug falls on a stone—woe to the jug; one way or another, it is all woe to the jug.

> *The Talmud.*

2

> If rich people can do good to the poor with their wealth, this comes about because the government, by favoring some, introduces inequality of property and thereby gives rise to the need for charity. And under such conditions, does the help that a rich man renders to a poor man deserve the name of charity at all, which people like to boast of as a merit?

> *Kant*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 178 -->

3

*The pleasures of the rich are acquired with the tears of the poor*.

4

> Granted, we do not steal gold and so many desyatinas of land; but all the same, by means of some deceit and concealment, we do the same thing on a smaller scale and as much as we can. When, for example, in trade agreements and in buying or selling something, we haggle and strive to pay less than is due, and try in every way to do so—is this not robbery? Is it not barbarism and plunder? Do not tell me that it was not a house you took, not slaves. Injustice is determined not by the value of what is stolen but by the intention of those who steal. Injustice and justice have the same force, in great things as in small. And I equally call a thief both the one who cuts a purse and takes another's money and the one who, buying something at the market, keeps back part of the real price. And a robber is not only the one who breaks through a wall and steals something from a house, but also the one who violates justice and takes something from his neighbor.

> *John Chrysostom.*

5

> “Do not be a robber of the poor because he is poor,” says Solomon. And yet this “robbing of the poor because he is poor” is the most ordinary thing: the rich man always uses the poor man's need in order to make him work for him or to buy what he sells at the lowest price.

> The robbing of the rich man on the highways because he is rich is met with far more rarely, because it is dangerous to rob the rich man, while the poor man can be robbed without risking anything.

> *John Ruskin.*

6

> It is true that wealth is an accumulation of labor; but usually one person produces the labor, and another the accumulation. And it is precisely this that wise people call “the division of labor”!

> *From the English.*

Righteous wealth can exist only among people of sufficient means. But where, as with us, there are hundreds of poor people for every rich man, it is impossible to be righteously rich.
