<!-- vol. 41, p. 59 -->

26 January

A rich man cannot help being unmerciful. Let him give free rein to the natural feeling of mercy, and he will soon cease to be rich.

1

> Is it not the utmost absurdity that we ourselves sit at table in laughter and surfeit, and meanwhile, hearing others weep as they pass along the street, we not only pay no attention to their weeping, but even resent them and call them deceivers? You say: deceivers. But will anyone really deceive for the sake of a piece of bread? And if you think that he deceives, then all the more must you take pity on him, and all the more must you deliver him from want. But if you do not want to give, at least do not insult.

> *John Chrysostom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 60 -->

2

> First give up plunder, and then give alms. Keep your hands from extortion, and then stretch them out in almsgiving. But if with the same hands we strip some and clothe others, then almsgiving will be an occasion for crime. It is better not to show mercy than to show such mercy.

> *John Chrysostom.*

3

Nowhere is the cruelty of the life of the rich so plainly seen as in their attempts at mercy.

4

A rich man has 15 rooms for 3 people, and a beggar cannot be let in to warm himself and spend the night.

A peasant has a hut seven arshins long for 7 souls, and he gladly lets in a wanderer.

5

> We love things for their imperfection, which is divinely ordained so that the law of human life should be effort, and the law of human judgment mercy.

> *John Ruskin.*

6

> As the first rule of wisdom consists in knowing oneself—though this is hardest of all—so the first rule of mercy consists in being content with little, though this is just as hard; and only a person so contented and at peace will prove strong enough to show mercy to others.

> *John Ruskin.*

7

> *Whoever has the goods of this world, but seeing his brother in need shuts up his heart from him—how does the love of God abide in him? My children! let us love not in word or with the tongue, but in deed and in truth.*

> *1 John 3:17–18.*

In order to love not in word or with the tongue, but in deed and in truth, a rich man must give to him who asks, as Christ said.

And if one gives to him who asks, then however much property a man has, he will soon cease to be rich. And if he ceases to be rich, he will fulfill what Christ said to the rich young man.
