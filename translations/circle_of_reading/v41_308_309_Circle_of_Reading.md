<!-- vol. 41, p. 308 -->

6 May

Compassion for animals is so natural to us that only by habit, tradition, and suggestion can we be brought to pitilessness toward the suffering and death of animals.

1

> Compassion for animals is so closely bound up with kindness of character that one may confidently assert that whoever is cruel to animals cannot be a kind person. Compassion for animals flows from the same source as a kind attitude toward man. Thus, for example, a sensitive person, on being reminded that while in a bad temper, in anger, or heated with wine, he beat his dog, his horse, his monkey—undeservedly, or needlessly, or too painfully—will feel the same dissatisfaction with himself as on being reminded of a wrong done to a person, which in this case we call the punishing voice of conscience.

> *Schopenhauer.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 309 -->

2

> Fear God, do not torment animals. Use them while they serve willingly, and let them go when they are tired, and give the dumb creatures their fill of food and drink.

> *Muhammad.*

3

> Meat food cannot be obtained without harm to animals, and the killing of animals makes the path to bliss difficult. Therefore let a man abstain from eating meat.

> *From the Brahmanic law of Manu.*

4

> *It is not because he can torment them heartlessly that man is higher than the other animals, but because he pities them.*

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

5

> Do not let your children kill insects: with this the killing of people begins.

> *Pythagoras.*

The joys that the feeling of pity and compassion for animals gives a person repay him many times over for the pleasures he will lose by giving up hunting and the eating of meat.
