<!-- vol. 42, p. 77 -->

26 September

The moral law is expressed equally clearly both by true wisdom and by true faith.

1

> It does not require exceptional profundity to understand what must be done for the will to be good. Inexperienced in understanding the world as a whole, unable to make sense of, to give an account of, all the events taking place in it—I ask myself only one thing: do I agree that the motives by which I am guided in my actions should become a binding law for all? If not, then my motives are unfit, and unfit not only because of the harm that may befall me or others from them, but because they are not fit to become the basis of laws binding on all; and yet reason compels me to respect such laws directly; although I do not yet understand on what this respect is founded, I do understand that what I respect in these laws is something that in its value far surpasses everything my inclinations prompt in me, and that to act solely out of respect for the moral law is a duty before which every other motive must give way.

> *Kant.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 78 -->

2

> And one of them, a lawyer, tempting him, asked, saying: Teacher! which is the greatest commandment in the law? Jesus said to him: love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your understanding. This is the first and greatest commandment. And the second is like it: love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments rest the whole law and the prophets.

> *Matthew 22:35–40.*

3

> The whole world is subject to one law, and in all rational beings there is one reason. And therefore for rational people the notion of perfection is one.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

4

> Remember that there is a God who wants not praise or glory from the people he created in his own likeness, but that they, guided by the understanding given them, should become like him in their actions. The fig tree, after all, is true to its work, and the dog and the bee as well. And will man really not fulfill his calling? But these great, holy truths grow dim in your memory; the bustle of daily life, unreasonable fear, infirmity of spirit, and the habit of being a slave stifle it.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

5

> Two things fill the soul with ever new and constantly growing wonder and reverence, the more often and the more steadily reflection occupies itself with them: the starry sky above me and the law of morality within me.

> *Kant.*

6

> *In everything, as you want people to act toward you, so you too act toward them; for in this is the law and the prophets.*

> *Matthew 7:12.*

The moral law is so clear that people cannot plead ignorance of the law. One thing remains for them: to renounce reason; and this is what they do.
