<!-- vol. 41, p. 237 -->

10 April

The ever greater and greater liberation of the divine source in people that is now taking place inevitably leads to the change of the existing order and the establishment of another.

1

> The longer I live, the more work there is before me. We live in an important time. Never have people had so much work before them. Our age is an age of revolution in the best sense of this word—not a material but a moral revolution. A higher idea of the social order and of human perfection is being worked out. We shall not live to see the harvest, but to sow with faith is a great happiness.

> *Channing.*

2

> Listen to that deep dissatisfaction with the present form of Christianity which is spreading in society and is expressed in murmuring, sometimes in bitterness, in sadness. All thirst for the coming of the kingdom of God. And it is drawing near.

> *A purer Christianity, though slowly, yet more and more takes the place of the one that is called by this name.*

> *Channing.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 238 -->

3

> Just as dryness (the absence of dampness) in nature depends on two causes opposite to each other: on great cold (winter frosts) and on great heat (summer heats), so also decisiveness of character (the absence of hesitation) in a person is conditioned by two causes opposite to each other: by a person's purely pagan self-consciousness on the one hand, and by a purely Christian one on the other.

> And just as in spring, that is, in the time of transition from winter to summer, the least dryness is observed and, on the contrary, the most dampness appears—so also in a person, during his state of transition from paganism to Christianity, the least decisiveness of character appears and, on the contrary, the most hesitation shows itself as to what he should do and how he should act.

> Only people who do not understand what this time and this state are caused by can fail to rejoice both at the springtime and at the state of transition from paganism to Christianity. But people who understand that the spring dampness in nature and the indecision, the hesitation in a person are caused by a state of transition in nature and in the person, namely—by the turning of the sun toward summer in the first case and by the raising of the conception of life in the second, will not only not grieve at the dampness and indecision they observe, but will rejoice at both, as at clear signs of the approach of summer in nature and of the kingdom of God in humanity.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

4

In our time of the general religious consciousness of the brotherhood of people, true science must point out the ways of applying this consciousness to life, and art must translate this consciousness into feeling.

5

> *The more distant the goal, the more necessary it is to go forward. Without hurrying, but also without resting.*

> *Mazzini.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 239 -->

6

> I see before me a people in the livery of slavery and of political lack of rights, a people in rags, hungry, worn out, picking up the crumbs that are insultingly thrown to it from the luxurious feast of the rich, or else I see it rushing about in the fury of a terrible revolt, drunk with bestial malice and savage joy, and I remember at this that these brutalized faces bear on them the imprint of the finger of God and have a common destiny with us. Then I turn my eyes to the future, and before me appears a people rising up in all its greatness, as brothers in faith, united by the same common bonds of equality and love; I see this people of the future, not corrupted by luxury, not brutalized by poverty, imbued with the consciousness of its human dignity. And at this vision my heart is painfully wrung for the present and trembles joyfully for the future.

> *Mazzini.*

“Let not your heart be troubled: believe in God and in me believe,” that is, believe in the divinity of your nature, which Christ revealed to you. The consciousness of this divinity cannot fail to be acknowledged by man and therefore cannot fail to be realized.
