<!-- vol. 41, p. 319 -->

12 May

Life is an unceasing approach to death, and therefore life can be a good only when death does not appear to be an evil.

1

> The days of our years are seventy years, and with greater strength eighty years; and the very best time of them is labor and sickness, for they pass quickly, and we fly.

> *Psalm 90:10.*

2

> When we are in the full strength of health and mind, we think about people and about the most trivial cares, and not about God: just as if propriety and custom required that we think about God only <!-- vol. 41, p. 320 --> in such a state when we have only so much reason left as to confess that we no longer possess it.

> *La Bruyère.*

3

> Imagine a crowd of people in chains. All of them are sentenced to death, and every day some of them are put to death before the eyes of the others. Those remaining, seeing these dying and waiting their turn, see their own lot.

> How should people live when they are in such a position? Can it be by busying themselves with beating, tormenting, killing one another? The most wicked robbers in such a position would not do evil to one another. And yet all people are in this position—and what is it they do?

> *After Pascal.*

4

> We see how a man occupying an important place falls and suddenly dies; how another visibly, little by little, wastes away, growing weaker every day, and at last goes out. Such striking events remain unnoticed, touching no one. People pay no more attention to them than to a flower that withers or to a falling leaf. They envy the places left vacant or inquire whether they are taken, and by whom.

> *La Bruyère.*

5

> “Here I will dwell during the rains, there I will settle in summer.” So the fool dreams and does not think of death, and it suddenly comes and carries off the man who is preoccupied, self-seeking, distracted.

> Neither son, nor father, nor relatives and those close to us—no one will help us when death strikes us; the good and wise man, having clearly grasped the meaning of this, clears the path leading to peace.

> *Buddhist wisdom.*

6

> A person comes into the world with clenched palms and, as it were, says: the whole world is mine; and he leaves it with open palms and, as it were, says: look, I take nothing with me.

> *The Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 321 -->

7

> And Christ told a parable: a certain rich man had a good harvest in the field; and he reasoned with himself: what am I to do? I have nowhere to gather my fruits. And he said: this is what I will do: I will pull down my barns and build bigger ones and gather there all my grain and all my goods, and I will say to my soul: soul! many goods are laid up for you for many years; rest, eat, drink, be merry. But God said to him: fool! this night your soul will be taken from you; and whose will be what you have prepared?

> *Luke 12:16–20.*

8

> “These sons belong to me, these riches belong to me”—such are the thoughts of a fool. How can sons and riches belong to him when he himself does not belong to himself?

> *Buddhist wisdom.*

9

> We carelessly rush into the abyss, holding a screen before us so as not to see it.

> *Pascal.*

10

> *Live as though you must take leave of life right now, as though the time left to you is an unexpected gift.*

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

11

> Your whole life is a tiny particle of infinite time. So mind you make of it everything that is possible.

> *Said ben Hamed.*

Remember that you do not live in the world, but pass through it.
