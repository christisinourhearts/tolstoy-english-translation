19 October

The meaning of life is revealed at once to him who is ready to submit to what will be revealed to him, and is hidden from him who has already decided in advance that he will recognize as true only such a meaning of life as will not disturb the life he loves and is accustomed to.

1

*“What am I? What must I do? What can I believe and what can I hope for?* to this everything in philosophy comes down,” says the philosopher Lichtenberg.—Of these questions the chief one is the middle one. If a person learns what he is to do, he will know everything he needs to know.

2

> On how to keep clothes from moths, iron from rust, potatoes from rotting, and the like, my opinion may change, but <!-- vol. 42, p. 158 --> on how to keep the soul from rotting I have nothing to learn; I need only carry out *what I know.*

> *Thoreau.*

3

> Blessed is he who has found his work; let him not seek any other blessedness. He has work and an aim in life.

> *Carlyle.*

4

> *Woe to people who see, not knowing what, who stand, not knowing on what.*

> *The Talmud.*

5

> Unhappy are people who do not know the meaning of their life, and yet the conviction that this cannot be known is so widespread among people that they even pride themselves, as on wisdom, on not wishing to know it.

> *Pascal.*

6

> A man is sitting in prison and does not know what sentence has been passed on him. He has only an hour left in which to learn about it, and if he learns that he has been sentenced to death, this hour is enough for him to obtain the repeal of the sentence. Would he really use this hour not to find out what the sentence is, but to play cards? That would be utterly absurd. And yet that is exactly how people act who do not think about God and about eternity.

> *Pascal.*

7

> Every bird knows where to make its dwelling. Knowing where its dwelling is, it shows that it knows its purpose. Can it be that man, the most intelligent of all beings, cannot know what a bird knows: what the purpose of his life is?

> *Chinese wisdom.*

Comprehension of the true meaning of life is either inaccessible: if one seeks the meaning of the life of the whole world, or so simple that it is revealed to the simple-minded and to babes: if what is sought is the meaning of my life—what I am to do.
