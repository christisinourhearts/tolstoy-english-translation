<!-- vol. 41, p. 92 -->

9 February

The material evil produced by war, however enormous it may be, is insignificant in comparison with the evil of the perversion of the concepts of good and evil that war brings into the souls of the simple, little-thinking people of the working folk.

1

> The passions aroused by war, international hatred, reverence for military glory, the thirst for victory or revenge stifle the conscience of the people, turn the mutual goodwill of people toward one another into a base, senseless self-love called patriotism, kill the love of freedom, bring people to submit to oppressors out of a savage wish to cut the throats of other people or out of fear that other people may cut their throats. The passions aroused by war so pervert the religious feeling of people that the recognized teachers of Christianity bless, in the name of Christ, the implements of murder and robbery and offer thanksgivings to the God of peace for victories in which the earth is covered with mountains of mutilated corpses and sorrow fills the hearts of innocent people.

> *Henry George.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 93 -->

2

*A child, meeting a child with a smile, expresses benevolent joy, and so does every uncorrupted person. And yet a person of one people, without even seeing a person of another tribe, already hates him and is ready to inflict suffering and death on him. What great criminals, then, are those who call forth these feelings and actions in people!*

3

> The most beautiful weapon is an unblessed weapon. And therefore the reasonable person does not rely on it. He values peace and quiet most of all. He conquers, but not with weapons.

> *Laozi.*

4

> “Divide and rule”—in this lies the chief trick of all oppressors. Only by stirring up tribal enmity, international hatred, and local prejudices, only by setting some peoples against others, can aristocracy and autocracy be established and maintained. Therefore whoever wants to free people must raise them above feelings of hatred; otherwise he will not attain his aim.

> *Henry George.*

War is a state of people in which the lowest and most vicious people receive power and glory.
