<!-- vol. 41, p. 165 -->

13 March

The condition of wisdom is moral purity; its consequence is peace of soul.

1

A good person cares more about doing what ought to be done than about what happens to him. To do what ought to be done is my business, such a person says; what happens is God's business. And whatever happens, nothing can prevent me from doing what I ought.

2

A person who makes it a rule for himself to do what he wants will not want for long to do what he does.

3

> When we feel weakest of all in body, we can be strongest of all in spirit.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

*The best proof of wisdom is a continually good disposition of spirit.*

*Montaigne.*

Do only what raises you up spiritually, and be sure that by this very thing you can be most useful to society.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 166 -->

6

When something grieves and torments you, think: 1) how much that is still more unpleasant could happen to you and does happen to other people; 2) recall how in former times events or circumstances, which you now recall calmly and completely indifferently, grieved you and tormented you exactly as now; and 3), most important—think about this: that what grieves and torments you is only a trial in which you can show your spiritual strength and strengthen it.

7

> People's souls are at times in a state of the greatest perfection, and at times in a state of the greatest depravity. Treasure the good hours, hold on to them, and drive the bad ones away from you, and then more and more often you will live in good hours, and more and more rarely in bad ones.

> *After Bacon.*

8

Only he can be wise who does not consider himself wise. And only he does not consider himself wise who always sees before him the perfection of God.

9

> Very rich is he who has nothing to lose.

> *Chinese proverb.*

Wisdom is infinite—the further you advance toward it, the more necessary it becomes.

A person can always become better.
