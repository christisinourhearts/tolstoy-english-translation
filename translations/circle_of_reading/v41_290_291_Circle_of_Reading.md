<!-- vol. 41, p. 290 -->

29 April

A person can fulfill his purpose equally well in a sick state as in a healthy one.

1

If a person did not doubt the indestructibility of his life after death, all illnesses would appear to him only as an approach to the transition from one life to another—a transition more desirable than undesirable—and then he would bear the pain of illness just as we bear the pain from the strain of labor which, we know, will end in good. During pains we would have an explanation of what is taking place in us and would prepare ourselves for a new state.

2

People usually think that one can serve God and be useful to people only while healthy. Not true! Often the reverse. Christ served God and people most of all when he was at the very point of death on the cross, when he forgave those who were killing him. Every sick person can do the same. And one cannot say which state, health or illness, is more favorable for serving God and people.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 291 -->

3

Ever since people began to think, they have recognized that nothing so furthers the moral life of people as the remembrance of death. But the misdirected art of medicine, instead of concerning itself with the relief of sufferings, sets itself the goal of delivering people from death, and teaches them to hope for deliverance from death, for the removal of the thought of death from themselves, and thereby deprives people of the chief incentive to a moral life.

4

*For oneself only, for serving oneself, one needs more health and strength, but for serving God they are not only not needed, but often—the reverse.*

5

How often, when dealing with the sick, we forget that the main thing the sick person needs is not hiding the approaching death from him but, on the contrary, calling him to the consciousness of his spiritual, growing divine nature, which is not subject to diminution or death.

Illnesses almost always, in destroying bodily strength, set free spiritual strength. And for a person who has transferred his consciousness into the spiritual realm, they do not deprive him of the good but, on the contrary, increase it.
