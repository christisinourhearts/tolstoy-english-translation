<!-- vol. 42, p. 338 -->

12 December

Kindness conquers everything, and is itself unconquerable.

1

> *One can hold out against everything, but not against kindness.*

> *Rousseau.*

2

> Not the condemnation of evil but the exaltation of good establishes harmony and unity in personal and world life. A person condemns evil and the one who does it, and this very condemnation of evil and of those who do it only furthers the growth of evil, whereas disregard of evil and care only for the good destroy evil.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

3

If a good deed has a motivating cause, it is no longer good; if it has as its foreseen consequence a reward, it is also not good. Good is outside the chain of causes and effects.

4

> Just as torches and fireworks pale and become invisible in the light of the sun, so the mind, even genius, and likewise beauty, fade and are eclipsed before kindness of heart.

> *Schopenhauer.*

5

> Boundless tenderness is the greatest gift and possession of all truly great people.

> *John Ruskin.*

6

> The most tender plants make their way through the hardest earth, through the cracks of rocks. So it is with kindness. What <!-- vol. 42, p. 339 --> wedge, what hammer, what battering ram can compare with the strength of a kind, sincere person? Nothing can resist him.

> *Thoreau.*

7

> Where there is a person, there is also an occasion to do him good.

> *Seneca.*

8

It seems to us that we love those whom we like, who praise us, who do us good; but this is not love, but either partiality or an exchange of advantage: he praises us, and we praise him; he does us good, and we pay him back in kind. There is nothing bad in such a feeling, but this is not true love, not divine love. We love with divine, true love only when we love a person not because he is pleasant to us or has done us good, but because in him, as in every person, we see the spirit of God.

Only when we love people in this way can we love, as Christ taught, not only those who love us, but also people who are bad, harmful to us and to the whole world, our enemies. And such love not only does not diminish because people are bad and hate us, but, on the contrary, becomes stronger and more lasting. It becomes stronger because the more a person is possessed by malice, the more he needs love. And such love is more lasting than partial love and love for those who love us, because no changes in the one we love can change it.

To answer an evil word with a kind one, to render a service for an injury, to turn the other cheek when one has been struck on one, is a sure means, always available to all, of taming malice.
