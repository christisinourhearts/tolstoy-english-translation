## NON-RESISTANCE TO EVIL BY VIOLENCE

“You have heard that it was said: ‘an eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth.’ But I say to you: ‘do not resist evil’” (Matthew 5:38, 39).

Christ teaches not to resist evil. This teaching is true because it tears evil out by the root from the heart both of the offended and of the offender. This teaching forbids doing that by which evil in the world is multiplied and not stopped. When one person attacks another, offends him, he thereby kindles in the other a feeling of hatred, the root of all evil. What, then, are we to do to put out this feeling of evil? Are we really to do the very thing that calls forth this feeling of evil—offend the other, that is, repeat the bad deed? To act so means, instead of driving out the devil, to strengthen him. Satan cannot be driven out by Satan, wrong cannot be cleansed by wrong, and evil cannot be conquered by evil.

Therefore *non-resistance* to evil by evil is the only means of conquering evil. It kills the evil feeling both in the one who did the evil and in the one who suffered it.

“But, they ask, even if the idea of the teaching is true, can it be fulfilled?”—It can be fulfilled just as every good prescribed by the law of God can. Good cannot be fulfilled in all circumstances without self-renunciation, privation, suffering, and, in extreme cases, without the loss of life itself. But he who values life more than the fulfillment of the will of God is already dead to the only true life. Such a person, trying to save his life, will lose it. Besides, and in general, where non-resistance costs the sacrifice of one life or of some essential good of life, resistance costs a thousand such sacrifices.

*Non-resistance preserves, resistance destroys.*

It is incomparably safer to act justly than unjustly; to bear an offense than to resist it with violence— <!-- vol. 41, p. 175 --> safer even with regard to the present life. If all people did not resist evil with evil, our world would be blessed.

“But when only a few act so, what will become of them?”—If even only one person acted so, and all the rest agreed to crucify him, would it not be more glorious for him to die praying for his enemies than to be a king wearing a crown spattered with the blood of the slain? But whether one or thousands of people have firmly resolved not to resist evil with evil, it is all the same: they, whether among enlightened or among savage neighbors, are much safer from violence than those who rely on violence. A robber, a murderer, a deceiver will sooner leave them in peace than those who resist with weapons. Those who have taken the sword will perish by the sword, while those who seek peace, who act in a friendly way, inoffensively, who forget and forgive offenses, for the most part enjoy peace, or, if they die, die blessed.

Thus, if all kept the commandment of non-resistance, then obviously there would be neither offense nor villainy. If such people were the majority, they would establish the rule of love and goodwill even over those who offend, never resisting evil with evil, never using violence. If such people were a fairly numerous minority, they would exert such a moral influence on society that every cruel punishment would be abolished, and violence and enmity would be replaced by peace and love. If they were only a small minority, it would rarely experience anything worse than the contempt of the world, while the world meanwhile, without itself feeling it and without being grateful for it, would constantly become wiser and better.

And if, in the very worst case, some of the members of the minority were persecuted to death, then these who perished for the truth would leave behind them their teaching, already sanctified by their martyrs' blood.

*Ballou.* [^1]

## Notes

[^1]: The leader of one of the American spiritual communities, Adin Ballou, who died in August 1890, for 50 years wrote and published books chiefly on the question of non-resistance to evil by violence. In these writings, admirable for the clarity and beauty of their exposition, the question is examined from all possible sides. One of his principal works is the “Catechism of Non-Resistance.”
