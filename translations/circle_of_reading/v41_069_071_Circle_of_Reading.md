<!-- vol. 41, p. 69 -->

29 January

Do not think that wisdom is a property only of special people. Wisdom is necessary to all people and is therefore proper to all people. Wisdom lies in knowing one's purpose and the means of fulfilling it.

1

> By three paths can we come to wisdom: first, by the path of reflection, which is the noblest path; second, by the path of imitation, which is the easiest path; and third, by the path of experience, which is the hardest path.

> *Confucius.*

2

> *A person's worth is measured not by the truth he possesses, but by the labor he has put into acquiring it.*

> *Lessing.*

3

> Life is a school in which failure is a better teacher than success.

> *Suleiman of Granada.*

4

> Would you study yourself—look at people and their deeds.

> Would you study people—look into your own heart.

> *Schiller.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 70 -->

5

> To understand things means to have been in them and then to come out of them. So there must be captivity and then deliverance; enchantment and then disenchantment; infatuation and cooling. Whoever is still under the enchantment, just like whoever has never been enchanted, is equally unable to understand. We know well only what we first believed and then examined. To understand, one must be free, but before that one must be captive.

> *Amiel.*

6

> When the duration of the life of a person or a people seems to us as insignificant as the life of a gnat, and, conversely, the life of a gnat as infinite as the life of a heavenly body with all its dust of peoples, we feel ourselves both very small and very great, and from the whole height of the heavenly spaces we can survey our own existence and those little whirlwinds that agitate our little Europe. This is what makes thought free.

> *Amiel.*

7

> Light shines through us from within or from behind—we can know that we are nothing, and that this light is everything. What we usually call a man—an eating, drinking, planting, counting being—does not present man to us in his true light, but, on the contrary, shows him in a false one. The true man is the soul that lives in him. If only he showed it in his deeds, we would bow down before it.

> A wise proverb says: “God comes to us without bells.” This means that there is no partition between us and the source of all, no wall between man, the effect, and God, the cause.

> *Emerson.*

8

> The soul is in itself both its own judge and its own refuge. Do not offend your conscious soul, the supreme inward judge.

> *Manu.*

There are no situations, and no deeds so insignificant, that wisdom could not show itself in them.
