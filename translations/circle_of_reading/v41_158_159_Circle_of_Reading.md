<!-- vol. 41, p. 158 -->

10 March

That which gives life is one in all.

1

> Every living thing fears torment, every living thing fears death; understand yourself in every living being, do not torment and do not kill, do not cause suffering and death.

> Every living thing wants the same as you, every living thing values its life; understand yourself, then, in every living being.

> *Buddhist wisdom. The Dhammapada.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 159 -->

2

> All that you see, all in which there is the divine and the human—all this is one: we are members of one great body. Nature made us kin, bringing us into the world from one and the same material and for one and the same purpose. She put mutual love in us and made us sociable, friendly; she established in us the striving for justice and the sense of duty; by her ordinance, to destroy is worse than to perish; by her command, the hand must always be ready to help. We are born for union. Our union is like a stone vault, which would collapse if the stones did not rest on one another.

> *Seneca.*

3

A person finds his happiness only in serving his neighbors, and, thanks to this service, he becomes one with the foundation of the life of the world.

4

I am vividly conscious of and feel my unity with man. The same unity I feel (though more weakly) between myself and an animal. Still more weakly I feel it with insects, with plants, and the consciousness of this unity disappears completely for me in relation to microscopic and telescopic beings. But the fact that I have no sense organ for knowing this unity does not prove that it does not exist.

5

> The path of life is one, and all of us sooner or later will come together on this path. The knowledge of this path is too clearly laid in our heart, this road is too wide and noticeable for us not to come upon it. At the end of this road—God, and he calls us to himself, and how painful it is to look at people when they go past this road, by the road of death.

> The path of life is wide, but many do not know it and go by the road of death.

> *After Gogol.*

*Drive away from yourself everything that hinders you from feeling your connection with all living things*.
