<!-- vol. 42, p. 80 -->

28 September

The greater part of people's actions is performed not by reasoning, not even by feeling, but by unconscious imitation, by suggestion.

1

Actions performed by suggestion can be both good and evil. Only actions performed consciously, by the demands of conscience, cannot be bad. And yet for thousands of actions performed by us by suggestion there will hardly be found one performed consciously.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 81 -->

2

> Enlightenment is a person's emergence from his own childishness, which he himself sustains. Childishness consists in his inability to use his reason without the guidance of another. And this childishness is sustained by the person himself when its cause lies not in a lack of reason but in a lack of resolve and courage to use it without the guidance of another.

> *Kant.*

3

> *Have the courage to use your own reason. This is the guiding rule for enlightenment.*

> *Kant.*

4

If a person could, out of all the voices speaking in his soul, unerringly recognize the voice of his true, eternal *I*, he would never err, never do evil. It is for just this that one needs to know oneself.

5

Look attentively at the causes of the ignorance of the popular masses, and you will see that the chief cause lies by no means in a lack of schools and libraries, as we are accustomed to think, but in those superstitions which are instilled in them and unceasingly maintained in them, by all the means of suggestion, by those people to whom these superstitions are profitable.

6

True enlightenment is spread only by the example of a moral life. And all the supposedly enlightening activity of schools, books, newspapers, theaters, and the like not only has nothing in common with enlightenment, but for the most part is directly opposed to it.

Every time you feel that you are ready to act not by reasoning and not by your own inner impulse, but by an outside, alien influence, stop and think whether the suggestion that draws you is good or bad.
