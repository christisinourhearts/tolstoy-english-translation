## I THE ORDER OF THE WORLD

The world is society such as it was in the times of Jesus and such as it essentially is now too, since eighteen centuries of Christianity have not changed its foundations, but have only softened their manifestations. Despite the change of outward forms, this society everywhere rests on force and self-love.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 498 --> They command only because they have power; they oppress and torment because they command for themselves. Such is the world, and between the world and Jesus there is eternal struggle, because what Jesus wants is directly opposed to what the world wants. Jesus wants people to be free, wants that, being equal before the common father, they should be equal also before one another, that brotherly love should unite them into one family. The world, on the other hand, wants the subjection of almost all to a few; it wants not brothers, but small and great—the small, deprived of all rights, and the great, to whom they would belong and who would dispose of them as they please.

Jesus wants power to be service; the world wants it to be domination. Therefore Jesus condemns the world, and the world hates Jesus, and this hatred, extending to the disciples of Jesus, subjects them to persecution from the world. If the world tolerated them, if there were any bond whatever between it and them, they would be not disciples of Jesus but betrayers of his teaching, accomplices of the one who betrayed him with a kiss.

And so, you—those who want what Jesus wanted, those whom he chose to continue his work—be ready for what awaits you in the world; but know too that the world will not be the stronger to the end, but will be vanquished, because the truth that must conquer is already beginning to shine before the eyes of all, is beginning to stir all consciences, and the world tries in vain to kill it, as it killed Jesus. The times are drawing near; a muffled murmur foretells deliverance; on all sides is heard the cracking of breaking chains; the strong are troubled—they feel that they are growing weak; while the weak raise their heads. The last battle must take place. Let each stand firm in this battle, which decides the question whether mankind will be set free by Christ according to his promise, or will forever be the slave of the sons of him who was a murderer from the beginning.

*Lamennais.*
