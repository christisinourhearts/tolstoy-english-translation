<!-- vol. 42, p. 58 -->

20 September

Everything good is acquired only by effort.

1

> If there are people who do not engage in study, and if they do engage in it, do not succeed in it, let these people not despair and not stop; if there are people who do not ask enlightened people about doubtful things they do not know, and if they do ask, do not become more enlightened by it—let them not despair; if there are people who do not reflect, and if they do reflect, cannot acquire a clear understanding of the meaning <!-- vol. 42, p. 59 --> of life—let them not despair; if there are people who do not distinguish good from evil, and if they do distinguish, have no clear notion of this distinction—let them not despair; if there are people who do not do good, or if they do, do not give all their strength to it—let them not despair; what others would do in one attempt, they will do in ten. What others would do in a hundred attempts, they will do in a thousand.

> He who truly follows this rule of constancy, however ignorant he may be, will certainly become enlightened, and however weak he may be, will certainly become strong.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

2

> Enter by the narrow gate; because wide is the gate and broad is the way that lead to destruction, and many go by it; because narrow is the gate and tight is the way that lead to life, and few find it.

> *Matthew 7:13–14.*

3

*Bad deeds are easily done—deeds that bring misfortune to ourselves; but what is beneficial and good for us is done only with difficulty and effort.*

*Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

Never does the path to good knowledge run over silky turf strewn with lilies; a person always has to climb over bare rocks.

*John Ruskin.*

5

> The search for truth is carried on not with gladness but with agitation and unease; but all the same one must seek it, because, without finding the truth and loving it, you will perish. But, you will say, if the truth wanted me to seek it and love it, it would itself

> *Pascal.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 60 --> reveal itself to me. And it does reveal itself to you, but you pay no attention to this. Seek the truth, then—it wants this.

6

Only he can live a good life who constantly thinks about it.

A person would cry out with pain if, while not working, he felt in his muscles the pain that he experiences, without noticing it, when working. In just the same way a person who does not do spiritual work on his inner world experiences agonizing pain from those adversities that are borne, without noticing them, by a person who places the chief business of life in moral perfecting.
