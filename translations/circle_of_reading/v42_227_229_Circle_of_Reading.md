<!-- vol. 42, p. 227 -->

4 November

Arguments always do more to obscure the truth than to make it clear.

Truth must ripen in solitude. And when it has ripened, it is so clear that it is accepted without argument.

1

*There is great strength in a person who is able to keep silent, even though he is right.*

*Cato.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 228 -->

2

> Refrain from argument—argument is the most unfavorable condition for persuasion. Opinions are like nails: the more you hammer at them, the deeper they go in.

> *Juvenal.*

3

> If someone has grieved or offended you, beware of answering back while you are agitated, and if a justification is necessary, then first of all calm the agitation of your soul.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

4

> Do not assert what you are not completely sure of. Do not lightly believe everything you hear.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

5

> If you cannot at once quiet your anger, hold your tongue. Keep silent, and you will calm down sooner.

> *Baxter.*

6

Can you reasonably be indignant with a person afflicted with some repulsive ailment. How is he to blame that being near him is repugnant to you. Treat moral ailments in exactly the same way.

“But,” you will say, “a person has reason, with the help of which he can recognize his vices.” That is true. Consequently you too possess reason and can, by reasonable treatment, bring your neighbor to recognize his shortcomings; so show your reason, then, know how to awaken the conscience in a person, and heal his blindness without anger, impatience, or haughtiness.

*Marcus Aurelius.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 229 -->

7

> The word is the key of the heart. If a conversation leads nowhere, then even one word is superfluous.

> *Chinese saying.*

8

> When you are alone, think of your own sins; when in company, forget the sins of others.

> *Chinese saying.*

The more you want to talk, the greater the danger that you will say something bad.
