<!-- vol. 42, p. 307 -->

3 December

Art is a human activity consisting in this, that one person consciously, by certain external means, conveys to others the feelings he experiences, and other people are infected by these feelings and experience them.

1

A genuine work of art brings it about that in the consciousness of the one who receives it the separation between him and the artist is destroyed, and not only between him and the artist, but between him and all the people who receive the same work of art. It is in this liberation of the personality from its separation from other people, from its loneliness, in this merging of the personality with others, that the chief attractive power and the good quality of art lie.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 308 -->

2

*Just as a work of thought is a work of thought only when it conveys new considerations and thoughts and does not repeat what is known, so in exactly the same way a work of art is a work of art only when it brings a new feeling into the common round of human life.*

3

Art is one of the two organs of the progress of humanity. Through the word a person communicates in thought; through the images of art he communicates in feeling with all people, not only of the present but also of the future.

Just as the perfecting of knowledge takes place, that is, truer, more needed knowledge displaces and replaces mistaken and unneeded knowledge, so in exactly the same way the perfecting of feelings takes place by means of art, displacing lower feelings, less good and less needed for the good of people, with higher ones, better and more needed for that good.

In this lies the purpose of art.

4

Music shows a person those possibilities of greatness that are in his soul, says Emerson. The same can be said of all true art.

5

Art is the flower of the life of a whole society. The flower of such a society as the society of cruel parasites of the upper circle of our Christian world cannot be good. It will inevitably be depraved and ugly.

Such indeed is the art of our society, which has recently reached the highest degree of depravity and ugliness.

6

If the question were put as to what is better for our Christian world: to be deprived of all that is now considered <!-- vol. 42, p. 309 --> art, together with the false, and of all the good that there is now, then I think that every reasonable and moral person would again decide the question as Plato decided it for his republic and as all the Christian and Muslim teachers of humanity decided it, that is, would say: “Better that there should be no art at all than that the depraved art, or the semblance of it, that exists now should continue.”

7

The practitioners of present-day science and art have not fulfilled and cannot fulfill their calling, because they have made rights out of their duties.

Our refined, depraved art could arise only on the slavery of the masses of the people and can continue only as long as that slavery lasts.
