<!-- vol. 41, p. 467 -->

4 July

Punishment is a notion that humanity is beginning to outgrow.

1

> Another parable he set before them, saying:

> The kingdom of heaven is like a man who sowed good seed in his field. But while people slept, his enemy came and sowed <!-- vol. 41, p. 468 --> weeds among the wheat and went away. When the green came up and the fruit appeared, then the weeds appeared too. And the slaves of the householder came and said to him: master! did you not sow good seed in your field? where then do the weeds on it come from? He said to them: an enemy has done this. And the slaves said: do you want us to go and gather them out? But he said: no, lest in gathering out the weeds you pull up the wheat together with them.

> *Matthew 13:24–29.*

2

When a child beats the floor he has bumped himself against, this is very unreasonable, but as understandable as a person's jumping when he has hurt himself badly. It is also understandable when a person who has received a blow, under the influence of the pain, answers the one who struck him in the same way. But to do evil to a person in cold blood because he once did evil before, and to explain this by reasonable arguments, would be completely incomprehensible if man did not have the ability and the inclination to invent reasonable explanations for his bad acts.

3

People have come to believe so much in the justifications they invented for their vindictiveness that they ascribed vindictiveness to God, calling vindictiveness justice, retribution.

4

A person has done evil. And so another person or people, to counteract this evil, find nothing better than to do yet another evil, which they call punishment.

5

All punishment is based by no means on reasoning or on a sense of justice, but solely on the bad desire to do evil to the one who has done evil to you or to another.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 469 -->

6

Punishment, applied to upbringing, to the social order, to religious understanding, not only has not contributed and does not contribute to the improvement of children, of societies, and of all people who believe in punishment beyond the grave, but has produced and produces countless calamities, hardening children, corrupting society, and, by the promise of hell, depriving virtue of its chief foundation.

7

Already many years ago people began to understand the unreasonableness of punishment and began to devise various theories of intimidation, prevention, correction. But all these theories fall one after another, because at the basis of them all there is only revenge, and all the theories only try to hide this basis. They devise a great deal, but do not dare to do the one thing needed, namely, to do nothing, but to leave the one who has sinned to repent or not repent, to amend or not amend; and for those who devise the theories and apply them, to live a good life themselves.

8

Punishment and the whole criminal law will be the same object of perplexity and astonishment for future generations as cannibalism, the offering of people as sacrifices to a deity, and the like are for us. “How could they not see all the senselessness, cruelty, and harmfulness of what they were doing?” our descendants will say.

9

Capital punishment is the most obvious proof that the order of our society is completely alien to Christianity.

10

*To impose punishment is the same as to heat fire. Every crime always carries with it a punishment both crueler, and more reasonable, and more acceptable than the one people can impose.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 470 -->

11

Most of the people who fill our prisons and die on the gallows are all beings disinherited by the very law that arrogates to itself the right to punish them.

*Herbert Bigelow.*

One must know and remember that the desire to punish is a lower, animal feeling, which requires its suppression, not its elevation into a reasonable activity.
