6 February

The desires that seize us most are lustful desires, desires that are never satisfied, and the more they are satisfied, the more they grow.

1

> Look at how a slave wants to live. First of all he wants to be set free. He thinks that without this he can be neither free nor happy. He says this: if I were set free, I would at once be completely happy: I would not be forced to please and fawn on my master, I could talk with anyone as with my equal, I could go where I like without asking anyone's leave.

> But as soon as he is set free, he at once looks for someone to flatter so as to get a dinner, because the master no longer feeds him. For this he is ready to stoop to every kind of vileness, and he falls again into slavery, harder than before.

> When things become especially hard for him, he remembers his former slavery and says:

> — And yet I didn't have it badly with my master! I didn't look after myself; they clothed me, shod me, fed me, and when I was sick, they looked after me. And the service was not hard. But now <!-- vol. 41, p. 88 --> how many troubles! I had one master, and now how many I have! How many people I must please to get rich!

> To get rich, he endures every hardship, and when he gets what he wanted, it again turns out that he has entangled himself in various unpleasant cares.

> And still he does not take hold of reason. He thinks: now if I became a great commander, all my misfortunes would end: how they would praise me! And he sets off on campaign. He endures every privation, suffers like a convict, and still asks to go on campaign a second and a third time. And his life gets worse and worse.

> If he wants to rid himself of all his troubles and misfortunes, let him come to his senses. Let him learn what the true good of life is. The true good is to act at every step of his life according to the laws of truth and good inscribed in the soul of every person. Only by acting so will a person receive both true freedom and that good which every human heart desires.

> *After Epictetus.*

2

> Whoever is seized by the base thirst for bodily pleasures—by this thirst full of poison, around him sufferings will twine, like climbing dodder.

> But whoever conquers this thirst, from him all sufferings fall away, as raindrops roll off a lotus leaf.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

3

*They desire, they are agitated, they suffer over what is bad. What is truly good often comes not only independently of our desires but contrary to them, and often only after agitation and suffering over what is bad.*

4

People are often prouder of the strength of their desires than of the strength of their power over their desires. What a strange error!

Remember how passionately you desired in the past many things that now arouse in you, if not disgust, then disdain. The same will happen with the desires that now agitate you.

Remember how much you lost trying to satisfy your former desires. The same will happen now too. Humble them, quiet them; this is always the most advantageous thing and at the same time always possible.
