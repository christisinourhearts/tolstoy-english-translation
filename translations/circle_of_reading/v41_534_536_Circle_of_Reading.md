## I REPENTANCE

Not to resist evil does not mean not to fight against evil: on the contrary, it means to fight with evil, but not to fight with the person, to fight only against what is evil, erroneous in the person, to fight with evil while feeling compassion for and loving the person possessed by evil.

A person's words and actions depend on the direction he gives to his thoughts. And therefore the struggle with evil consists in trying to change the thoughts, or in being able to call forth a change of thought, of the person doing evil. In this sense the possibility of an apostolic struggle with evil opens up. People who feel in their soul the need of a feat, of self-denial, can go singly to meet the evildoers in their camp, as close brothers, as housemates to them, and at the cost of personal suffering and humiliation, by the effort of their whole life, awaken the son of God in their fallen brothers. The way consists in this: having dedicated oneself to God, to bind one's life with the life of the evildoer, and in all the good deeds of everyday life to share the good of life with him as a brother, and to hinder his evildoing by one's presence and one's persuasion, risking one's personal bodily life and in this way <!-- vol. 41, p. 535 --> dedicating it to God. This is a fine outlet for a great soul in our time, when there are so many easy ways of telling the truth to the enormous majority of people who thirst for it, when the special calling of apostleship among peaceful brothers no longer has a place, when the brotherhood of people and nations, if not yet exalted, is already recognized by people; and the time is not far off when all people will be taught by God, and when, one may hope, in place of evildoers there will turn out to be sick, unhappy people who did evil only because they had been neglected. In any case, the enemy can be conquered only when the best forces go out to struggle with him. The struggle with evil must always be the lot of the best representatives of humanity, and not, as now in the existing order, of policemen, jailers, officials, people of greed, vanity, pride, hypocrisy, and partiality, so that if these vices were excluded from the privileges of the present fighters against evil, these large salaries, reprehensible incomes, ranks, decorations, subordination, and servility, then all these fighters and orators, now so zealous—all of them would run away.

To bind the evil in a person, one must call forth in him a change of thoughts. By deception a change of thoughts can, though unreliably, still be called forth, but by violence never and in no case.

There is an undoubted gain for people in not resisting the evildoer—the gain of acquiring, by non-resistance and good, a repenting person. And we cannot even approximately imagine with what a soul cleansed by repentance may reward us. In every evildoer to whom we do violence and whom we punish, we lose, perhaps, an ascetic. The Gospel says that “in heaven, among the angels of God, there is more joy over one repenting sinner than over ninety-nine righteous people who have no need of repentance.” If, if not all, then at least a hundredth part of all the so-called criminals who have been and are being senselessly killed, who have suffered and are suffering while serving their punishment, placing their atonement in it; if at least a thousandth part of them, under the influence of non-resistance and good, repented to the end and themselves sought out the means of their atonement, then how can we know what these repentant souls might give us. Perhaps the kingdom of God would long ago have been among us in glory and in power, and we would long ago have ceased to see all those <!-- vol. 41, p. 536 --> calamities amid which we now see no glimmer of light. So that, in resisting evil, we not only violate the proportion, the rightness of our life, and harm our spiritual health, but we also lose extraordinarily, immeasurably much: we lose what we could gain if we wished.

*Buka.*
