<!-- vol. 41, p. 391 -->

10 June

There is something in our soul that is not subject to death. We may be conscious of this something, and we may not be conscious of it.

1

> He who knows other people is clever; he who knows himself is enlightened.

> He who conquers others is strong; he who conquers himself is mighty.

> But he who, dying, knows that he is not destroyed is eternal.

> *Laozi.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 392 -->

2

And people are born and live only as certain details of God, which therefore cannot be destroyed—they can disappear from our eyes, but not be destroyed.

3

The fact that one person took longer to pass through the field of vision open to me, and another passed through it quickly, can in no way make me ascribe more actual life to the first and less to the second. I know without doubt that if I saw a person passing by my window—quickly or slowly, it makes no difference—I know without doubt that this person existed before the time when I saw him, and will go on existing after he has disappeared from my eyes.

4

> I do not believe in any one of the existing religions and therefore cannot be suspected of blindly following some tradition or the influences of upbringing. But throughout my whole life I have thought, as deeply as I was able, about the law of our life. I sought it in the history of mankind and in my own consciousness, and I came to the unshakable conviction that death does not exist; that life cannot be other than eternal; that infinite perfecting is the law of life; that every faculty, every thought, every aspiration placed in me must have its practical development; that there are in us thoughts and aspirations far exceeding the possibilities of our earthly life; that the very fact that these aspirations are in us and that we cannot trace their origin to our senses serves as proof that they enter us from a realm lying outside the earth and can be realized only outside it; that nothing perishes here on earth except various forms of matter, and that to think that *we* die because our body dies is *the same as thinking that a workman has died because his tools have worn out*.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 393 -->

5

> He who knows that the foundation of his life is spirit is beyond all danger. Closing the gates of his senses at the end of life, he feels no unease.

> *After Laozi.*

6

> God is eternal and universal life in infinite time and space. He is all that is, and there is no other god but God. Everything is in him, and nothing exists outside him. And therefore all existence is a manifestation of his life, and all life at birth comes not out of nonexistence but out of him, and at death does not cease to exist but returns to him.

> *Enfantin.*

7

True life is outside time and space, and therefore death can only change the manifestation of life in this world, but can in no way destroy life itself.

8

Faith in immortality cannot be received from someone else; one cannot convince oneself of immortality. For there to be faith in immortality, there must be immortality, and for there to be immortality, one must understand one's life in that in which it is immortal.

9

*Only he can believe in a future life who has established in his consciousness that new relation to the world which does not fit within this life.*

Live by that part of your soul which is conscious of itself as immortal, which does not fear death. This part of the soul is love.
