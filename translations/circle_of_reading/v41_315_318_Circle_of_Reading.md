<!-- vol. 41, p. 315 -->

10 May

Only the spiritual truly exists. Everything bodily is only appearance.

1

No one can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will be zealous for the one and not care about the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.

*Matthew 6:24.*

These two masters are: the soul and the body.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 316 -->

2

> One cannot care at the same time both for one's soul and for worldly goods. If you want worldly goods, give up your soul; if you want to keep your soul safe, renounce worldly goods. Otherwise you will be constantly divided in two and will get neither the one nor the other.

> *Epictetus.*

3

> *If people think that only what they can touch with their hands exists, then such people are still very ignorant.*

> *Plato.*

4

> A person can live in two ways: by the true (inner) life, or by a false, illusory (outer) life. By the inner life I mean that life when a person no longer lives by impressions alone, but when through everything he sees one haven and shore—God—and in his name strives and hastens to put to use the talent given him, and not to bury it in the ground—knowing that life was not given him for his own pleasures.

> *Gogol.*

5

> The sense of duty makes us feel the reality of the material world and take part in its life, and at the same time tears us away from it and shows us its unreality.

> *After Amiel.*

6

Only that is real which is invisible, intangible, which is spiritual and which we come to know in ourselves and through ourselves. Everything visible, tangible, is a product of our senses and therefore only seeming.

7

> There are teachings of the body and teachings of the spirit. Beware of the first; they lead peoples to slavery. Whoever labors only for the body forges for himself shackles in which he will soon be chained. Woe to him who <!-- vol. 41, p. 317 --> lives a sensual life, forgetting the life of the spirit! be it a man or a whole people that has fallen so far that it has sunk wholly into the interests of the flesh and feeds on these interests, fattens itself—it is preparing a feast for worms. Only the teachings of the spirit give freedom, life, salvation; by them alone what was already dead is reborn again to life. Listen, then, to the voice of the spirit, you who want to be reborn, who want to come out of the grave of the old world, full of rot and bones. Where this voice comes from, no one knows, for it is not the voice of anything known. This voice you will not hear from pulpits, you will not hear in public assemblies, where people gather to listen to the empty words of an indifferent teaching. It is like a breath from the desert, of which no one can say: this breath arose here, or there. How far this voice will reach is unknown; today it is here, and tomorrow there, everywhere it finds attentive ears and prepared hearts. And it is unknown to what limits it will lead those who say to this voice: guide me.

> *Lamennais.*

8

> In essence there is only one subject of study: it is the various kinds and transformations of the spirit. All other subjects come down to this same one; all other studies come down to the same thing.

> *Amiel.*

9

> I can send my thoughts to different people; they will cross seas and take possession of all lands, if only there is in them the divine power of love and wisdom. My thoughts are the spiritual parts of my self and consequently can be in thousands of places at once, while my body at any given moment can be in only some one place.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

10

> Nature is unjust. If we are a product of nature, why then are we dissatisfied with the injustice of nature? Why does the effect rise up against the cause? Does this indignation come from empty and childish vanity? No, it is a cry, <!-- vol. 41, p. 318 --> torn from the depths of our being, which recognizes itself as independent of nature and demands justice at all costs! Heaven and earth may be destroyed, but good must exist, and injustice must be destroyed. Such is the consciousness of all humanity. Spirit does not depend on nature.

> *After Amiel.*

It always seems to us that the clearest, most comprehensible, most really existing thing is everything bodily, known through our senses, and yet it is precisely this that is the most unclear, incomprehensible, contradictory, and unreal.
