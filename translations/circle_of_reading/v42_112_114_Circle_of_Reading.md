<!-- vol. 42, p. 112 -->

3 October

Wealth never gives satisfaction: needs always grow with the increase of wealth in such a way that the more wealth there is, the less it suffices for the satisfaction of needs.

1

> It is difficult, if not impossible, to find a reasonable limit to our desires for property. In fact: the contentment of this or that person in this respect depends not on an absolute but on a certain purely relative quantity, namely on the relation between his demands and his possessions. Therefore possessions in themselves mean as little as a numerator without a <!-- vol. 42, p. 113 --> denominator. A person can be entirely content without having those things which it never even entered his head to demand and which are therefore superfluous to him; while another, who has a hundred times more, feels unhappy because he lacks what he has come to demand.

> *Schopenhauer.*

2

> Whoever has less than he wishes must know that he has more than he deserves.

> *Lichtenberg.*

3

> *Not he is poor who has little, but he who wants more.*

> *Seneca.*

4

> Nothing can be more “decent” than to have few needs and to satisfy them oneself, so as to be able to give, instead of using every occasion to grab.

> It is far more “decent” to satisfy one's needs oneself than to be served in luxury; this may seem indecent nowadays and to a small number, but it is the only “decency” always and for everyone.

> *Emerson.*

5

> Do not gather treasures for yourselves on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves dig in and steal; but gather treasures for yourselves in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys and where thieves do not dig in and do not steal; for where your treasure is, there your heart will be too.

> *Matthew 6:19–21.*

6

> Acquire for yourself that wealth which thieves cannot steal, on which the strong dare not encroach, which even after death will remain with <!-- vol. 42, p. 114 --> you, never diminishing and never decaying. This wealth is your soul.

> *Indian proverb.*

There are two means of not suffering from poverty: to increase one's wealth and to reduce one's needs. The first is not in our power, the second is always in our power.
