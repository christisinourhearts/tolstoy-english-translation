<!-- vol. 41, p. 73 -->

31 January

The highest degree of insolence is for some people to establish a religious law that is not subject to discussion by others and must be accepted by other people on faith.

What could people need this for?

1

> If something is true, then let us all believe it: the poor, the rich, men, women, and children. But if it is untrue, then let none of us believe it: neither the rich nor the poor, neither crowds of people, nor women, nor children. The truth must be proclaimed from the housetops.

> People constantly whisper that it is dangerous to reveal certain things to the people. They say: we know that this is untrue, but it is so useful for the people; much harm can be done by shaking their faith.

> But crooked ways remain crooked, even if they are meant to deceive great masses of the people rather than individual persons. And therefore we recognize only one motive: to follow the truth that we know, wherever it may lead us.

> *Clifford.*

2

The ignorance and superstition of the people are due in large part to the fact that there have always been, and still are, cruel people who, having become enlightened themselves, use their light not for what they ought to use it for—to help the people struggling out of the darkness of ignorance—but only to keep them fixed in it.

3

> A strange thing! In all ages scoundrels have tried to mask their vile deeds with devotion to the interests of religion, morality, and love of the fatherland.

> *Heine.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 74 -->

4

> Beware of the scribes, who like to walk about in long robes, and love greetings in the public assemblies, the chief seats in the synagogues, and the places of honor at feasts; who devour widows' houses and for a pretense make long prayers; they will receive the greater condemnation.

> *Luke 20:46–47.*

5

> But you are not to be called teachers, for you have one teacher, Christ, and you are all brothers; and call no one on earth your father, for you have one father, who is in heaven; and do not be called instructors, for you have one instructor, Christ.

> *Matthew 23:8–10.*

6

The whole teaching of Christ is in doing his commandments. Those who will enter the kingdom of heaven are not those who say “Lord! Lord!” but those who do the will of the father.

Christ taught people that no intermediaries are needed between God and man. He taught that all people are sons of God. What intermediaries, then, could be needed between a father and his son?
