<!-- vol. 41, p. 470 -->

5 July

*Only man himself can do evil. What is done apart from man's will is all good.*

1

> Solomon and Job knew best and spoke best of the nothingness of worldly life: the one was the happiest, the other—the unhappiest; the one experienced the vanity of pleasures, the other—the reality of calamities.

> *Pascal.*

2

> To every creature not only is everything useful that is sent to it by providence, but useful at the very time when it is sent.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

The life of man is a striving toward the good; what he strives toward is given to him—a life that cannot be death, and a good that cannot be evil.

4

> When will not the flesh but you be the head in man? When will you understand the blessedness of love for everyone, when will you free yourself, by the understanding of life, from sorrows and lusts, not needing for <!-- vol. 41, p. 471 --> your happiness that people should serve you with their life or their death; when will you understand that the true good is always in your power and does not depend on other people?

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

5

> Be convinced that you have no property other than your soul. Choose resolutely the very best way of life; habit will make it pleasant for you. Wealth is an unreliable anchor, and fame is worse still. The same with the body, the same with power, the same with honor: all this is worthless and powerless. Where, then, is a reliable anchor for life? Only in virtue. Such is the divine law, that only virtue holds firm and unshakable; all the rest is nothing.

> *Pythagoras of Samos.*

6

> You are already unhappy if you dread unhappiness, and he who deserves it dreads it forever.

> *Chinese proverb.*

7

> It is proper to man's true nature to acquire a living, inexhaustible spiritual wealth. But dependence on external material goods brings us to slavish subjection to people and to accidents.

> *Emerson.*

8

> Some say: go into yourself, and you will find peace. This is not yet the whole truth.

> Others, on the contrary, say: go out of yourself, try to forget yourself, and find happiness in amusements. And this is not right either, if only because by this path one cannot be rid, for example, of illnesses.

> Peace and happiness are not within us, not outside us; they are in God, who is both within and outside us.

> *Pascal.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 472 -->

9

> External obstacles do no harm to a person strong in spirit, for harm is all that disfigures and weakens—as happens with animals, which obstacles embitter; but to a person who meets them with the strength of spirit that is given him, every obstacle adds moral beauty and strength.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

10

Everything is from God, and therefore everything is good—evil is only a good that we do not see because of our shortsightedness.

All the external calamities that may befall a person who has understood that evil for him can be only in his own acts are nothing in comparison with the good of the tranquility and freedom that he experiences.
