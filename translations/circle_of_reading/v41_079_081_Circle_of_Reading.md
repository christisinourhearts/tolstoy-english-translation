<!-- vol. 41, p. 79 -->

3 February

Kindness is for the soul the same as health is for the body: it is unnoticeable when you possess it.

1.

People who are truly virtuous do not consider themselves virtuous; therefore they are virtuous. People who are not truly virtuous never forget about their virtue, and therefore <!-- vol. 41, p. 80 --> are not virtuous. Real virtue does not assert itself and does not make a show of itself. Virtue that is not real asserts itself and makes a show of itself.

True good-heartedness does not know itself and does not try to make a show of itself. Good-heartedness that is not true asserts itself and tries to make a show of itself. True justice manifests itself when it is needed, but does not try to make a show of itself. Justice that is not true always manifests itself and tries to make a show of itself.

True propriety manifests itself when it is needed and does not try to make a show of itself. Propriety that is not true manifests itself always and, when no one responds to it, compels by force the keeping of its rules.

When true virtue is lost, good-heartedness appears; when good-heartedness is lost, justice appears; and when justice is lost, propriety appears.

The rules of propriety are only semblances of truth and the beginning of all disorder.

*Laozi.*

2

> A man of true virtue tries to go by the straight path to the end. To go half the way and then grow weak—that is what one must fear.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

3

> Virtue in a person must have the property of a precious stone, which unchangingly keeps its natural beauty, whatever may befall it.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

4

Do good in secret and be sorry when people find out about it, and you will learn the joy of doing good. The consciousness of a good life, without people's approval of it, is the best reward of a good life.

5

> *A person increases his happiness in the measure in which he provides it to others.*

> *Bentham.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 81 -->

6

> The will of God is that we should live by the happiness and the life of one another.

> *John Ruskin.*

7

As the good of a plant is in light, and therefore, as a plant that nothing covers cannot ask and does not ask in which direction it should grow, and whether the light is good, and whether it should not wait for another, better light, but takes the one light there is in the world and reaches toward it—so too a person who has renounced the good of personality does not reason about whom he should love: whether those whom he loves now, or whether there is not some still better love than the one possible now, but gives himself to the love that is accessible to him and is before him.

8

There is no love other than the love that gives its soul for its friends. Love is love only when it is a sacrifice of self. Only when a person forgets himself and lives the life of the one he loves—only such love is true, and only in such love do we find the good, the reward of love. And only because there is such love in people, only by this does the world stand.

Nothing so adorns life, both one's own and other people's, as an established habit of being kind.
