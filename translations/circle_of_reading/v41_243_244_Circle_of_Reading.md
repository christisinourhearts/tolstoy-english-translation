<!-- vol. 41, p. 243 -->

13 April

The spiritual, divine source of our life we are conscious of, on the one hand, by reason, on the other hand—by love.

1

The quality of a wise man consists in three things: first—to do himself what he advises others to do, second—never to act against justice, and third—to bear patiently the weaknesses of the people around him.

2

> *Great thoughts come from the heart.*

> *Vauvenargues.*

3

> Our moral feelings are so interwoven with the mental powers that we cannot touch the one without touching the other as well. A great mind, in the absence of moral feeling, is a source of great calamities.

> *John Ruskin.*

4

Examine everything. Believe only what agrees with reason.

5

Reason and mind are two completely different qualities. There are many people with a great mind who are devoid of reason. Mind is the ability to understand and think out the everyday, worldly conditions; reason is the divine essence of the soul that reveals to it its relation to the world and to God. Reason is not only not the same thing as mind, but is opposed to it: reason frees a person from those temptations and deceptions that the mind lays upon a person. In this is the chief activity of reason; destroying temptations, reason frees the essence of the human soul—love—and gives it the possibility of manifesting itself.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 244 -->

6

> Usually a distinction is made between reason and conscience, by saying that good deeds are more important than strong thinking. But by such a separation of the inseparably united powers of the soul we cripple our nature. Take thought away from virtue, what will remain? Without the power of thought, what we call conscience degenerates into reveries, exaggeration, justification of evil. The cruelest deeds in the world have been committed in the name of conscience. People have hated and killed one another out of the duty of conscience.

> *Channing.*

A reasonable person cannot be evil. A good person is always reasonable. Increase goodness in yourself by the exercise of reason, and reason by exercise in love.
