<!-- vol. 41, p. 71 -->

30 January

Land cannot be an object of ownership.

1

> When Socrates was asked where he came from, he said that he was a citizen of the whole world; he considered himself an inhabitant and citizen of the whole universe.

> *Cicero.*

2

> If we suppose that all the habitable land can be the property of landowners and that they have a right to its surface, then all who are not landowners have no right to it. So that those who are not landowners can exist on the land only on condition of the landowners' consent. They receive a right to the place occupied by their feet only on condition of the landowners' consent. So that if the landowners did not wish to give them a place, they would have to be cast off the globe.

> *Herbert Spencer.*

3

> Property in land, like property in slaves, differs in its very essence from property in objects created by labor.

> Take from a person or a people their money, goods, or cattle, and your robbery will end with your departure. The passage of time will not, of course, make your crime a good deed, but it will wipe out its consequences. It quickly recedes into the distance of the past together with the people who took part in it.

> But take from a people their land, and your robbery will go on forever. It will be a new robbery for each new rank of succeeding generations, for each new year, for each new day.

> *Henry George.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 72 -->

4

> We occupy an island on which we live by the labor of our own hands. A shipwrecked sailor is cast up on our shore. What is his right? Can he say: I too am a man, I too have a natural right to cultivate the land; I may, on the same ground as you, occupy a bit of land in order to feed myself by my labor.

> *Laveleye.*

5

> The cause of the greatest calamities is the crude and monstrous assertion that land can be anyone's private property. This is as unjust and cruel as slavery.

> *Newman.*

6

> *If there is a person who has no right to land, then the right to land—mine, yours, every person's—is unlawful.*

> *Emerson.*

7

> The earth is our common mother; she feeds us, gives us shelter, gladdens us, and lovingly warms us; from the moment of birth until we rest in eternal sleep on her motherly breast, she constantly cherishes us in her tender embrace.

> And yet, in spite of this, people talk of selling her, and indeed in our venal age the land is put on the market to be appraised and for so-called sale. But the sale of land created by the heavenly creator is a savage absurdity. The land can belong only to almighty God and to all the sons of men who work on it, or who will work on it. It is the property not of any one generation, but of all generations, past, present, and future, who work on it.

> *Carlyle.*

No one can have a right of property in land.
