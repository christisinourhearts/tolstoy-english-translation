## CATHOLICISM AND CHRISTIANITY

Christianity as a historical fact must not be confused with the primary source from which it arose. Only thanks to unexampled dishonesty could holiness be ascribed to what is now called “the Catholic faith.” What did Christ deny? Precisely what is now called the Catholic Church.

The Catholic Church is the complete opposite of what served as the beginning of the Christian teaching. Precisely what is Christ's in the Catholic church sense is, at its root, not Christ's. Instead of symbols, there, in the church—objects and persons; instead of eternal events—history; instead of the practice of life—Catholic rules, rites, and dogmas. Christianity in its essence is indifferent to cult, priests, church, divine service.

The practice of Christianity has no phantasmagorias in it; it is a means of being happy:

“One must make no distinctions between strangers and one's own. One must not be angry; one must not humiliate anyone. Give alms in secret. One must not swear. One must not judge. One must make peace and forgive. One must pray in secret.”

Jesus turns straight to the heart of the matter, to the “kingdom of God” in the human heart, and the ways to it that he points out are not external ones, consisting in keeping the rules of the Jewish church, which he does not recognize, but internal ones. He thinks not of the external but of the internal.

He takes the same attitude toward all crude methods of communion with God: he teaches how one must live in order to feel oneself “deified,” teaches that one cannot come to this state through self-torment. To become divine, the main thing is to renounce oneself.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 581 --> Catholicism is something radically different from what Christ did and what he wanted. Christianity is a great anti-pagan movement; but this teaching, the life and word of Christ, were subjected to a completely arbitrary interpretation for the sake of aims completely alien to Christianity, and were then translated into the language of already existing religions.

Whereas Jesus taught peace and happiness, Catholicism turned out to be the expression of a gloomy attitude toward life, and moreover the attitude of the weak, the infirm, the oppressed, the suffering.

The Gospel proclaims that access to happiness is open to the humiliated and the poor; for this one need only free oneself from all the established tutelage of the higher classes. Property, acquisitions, homeland, estate and position, courts, police, state, church, education, art, army—all these are obstacles to attaining happiness—errors, delusions of the devil, which the Gospel threatens with the Last Judgment.

Catholicism made out of Christianity a teaching that in the end makes its peace with the state: it wages war, judges, tortures, swears oaths, and hates.

It needs to put the notion of guilt, of sin, in first place; it needs not a new life according to Christ's teaching, but a new cult, a new faith in a miraculous transformation (“redemption” through faith).

It, Catholicism, made the most arbitrary selection from the history of Christ's life and death, emphasized everything in it in its own way, shifting the centers of gravity everywhere—in a word, it destroyed original Christianity.

The struggle against the pagan and Jewish priests and church came down, thanks to Catholicism, to the creation of new priests and theology—to a new class of rulers—again to a church.

In this lies all the humor, the tragic humor: Catholicism restored, in general outline, everything that Christ had destroyed. In the end, when the Catholic Church had been formed anew, it even took the state under its protection.

Catholicism is precisely what Christ preached against and what he commanded his disciples to fight.

When the criminal, the robber on the cross, suffering a hard death, reasons: “It is right to suffer and die as <!-- vol. 41, p. 582 --> Jesus did, without murmuring or anger, with kindness and submission”—he affirms the Gospel and goes to paradise.

Christianity can be carried out at any given minute; it needs neither metaphysics, nor asceticism, nor the “natural sciences.” Christianity is life. It teaches how to act.

Whoever says: “I do not want to be a soldier,” “I have nothing to do with the courts,” “I have no need of the police,” “I do not want to do anything that might disturb my inner peace,” and “if I suffer for this, nothing will give me such peace as this suffering”—he will be a true Christian.

*Nietzsche.*
