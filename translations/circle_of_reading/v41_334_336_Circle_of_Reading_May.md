## THE LAW OF VIOLENCE AND THE LAW OF LOVE

A Christian must not do violence. It is said: “If anyone strikes you on your right cheek, turn to him the other also.” The meaning of these words is that if you have been struck, it is better to offer your cheek than to give blow for blow. Such is the law of God for a Christian. Whoever has done violence, and for whatever purpose it has been done, it is all the same—evil, like the evil of murder, of fornication—all the same, for whatever purpose it has been done, and whoever has done it, whether one person or millions of people—evil is still evil, because before God all people are equal and because the commandment of God is not like human commandments with their exceptions, notes, and evasions according to time and place. The commandment of God is one for all people, because the spirit living in us is one and the same in all. For a Christian, in the extreme case, it is better to be killed than to be a killer; better to be subjected to violence than to <!-- vol. 41, p. 335 --> do violence oneself. If people wrong me, then I, as a Christian, must reason thus: I too have wronged people, and therefore it is good that God sends me a trial for my correction and cleansing from sins. But if people wrong me when I am in the right, then it is doubly good for me, because through this I become a comrade of the foremost fighters for life, for light, for freedom. One cannot save one's soul by evil; one cannot come to good by the road of evil, just as one cannot come home by walking away from home. Satan does not cast out Satan: evil is not conquered by evil; evil is only laid upon evil and grows stronger. Evil is conquered only by the opposite spirit, by truthfulness and good. By good, only by good and patience and suffering, can and should evil be put out.

But people do not live by the Christian law, the law of understanding, humility, self-renunciation, forgiveness, and brotherly love, but live by the animal law, the law of beasts, according to which “whoever can overpower another, gnaws him.” One may allow that a person will use violence on someone sick with fever, on a drunk, on a madman, on a foolish child, not with the aim of doing him evil but with the aim of preventing misfortune. One may tolerate, forgive, and allow such violence as an inevitable evil, but not exalt it. But when the law of bestial life is raised into a public matter as a law for all, and exalted as a law supposedly divine, then for reasonable people, especially for Christians, this becomes an unnatural thing, a thing of the antichrist, blasphemy against the spirit of Christ—this is an unforgivable sin.

Christ and antichrist have lived from time immemorial as two opposing forces. To live according to Christ means to live humanly, to love people, to do good, and to repay evil with good. To live according to antichrist means to live like a beast, to love only oneself, and to repay both evil and good with evil. The more we try to live according to Christ in our ordinary, hourly life, the more love and happiness there will be among people. The more we hold to the teaching of antichrist, the more calamitous people's life will be. The commandment of non-resistance to evil clearly shows two different paths: the path of truth, the path of Christ, the path of sincerity of thought and feeling—the path of life; and the other path: the path of deception, the path of the devil, the path of every hypocrisy—the path of death. And let it be frightening to take upon oneself the cross of the commandment of non-resistance to evil, let it be frightening to give oneself up as a victim to the evildoer, still we know where the road of good is, the road of salvation.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 336 --> Let us, then, make the effort to walk along it, and let us light this path with the light of our understanding—knowing that we are not running up against a wall, but that ahead there is a road and light.

Not to resist evil by force does not mean that one must give up protecting both the life and the labors of oneself and of other people; it means only that all this must be protected in a different way, so that this protection is not contrary to reason. The life and labors of other people and one's own must be protected by trying to awaken a good feeling in the attacking evildoer. And for a person to be able to do this, he must himself be good and reasonable. If I see, for example, that one man intends to kill another, then the best thing I can do is to put myself in the place of the one being killed—and defend that man, cover him with myself, and, if possible, save him, drag him away, hide him—just as I would save a person from the flames of a fire or a drowning person: either perish myself or save him. If, however, I prove powerless in this way, because I myself am an erring sinner, then this powerlessness of mine does not give me the right to awaken the beast in myself and bring disorder into the world by the evil of violence and its justification.

*Buka.*
