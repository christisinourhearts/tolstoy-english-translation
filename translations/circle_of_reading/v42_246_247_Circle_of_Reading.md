<!-- vol. 42, p. 246 -->

11 November

Moral perfection is unattainable, but drawing near to it is the law of human life.

1

> There is no moral law if I cannot fulfill it. People say: we are born egoists, stingy, lustful, and cannot be otherwise.

> No, we can. The first thing is to feel with the heart what we ought to be. And this feeling will give us strength.

> *Salter.*

2

> You are free agents, and you feel it. All the possible sophisms of that pitiful philosophy which tries to set the fatalist teaching against the loud voice of human conscience, of human consciousness, have not had the power to silence two incorruptible witnesses of human freedom: the reproaches of conscience and the greatness of martyrdom. Beginning <!-- vol. 42, p. 247 --> with Socrates and on to Christ, and from Christ right down to those people who from age to age die for the truth, all the martyrs of faith protest against this slavish teaching and say to us loudly: “We too loved life and all the people who made our life beautiful and who begged us to stop the struggle. Every beat of our heart loudly called to us: live! but for the sake of fulfilling our duty we preferred death.” And beginning with Cain and right down to the most pitiful spy of our days, all traitors and betrayers who have chosen the path of evil hear in the depths of their soul the voice of condemnation, of reproach, a voice that gives them no rest, which eternally repeats to them: “Why did you turn off the true path? You are free agents, and therefore you are responsible for your actions.”

> *Mazzini.*

3

> If you ask: “What, then, is to be done?” then allow me to answer that, such as you are now, you are powerless to do anything. The task that faces you now consists in ceasing, as far as possible, to be an empty echo of your own and others' selfishness and frivolity, and in becoming a soul, if not a great one, then an honest one. You must look into yourself and make sure whether there is even a trace of a soul there. Until then nothing can be done. O brothers, we must, as far as possible, restore soul and conscience in ourselves, replace our frivolity with sincerity and our dead, stony hearts with living ones! Only then shall we begin to understand the endless series of good deeds that lies before us in its more or less clear sequence. Take the first step, and the second will be easier, clearer, more practicable.

> *Carlyle.*

4

A man dropped a precious pearl into the sea and, to get it, began to scoop up the water with a dipper. The spirit of the sea came out and asked: “Will you stop soon?” The man said: “When I have scooped out the sea and got the pearl.” The spirit of the sea brought him out the pearl.

*External consequences are not in our power, but effort is always possible, and beneficial inner consequences always accompany effort.*
