<!-- vol. 41, p. 571 -->

14 August

People have become so used to maintaining the external order of life by violence that a life of people without violence seems impossible to them. And yet, if people establish a just (in appearance) life by violence, then those people who establish such a life must know what justice consists in and must themselves be just. And if some people can know what justice consists in and can be just, then why should not all people know this and be just?

1

> Force is the instrument by which ignorance makes its followers do those deeds to which they are not inclined by their nature; and (like an attempt to make water rise above its own level) at the moment this instrument ceases to act, its effects cease as well.

> *Combe.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 572 --> Persuasion, on the other hand, is like an outlet made in a river, which need only be made for the river to flow of itself, without our attention and effort. There are only two means of directing human activity. One consists in making a person act contrary to his inclinations and judgments, and the other in taking hold of his inclinations, in persuading by reasoning. The one method is used by ignorance, and its consequences are disappointment; the other is confirmed by experience and is always crowned with success. When a child cries to be given a rattle, he wants to get it by force. When parents beat their children, it is in order to make them behave well by force. When a drunken husband beats his wife, he does it with a view to correcting her by force. When a criminal is punished, this is done in order to improve the world by force. When one person goes to law against another, it is in order to attain justice by means of force. When a priest speaks of the horrors of the torments of hell, he does it with the aim of directing his listeners to heaven by force. When one people makes war on another, its aim is to acquire a desirable position by force. And a surprising thing: up to now ignorance has led and is leading mankind along that very path of violence which has always led and is leading to disappointment.

2

> The right of the strong is not a right but a simple fact, which can be right only so long as it meets no protest and resistance. It is like cold, darkness, weight, which must be endured until one finds heating, lighting, a lever. All human industry is a liberation from under the power of crude nature; and the progress of justice is nothing other than a series of limitations to which the tyranny of the strong has been subjected. As medicine consists in victory over disease, so the good consists in victory over the blind brutality and unbridled lusts of the man-beast. Thus I always see one and the same law: the increasing liberation of the personality, the drawing of people nearer to the good, to justice, to wisdom.

> *Amiel.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 573 -->

3

> *Without God one can compel, but not persuade; one can become a tyrant, but not an educator.*

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

4

Violence, producing a semblance of justice, only removes people further from the possibility of living justly without violence.

People are reasonable beings and therefore can live guided by reason, and they must inevitably replace violence with free agreement. But every act of violence committed puts this time further off.
