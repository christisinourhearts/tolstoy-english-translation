<!-- vol. 42, p. 128 -->

7 October

It is possible not to name God, to avoid this word, but it is impossible not to acknowledge his existence. Nothing exists if he does not exist.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 129 -->

1

Everything that I know, I know because there is God, and I know him.

Only on this can one take a firm stand, both in one's relations to people, and to oneself, and to the life beyond the earth and beyond time. Not only do I not find this mystical, but I find that the opposite view is mysticism, and that this is the one reality most understandable and accessible to all. To the question: what is God? I answer thus: God is the infinite, the all, of which I am conscious of myself as a part.

God for me is that toward which I strive, that in striving toward which my life consists, and which for that reason exists for me; but it is without fail something that I cannot understand, cannot name. If I understood him, I would have reached him, and there would be nowhere to strive, and there would be no life.

I cannot understand him or name him, and at the same time I know him, I know the direction toward him, and of all my knowledge this is even the most certain. I am always afraid when I am without him; and only then am I not afraid, when I am with him.

2

> Religious acts that proceed from self-loving aims, such as prayers for rain or a sacrifice for a reward in the future world, are always self-interested, and only a deed done as a consequence of the knowledge of God is worthy of him.

> Recognizing the supreme reason in all beings and things, the true believer offers up his mind as a sacrifice, directing it toward the spirit of God, and draws near to the nature of him who shines with his own light.

> Let each one attentively regard nature, visible and invisible, as existing in the divine reason, because, contemplating the infinite world in the divine reason, he can no longer give himself up to evil thoughts.

> *The Laws of Manu.*

3

> When I speak to you of God, do not think that I am speaking to you of some object made of gold or silver. That God of whom I speak to you—you feel him in your <!-- vol. 42, p. 130 --> soul. You carry him in your very self, and with your impure thoughts and abominable deeds you defile his image in your soul. Before a golden idol, which you revere as a god, you take care not to do anything unseemly, but before the face of that God who is in you yourself and who sees and hears everything, you do not even blush when you give yourself up to your vile thoughts and deeds.

> If only we constantly remembered that God in us is the witness of all that we do and think, we would stop sinning, and God would abide in us without departing. Let us, then, remember God, think and talk of him as often as possible.

> *Epictetus.*

4

> *God is not an idol to which one must pray and which one must flatter; God is an ideal that a person must embody in his everyday life.*

> *Lucy Mallory.*

5

> Not when I go toward him, but precisely when I turn away from him, leave him—precisely then do I acknowledge that God exists. I say “God,” but I do not know for certain whether I am naming it rightly. You will understand what I mean.

> *Thoreau.*

6

One should never go to God on purpose: “Let me go to God, I'll start living in God's way. I've lived the devil's way, let me live a while in God's way, I'll try, maybe it's no trouble”... It is trouble, and big trouble. To God, as with getting married, one must go only when one would be glad not to go and glad not to marry, but I can't... And therefore it is not that I will say to everyone: go into temptations on purpose; but to the one who puts the question thus: “And what, won't I lose out if I go not to the devil but to God?” I will shout at the top of my voice: “Go, go to the devil, by all means to the devil!” It is a hundred times better to get a good burn on the devil than to stand at the crossroads or go hypocritically to God.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 131 -->

7

Even if a person does not know that he breathes air, he knows that he has been deprived of something when he is suffocating. It is the same with a person who has been deprived of God, even though he does not acknowledge him.

To remember God is a great thing. Not to make mention of him in words, but to live as though he were watching each of my acts, condemning or approving. Russian peasants say: or have you forgotten God?
