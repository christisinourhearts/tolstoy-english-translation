<!-- vol. 42, p. 341 -->

14 December

The human soul is of one nature with divinity.

1

> A person is joined to God to the degree that God already lives in him, and, as Angelus, a mystical poet of the 17th century, says, that eye of mine with which I see God is the very same eye with which God sees me.

> *Amiel.*

2

> *The soul of man is the lamp of God.*

> *The Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 342 -->

3

> Once the fish in a river heard that people say fish can live only in water. And, having heard this, the fish were very surprised and began asking one another whether anyone knew what water is. Then one clever fish said: “They say an old, wise, and learned fish lives in the sea; she knows everything; let us swim to her and ask her what water is.” And so the fish swam into the sea, to the place where the wise fish lived, and asked her what water is and how to come to know it. And the old wise fish said: “Water is that by which we live, in which we live. You do not know water just because you live in it and live by it.”

> So too people do not know God, although—they live by him and live in him.

> *Sufi.*

4

For one who rises in thought to the sky there will always be clear days: above the clouds the sun always shines.

5

> The spirit of God embraces our souls and penetrates them. We do not see it, because it is too close to us. And it is close to us not only so that we may know it, but so that it may act on us, influence us, impart its divinity to us. In this is the great fatherly gift of God.

> *Channing.*

6

If you desire something or fear something, this means that you do not believe in that God of love who is in you. If you believed in him, you could not desire anything, because all the desires of that God who lives in you are always fulfilled, and you would not fear anything, because to God nothing is frightening.

7

> The nature of the soul is so deep that however we may try to know it, we will never be able to define it.

> *Heraclitus*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 343 -->

8

> If we compare our powers with the powers of nature, we are insignificant playthings of fate. But if, instead of comparing ourselves with material creation, we have recognized in ourselves the soul of the creator, we are conscious of ourselves as incommensurable with the whole material world and of one kin with the universal spirit.

> *After Emerson.*

*Whatever befalls you, you cannot be unhappy while you are conscious of your unity with God.*
