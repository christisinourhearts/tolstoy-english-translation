<!-- vol. 41, p. 443 -->

28 June

The family bond is firm and gives people the good only when it is not only a family bond but also a religious one, when all the members of the family believe in one God and his law.

Without this the family is a source not of joy but of suffering.

1

Family egoism is crueler than personal egoism. A man who would be ashamed to sacrifice the goods of another for himself alone considers it his duty to make use of the misfortune, the need of people, for the good of the family.

2

The most usual and unjust justification of one's bad deeds is the good of the family.

Stinginess, bribe-taking, oppression of workers, dishonest trades—all this is justified by love for the family.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 444 -->

3

> The ties of family and fatherland cannot and must not confine the soul. From the day of his birth a man is surrounded by a small number of people so that the tenderness of these people may call forth in him a feeling of love for man. But when family and national attachments become exclusive and, because of this, exclude the universal demands of the human race, then, instead of being nurturers of the heart, they become its grave.

> *Channing.*

4

*Love for the family is a selfish feeling and therefore may be the cause, but not the justification, of unjust, unkind deeds.*

5

> And they let him know: your mother and your brothers are standing outside, wishing to see you. He said to them in answer: my mother and my brothers are those who hear the word of God and do it.

> *Luke 8:20–21.*

6

> Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me; and whoever loves son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me.

> *Matthew 10:37.*

7

“If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brothers, and sisters, and moreover his own life too, he cannot be my disciple” (Gospel of Luke 14:26). “Hate” in this verse does not mean that Christ rejected the family or taught hatred of it, but means only what is said in verse 24 of chapter 8 of Luke—that to Christ and to his disciples and imitators people are near and dear not through family ties but through their tie with God, and therefore with one another.

These verses are usually a temptation to people who have in view the state of a dissolute man and the morally higher <!-- vol. 41, p. 445 --> state of a family man, but do not have in view the state of a religious man, for whom the family state is not the highest state but, on the contrary, for the most part presents an obstacle to attaining this highest state.

8

> Some people seek the good in power, others in curiosity, in the sciences, still others in pleasures. These three kinds of lusts formed three different schools, and all philosophers followed one or another of the three. But those who came nearer than others to true philosophy understood that the universal good—the object of the striving of all people—must not consist in any one of the particular things that only some can possess and that, when divided, rather grieve their possessor by the absence of the part he lacks than give him enjoyment of the part that belongs to him. They understood that the true good must be such that all could possess it at once, without its diminishing and without envy, and that no one could lose it against his will.

> *Pascal.*

In love for the family there is, in the moral sense, nothing either good or bad, just as in love for one's own personality: the one and the other are natural phenomena. Love for the family, just like love for one's personality, when it goes beyond the bounds proper to it, may be a vice, but can never be a virtue.
