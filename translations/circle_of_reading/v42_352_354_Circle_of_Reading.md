17 December

That we are conscious of ourselves as beings separate from others, and of other beings as separate from ourselves and from one another, is a notion that flows from the conditions of life in time and space. The more this separateness is destroyed, the more we recognize our unity with all living beings, and the easier and more joyful our life becomes.

1

> But the body is not of one member, but of many. If the foot says: I do not belong to the body, because I am not a hand, does it for that reason not belong to the body?

> And if the ear says: I do not belong to the body, because I am not an eye, does it for that reason not belong to the body?

> If the whole body is an eye, then where is hearing? If the whole is hearing, then where is smell?

> The eye cannot say to the hand: I have no need of you; nor again the head to the feet: I do not need you.

> On the contrary, the members of the body that seem weakest are far more needed.

> Therefore, if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; if one member is honored, all the members rejoice with it.

> *1 Corinthians 12:14–17, 21, 22, 26.*

2

> A branch cut off from its bough has thereby been separated from the whole tree as well. A person in a quarrel with another person tears himself away from all mankind. But a branch is cut off by an outside hand, while a person estranges himself from his neighbor <!-- vol. 42, p. 353 --> by hatred and malice, not knowing, it is true, that by this he tears himself away from all mankind. But the divinity that called people, as brothers, to a common life endowed them with the freedom to be reconciled with one another again after a quarrel.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> God, having created heaven and earth, which do not feel the happiness of their existence, wished to create beings that would be conscious of this happiness and would make up a body of thinking members. All people are members of this body; in order to be happy, they must conform their will to the universal will that governs the whole body. And yet man often thinks that he is everything; not seeing the body on which he depends, he thinks that he depends only on himself, and he wants to make himself the center and the body. But in this position man is like a member separated from its body, which, having no source of life in itself, only wanders and marvels at the incomprehensibility of its being. When at last a man comes to the understanding of his purpose, he as it were returns to himself; he is conscious that he is not the whole body but only a member of the universal body, that to be a member means to have life only through the life and for the life of the whole body, that a member separated from its body has only a dying and perishing life, and that one must love oneself only for the sake of this body, or, to put it more truly, that one must love only this universal body, because in loving it one loves oneself, since life is only in it and through it.

> To determine the love that one must have for oneself, one must picture a body composed of thinking members, because we are members of the whole, and decide how each separate member ought to love itself.

> The body loves the hand, and the hand, if it had a will, ought to love itself as the body loves it. Every love greater than this is unlawful. If the hands and feet had their own particular will, they would be in order only if they submitted to the body; outside of this they are in disorder and misery; but in desiring the good of the body, they attain their own good.

> The members of our body do not feel the happiness of their union, of their wonderful concord; they do not feel how nature took care, <!-- vol. 42, p. 354 --> instilling in them the spirit of concord, to make them grow and exist. But if they, having received understanding, used it to keep in themselves the food they receive, without passing it on to the other members, they would be not only unjust but also unhappy; they would not love one another, but rather would hate one another: for their bliss, as well as their duty, lies in concord with the activity of the common soul to which they belong and which loves them more than they love themselves.

> *Pascal.*

*The consciousness of the unity of our being with all others shows itself in us as love. Love is the widening of one's life. The more we love, the wider, fuller, and more joyful our life becomes.*
