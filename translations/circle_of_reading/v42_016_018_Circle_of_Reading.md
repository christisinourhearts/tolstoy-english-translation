<!-- vol. 42, p. 16 -->

5 September

To punish—in Russian means to instruct. One can instruct only by example. But repaying evil with evil does not instruct; it corrupts.

1

> Then Peter came to him and said: Lord! how many times shall I forgive my brother who sins against me? Up to seven times? Jesus says to him: I do not say to you: up to seven, but up to seven times seventy times.

> *Matthew 18:21–22.*

2

If one admits the inadmissible, that a person has the right to punish, then who among people will take this right upon himself?

Only those people who have fallen so low that they do not remember and do not know their own sins.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 17 -->

3

> Then the scribes and Pharisees brought to Jesus a woman taken in adultery, and, having set her in the midst, said to him: Teacher! this woman has been taken in adultery; and Moses in the law commanded us to stone such women with stones. What do you say? They said this testing him, in order to find something to accuse him with. But Jesus, bending down low, wrote with his finger on the ground, paying no attention to them. And when they went on asking him, he straightened up and said to them: whichever of you is without sin, be the first to throw a stone at her. And again, bending down low, he wrote on the ground. But they, hearing this and being convicted by conscience, began to go out one after another, beginning with the eldest down to the last; and Jesus alone remained, and the woman standing in the midst. Jesus, straightening up and seeing no one but the woman, said to her: woman! where are your accusers? Has no one condemned you? She answered: no one, Lord! Jesus said to her: I too do not condemn you; go, and from now on do not sin.

> *John 8:3–11.*

4

*The greater part of people's calamities comes from the fact that sinful people have claimed for themselves the right of punishment. Vengeance is mine, and I shall repay.*

5

If it seems to you that someone is guilty toward you—forget it and forgive. And if you have not experienced this before, you will come to know a new joy—to forgive.

6

The real punishment for every bad deed is the one that takes place in the soul of the criminal himself and consists in the lessening of his capacity to enjoy the goods of life. Punishment from outside only irritates the criminal.

7

Punishment is always cruelly tormenting. If it were not cruelly tormenting, it would not be imposed. Imprisonment <!-- vol. 42, p. 18 --> for the people of our time is just as cruelly tormenting as flogging with the knout was a hundred years ago.

8

> The American Indians never submitted to any laws, to any authority, or to the shadow of any government. Their only guide is custom and that moral consciousness of good and evil which, like the sense of taste and of touch, forms in every person a part of his nature. Acts that violate what is considered proper among them are punished by contempt and exclusion from society; but in more important cases, such as robbery or murder, punishment is left to those persons who have suffered. However imperfect these methods of punishment may seem, crimes are very rare among them.

> If one asks what inclines a person more to evil: the absence of law, as among the wild Americans, or the excess of laws, as among civilized Europeans, then whoever has seen both these conditions of existence will surely answer that it is, of course, the excess, and that sheep are happier left to themselves than to the care of wolves.

> *Jefferson.*

The clearest proof of how often the word “science” is taken to mean not only the most trivial but also the most harmful teachings is the fact that there exists a science of punishment, that is, of performing the most crudely ignorant act, proper only to a person at the lowest stage of development—a child, a savage.
