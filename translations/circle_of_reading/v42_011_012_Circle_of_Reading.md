<!-- vol. 42, p. 11 -->

2 September

The closer people are to truth, the more tolerant they are of others' errors. And the reverse.

1

Only unbelieving people, that is, people who do not believe in the spiritual foundation of life and who take for faith the external practices they have adopted, can be intolerant. They are intolerant because they do not understand that true faith does not depend on a person's will. It is from this that it came about and comes about that, beginning with the Pharisees, who tormented Christ to death, and down to the present-day secular authorities, the most unbelieving people have always persecuted and persecute believers. From this too it came about and comes about that these persecutions have always not only not weakened but always strengthened the faith of believers.

2

> God establishes faith in a person's heart by means of conscience and reason. Faith cannot be established by force and threats; by force and threats what is established is not faith but terror. One should not condemn and reproach unbelievers and those in error; they are unhappy enough from their errors without that. One ought to reproach them only if this could bring them benefit, but this, on the contrary, only repels them more and thereby does them harm.

> *Pascal.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 12 -->

3

*There is an unquestionable rule that we must always remember: it is that if a good deed cannot be accomplished without departing from good, then either this deed is not good or the time for this deed has not yet come.*

4

> There is nothing more unworthy of a rational being than to lament that what our fathers considered truth has turned out to be a lie.

> Is it not better to seek new foundations of union, such as would replace the former ones.

> *Martineau.*

5

> Faith, like love, cannot be called forth by force. Therefore to introduce it or to try to establish it by state measures is a risky business, for, just as an attempt to compel love calls forth hatred, so an attempt to compel faith calls forth unbelief.

> *Schopenhauer.*

6

> The denial of religion is a natural consequence of the intolerance and love of power of the clergy.

> *Warburton.*

7

> Unbelieving people can be just as intolerant as crudely believing ones.

> *Duclos.*

True faith does not need the external support either of violence or of solemn splendor. Nor does it need concern for its spreading. (God has much time. Thousands of years are as one year.) Whoever wants to support his faith by violence or by external solemn practices, whoever wants to spread it as soon as possible—he himself believes little or not at all.
