<!-- vol. 41, p. 234 -->

8 April

People think that if they call the crime of murder “war,” then murder will cease to be murder, a crime.

1

> One can deny Christ in various ways: first, one can crudely blaspheme, mock what is majestic—but such a way is not dangerous. Religion is too dear to people for anyone's mockery to be able to take it from them. But there is another way: this is to call Christ Lord and not to fulfill his commandments; to stifle free thought with his words and to cover, to sanctify with his name all the follies, errors, and sins of people. This second way is especially dangerous.

> *Theodore Parker.*

2

> It is not true that war against the foreigner is sacred; it is not true that the earth thirsts for blood. The earth asks of heaven the water of its rivers and the pure dew of heaven's clouds, and not blood. War is cursed by God and even by those very people who take part in it.

> *Alfred de Vigny.*

3

> Only your iniquities have been a dividing wall between you and your God, and your sins have hidden the face of God from you, so that he does not hear: for your hands are defiled with blood and your fingers with iniquity; your lips speak lies, your tongue utters falsehood. No one lifts up his head for right, and no one stands up for truth; they trust in what is empty and speak lies, they conceive trouble and give birth to iniquity. Their deeds are sinful deeds, and their hands do violence; their feet run to evil, and they hasten to shed innocent blood; their thoughts are sinful thoughts; desolation and ruin are on their way; they do not know the way of peace, and there is no justice in their paths; they themselves have made their ways crooked; no one who walks in them knows peace. That is why justice is far from us, and right does not reach us; we wait for light, but behold, darkness; we wait for radiance, but <!-- vol. 41, p. 235 --> we walk in gloom; we feel along the wall like the blind, and walk groping like the eyeless; at noon we stumble as at twilight; among the living we are like dead men.

> *Isaiah 59:2–10.*

4

> An astonishing and terrible thing is being done in this land: the prophets prophesy lies, and the priests rule by their means, and my people love it. What then will you do after all this?

> *Jeremiah 5:30–31.*

5

> *And because iniquity is multiplied, the love of many will grow cold.*

> *Matthew 24:12.*

6

> Now is your time and the power of darkness.

> *Luke 22:53.*

7

> War is a curtain behind which people and nations give themselves up to such sins as the world would not otherwise tolerate.

> *Springfield.*

8

> They will forge their swords into plows and their spears into sickles; nation will not raise the sword against nation, and they will no longer learn to make war, but each will sit under his own vine and under his own fig tree, and no one will frighten them. For the mouth of the Lord Sabaoth has spoken this.

> *Micah 4:3–4.*

The crime of murder is always a crime, whoever may permit it and whatever its justification may be. And therefore murderers, who commit murders or prepare for them, are criminals, toward whom what is needed is not respect, approval, and admiration, but pity, correction, and admonition.
