<!-- vol. 41, p. 149 -->

5 March

As a person cannot lift himself up, so neither can a person extol himself. On the contrary, every attempt of a person to praise himself lowers him in the eyes of people.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 150 -->

1

Do not praise and do not scold yourself before people. If you praise yourself, people will not believe it; if you disparage yourself, they will think still worse of you than you said. And therefore the best thing is to say nothing about yourself.

2

> He is not humble who says of himself that he is humble. He who says, I know nothing—he is wise; he who says: I am learned—he is a chatterer. He who is silent—he is wiser and better than all.

> *Vamana Purana.*

3

The Persian Saadi tells how once, sitting by his father, he read the holy book, the Koran, all night without closing his eyes, while all the household were sound asleep. In the middle of the night, Saadi tells, I tore myself away from the book and said to my father: “No one will say a prayer or listen to the holy book, but all are asleep like the dead.” — “You too would do better to sleep,” said my father, “than to condemn people.”

4

> He who boasts sees nothing except himself. It would be better for him to be blind than to see only himself.

> *Saadi.*

5

> *If you want to be spoken well of, do not speak good of yourself.*

> *Pascal.*

6

Thought and its expression—the word—are a serious matter. It is not good to play with thoughts and words to justify one's actions.

7

He who listens to what people say about him will never be at peace.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 151 -->

8

A flatterer flatters only because he has no high opinion either of himself or of others.

*La Bruyère.*

If you want a good name, or at least not a bad name, not only do not praise yourself, but do not allow others to praise you either.
