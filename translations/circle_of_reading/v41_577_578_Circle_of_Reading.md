<!-- vol. 41, p. 577 -->

17 August

What a necessary seasoning to everything kindness is! The very best qualities are worth nothing without kindness, and the very worst vices are easily forgiven with it.

1

There is a natural kindness, which depends on external and bodily causes—on heredity, on good digestion, on success. This kindness is very pleasant both for the one who experiences it and for other people. And there is a kindness that comes from spiritual, inner work. Such kindness is less attractive, but on the other hand the first may not only disappear but turn into malice, while the second not only never disappears but constantly increases.

2

If, while doing a work that you consider good, you feel hostility or make others feel hostility toward you, stop at once: it means you do not yet know how to do the work you have taken on. If, while doing a work, you are in physical pain, you stop and learn to do the work so that it does not hurt you. It is the same with any work if it arouses unkind feelings. This is a sure indication that you do not yet know how to do the work you have taken on: you must learn.

3

> We must value even the mere appearance of good in other people, because out of this play of pretense, by which they win respect for themselves—maybe undeserved—something serious may perhaps arise in the end.

> Only the appearance of good in *ourselves* must we mercilessly wipe away—tear off the veil with which self-love covers our faults.

> *Kant.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 578 -->

4

Good that has been done gladdens but does not satisfy. It always seems that one should have done more.

5

> However much good you do, there will always remain the wish to do still more.

> *Confucius.*

6

> There is no direct inclination toward actions that are bad in the moral sense, but there undoubtedly is such a direct inclination toward good actions.

> *Kant.*

7

In order to find joy in serving people and living beings in general, one must first of all train oneself not to do evil to people and living beings, not to build one's life on the sufferings and the lives of others.

*Kindness is the basic property of the soul. If a person is not kind, it is only because he has been subjected to some deception, temptation, or passion, which has violated this natural property of his.*
