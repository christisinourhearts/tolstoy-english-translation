<!-- vol. 41, p. 503 -->

15 July

My bodily life is subject to suffering and death, and no efforts of mine can deliver me either from suffering or from death. But my spiritual life is subject neither to suffering nor to death. And therefore my salvation from suffering and death lies in one thing only: in transferring my consciousness into my spiritual “I.”

1

There are two ways of knowing the external world.

One—the crudest and the inevitable one—is the way of knowing by the five senses. If this way of knowing were the only one, then out of this way of knowing there would not have formed in us the world that we know, but there would be a meaningless chaos.

The other way consists in this: having come to know oneself by love for oneself, to come to know by that same love other beings: people, animals, plants, stones, heavenly bodies, and again, by means of love, the relations of beings with one another, and out of these relations to form the whole world as we know it.

This way of knowing is the restoration of the union between beings that was broken by the first way of knowing. This way of knowing is based on love, that is, on the merging of oneself with all other beings, with God.

3

> Only one thing is needed: to be conscious of God. All feelings, all powers of the soul and mind, all outward means of understanding are only openings onto the divinity, are only ways of adoring God. One must <!-- vol. 41, p. 504 --> be able to tear oneself away from everything that can be lost and attach oneself exclusively to the eternal and fundamental, and to enjoy all the rest as something lent for a time. To adore, to understand, to receive, to feel, to give, to act—this is your law, your duty, your happiness, your heaven—come what may, even death. Establish inner harmony, live before God, in communion with him, and leave it to the eternal powers, against which you cannot struggle, to guide your life. If death does not yet take you, so much the better. If it carries you off, again so much the better. If it half kills you, still so much the better: it closes to you the field of success in order to open to you the field of heroic deed, of self-renunciation, of moral greatness. Every life has its greatness, *and since it is impossible for you to go out of God, it is better to choose him consciously as your dwelling place.*

> *Amiel.*

4

> How many moral torments—and all this in order to die in a few minutes! What is there to take an interest in, and what for?

> But time is nothing, and your life is full, and this day is worth hundreds of years, if in the course of this day you find God.

> *Amiel.*

5

> The center of life is not in thought, not in feeling, not in will, not even in consciousness insofar as it thinks, feels, and wills, because a moral truth may be assimilated by all these means and still escape us. *Deeper than our consciousness lies our being:* our essence, our true foundation. Only those truths that enter this region, having become ourselves, unexpectedly and involuntarily, instinctively and unconsciously, only those make up our real life, that is, our true “I.” As long as we discern any space between the truth and ourselves, we are outside it. Thought, feeling, desire, consciousness of life—all this is not yet life. In essence, we can find peace and rest only in life, and in eternal life. And eternal life is divine life, it is God. To be a divine being—this, then, is the aim of life; only then can the truth not be <!-- vol. 41, p. 505 --> lost by us, because it is no longer outside us, nor even in us, but we make up the truth, and the truth makes up us; we are then the truth, the will, and the work of God. Freedom then becomes our nature; the creature makes one with the creator, is united with him by love, becomes what it ought to be. Its education is finished, and its final blessedness begins. The sun of time sets, and the light of eternal blessedness appears.

> *Amiel.*

6

> The essence of love for God consists in the striving of the soul and its attraction toward the creator, in order to merge with his highest light.

> *The Talmud.*

7

> If you want to attain knowledge of the all-embracing “I,” you must first of all come to know yourself. In order to know yourself, you must sacrifice your “I” to the universal “I.” Sacrifice your life if you want to live in the spirit. Withdraw your thoughts from outward things and from everything that presents itself from outside. Try to remove from yourself the images that arise, so that they do not cast a dark shadow on your soul.

> Your shadows live and vanish. That which is eternal in you, that which understands, belongs to the life that does not pass away. This eternal is the being that was, is, and will be, and whose hour will never strike.

> *Brahmanic wisdom.*

What we call the happiness and unhappiness of our animal “I” is outside our will, depends on a higher will; but the good or the evil of our spiritual “I” depends on us, on our obedience or disobedience to this higher will.
