<!-- vol. 42, p. 22 -->

8 September

All the greatest possibilities are in children.

1

> And Jesus said: truly I tell you, if you do not turn and become like children, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven. So whoever makes himself small, like this child, he is the greater in the kingdom of heaven. But whoever tempts one of these little ones who believe in me, it would be better for him if a millstone were hung around his neck and he were drowned in the depth of the sea.

> *Matthew 18:3–4, 6.*

2

> I glorify you, father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you have hidden this from the wise and the reasonable and revealed it to infants! Yes, father! For such was your good pleasure.

> *Matthew 11:25–26.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 23 -->

3

> *Why are children morally higher than most people? Because their reason is not perverted either by superstitions, or by temptations, or by sins. On the path to perfection nothing stands in their way. Whereas in the way of adults stand sins, temptations, and superstitions.*

> *Children need only to live, adults need to struggle.*

4

> How terrible the world would be if children were not constantly being born, bringing with them innocence and the possibility of every perfection!

> *John Ruskin.*

5

> Blessed is childhood, which amid the cruelty of the earth gives at least a little of heaven. These eighty thousand daily births of which statistics speak are, as it were, outpourings of innocence and freshness, which struggles not only against the destruction of the race but also against human corruption and the universal infection by sin. All the good feelings called forth around the cradle and childhood constitute one of the mysteries of great providence; destroy this refreshing dew, and the whirlwind of egoistic passions will dry up human society as if with fire.

> If we suppose that mankind consisted of a billion immortal beings, whose number could neither increase nor decrease, where would we be and what would we be, great God! We would become, without doubt, a thousand times more learned, but also a thousand times worse. Knowledge would accumulate, but all the virtues that are born of suffering and devotion, that is, the family and society, would be dead. There would be no compensation.

> Blessed is childhood for the good that it itself gives, and for the good that it produces without knowing or wishing it, only by making itself loved, letting itself be loved. Only thanks to it do we see on earth a particle of paradise. Blessed too is death. Angels can have no need either of birth or of death in order to live; but for people both the one and the other are necessary, inevitable.

> *Amiel.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 24 -->

6

> Lord our God!.. Out of the mouth of infants and of children at the breast you have ordained praise, because of your enemies, so as to make silent the enemy and the avenger.

> *Psalm 8:2.*

7

> Childhood often holds in its weak fingers a truth that grown people cannot hold in their manly hands and the discovery of which constitutes the pride of later years.

> *John Ruskin.*

8

A child guards his soul as the eyelid guards the eye, and without the key of love he lets no one into it.

9

Children know truth the way people often know a foreign language, though they cannot speak it. They will not be able to tell you what good consists in, but they will unerringly turn away from everything that is not good. Pretense in anything whatever can deceive the most intelligent, perceptive person, but the most limited child, however skillfully it is concealed, will recognize it and be repelled.

10

Can there be anything more perverse than to begin at once talking about another world to those who have only just entered this world?

Kant.

11

What time can be better than childhood, when the two best virtues—innocent gaiety and the need of love—are the only motives in life.

Respect every person, but respect a child a hundred times more, and take care not to violate the virginal purity of his soul.
