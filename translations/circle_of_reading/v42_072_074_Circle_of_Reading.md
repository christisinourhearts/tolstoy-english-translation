<!-- vol. 42, p. 72 -->

23 September

*However great people's knowledge may seem in comparison with former ignorance, it is always only an infinitely small part of all possible knowledge.*

1

> Socrates did not have the weakness, so common among learned men, of discoursing about everything that exists, seeking out the origin of what the sophists call nature, and going back to the fundamental causes from which the heavenly bodies came. “Can it be,” he said, “that people occupy themselves so much with what concerns <!-- vol. 42, p. 73 --> man so little because they think they have grasped everything that it is important for man to know; or do they think that one may neglect those things that are subject to our study and plunge into those mysteries that are not subject to it?”

> He marveled especially at the blindness of those false learned men who do not understand that the human mind cannot penetrate these mysteries. “That is why,” he said, “all these people who imagine that they dare to discourse about them are far from agreeing in their fundamental opinions, and when you listen to them all together, it seems that you are among madmen. And indeed, what are the distinguishing marks of the unfortunate who are possessed by madness? They fear what has nothing frightening in it, and do not fear what is really dangerous.”

> *Xenophon.*

2

> It is strange to think that the sciences can ever be hostile to religion. The sciences, if they are vain, become hostile not only to religion but also to truth; true science, on the other hand, is not only not hostile to religion but always assists it.

> *John Ruskin.*

3

> It would be better not to be born for him who hopes to lift the veil from what is above and below us, what was before and what will be after.

> *The Talmud.*

4

> Knowledge is infinite, and the man who is most learned in people's opinion is as far from true knowledge as the illiterate peasant.

> *John Ruskin.*

5

> We cannot conceive our ignorance otherwise than with the help of science, just as a blind man cannot conceive darkness until he recovers his sight.

> *Kant.*

It is better to know less than one can than to know more than one needs. Do not fear ignorance; fear superfluous, burdensome knowledge, acquired only for vanity.
