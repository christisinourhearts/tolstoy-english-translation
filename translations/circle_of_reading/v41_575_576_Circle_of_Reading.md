<!-- vol. 41, p. 575 -->

16 August

We are inseparably joined by a spiritual bond not only with all people but also with all living beings.

1

> Someone once told me that in every person there lurks something very good and humane, and also something very bad and malevolent; and depending on how he is disposed, now the one, now the other shows itself. This is perfectly true!

> The sight of another's sufferings arouses, not only in different people but in one and the same person, at one time boundless compassion and at another a certain satisfaction, which can grow into the cruelest gloating.

> I notice in myself too that I look at all beings sometimes with heartfelt compassion, sometimes with the greatest indifference, and at times with hatred and even gloating.

> All this serves as a clear indication that we have two different and even directly opposite modes of cognition: one by the principle of separateness, disunity, estrangement, when all beings seem to us completely alien, when they are decidedly not I: we can then feel nothing toward them except indifference, envy, hatred, gloating.

> And the other mode of cognition, which I would like to call cognition by the consciousness of our unity with all. In this mode of cognition all beings seem to us identical with our *I*, and therefore the sight of them arouses compassion and love in us.

> *Schopenhauer.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 576 --> The one type separates us from one another by an impenetrable wall, the other removes the wall, and we merge into one. The one mode teaches us to feel about every being that it is I, and the other, that it is not I.

2

> All people are of one origin, are subject to one law, and are all destined for one goal.

> And therefore we must have one faith, one goal of our actions, one banner under which we must all fight.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

3

> *We must always try to seek out not what separates us from other people, but what we have in common with them.*

> *John Ruskin.*

4

> Even if you wished it, you cannot separate your life from mankind. You live in it, by it, and for it. Your soul cannot free itself from the conditions in which it moves, because we are all created for working together, like feet, hands, eyes. To act against one another, to be angry with one another, and to turn away is to act contrary to nature.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

One cannot say that monkeys, dogs, horses, birds are not our brothers. If we say that they are strangers to us, then why not say that African Negroes too are not our brothers? And if Negroes are not our brothers, then people of a color other than ours are not our brothers either. Who, then, is the neighbor? To this there is only one answer, that of the parable of the Samaritan: do not ask who the neighbor is, but be conscious of your unity with all that lives, have compassion for and serve all that lives.
