<!-- vol. 41, p. 421 -->

21 June

*The sufferings of an unreasonable life lead to the consciousness of the necessity of a reasonable life.*

1

I, like the robber, knew that I had lived and was living badly, saw that most people around me lived the same way. I, just like the robber, knew that I was unhappy and suffering and that the people around me were likewise unhappy and suffering, and I saw no way out of this situation except death. I, just like the robber to the cross, was nailed by some force to this life of suffering and evil. And as the terrible darkness of death awaited the robber after the senseless sufferings and evil of life, so the same awaited me.

In all this I was entirely like the robber, but the difference between me and the robber was that he was already dying, while I was still living. The robber could believe that his salvation would be there, beyond the grave, but I could not believe this, because besides the life beyond the grave there still lay before me life here as well. And I did not understand <!-- vol. 41, p. 422 --> this life. It seemed terrible to me. And suddenly I heard the words of Christ, understood them, and life and death ceased to seem evil to me, and instead of despair I experienced the joy and happiness of life, not to be broken by death.

2

> Most people are prodigal sons and daughters who squander their vital strength and means on what has no value at all. They go farther and farther from the “father's house,” feeding, like the prodigal son, on pods; at last spiritual poverty forces them to return to the “father's house,” and then they have to learn, like infants, from the very beginning, the way of true life.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

3

> We come to know wisdom by three ways: by *reflection*—the noblest way; by *imitation*—this is the easiest; and the third—*by experience*—this is the hardest.

> *Confucius.*

4

In every suffering that befalls you, think not so much of how you can rid yourself of the suffering as of what, what efforts toward moral perfecting, it requires of you.

All the calamities both of all mankind and of individual people are not useless, and they lead mankind and people, though by a roundabout way, always toward the same one goal that is set for people: the manifestation of God by each person in himself and in all mankind.
