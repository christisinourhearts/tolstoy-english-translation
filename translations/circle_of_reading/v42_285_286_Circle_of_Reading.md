<!-- vol. 42, p. 285 -->

25 November

Most people now understand not only the uselessness of war for them, but also its madness and cruelty, but they cannot rid themselves of it because they seek deliverance in general government decisions, and not in their own actions, those of individual people.

1

> One cannot fail to recognize that the 19th century is striving to step onto a new path. The people of this century are beginning to understand that there must exist *laws and courts for peoples too*, and that crimes of a people against a people, though committed on a great scale, are no less hateful than crimes of man against man.

> *Quetelet.*

2

> When you study the various human activities not superficially but thoroughly, you cannot refrain from the following sad reflection: how many lives are spent for the continuation on earth of the kingdom of evil, and how this evil is furthered most of all by the institution of standing armies.

> Astonishment and the feeling of sadness increase still more at the thought that all this is unnecessary, that this evil, accepted so complacently by the enormous majority of people, comes only from their stupidity, only from the fact that they allow a relatively small number of people, skillful and depraved, to exploit them.

> *Patrice Larroque.*

3

> The inhabitants of the earthly planet are still in such a state of absurdity, unreason, and dullness that every day you read in the journals of civilized countries discussions of the diplomatic relations of heads of state, having as their aim alliances against a supposed enemy, the preparation of wars, in which the peoples allow their leaders to dispose of them like cattle led to the slaughterhouse, as if not suspecting that the life of each man is his personal property.

> *Flammarion.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 286 --> The inhabitants of this strange planet are all brought up in the conviction that there are peoples, borders, banners, and all have such a weak consciousness of humanity that this feeling disappears completely before the idea of the fatherland. It is true that if thinking people could manage to agree, this situation would change, since personally no one wants war. But there are such political entanglements, in consequence of which millions of parasites still exist, and it is these parasites who need war, and it is they who prevent people from agreeing.

4

*Bears are killed by hanging a heavy log on a rope over a trough of honey. The bear pushes the log away in order to eat the honey. The log swings back and strikes him; the bear gets angry and pushes the log harder; it strikes him harder. And this goes on until the log kills the bear. Can it be that people cannot be more reasonable than bears?*

5

War is murder. And however many people gather together to commit murder, and whatever they call themselves, murder is still the worst sin in the world.

As long as the power of government and its right to rule the people, to impose taxes, to establish courts, to punish are recognized, war will never cease. War is a consequence of the power of government.
