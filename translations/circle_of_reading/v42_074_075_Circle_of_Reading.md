<!-- vol. 42, p. 74 -->

24 September

It would be excusable not to give up meat-eating if it were necessary and were justified by any considerations whatever. But this is not so. It is simply a bad deed, which in our time has no justification of any kind.

1

> *What struggle for existence or what irresistible madness has driven you to stain your hands with blood in order to feed on the flesh of animals? Why do you, who enjoy everything necessary and all the comforts of existence, do this? Why do you slander the earth, as if it were unable to feed you without the flesh of animals?*

> *Plutarch.*

2

> If we were not so blindly subject to the custom that has enslaved us, then no one among people of any sensitivity at all could reconcile himself to the thought that for our nourishment such a multitude of animals has to be killed every day, despite the fact that the beneficent earth endows us with the most varied vegetable treasures.

> *Bernard de Mandeville.*

3

> You ask me on what grounds Pythagoras abstained from eating the flesh of animals? I, for my part, do not understand what sort of feeling, thought, or reason guided the man who first resolved to defile his mouth with blood and allowed his lips to touch the flesh of a slain creature. I marvel at him who admitted onto his <!-- vol. 42, p. 75 --> table the distorted forms of dead bodies and demanded for his daily food what had so recently been beings endowed with movement, understanding, and voice.

> *Plutarch.*

4

> An excuse for those pitiful beings who first resorted to meat-eating may be the complete absence and lack of means of life, since they (primitive peoples) acquired bloodthirsty habits not from indulging their whims and not in order to give themselves up to abnormal sensuality amid an abundance of everything necessary, but from need. But what justification can there be for us in our time?

> *After Plutarch.*

5

> As one of the proofs that meat food is not natural to man, one can point to the indifference of children to it and to the preference they always show for vegetables, milk dishes, baked things, fruit, and the like.

> *Rousseau.*

6

> A sheep is far less destined for man than man is for the tiger, since the tiger is a carnivorous animal, while man is not created such.

> *Ritson.*

There is a great difference between a man who has no other food than meat, or one who has heard nothing of the sin of meat-eating and naively believes in the Bible, which permits the eating of animals, and any literate man of our time, living in a country where there are vegetables and milk, who knows all that has been said by the teachers of humanity against meat-eating. Such a man commits a great sin in continuing to do what he can no longer help recognizing as bad.
