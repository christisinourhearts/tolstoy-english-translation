<!-- vol. 41, p. 584 -->

21 August

Fruitful prayer is the restoring in your consciousness of that highest understanding of the meaning of your life which you reached in your very best moments.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 585 -->

1

> Prayer, understood as an inner formal worship and therefore as a means of winning favor for oneself, is a superstitious delusion. For it is nothing but a declared wish addressed to a being that needs no declaration. By such a prayer we in essence do nothing and fulfill none of those duties which are laid upon us as God's commandments, and consequently we do not really serve God.

> The heartfelt wish to be pleasing to God in all our conduct—in other words, that all our actions be accompanied by the disposition to perform them as if, in performing them, we were in God's service—this is what the spirit of prayer consists in, which can and must abide in us unfailingly. But to clothe this wish in words and formulas (even if only inwardly) can have—at the most—only the value of a means of enlivening such a disposition in ourselves.

> *Kant.*

2

> So if you bring your gift to the altar and there remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there before the altar, and go first and make peace with your brother, and then come and bring your gift.

> *Matthew 5:23–24.*

3

Sometimes one wants, like a child, to complain to someone (God), to ask for help. Is this feeling good? It is not good—it is weakness, unbelief. What most resembles faith, petitionary prayer, is precisely unbelief—unbelief in the fact that there is no evil, that there is nothing to ask for, that if things are bad for you, this only shows you that you need to set yourself right, that what is happening is the very thing that must be, and that meanwhile you must do what you must.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 586 -->

4

> And when you pray, do not say anything superfluous, like the pagans; for they think that in their many words they will be heard; do not be like them; for your father knows what you need before you ask him.

> *Matthew 6:7–8.*

5

> *One hour of honest, serious thought is worth more than weeks of rapturous adoration, when the adoration does not show itself in deeds.*

> *Garrison.*

6

Devotion to the will of God—the necessary condition of the Christian life—excludes the possibility of definite desire, and therefore of petition, of prayer that this or that should happen.

Pray every hour. The most necessary and the most difficult prayer is remembering, amid the movement of life, your duties to God and his law. You took fright, got angry, got confused, got carried away—remember who you are and what you must do. In this is prayer. It is hard at first, but this habit can be developed.
