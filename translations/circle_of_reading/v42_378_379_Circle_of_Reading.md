<!-- vol. 42, p. 378 -->

25 December

Mercy, in order to be true, must be completely independent of people's approval and of a supposed reward in the life beyond the grave.

1

> See that you do not do your alms before people so that they may see you: otherwise you will have no reward from your heavenly father. So, when you do alms, do not trumpet before yourself, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and in the streets, so that people may glorify them. Truly I say to you: they are already receiving their reward.

> But with you, when you do alms, let your left hand not know what the right is doing.

> So that your alms may be in secret, and your father, who sees what is secret, will repay you openly.

> *Matthew 6:1–4.*

2

The poor widow's mite is not only equal in value to the richest gifts, but only this mite is real mercy.

Only the poor, the laboring, can have the happiness of mercy. The rich, the idle, are deprived of it.

3

Charitable institutions can be useless or harmful, they can be useful (which happens very rarely), but they can never <!-- vol. 42, p. 379 --> be moral. Such institutions only point most clearly to the complete disappearance, in the people who set them up, not only of the feeling but even of the concept of compassion and of the mercy that flows from it.

4

*Mercy begins at home. If in order to show mercy you need to go somewhere, then what you want to show is hardly mercy.*

5

Help that rich people give openly to the poor is at best a matter of courtesy, but by no means of mercy. A person asks you how to get to such and such a place? Out of courtesy one must stop and tell him. Another asks you to give him 5 kopecks, 5 or 50 rubles. If you have a spare 5 kopecks, rubles, or tens of rubles, you must give them to him; this too will be a matter of courtesy, but there will be nothing in this act in common with mercy.

Material mercy is good only when it is a sacrifice. Only then does the one who receives a material gift receive a spiritual gift as well.

But if it is not a sacrifice but a surplus, then it only irritates the one who receives it.
