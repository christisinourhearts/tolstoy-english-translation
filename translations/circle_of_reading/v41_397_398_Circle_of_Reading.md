<!-- vol. 41, p. 397 -->

13 June

Reason is the property of man that distinguishes him from the animals.

1

The Buddha said: in reflection, in life, in conversation, in study I never forget the main thing: the demands of reason.

2

The reasonable and the moral always coincide.

3

> If a self-confident person associates with a wise person all his life, he will come to know the truth as little as a spoon knows the taste of what it carries into the mouth.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

4

> The fact that we recognize a dignity in the very title “man” obliges us to respect in a man also the way he uses his reason. A man must not reproach another for his incongruities, must not call them stupidities, must not say that they are absurd, but, on the contrary, must suppose that at their basis there must be something reasonable, and try to find it. To this are joined still further duties: to uncover the false notions that deceived him and, by thus explaining the cause of his error, to sustain his trust in his own reason. And indeed, how can we convince a man when we have not recognized reason in him? The same applies to reproaches for vices. Such reproaches must never go as far as contempt for the vicious man. One must not deny a man his moral dignity, one must not suppose that his moral character cannot be restored, since such a supposition is contrary to the concept of man as a moral being who can never lose the capacity for good will.

> *Kant.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 398 -->

5

> *I cannot make anyone better except by means of the remnants of good contained in him himself. I cannot make anyone wiser except by means of the remnants of good sense contained in him.*

> *Kant.*

6

People who assure others that reason cannot be the guide of life are those people who, having renounced reason, have spoiled their lives and do not want to change them.

Reason is one in all people. Communication between people is founded on reason. And therefore the demands of the reason that is one for all are binding on every person.
