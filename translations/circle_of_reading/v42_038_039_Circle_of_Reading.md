<!-- vol. 42, p. 38 -->

11 September

True faith attracts not so much by promising the good to the believer as by representing the one refuge of salvation not only from all the misfortunes of this life but also from the fear of death.

1

*If you are aware that you have no faith, know that you are in the most dangerous position in which a man can possibly be in this world.*

2

It is bad if a man does not have something for which he is ready to die.

3

> The worshipers of utility have no morality but the morality of profit, and no religion but the religion of material good. They found man's body mutilated and exhausted by poverty, and in their thoughtless zeal they said to themselves: “Let us heal this body; when it is strong, fat, well fed, the soul will return to it.” But I say that this body can be healed only by healing the soul. In it is the root of the disease, and bodily ailments are only outward manifestations of this disease. Present-day mankind <!-- vol. 42, p. 39 --> is dying from the absence of a common faith binding earth with heaven, the universe with God. From the absence of this religion of the spirit, of which only empty words and lifeless forms remain, from the complete absence of a consciousness of duty, of the capacity to sacrifice oneself, man, like a savage, fell prostrate in the dust and set up on the empty altar an idol to “profit.” The despots and princes of this world became its high priests. It was from them that the loathsome teaching of profit arose, which proclaims: “Each only for his own, each only for himself.”

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

4

Examining the causes of the calamities from which mankind suffers, going up from the nearest causes to the more fundamental ones, you will always arrive at the fundamental cause of all the calamities of every kind that befall people: at the absence or weakness of faith, that is, the unclearness or falseness of the established relation of man to the world and to its source.

5

A man who professes an external law is a man standing in the light of a lantern hung on a post. He stands in the light of this lantern, it is light for him, and there is nowhere further for him to go.

A religious man carries the lantern before him on a more or less long pole; the light is always ahead of him, and always urges him to follow it, and opens before him anew a new, lighted space that draws him on.

Salvation is not in rites, in sacraments, not in professing this or that faith, but in a clear understanding of the meaning of one's life.
