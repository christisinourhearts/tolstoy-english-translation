## THE POWER OF CHILDHOOD

— Kill him!.. Shoot him!.. Shoot the scoundrel right now!.. Kill him!.. Cut the murderer's throat!.. Kill him, kill him!.. — shouted the men's and women's voices of the crowd.

An enormous crowd of people was leading a bound man along the street. This man, tall and straight, walked with a firm step, holding his head high. On his handsome, manly face was an expression of contempt and malice toward the people around him.

This was one of those people who, in the war of the people against the authorities, fight on the side of the authorities. He had been seized now and was being led to execution.

“What's to be done! Strength is not always on our side. What's to be done? Now the power is theirs. If it's to die, then die; evidently it has to be so,” this man thought, and, shrugging his shoulders, smiled coldly at the cries that went on in the crowd.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 32 --> — This is a policeman, he was shooting at us this very morning! — they shouted in the crowd.

But the crowd did not stop, and he was led on. And when they came to the street where the bodies of those killed by the troops the day before still lay on the pavement, not yet removed, the crowd grew ferocious.

— No use putting it off! Shoot the villain right here and now, why lead him any farther? — people shouted.

The prisoner frowned and only held his head higher. He seemed to hate the crowd even more than the crowd hated him.

— Kill them all! Spies! Tsars! Priests! And these scoundrels! Kill them, kill them now! — shrieked women's voices.

But the leaders of the crowd decided to take him as far as the square and deal with him there.

It was already not far to the square when, in a moment of quiet, a crying child's voice was heard in the back rows of the crowd.

— Dad! Dad! — a six-year-old boy was shouting, sobbing, as he squeezed into the crowd to get to the prisoner.

— Dad! What are they doing to you? Wait, wait, take me, take me!..

The shouts stopped on the side of the crowd from which the child was coming, and the crowd, parting before him as before a force, let the child through closer and closer to his father.

— Oh, what a dear little one! — said one woman.

— Who is it you want? — said another, bending down to the boy.

— Dad! Let me through to Dad! — the boy squeaked.

— How old are you, boy?

— What do you want to do with Dad? — the boy answered.

— Go home, boy, go to your mother, — one of the men said to the boy.

The prisoner had already heard the boy's voice, and heard what was said to him. His face became still more gloomy.

— He has no mother! — he shouted at the words of the one who was sending the child to his mother.

Squeezing his way closer and closer through the crowd, the boy reached his father and climbed up into his arms.

In the crowd they were shouting the same thing: “Kill him! Hang him! Shoot the scoundrel!”

— Why did you leave home? — the father said to the boy.

— What do they want to do with you? — the boy was saying.

— Here's what you do, — said the father.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 33 --> — Well?

— You know Katyusha?

— The neighbor? How could I not know her.

— Well then, go to her and stay there a while. And I... I'll come.

— I won't go without you, — said the boy, and began to cry.

— Why won't you go?

— They'll beat you.

— Oh no, they're all right, they don't mean it.

And the prisoner let the boy down from his arms and went up to the man who was giving orders in the crowd.

— Listen, — he said, — kill me how and where you like, only not in front of him, — he pointed at the boy. — Untie me for two minutes and hold me by the arm, and I'll tell him that you and I are taking a walk, that you're a friend of mine, and he'll go away. And then... then kill me as you like.

The leader agreed.

Then the prisoner again took the boy up in his arms and said:

— Be a good boy, go to Katya.

— And what about you?

— Why, you see, I'm taking a walk with this friend here, we'll go a little farther, and you go, and I'll come. Go on, be a good boy.

The boy stared at his father, bent his little head to one side, then to the other, and fell to thinking.

— Go, dear, I'll come.

— You'll come?

And the child obeyed. One woman led him out of the crowd.

When the child had disappeared, the prisoner said:

— Now I'm ready, kill me.

And here something quite incomprehensible, unexpected happened. One and the same spirit awoke in all these people, who a minute before had been cruel, pitiless, and full of hatred, and one woman said:

— You know what. We ought to let him go.

— That's so, God be with him, — someone else said. — Let him go.

— Let him go, let him go! — the crowd thundered.

And the proud, pitiless man, who a minute before had hated the crowd, burst into sobs, covered his face with his hands, and, like one who was guilty, ran out of the crowd, and no one stopped him.

*Victor Hugo. Retold by L. N. Tolstoy.*
