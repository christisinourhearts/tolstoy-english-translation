<!-- vol. 41, p. 166 -->

14 March

Love draws people toward union; reason, which is one in all, affirms this union.

1

> Man thinks—so he is made. It is clear that he must think reasonably. A reasonably thinking man first of all thinks about the purpose for which he must live: he thinks about his soul, about God. But look, what do worldly people think about? About anything you like, <!-- vol. 41, p. 167 --> only not about this. They think about dancing, about music, about singing; they think about buildings, about wealth, about power; they envy the position of rich men and kings. But they do not think at all about what it means to be a man.

> *Pascal.*

2

> One of the chief duties of a person consists in making that bright principle of reason which we receive from heaven shine with all its force.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

3

Only what all people acknowledge and cannot help acknowledging, only that is a manifestation of true reason.

4

> Whoever wants to become a true man must cast off pleasing the world; whoever wants to live a true life, let him not be guided by what it is customary to consider good, but let him carefully search out where and what true good is. There is nothing holier or more productive than independent inquisitiveness of soul. First of all establish in yourselves such a relation to the phenomena of life, and then decide for yourselves all the questions that arise.

> *Emerson.*

5

> We insult truth when, doubting its strength, we permit or forbid the expression of these or those thoughts. Let truth and falsehood come to hand-to-hand fighting: truth is invincible in a free and equal fight. By refuting falsehood it destroys it better than any prohibitions.

> *Milton.*

6

> Our church Christianity is built on empty and shaky foundations; those who rely on it are in constant danger and are always afraid of something. A strongly expressed doubt, shaking all its foundations, calls forth thunder and lightning from the representatives of the church, and the more well-founded the doubts, the stronger the alarm.

> *Parker.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 168 --> Are people afraid that the mountains will fall in? But church tradition is ready to collapse at any minute. “Maybe it is true, maybe not”—that is all that the people who rely on it can say of it. Nevertheless, it has been made the foundation of religion. Authority is taken for truth, and blind trust has become the essence of religion.

Nothing can change the decisions of reason. Everything that we know, we know through reason. And therefore do not believe those who say that one need not follow reason. Those who say so are like people who would advise putting out the light of the only lantern that guides us in the darkness.
