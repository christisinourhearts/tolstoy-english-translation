<!-- vol. 41, p. 63 -->

28 January

For a person to know the law to which he is subject and which gives him freedom, he must rise from bodily life into spiritual life.

1

He who sent me is true, and what I have heard from him, that I speak to the world.

They did not understand that he was speaking to them of the father.

So Jesus said to them: when you lift up the son of man, then you will know that it is I, and that I do nothing of myself, but as my father taught me, so I speak.

*John 8:26–28.*

The recognition of one's life not in one's personality but in the spirit of God that lives in every person, Christ calls the lifting up of the son of man.

2

> Christ was a true prophet. He saw the mystery of the soul. He saw the greatness of man. He was faithful to what lives in you and in me. He saw God incarnate in man. And in a state of majestic rapture he said: “I am divine; God acts through me, speaks through me. Do you want to see? Look at yourself when you think and feel as I now think and feel.” And people heard these words, and in the next generation they said: “This was Jehovah come down from heaven. And I will kill all who say that this was a man.”

> His ways of expression, his language, his parables took the place of his truth; churches were founded not on his truths but on his comparisons. And Christianity became a myth, just like the earlier poetic teachings of the Greeks and the Egyptians.

> *Emerson.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 64 --> He respected Moses and the prophets, but did not think it necessary to hold on to their primitive revelations, and he subordinated them to the eternal revelation of the heart. Having come to know that commanding law which lives in the hearts of people, he subordinated this law to no other. He recognized this law as God himself.

3

> I and God are one!—said the teacher.—But if you take my body for God, you are mistaken. If you take for God my incorporeal being, separate from other beings, you are also mistaken. You will not be mistaken only when you understand in yourselves my true *I*, the one that is truly one with God and one in all people. To understand this *I*, one must raise up the man in oneself. And when you raise him up, you will see that there is no difference between you and other people.

> It only seems to us that we are separate beings, as it seems to every blossom on an apple tree that it is a separate being, while they are all only the blossom of one apple tree and all came from one seed.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

4

> *One must live the short span of this life by the law of eternal life.*

> *Thoreau.*

5

“The human soul is by nature Christian.”

Christianity is always received by people as something forgotten and suddenly remembered. Christianity raises a person to such a height that a joyful world, subject to a reasonable law, opens before him. The feeling of a person who comes to know the truth of Christianity is like what a person would feel who had been shut up in a dark, stifling tower, if he climbed to the highest, open platform of the tower and saw from it a beautiful world he had not seen before.

The consciousness of submission to human law enslaves; the consciousness of submission to divine law sets free.
