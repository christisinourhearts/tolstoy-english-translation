<!-- vol. 42, p. 363 -->

22 December

Nothing hinders the improvement of the social order so much as the supposition that such improvement can be attained by changing outward forms. This false supposition directs people's activity toward what cannot contribute, and distracts it from what can contribute, to the improvement of people's life.

1

> Social life rests on consciousness, not on science. Civilization is above all a moral matter. If there is no honesty, no respect for right, no respect for duties, no love for one's neighbor—in a word, if there is no virtue, everything is in danger, everything collapses: and neither the sciences, nor the arts, nor luxury, nor industry, nor rhetoric, nor the police, nor the customs house can hold up a building that hangs in the air, having no foundation. A state founded only on calculation and held together by fear is a structure both vile and unstable. Only the morality of the masses is the firm foundation of any civilization; its cornerstone is duty. Those who quietly fulfill it, thereby giving a good example, are in this way the salvation and support of that brilliant world which does not even know <!-- vol. 42, p. 364 --> about them. Nine righteous men could have saved Sodom, but thousands and thousands of good people are needed to save a people from corruption and ruin.

> *Amiel.*

2

> The true direction of thought consists not in establishing new laws for secular or spiritual power, but in recognizing the moral dignity of every person. Such a direction of thought will contribute to the progress of mankind incomparably more than all the unhappy attempts of the blind to lead the blind, in which they all fall into the pit of dogmas and authorities.

> *Iats.*

3

> The question is not at all which of the two to choose: Christianity or socialism?

> One cannot even compare these two teachings with each other—so different are they in essence.

> Christianity teaches about the eternal meaning of the whole universe, about the divinity and therefore the indestructibility of our spiritual essence, about the vocation of man, and, among other things, about the right way, following from this, of satisfying his material needs.

> Socialism, in comparison with Christianity, is a small, secondary question about the material needs of the working class, standing outside any connection with the chief question of the meaning of human life.

> One may raise questions about the compatibility of Christianity with socialism, but one cannot raise the question of which of the two to choose: Christianity or socialism?

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

4

The anarchists are right in everything: both in their denial of what exists and in their assertion that, with the existing morals, nothing can be worse than the violence of power; but they are grossly mistaken in thinking that anarchy can be established by revolution. Anarchy will be established, but it will be established only by there being more and more <!-- vol. 42, p. 365 --> people who will not need the protection of governmental power, and more and more people who will be ashamed to apply this power or to take part in it.

5

> I think that we must be men first of all, and only then subjects. It is undesirable to cultivate in oneself a respect for the law such as one has for the good. The law has never made people more just; on the contrary, owing to respect for the law, good people become the agents of injustice.

> *Thoreau.*

6

> We people must understand that we are all children of one father, called to fulfill here on earth one common law; that each of us must live not for himself but for others; that the aim of life is not to be more or less happy, but to become more virtuous oneself and to help others to be so; that to fight against injustice and error wherever we meet them is not only our right but also our duty—the duty of our whole life, which we cannot neglect or violate without falling into grave sin.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

7

Anarchy does not mean the absence of institutions, but only the absence of such institutions as people are made to submit to by force. It would seem that a society of beings endowed with reason could not and should not be organized otherwise than without violence.

8

> The social task knows no boundaries.

> *Victor Hugo.*

*Recognizing false and violent laws and submitting to them, one cannot establish right, nor even diminish wrong.*
