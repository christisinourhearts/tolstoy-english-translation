<!-- vol. 41, p. 351 -->

27 May

Often the whole activity of people's minds is directed not toward discovering the truth but toward hiding it. Such activity of the mind is the chief cause of temptations.

1

The court has as its aim only the preservation of society in its present position, and for this it persecutes and executes both those who stand above the general level and want to raise it and those who stand below it.

2

In every practical moral precept there is the possibility of a contradiction between this precept and other precepts flowing from the same foundation.

Abstinence: what then, not eat and become incapable of serving people? Not to kill animals: what then, let them eat you? Not to drink wine: what then, not take communion, not be treated with wine? To be chaste: what then, desire the cessation of the human race? Not to resist evil by violence: what then, let a man kill you yourself and others?

The searching out of these contradictions shows only that the person occupied with it does not want to follow the moral rule.

It is always one and the same story: because of one person who needs to be treated with wine—not to resist drunkenness. Because of fear of the cessation of the human race—not to abstain from debauchery. Because of one imaginary violent man—to kill, execute, imprison.

3

> A man cannot do everything, but the fact that he cannot do everything does not prove that he must do what is bad.

> *Thoreau.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 352 -->

4

Ever since there have been people, reasonable beings, they have distinguished good from evil and made use of what people before them had done in this distinguishing—they struggled with evil, sought the true, best path, and slowly but unswervingly moved forward on this path. And always, barring this path, various deceptions have stood before people, aiming to show them that this need not be done, but that one should live as life goes.

5

> I love peasants: they are not learned enough to reason wrongly.

> *Montesquieu.*

6

*Sometimes you wonder why a person defends the strangest, most unreasonable positions: religious, political, scientific. Search—and you will find that he is defending his own position.*

As soon as an action is explained by complicated reasoning, be sure that the action is bad. The decisions of conscience are direct and simple.
