<!-- vol. 41, p. 422 -->

22 June

True religion is one for all people.

1

> It is bad that people do not know God, but worst of all is that people recognize as God what is not God.

> *Lactantius.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 423 -->

2

> *Difference of religions*—what a strange expression! Of course, there may be different beliefs in historical events, handed down from generation to generation to strengthen religion. In the same way, there may also be different religious books (the Zend-Avesta, the Vedas, the Quran, and so on). But there can be only one *religion*, valid for all times. All the different beliefs, however, can contain nothing other than an auxiliary means for religion, which is accidental and may be different according to the difference of time and place.

> *Kant.*

3

*One can believe only in that of which we know that it undoubtedly exists, but which we cannot embrace with reason and express in words.*

4

> We do most people too much honor when we say of them that they profess this or that religion. For they neither know nor seek any religion; the statutory church faith—that is all they mean by this word. And the so-called religious wars, which so often shook the world and drenched it in blood, were never anything other than squabbles over church faith; and the people who complained of religious oppression complained, in essence, not that they were prevented from belonging to their religion, for no external force can do that, but that they were not allowed to follow their church faith publicly.

> *Kant.*

5

The assertion that you are in a lie and I am in the truth is the cruelest word that one person can say to another, especially when it concerns the most important matter in the world. And yet this very thing is what people who argue about religion say to one another.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 424 -->

6

> If you are a Muslim, go and live with Christians; if a Christian—live with Jews; if a Catholic—live with the Orthodox. Whatever your religion may be, associate with people of other beliefs. If their talk does not outrage you and you can freely associate with them, you have attained peace.

> Hafiz said: the object of all religions is one and the same; all people seek love, and the whole world is the dwelling place of love, so why speak of a mosque or of a church!

> *Sufi wisdom.*

7

> A truly believing person is not the one who blindly follows a certain teaching or the Bible, but the one who places his faith in a pure conscience and clear thought—the surest exponents of the will of God.

> *Herbert Bigelow.*

Do not be afraid of doubts—boldly examine with reason the propositions of faith offered to you.
