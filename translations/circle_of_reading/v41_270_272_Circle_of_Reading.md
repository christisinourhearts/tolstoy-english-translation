<!-- vol. 41, p. 270 -->

20 April

Self-renunciation, for a person conscious of his spirituality, is just as much a good as the satisfaction of passions and lusts is for a person living an animal life.

1

> He is good who does good to others. If he suffers for doing good, he is better still; if he suffers from those to whom he does good, he attains the highest goodness, which can be heightened only by an increase of sufferings for what he continues to do; if he dies for it—this is the very highest perfection.

> *La Bruyère.*

2

> Whoever loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me; and whoever loves son or daughter more than me is not worthy of me; and whoever does not take his cross and follow me is not worthy of me. He who has saved his soul will lose it, and he who has lost his soul for my sake will save it.

> *Matthew 10:37–39.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 271 -->

3

> For man there is no higher good than to work unselfishly for the good of others—to work for the eternal good. When people submit to the common interest as they now submit to their personal strivings, then they will know peace and happiness, and before them will open those infinite manifestations of heavenly wisdom which they do not see now.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> Then Jesus said to his disciples: if anyone wants to go after me, let him deny himself, and take his cross, and follow me; for whoever wants to save his soul (life) will lose it; but whoever loses his soul for my sake will find it. What profit is it to a man if he gains the whole world but harms his own soul? or what ransom will a man give for his soul?

> *Matthew 16:24–26.*

5

As fire destroys a candle, so good destroys personal life.

As wax melts before the face of fire, so the consciousness of personal life is destroyed by participation in good.

Death destroys the body as the scaffolding is destroyed when the building has been built. And he whose building has been built rejoices at the destruction of the scaffolding, that is, of his body.

6

There is always a dark spot in our sunlight: it is the shadow that falls from our personality.

*Carlyle.*

7

> Self-love is a prison for the spirit, which deprives one of happiness as surely as a jail deprives one of physical freedom.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 272 -->

8

*We truly live for ourselves only when we live for others. This seems strange, but try it, and you will be convinced of it by experience.*

If a person lives a spiritual life, then renunciation of worldly goods cannot appear to him a merit. He cannot act otherwise. In doing so he improves, and does not worsen, his position.
