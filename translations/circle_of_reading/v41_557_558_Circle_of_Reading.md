<!-- vol. 41, p. 557 -->

8 August

Ascribing special, exclusive significance and importance to writers whom the majority recognizes as great is a great obstacle to the knowledge of truth. Divine truth may show itself in the babble of a child, in the chatter of a simpleton, in the ravings of a madman, not to speak of the conversations and letters of simple people, and very weak and false thoughts are met with in books considered great or sacred.

1

> *There are many traditional truths that seem probable to us only because we have never seriously thought about them.*

> *Rod.*

2

One must consider the Gospel holy not because the apostles wrote it, but because there is truth in it. Truth spoken by the Buddha, by Muhammad, by any person, is just as important as the truth of the Gospel.

3

> To insist on applying the ancient and outdated laws found in our statutes is the same as forcing the people of our time to live in the houses and use the tools of our ancestors who lived several generations before us.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> For the greater part of mankind religion is custom, or rather, custom is religion. However strange this may seem, I am convinced that the first step toward moral perfecting <!-- vol. 41, p. 558 --> is freeing oneself from the religion in which one grew up. Not one person has gone toward perfecting except by this path.

> *Thoreau.*

5

A thought expressed in the Gospel, the Bible, the Quran, the Upanishads does not become true because it is expressed in books considered sacred. To consider true everything that is said in a book recognized as sacred is idolatry of books, more harmful than any other idolatry.

Every thought, whoever it may come from, is subject to discussion, and every thought, whoever it may come from, deserves attention.
