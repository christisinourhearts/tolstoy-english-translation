<!-- vol. 42, p. 229 -->

5 November

Thought is the clarifying of truth, and therefore bad thoughts are only thoughts that have not been thought through.

1

> What is still at rest can be kept at rest. What has not yet appeared can easily be prevented. What is still weak can easily be broken. What is still little can easily be scattered.

> Take care of things before they exist. Establish order before disorders begin.

> A thick tree began from a thin twig. A nine-story tower began with the laying of small bricks. A journey of a thousand versts begins with one step. Be attentive to your thoughts—they are the beginning of deeds.

> *After Laozi.*

2

> From the very morning one must keep watch over oneself and say to oneself: I may now come into conflict with a person who is impudent, ungrateful, insolent, hypocritical, tiresome, or embittered, for everyone who does not know what is good <!-- vol. 42, p. 230 --> and what is bad is possessed by such vices. But if I myself know firmly wherein good and evil lie, and understand that evil for me is only the bad deed that I myself do, then no offender can harm me, for no one can force me, against my will, to do evil. And if, in addition, I also remember how near to me every person is, not by flesh and blood but by the spirit, which in each of us is from God and constitutes that essence of ours which is higher than the flesh, then I cannot be angry or indignant with a being so near to me, for we are created for one another, called to help one another, as hand helps hand and foot helps foot, like the eyes and the teeth, which always help one another in concert. Therefore to turn away from a neighbor who has offended us is contrary to our true nature. Yet every person who hates another for an offense sins against this.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> O you who seek the truth, master your thoughts if you wish to reach your goal! Fix the gaze of your soul on that one pure light which is free from passion.

> *Brahmanic wisdom.*

4

> In order for the flame to give a steady light, the lamp must be set in a place sheltered from the wind. But if the flame is exposed to changing winds, it will flicker and cast deceptive shadows, dark and strange. Bad thoughts will cast the same kind of shadows on the white surface of your soul.

> *Brahmanic wisdom.*

5

In the bustle of the world and amid the excitement of temptations there is no time to seek means of counteracting our desires.

*Set your aims when you are alone and temptations are absent. Only then will you be strong enough to struggle with the temptations that tempt you.*

*Bentham.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 231 -->

6

> Reflection is the path to immortality, thoughtlessness the path to death. Those who are awake in reflection never die; the thoughtless, the unknowing, are like the dead.

> Rouse yourself—then, guarded by yourself and alertly attentive, you will be unchanging.

> *Buddhist wisdom (Dhammapada.)*

One cannot drive away a bad thought when it has appeared in the mind, but one can understand that the thought that has appeared is bad, and one can call up those thoughts that will weaken or destroy it. A thought has appeared about the shortcomings of a neighbor: I cannot drive it away, but, having understood that this thought is bad, I can call up the thought that condemnation is bad, that I myself am bad, that in him is the same God as in me, and that therefore I cannot help loving him.
