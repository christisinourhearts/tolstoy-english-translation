<!-- vol. 41, p. 136 -->

28 February

*Art is one of the means of uniting people.*

1

> If the fine arts are not imbued with moral ideas common to all humanity, which alone unite people, then such arts serve only as a diversion, <!-- vol. 41, p. 137 --> for which people feel all the greater need the more they resort to it in order to drown out their dissatisfaction with themselves; but by this they constantly make themselves still more useless and still more dissatisfied.

> *Kant.*

2

> One may allow that art will die, but it is unthinkable that art could live while groveling before wealth and mocking poverty.

> *Morris.*

3

Art is one of the very mightiest means of suggestion. And since both what is vicious (and the vicious is always more easily suggested) and what is good can be suggested, there is no means of suggestion against which one needs to be more on guard than against the suggestion of art.

4

The less influence of suggestion there is in a religious teaching, the higher it is, and vice versa.

5

> *The dignity of art and the dignity of science lie in selfless service for the benefit of people.*

> *John Ruskin.*

6

> The artist is one of two things: either a high priest or a more or less skillful buffoon.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

7

> Art is in its proper place only when its aim is moral perfecting. The task of art is to teach lovingly. But if art does not help people to discover the truth, but only provides a pleasant pastime, then it is a shameful and not an exalted pursuit.

> *John Ruskin.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 138 -->

8

Our art, having set itself the aim of supplying amusements for the rich classes, not only resembles prostitution but is nothing other than prostitution.

Discussions of art are the most idle of discussions. He who understands art knows that each art speaks its own special language and that to speak about art in words is useless. That is why it happens that those who talk most about art are those who do not understand it, do not feel it.
