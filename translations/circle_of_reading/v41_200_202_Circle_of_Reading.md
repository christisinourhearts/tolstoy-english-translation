<!-- vol. 41, p. 200 -->

29 March

If you sometimes feel that, in spite of all your desire to conquer a passion, the passion overpowers you, do not think that you cannot conquer the passion. This proves only that this time you could not do it. A driver does not throw down the reins because he does not stop the horses at once, but keeps pulling—and the horses stop. So it is with you: you did not hold back once; keep on struggling, and surely it is you who will win, and not the passions.

1

> *To set one's mind above one's inclinations—this is the very meaning of abstinence.* Of it one of the fathers of the church says that it is not a virtue but a great work of virtue.

> *Johnson.*

2

*Accustom yourself to rule over greed, sleep, luxury, anger.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 201 -->

3

> He who has conquered himself is a far greater conqueror than he who has conquered a thousand times a thousand people in battle. It is better to conquer oneself than all other people.

> He who has conquered other people in battle may be conquered, but he who has conquered himself and is master of himself will remain a conqueror forever.

> *Dhammapada.*

4

> To master oneself so far as to respect others as oneself, and to deal with them as we wish to be dealt with—that is what may be called the teaching of love of humanity. There is nothing higher than this.

> *Confucius.*

5

> Young man! Deny yourself the satisfaction of your desires (in amusements, in luxury, and the like), if not with the intention of renouncing their satisfaction entirely, then from the wish to keep in view an ever-increasing enjoyment. Such thrift with your feeling of life will, thanks to the postponement of enjoyment, make you in reality richer. The consciousness that enjoyment is in your power is more fruitful and more far-reaching than the feeling satisfied by means of this enjoyment, because together with satisfaction it is destroyed.

> *Kant.*

6

> Passion in a person's heart is at first a cobweb, then a thick rope.

> Passion at first is like a stranger, afterward like a guest, and finally like the master of the house.

> *The Talmud.*

7

> Every intemperance is the germ of suicide; it is an invisible stream under a house, which sooner or later will wash away its foundation.

> *Blackie.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 202 -->

8

> Truly mighty is he who conquers himself.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

9

> My chief desires are never to be angry, always to speak the truth, to speak it lovingly, so as to offend no one, to be tolerant with the intolerant, kind with those who condemn, free from passion among the passionate. That is what my chief desires are.

> *Dhammapada.*

Abstinence is not attained at once, but it can always be attained. The life of every person moves not toward the strengthening of the passions but toward their weakening.

Time helps efforts and abstinence.
