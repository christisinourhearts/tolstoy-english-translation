<!-- vol. 42, p. 391 -->

31 December

There is no past, the future has not come. The present is the infinitely small point of contact of the nonexistent past with the nonexistent future. And it is in it, in this timeless point, that the true life of man takes place.

1

> “Time passes!” we are used to saying. There is no time; it is we who move.

> *After the Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 392 -->

2

*Time is behind us, time is before us, with us there is none.*

3

> I consist of spirit and body. To the body all things are indifferent, for matter is deprived of the capacity to distinguish anything whatever. To the spirit, in turn, all that does not proceed from the spirit is also indifferent, for the life of the spirit is independent. But the life of the spirit has not the slightest significance either in the past or in the future. All its importance is concentrated in the present time.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

4

> Time is the greatest illusion. It is only the inner prism through which we decompose being and life, the image under which we gradually see what is timeless, in the idea. The eye does not see the whole sphere at once, although the sphere exists whole at once. One of two things is needed: either that the sphere turn before the eye that looks at it, or that the eye go around the sphere it is observing.

> In the first case—this is the world unfolding, or as if unfolding, in time; in the second case—this is our thought, analyzing and gradually restoring. For the higher reason there is no time: what will be, is. Time and space are a crumbling of the infinite for the use of finite beings.

> *Amiel.*

5

> One can imagine a thinking being for whom it is easier to foresee the future than to remember the past. Already in the instincts of insects there is something that makes us suppose that they are guided by the future more than by the past. If animals possessed recollection of the past to the same degree as presentiment of the future, then some insects <!-- vol. 42, p. 393 --> would surpass us; but in fact the power of presentiment always, it seems, stands in inverse relation to memory of the past.

> *Lichtenberg.*

6

> Our soul is cast into a body, where it finds number, time, dimension. It reasons about this and calls it nature, necessity, and cannot think otherwise.

> *Pascal.*

There is no time. There is only the infinitely small present. It is in it that life takes place. And therefore it is on the present alone that a man must direct all his spiritual powers.
