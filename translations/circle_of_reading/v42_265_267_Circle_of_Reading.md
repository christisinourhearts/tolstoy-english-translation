<!-- vol. 42, p. 265 -->

18 November

Good cannot be measured either by the need of the one who receives or by the sacrifice of the one who gives, but only by that communion in God which is established between the giver and the receiver.

1

> *Life is not always a good. Only a good life is a good.*

> *Seneca.*

2

> Nature has so arranged things that wrongs are remembered more than good deeds. Good is forgotten, but wrongs hold on stubbornly in the memory.

> *Seneca.*

3

> It is not virtue but only a deceptive cast and likeness of it when we are brought to the fulfillment of duty by the expectation of reward.

> *Cicero.*

4

> Do not slander, lest slander and dishonor turn upon you yourself, for every evil spirit attacks from in front, but slander always from behind.

> Do not give way to anger, because a person who has given way to anger forgets his duties and neglects his good deeds.

> Beware of lust, because its fruits will be sickness and repentance.

> Do not keep envy in your heart, so as not to poison your existence.

> Do not fall into sin out of shame.

> Be diligent and silent, live by your own labor, and put aside from your earnings for those who have nothing. This custom will be the worthiest deed in your activity.

> Do not steal another's goods and do not neglect your own work, for whoever does not feed himself by his own work but makes others feed him is a cannibal.

> Do not enter into alliance with a greedy person and do not trust yourself to his guidance.

> Do not enter into explanations with a fool, take no money from an evildoer, and have no dealings with a slanderer.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 266 --> Do not get drawn into argument with a crafty person, and better leave him altogether in peace.

5

> When people ask what, properly speaking, pure morality is, by which, as by a touchstone, we must test the moral content of every action, then I must confess that only philosophers could make the answer to this question doubtful, for to sound human sense this question was settled long ago—not, it is true, by means of abstract general reasoning, but by means of the distinction between the good and evil acts that are done, which we distinguish as surely as the right hand from the left.

> *Kant.*

6

> Do good to your friends, that they may love you still more; do it to your enemies, that they may someday become your friends.

> When you speak of your enemy, remember that a day may come when he will become your friend.

> *Cleobulus.*

7

All people more or less approach one of two opposite limits: one—life only for oneself, the other—only for God.

8

> To join one good deed to another so that there is no interval between them—that is what I call a happy life.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

*True good is done by us only when we do not notice it, but go out of ourselves in order to live in another.*
