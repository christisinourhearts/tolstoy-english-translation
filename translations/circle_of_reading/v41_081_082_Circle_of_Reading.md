<!-- vol. 41, p. 81 -->

4 February

A person is free only when he is in truth. Truth is revealed by reason.

1

> *Remember that the distinguishing property of a rational being is free submission to his fate, and not the shameful struggle against it characteristic of animals.*
>
> *Marcus Aurelius.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 82 -->

2

> If a person did not know that eyes can see, and never opened them, he would be very pitiable. Just so, and even more pitiable, is a person if he does not understand that reason has been given to him so that he may calmly bear every misfortune. If a person lives reasonably, it is easy for him to bear every misfortune, because reason will tell him that every misfortune passes and often turns into good. Yet people, instead of looking misfortune straight in the eye, try to dodge it. Would it not be better to rejoice that God has given us the power not to be distressed by what happens to us against our will, and to thank him that he has subjected our soul only to what is in our power—our reason. He has not subjected our soul to our parents, our brothers, wealth, our body, or death. He has subjected it to that alone which depends on us—our reason.
>
> *After Epictetus.*

3

> Scatter nuts and gingerbread in the street—children will immediately come running, begin picking them up, and fight among themselves. Adults will not fight over this. And even children will not pick up empty shells.
>
> For a rational person, wealth, honors, and fame are either children's sweets or empty shells. Let children pick them up, let them fight over them, let them kiss the hands of rich people and rulers and their servants: for a rational person all this is shells. If some nut happens to come into a rational person's hands, why not eat it. But to bend down in order to pick it up, to fight over it, to knock someone down or fall down oneself—such trifles are not worth it.
>
> *Epictetus.*

We are not free and are subject both to our passions and to other people to the extent that we depart from the demands of reason. True liberation is accomplished only by reason.
