<!-- vol. 41, p. 533 -->

28 July

Perfecting is always preceded by repentance. It is bad if a person considers himself or his people not to be in need of repentance.

1

> It is dangerous to seek the cause of evil outside ourselves. Repentance then becomes impossible.

> *Robertson.*

2

> You should live so that you could entrust to your worst enemy everything that is in your soul.

> *Seneca.*

3

*Not to confess one's misdeeds means to increase them.*

4

> If a person is unhappy, what should he do first of all? Complain of this or that person, of this or that circumstance? Fill the whole world with laments and reproaches? Of course not. All preachers of morality advise blaming no one but oneself alone. The unhappy man must first of all acknowledge that he is unhappy precisely because he has been unreasonable. If he had been faithful to nature and its laws, then nature with its unchanging laws would have given him the good and happiness; but he did not follow the laws of nature, and nature, having exhausted its patience, leaves him without consolation and says to him: not by this way but by another will you attain well-being; this way, as you yourself see, leads only to ill-being: abandon it. All teachers of morality advise a person, having repented, to say to himself: “Yes, I was not reasonable enough, I departed from the laws of God and followed imaginary, false laws—the laws of the devil. That is why I have come to this state.”

> *Carlyle.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 534 -->

5

> My soul is heavy. In all my long life I have made no one happy: not my friends, not my family, not even myself. I have done much, much evil... I was the cause of three great wars. Because of me more than 800,000 people perished on the fields of battle; now mothers, brothers, widows mourn them... And all this stands between me and God.

> *Bismarck.*

A person is the better the faster he moves toward infinite perfection, regardless of the step on which he stands. And the speed of the movement always depends on the degree of satisfaction or dissatisfaction with oneself.
