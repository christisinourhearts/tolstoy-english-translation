<!-- vol. 41, p. 154 -->

7 March

Labor, the exercise of one's powers, is a necessary condition of life. A person can force others to do what he needs, but he cannot free himself from the bodily need for work. If he does not work at what is needed and reasonable, he will work at what is not needed and foolish.

1

Man, like every animal, is so created that he must work in order not to die of hunger and cold. And this work, to feed himself and protect himself from bad weather, is for man, as for every animal, not a torment but a joy. But people have so arranged their life that some do no work at all themselves, but force others to work for them and are bored because they do not know what to do, and invent all sorts of foolishness and nastiness to occupy themselves, while others work beyond their strength and are bored with work, bored because they have to work not for themselves but for others.

It is not good for either the ones or the others. For the first, those who do not work, it is bad because through idleness they ruin their souls, and what is bad for the second is that they spend their bodies beyond their strength.

But those who work are still better off than those who do not work. The soul is more precious than the body.

2

> If work is the main thing for you, and pay a secondary thing, then your master will be labor and its creator—God. But if work is a secondary thing for you, and pay the main thing, then you are slaves of pay and of its creator—the devil, and moreover the lowest and last of devils.

> *Ruskin*

3

The devil, catching people on his line, puts on various baits. But for an idle person none are needed; he takes the bare hook.

4

A European extols to a Chinese man the advantage of machine production: “It frees man from labor.” — <!-- vol. 41, p. 155 --> But labor is a good. Freedom from labor would be a great calamity, — answers the Chinese man.

5

> *All manual labor ennobles a person. Not to teach a son manual labor is the same as preparing him for robbery.*

> *The Talmud.*

Without exercise of the muscles an animal cannot live, and a person cannot live either.

And in order that this exercise should satisfy and gladden, one must exercise it on what is useful, and best of all on serving others. This is its best use.
