<!-- vol. 41, p. 417 -->

19 June

Conscience is the consciousness of one's spiritual principle. And only when it is such a consciousness is it a faithful guide of people's life.

1

In the period of conscious life a person can often notice in himself two separate beings: one—blind, sensual, and the other—seeing, spiritual. The blind, animal one eats, drinks, rests, sleeps, breeds, and moves as a wound-up machine moves; the seeing, spiritual being, bound up with the animal one, does nothing itself, but only appraises the activity of the animal being by coinciding with it when it approves this activity, and diverging from it when it does not approve it.

This seeing, spiritual being, whose manifestation we call conscience in plain speech, can be compared to a compass needle that would always point with one end toward good and with the other, opposite end, toward evil, and it is not visible to us until we deviate from the direction it gives, that is, from good toward evil. But one need only commit an act contrary to the direction of conscience, and the consciousness of the spiritual being appears, pointing out the deviation of the animal activity from the direction indicated by conscience.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 418 -->

2

> God has given you tradition, or the consciousness of all humanity, and your personal consciousness, that is, your conscience, as those two wings with the help of which you can draw near and rise to him and come to know the truth. Why then do you want to clip one of these wings? Why withdraw from the world or be swallowed up by the world? Why drown out either the voice of your conscience or the voice of humanity? Both are sacred. God speaks to you through the one and the other. Each time they coincide, when the voice of your consciousness or conscience is confirmed by the consciousness of humanity, you are in the presence of God and can be sure that you have found the truth or at least read a part of the law of God, since one voice serves as a check on the other.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

3

People speak of the tradition of moral teaching, or religion, and of conscience as of two separate guides of man. But in reality there is only one guide—conscience, because only conscience recognizes or does not recognize the tradition of moral teaching, or religion.

4

> Conscience! you are the divine, immortal, and heavenly voice, you are the only sure guide of an ignorant and limited, but rational and free being, you are the infallible judge of good, you alone make man like God! From you come the excellence of his nature and the morality of his actions. Without you there is nothing in me that raises me above the animal, except the sad advantage of getting tangled in errors as a result of a disordered intellect and of reason without guidance.

> *Rousseau.*

5

> You are young and are living through a time of enthusiasms and passions. All the more at this age listen to the voice of your conscience, revere it above all. Do not depart from it for the sake of lust, or for the sake of passion, <!-- vol. 41, p. 419 --> or for the sake of submission to people's suggestions, to customs, even if they are called law. Always ask yourself: is this in accord with my conscience? Be courageous and self-denying for the sake of the demands of conscience. Do not be afraid that you will part ways with the opinion of people.

> *Parker.*

6

> A person seems always to hear a voice behind him, but cannot turn his head and see the speaker. This voice speaks in all languages, governs all people, but no one has ever seen the speaker. If only a person begins to obey this voice exactly, takes it into himself so that he no longer separates himself from it in his thoughts, it will seem to him that he himself is this voice; he will merge with it. And the more attentively he listens to this voice, the greater the wisdom that will be imparted to him, and this voice will grow into a majestic and solemn call that will open to him a blessed life. But if he is occupied with worldly affairs and not with that truth for the sake of which affairs ought to be done, then this voice becomes weak and is heard only as a faint buzzing.

> *Emerson.*

7

> It depends on us whether to stifle our conscience or to be lit by its light, listening to it: if it commands us to do something and we do not do it, if it goes on warning us and we still do not heed it, then its voice begins little by little to grow weaker and at last falls completely silent. Therefore listen to it constantly. By not paying attention to trivial misdeeds we can easily fall into great transgressions. Insignificant faults most often instill dangerous habits in us. Let us try to cut off evil while it has not yet put down deep roots in us. Evil and good grow in us in proportion as we admit them into our heart.

> *From “Pious Thoughts”*

*Beware of everything that is not approved by your conscience.*
