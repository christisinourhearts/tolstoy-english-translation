<!-- vol. 41, p. 193 -->

25 March

People help people all their lives, says the proverb. Without such help people cannot live. This help ought to be mutual, but our life has taken such a shape that some people help, while others only make use of this help.

1

Every person makes use of the labors of other people, and therefore, in order not to be a thief, you must labor yourself and give your labors to other people for what you take from them.

To weigh how much you take and how much you give—this is quite impossible, and therefore, in order not to be a thief, you must try to take for yourself as little of others' labors as possible, and to give as much of your own to other people as possible.

2

*When acquiring any object and using it, remember that it is a product of human labor, and that in spending, destroying, spoiling it, you destroy labor, you spend human life*.

3

> Whatever middlemen may stand between you and the object you have acquired, this object was made by your brother man, whose labor you are obliged to respect. And this respect you can express only by your careful treatment of the products of your brothers' labor and by labor for them.

> *John Ruskin.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 194 -->

4

The rich, besides their relation to the labor of others, expressed by purchase, have a direct relation to working people, to servants. Nowhere is our nonrecognition of Christianity so visible as in our relation to servants. People give all their time to serving us, doing for us the dirtiest, most unpleasant, and most senseless work, and for the most part we think that we are square with them if we have paid them the agreed money. But they are our brothers, after all, and if the arrangement of our life is such that these people must serve us for money, then the least we can do is to establish human relations with them.

If they serve us, then why should we not eat together with them and the same as they? Why should we not rest, enjoy ourselves, and learn together with them?

5

> Look on all your talents and knowledge as means of helping others.

> The strong and wise man has been given his gifts not in order to oppress, but in order to help and support the weak.

> *John Ruskin.*

It is not enough that help to people should be mutual: people who receive help from their brothers must repay it not only with money, but also with respect and gratitude.
