<!-- vol. 41, p. 330 -->

17 May

*Perfect joy, in the words of Francis of Assisi, lies in bearing an undeserved reproach, enduring bodily suffering from it, and not feeling hostility toward the cause of the reproach and the suffering, joy in the consciousness of true faith and love, such as neither the evil of people nor one's own sufferings can disturb.*

1

> See that you do not do your alms before people so that they may see you: otherwise you will have no reward from your heavenly father. So, when you do alms, do not trumpet before yourself, as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and on the streets, so that people may glorify them. Truly I say to you: they are already receiving their reward.

> *Matthew 6:1–2.*

2

> Highest of all is when you are condemned for a good deed, and you do not grieve but rejoice.

> *After Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> To be unknown to people or not understood by them and not to grieve over this—is a trait of the truly virtuous person.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

4

They scold you, condemn you—rejoice; they praise you, approve of you—be afraid, be grieved.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 331 -->

5

Slander, a false bad opinion against which one cannot justify oneself, is the best school of good.

6

It is good to accustom oneself, when meeting people, to expect and await from them (to test oneself, to destroy one's pride) not approval and praise, but, on the contrary, humiliation, insult, and a perverse opinion of you.

What is called holy foolishness, that is, behavior that provokes people's condemnation and attacks, is unjust insofar as it provokes bad actions in people, but understandable and desirable as the only test of one's love for God and one's neighbor.
