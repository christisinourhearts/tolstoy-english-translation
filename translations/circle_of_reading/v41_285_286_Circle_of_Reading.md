<!-- vol. 41, p. 285 -->

27 April

Unkind feelings give rise to condemning people, but very often condemning people gives rise in us to unkind feelings toward them, and feelings all the more unkind the more we condemn them.

1

> Do not judge, that you may not be judged; for with what judgment you judge, with such you will be judged; and with what measure you measure, with such will it be measured to you. And why do you look at the twig in your brother's eye, but do not feel the log in your eye? Or how will you say to your brother: let me take the twig out of your eye; and here, in your eye is a log? Hypocrite! first take the log out of your eye, and then you will see how to take the twig out of your brother's eye.

> *Matthew 7:1–5.*

2

One of the most usual and widespread superstitions is that every person has his own definite qualities, that there are kind people, wicked, clever, stupid, hot-tempered, cold, and so on. People are not like that.

We can say of a person that he is more often kind than wicked, more often clever than stupid, more often hot-tempered than cold, and the reverse, but it will be untrue if we say of one person that he is always kind or clever, and of another that he is always wicked or stupid. And we always divide people in this way. And this is wrong.

3

> You see your neighbor's weakness, but you do not know that perhaps one of his acts is more pleasing to God than your whole life. Even if your neighbor has had the misfortune to fall, you have not seen the tears he shed before, nor the repentance that followed, and while God, the witness of his sorrow and contrition, has already justified him, you go on condemning him.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 286 -->

4

If there is enmity between two people, both are to blame. Whatever quantity is multiplied by zero, the result will be zero. And if enmity has been produced, then enmity was in each of those at enmity.

5

> If a quarrel has arisen between people, then both who quarrel are without fail to blame for it, even if in the most different degrees. For when the conduct of one of the sides is completely blameless, a quarrel can no more flare up than a match can be lit against an absolutely smooth surface, for example against a mirror.

> *B.*

6

> *Understand well and constantly remember that a person always acts as seems to him better for himself. If it really is better for him, then he is right; but if he is mistaken, then it is the worse for him, because every error is without fail followed by suffering as well.*

> *If you constantly remember this, you will neither be angry with anyone nor indignant at anyone, you will neither reproach nor scold anyone, and you will be at enmity with no one*.

> *Epictetus.*

When living with someone close to you, it is good to agree to stop each other as soon as one or the other begins to condemn his neighbor.
