<!-- vol. 41, p. 106 -->

15 February

There is natural simplicity and there is the simplicity of wisdom. Both the one and the other call forth love and respect.

1

Most of life's problems are solved like algebraic equations: by reducing them to the simplest form.

2

> *The words of truth are always without adornment and simple.*

> *Marcellinus.*

3

The greatest truths are the simplest.

4

Simplicity is always attractive. Hence the attractiveness of children and animals.

5

> Nature knows nothing of that hateful division which people have set up among themselves. She endows people with qualities of the soul without preferring the noble and the rich. Natural good feelings, it seems, are even met with more often among simple people.

> *Lessing.*

6

When people speak abstrusely, artfully, and floridly, they either want to deceive or want to give themselves airs. One should not believe such people, one should not imitate them.

Good speech is simple, understandable to all, and reasonable

7

> Simplicity is the consciousness of one's human dignity.

> *Boiste.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 107 -->

8

> Simplicity is always a consequence of loftiness of feeling.

> *D'Alembert.*

9

The word draws people together, and therefore one must try to speak so that everyone can understand you and so that everything you say is the truth.

Avoid everything artificial, exceptional, everything that can draw attention to you. Nothing so promotes the drawing together of people as simplicity.
