<!-- vol. 42, p. 18 -->

6 September

Error is the usual state of people. At certain times and in certain strata of society it is especially widespread. Such it is in our time in our Christian society, as indeed it cannot be otherwise among people who either know no higher law of life or know it but do not fulfill it.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 19 -->

1

“No matter by whom a sin has been committed, it is most terrible of all when a learned man commits it. An ignorant and dissolute common man is better than an intemperate learned man; for the first has lost his way through blindness, while the second fell into a well with his eyes open.”

*Saadi.*

Such is the sin of the people of our time, enlightened by Christianity and united by means of communication such as never existed before.

2

> Man has lost his soul; some time has passed, and now he begins to pine for it. This loss of soul truly constitutes our sore spot—the center of the worldwide social gangrene that threatens all present-day phenomena with a terrible death. We have neither religion nor God; man has lost his soul and carefully seeks means of healing; but the putrid leprosy, abating for a moment, appears again still stronger and more menacing.

> *Carlyle.*

3

> Our newspapers with their descriptions of crimes and every kind of horror are, as it were, a supplement to breakfasts of meat. Is there anything surprising in the fact that, having subjected their soul and body to the pernicious influence of the one and the other, people turn out afterward to be inclined to quarrels, wars, and suicides? Would it not be strange to see them happy after such a beginning of the day? The enervating influence of their spiritual and bodily food must inevitably bring them to a state of constant unrest, torment, and despair.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> *People seek pleasure, rushing from side to side, only because they feel the emptiness of their life, but do not yet feel the emptiness of that new amusement which attracts them.*

> *Pascal.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 20 -->

5

All that we do to secure our life is exactly the same as what the ostrich does, hiding its head so as not to see how it is being killed. We do worse than the ostrich: in order doubtfully to secure our doubtful life in a doubtful future, we surely ruin our certain life in the certain present.

6

One has only to look from outside at the life of our wealthy classes to see that everything they do for the imaginary securing of their life they do not do for this at all, but only so that, by occupying themselves with this, they may forget that life is never secured and cannot be secured.

7

People of our time try to believe that all the senselessness and cruelty of our life, with the insane wealth of a few, with the envious, embittered poverty of the majority, with violence, armaments, and wars, are visible to no one, and that nothing prevents them from going on living such a life.

An error does not cease to be an error because the majority shares it.
