<!-- vol. 41, p. 102 -->

13 February

Religion is a philosophy understandable to all.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 103 -->

1

> *A person can please God only by a good life. And therefore everything by which, apart from a good, pure, kind, humble life, a person thinks to please God, all of this is nothing but deception and false service of God.*

> *After Kant*

2

> The distinctive feature of the Christian teaching consists in representing the morally good and the morally bad as differing from each other not as heaven from earth, but as heaven from hell. The notion of hell with its eternal torments revolts the soul, but in its meaning this notion is correct. It serves us as a warning against thinking that good and evil, the kingdom of light and the kingdom of darkness, stand side by side and that there are constant transitional steps between them. This notion indicates that good and evil are separated from each other by an immeasurable abyss.

> *After Kant*

3

> The first and most ancient opinion in abstract matters is always the most probable, because the sound human mind hit upon it at once. Such is the existence of a universal principle—God.

> *After Lessing.*

4

Religion is wisdom simplified and addressed to the heart. Wisdom is religion justified by reason.

5

> From what people call religion flow their rules of upbringing, their politics, social economy, and art.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

6

A person without religion, that is, without any relation to the world, is as impossible as a person without a heart. A person may not know that he has a heart; but just as without a heart, so without religion a person cannot exist

<!-- vol. 41, p. 104 -->

7

One must consider the rules of a good life (do not kill, do not be angry, do not fornicate, do not pay evil for evil, and others) true and binding upon us not because they are God's commandments, but one must consider them God's commandments because we feel that they are inwardly binding upon us.

*After Kant*.

8

“How then is one to live, not knowing what will be, not knowing what awaits us?”

Only then does real life begin, when you do not know what awaits us. Only then do you create life and fulfill the will of God. *He* knows. Only such activity serves as testimony of faith in God and in his law. Only then is there both freedom and life.

Religion can illuminate philosophical reasonings. Philosophical reasonings can confirm religious truths. And therefore seek communion with truly religious people and with true philosophers, both living and dead
