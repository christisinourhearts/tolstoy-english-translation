<!-- vol. 42, p. 379 -->

26 December

*Childhood is the age that is subject to suggestion. And therefore the most important thing in upbringing is the choice of the suggestions that are able to influence the child.*

1

At the present time the majority of people only imagine that they, professing Christianity, hold to Christian morality. In reality they follow only pagan morality, and they set up this morality as the ideal of the upbringing of the young generation.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 380 -->

2

People are most susceptible to suggestion in childhood. Reasoning does not have on them 1/1000 of the influence that example has on them.

And therefore all the sermons on conduct read to children are vain and ridiculous when they see by example the opposite of the sermon.

3

> A child's religion depends on the manner of acting, and not on the verbal admonitions, of his parents. The inner and unconscious ideal that moves their life, their faith—that is what influences the child; their words, on the other hand, their reprimands, punishments, even outbursts, are for him nothing but accidents; but their faith he divines and feels by instinct.

> The child sees what we are through what we want to seem; this is the source of his reputation as a physiognomist.

> That is why the first principle of upbringing is the following: bring yourself up; and the first rule that must be followed in order to master the child's will—master your own.

> *Amiel.*

4

> Elders, perhaps, do explain to children that one should not be cruel to animals and to all weak creatures in general. But a child need only go into the kitchen, and he will at once see killed and plucked chickens and geese. What use, then, can fine reasonings about morality be to children, when they are so sharply contradicted by the barbarous and immoral acts of adults committed before their eyes?

> *Struve.*

5

> Reduction of needs—that is what ought to be instilled in young people at all costs, and for which one ought to try to harden them. *The fewer the needs, the happier*—an old but far from recognized truth.

> *Lichtenberg.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 381 -->

6

> The inclination to a pleasant life is worse for a person than all the calamities in life. Therefore it is in the highest degree important that children should be trained to work from their earliest years.

> *Kant.*

7

For children the most important thing is to accustom them to moderation, simplicity of life, labor, and mercy. But how can one accustom them to this when the children see that their parents value luxury and its increase, prefer idleness to labor, and live in abundance among people in need.

All the moral upbringing of children comes down to a good example. Live well, or at least try to live well, and you will bring up your children well to the degree of your success in a good life.
