<!-- vol. 42, p. 236 -->

9 November

Self-love is pride beginning. Pride is self-love that has not been held back and has come into full bloom.

1

> Whoever does not feel aversion to his self-love, to that quality which makes him place himself above everything in the world, is completely blinded, because this is contrary both to justice and to truth. It is contrary to justice because everyone wants the same thing, that is, to be above others, and it is contrary to truth because it is impossible to be above everything in the world.

> *Pascal.*

2

> There are two sorts of people: some are righteous but consider themselves sinners; others are sinful but consider themselves righteous.

> *Pascal.*

3

A person is a fraction. The numerator is his external, bodily, and mental qualities compared with others; the denominator is the person's estimate of himself. To increase his numerator <!-- vol. 42, p. 237 -->—his qualities—is not in a person's power, but to decrease his denominator—his opinion of himself, and by this decrease to come nearer to perfection—is in the power of every person.

4

The lighter and less dense a substance is, the more room it takes up. Just as lightweight are the merits that a proud person ascribes to himself.

5

> There are many people who set themselves up as teachers of others, when they themselves need only to be beginning to learn.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

6

The bad wheel always creaks louder. The empty ear of grain stands higher. Such too is the quality of pride.

7

Lower human nature is averse to humility, the human heart rebels at the mere thought of contempt, of humiliation, and we carefully hide everything that may humble us in the eyes of others, we try to hide what is bad in us even from ourselves; we do not want to see ourselves as we really are. The stronger this quality is in us, the more we need to struggle against it.

*The chief business of life is perfecting. And what perfecting is possible when a person, like every proud man, is entirely satisfied with himself.*
