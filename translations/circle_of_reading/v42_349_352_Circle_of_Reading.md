<!-- vol. 42, p. 349 -->

16 December

Only an increase of love among people can change the existing structure of society.

1

Beings destroy one another, but at the same time beings love and help one another. Life is sustained not <!-- vol. 42, p. 350 --> by the passion of destruction but by the feeling of reciprocity, which in the language of our heart is called love.

So far as I can see the development of the life of the world, I see in it the manifestation only of this law of mutual help. All history is nothing other than an ever clearer disclosure of this single law of the mutual concord of all beings.

2

Love is a dangerous word. In the name of love for the family evil deeds are done, in the name of love for the fatherland still worse ones, and in the name of love for mankind the most terrible horrors. That love gives meaning to human life has long been known, but what does love consist in? This question is unceasingly resolved by the wisdom of mankind, and it is always resolved in a negative way: it is shown that what was wrongly called love and passed under the label of love is not love.

3

> Love will give a new look to this tired, old world in which we live as pagans and enemies of one another; it will so warm the heart that people will soon see how easily both the vain diplomacy of statesmen, and the huge armies, and fleets, and lines of fortresses will vanish, and people will only wonder why their ancestors labored so long over these good-for-nothing, bad doings.

> *Emerson.*

4

> The power of love as applied to the great interests of human societies has become obsolete and forgotten. Once or twice in history it has been applied, and always with great success. But a time will come, and love will become the general law of the life of people, and all those calamities from which people now suffer will vanish, will melt in the universal light of the sun.

> *Emerson.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 351 -->

5

If it is possible to instill, and people do instill, respect for imaginary sacred things: communions, relics, books, then how many times more necessary is it to instill in children and in people who think little a respect not for anything imaginary, but for the most real, and understood by all, and joyful feeling of the love of people for people.

And the time will come, that very time of which Christ said that he languishes in waiting for it—the time will come when people will be proud not that they have taken possession by force of people and their labors, and will rejoice not that they inspire fear and envy in people, but will be proud that they love everyone, and will rejoice that, in spite of all the sorrows caused them by people, they experience this feeling, which frees them from everything bad.

6

Among the Chinese sages there was one, Mozi, who proposed to rulers that they instill in people respect not for force, for wealth, power, courage, but for love. He said: “People are brought up so that they value wealth and fame—and they value them. Bring them up so that they love love—and they will love love.” Mencius, a disciple of Confucius, disagreed with him and refuted him, and the teaching of Mozi did not triumph. But 2000 years have passed, and this teaching must come to be realized among us in the Christian world, after everything has been cast aside that hid from people the rays of the light of true Christianity, which preaches this very thing.

7

*There is one indubitable sign that divides the actions of people into good and evil: if an action increases love and the union of people—it is good; if it produces enmity and disunion—it is bad.*

The time of concord, forgiveness, and love, which must replace the time of discord, wars, executions, and hatred, cannot fail to come, because people already know, and know without doubt, <!-- vol. 42, p. 352 --> that hatred is ruinous both for the soul and for the body, both for the individual and for society, while love gives both inner and outer good, both to each person and to all people.

This time is drawing near. It depends on us to do everything that will bring it nearer, and to refrain from what puts it further off.
