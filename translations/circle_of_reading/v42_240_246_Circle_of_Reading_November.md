## CHRISTIANITY AND THE DIVISION OF PEOPLE

The Christian writer Petr Chelčický wrote in the 15th century a work, “The Net of Faith,” denouncing the church. In this work Petr Chelčický explains the decline of the Christian faith by the fact that the emperor and the pope, having acknowledged themselves Christians, perverted true Christianity. And Chelčický compares this perversion of the true faith by them to the tearing of a fishing net by big fish. Just as all the fish that had been caught escaped through the holes made by the big fish, so all the people caught in the net of Christ lost their faith as a result of its perversion by popes and emperors.

Further on we give the words of Chelčický himself.

Those caught by the apostles were long held in a whole, undamaged net, but when, after them, with the passing of time, people, feeling themselves safe, fell asleep, the enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and the tares multiplied so much that they overpowered the wheat and weakened it. Christians were held in a deep sleep <!-- vol. 42, p. 241 --> at the time when the emperor endowed the high priest with property and power; senseless from heavy sleep, they dared to reject the poverty in which they had lived in the name of Christ and to exchange it for dominion and honor that were imperial and even higher than imperial. At first they hid in pits, caves, and forests, and then, lo and behold, the emperor himself conveys the priest about Rome, having seated him on a white mare. By this the purity and innocence of the apostolic calling was violated. Therefore Peter's net was badly torn when *these two great whales entered it*, that is, the high priest with royal dominion and an honor exceeding the emperor's, and the emperor, who had barged in under the skin of faith with pagan power and offices. When both these whales turned about in the net, it was so torn that now little of it remains whole. From these two whales a multitude of false estates was born, which in their turn tear the net of faith: first, monks of various cut and color, then learned people, of the schools, of the universities, then the rectors of parishes; then, of the unlearned: various noble families adorned with coats of arms, then the estates of townspeople. Each of these assemblies and estates strives to dominate, acquiring lands for itself either by cunning, or by violence, or by purchase, or by inheritance. Some of them are spiritual lords, others secular.

The Roman church has divided into three parts: the secular lords, kings, and princes fight and defend the church; the clergy pray; and the third part is made up of the working folk, who must provide for the bodily needs of the first two parts. What inequality comes from such a division! The two sides are well off; they are idle, they gorge a great deal, they think nothing of spending money or of lying on the third side, having tucked it under themselves, while this third side carries on itself, in suffering, the luxury of those two gluttons. Such a division is contrary to the teaching of Christ, according to which the whole world must make up one multitude, one heart, and one spirit.

Most of all, two strong whales have torn the net of faith and constantly tear it: the chief spiritual lord and the chief secular lord. *The spiritual lord*—the pope—breaks the law of Christ in that, having rejected poverty, labor, preaching, and the other pastoral duties, he has acquired secular power and honor and demands that people bow to the ground before him, as before <!-- vol. 42, p. 242 --> God. He has multiplied his laws, which are contrary to the law of God and to faith, so that because of these laws people have forgotten the law of God and faith and think that faith is nothing other than the laws of the great priest. In all their sacred acts the clergy are guided by these laws: they do not know how to pray otherwise than by mumbling the hours prescribed by the laws and deliberately composed, with which thick books are written full. It is considered prayer when in church, for all to hear, one priest tosses words and verses back and forth with another. The ignorant people, without reasoning, take all this for the Christian faith, and no wonder, because about faith they have heard only that God can be seen in church, and that one must not plow on Sunday.

The other whale, which has barged into the net of faith and torn it, is *the emperor with pagan government*, pagan institutions, with pagan rights and laws. Before Constantine's acceptance of Christianity, Christians were guided by the one law of Christ without any admixture of papal and imperial decrees, had no king from their own midst, and had only to pay tribute and perform other duties, as subjects of pagans. But when the emperor Constantine was received into the faith with pagan government and pagan laws, then the innocence and purity of Christians was violated.

It is impossible to enumerate all the pagan features with which the true faith and the worship of God have been defiled; let us speak of some that have to do with the emperor. Wishing to rule over Christians, Constantine and his successors ought to have shown an example of the highest piety, and yet they live among Christians, departing from the faith and doing deeds most hateful to God. And their servants and household also lead a most unworthy life, so that in Christian society they are carrion, which infects everyone with its stench. And the clergy and the masters even justify them, as the third side of the satanic church, and say: “So it befits their rank; court people must be merry, free, unconstrained.”

The emperor uses pagan power willfully, with proud complacency and boldness, not at all thinking that he is a Christian and rules over Christians. *Not yet so important are the bodily oppressions* that the emperor inflicts on his subjects, laying taxes on them and the like: by this damage is done to <!-- vol. 42, p. 243 --> property, and people are burdened with heavy work, but conscience does not suffer from it, if only all these constraints are borne patiently. Far more important is that the secular power does not count it a sin to kill people and to commit every kind of violence, and *compels* Christians to make war on one another, and in this way *to transgress the commandment of Christ.*

The state of the first church, when pagans had nothing in common with Christians, was the most favorable for Christians and could have lasted until the present time, if, through the wiles of Satan and the blindness of two persons, Sylvester and Constantine, poison had not been poured into Christianity, that is, papal and imperial power. Something happened with the church of Christ like what happened with the Jews. Having come into the promised land, they lived there more than four hundred years, having no earthly rulers over them and being only under the protection of God and his law; but then, having rejected God, they began to ask Samuel for a king. Their wish was granted, but as a testimony to the great sin they had committed, God sent a sign: thunder and rain. Something like this happened with the Christians too, with only this difference, that the Jews wished to have a king out of attachment to what is earthly, hoping that their earthly affairs would go better under an earthly king than under a heavenly king; the Christians, however, did not reject God and did not wish to have a king with pagan government, but this was done *under the guise of a good for the church*, which they expected from the emperor's acceptance of the Christian faith. The consequences turned out to be the opposite: what the emperor could not formerly introduce among the Christians by subjecting them to torments, that he introduced under the guise of friendship toward them, and, uniting with them in faith, drew them into pagan unbelief. Sylvester and Constantine are guilty of this evil, but no less guilty are those later Christians who, considering themselves the most perfect and the wisest in understanding the faith, *argue the necessity of secular power for the good of the church.*

With the passing of time, to the multitude of fish caught by the apostles, or, to put it otherwise, to the multitude of believers, many fish, or rabbles of people, were joined, which tore the net of faith. These rabbles do not want to abide in the faith or to follow it, but drag the faith after them, and, each having its own peculiarities contrary to the faith, want these to be recognized as faith. First of all let us speak of the rabbles and generations adorned with coats of arms.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 244 --> These manifold generations adorned with coats of arms lead a life contrary to the commandments of God and surpass other people in the mocking of the son of God. These generations are born doubly in sin: 1) in Adam's sin, like all people, and 2) with a sinful consciousness of the nobility of their origin. By virtue of this nobility they try to set themselves apart from other people by everything they possibly can: by names, by their manner of bearing themselves, by clothing, food, the building of their dwellings, by rights and manners. Vanity is expressed in all their way of life, customs, and speech. They strive to possess all the goods of the body and of the world, in order to enjoy honor and glory, and they avoid everything unpleasant that people must bear for their sins. Strenuous labor, patience, persecution, simplicity, humiliation, obligingness are unseemly for them: they need a life that is free, idle, easy, a surfeit of earthly goods, cleanliness, beauty, clothes of special fanciful and elegant cuts; they must give luxurious feasts to the amazement of all, like gods and goddesses; they need clean and soft beds, speech sweet and ingratiating, full of flattery, with the refrain: “if it please your grace.” Nobility makes them resort to frequent and disgusting ablutions with the help of attendants, going as far as abomination; nobility makes them whiten themselves; finally, nobility demands pagan dominion, and indeed this generation adorned with coats of arms has seized the land and acquired power over other people. By the sufferings and sweat of serfs and “thick-skulled fools” it can prove its nobility, and a serf has only to stop working for all this nobility to fade and become equal to a shepherd's.

Nobility of origin is founded on the pagan custom of obtaining coats of arms from emperors and kings. Some acquire them by service, as a reward for some heroic deeds; others buy them for the honor, for example, a gate, a wolf's or a dog's head, a ladder, half a horse, a trumpet, knives, a pork sausage, and the like. All nobility rests on these coats of arms, and its whole worth is the same as that of the coats of arms. If there were no money to support it, hunger would force them to throw away the coats of arms and take up plows. Not in coats of arms but in money lies the chief strength of nobility, and when there is no money, the lord becomes equal to the serf and, being ashamed to set to work, has not even bread for dinner.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 245 --> The double birth of the noble estate—in Adam's sin and in the consciousness of its nobility, founded on coats of arms—brings new and numerous sins in its train. The consciousness of nobility gives birth to vanity, to the absence of humility and patience. Let someone call a lord base or a serf, and he will at once drag him to court, to clear himself of serfdom and baseness. Other sins flowing from the same source are idleness, the striving for luxury, for pagan dominion, cruelty, violence. And the clergy indulge these sins and say to the lords: “There is no harm in this; this is right, or this befits your rank.” By such and similar speeches they, as it were, moisten these sins so that they grow faster, and turn them into virtues.

These sins pass from the parents to the children, whom they bring up in the same errors in which they themselves abide, and in this way God's creation is taken away from him. By the nobility of their origin the lords consider it necessary to send their children to courts in the German lands, so that they may learn there various boastings and other abominations, proprieties, polite postures with bows, and drink their fill of that poison which is served at courts. All this comes from vanity: they love worldly greatness too much, and since it is hard for them to attain it at home, they send their children to great people, in order to attain some honor through them, so that the elders may have something to boast of: that, look, your son was chamberlain to the king, and the daughter straightens or carries the queen's train. These heraldic generations have multiplied to such a degree that the land is becoming too small for them. All want to lord it in wealth, but some have nothing to do it with: many are oppressed by poverty, yet they do not want to work, they are ashamed of work, and everyone's gullet is large. Some run into debt without end, coaxing out money with flattering speeches and various promises, but will not work for anything, so as not to disgrace their noble origin with labor. These lords have taken possession of the most extensive and best lands, and these lands have turned into wildernesses, and wolves run over them, while the lords themselves rise and sit down in courtly fashion and spend all their time in endless conversations about various news. Nowhere in holy scripture are there indications that some people are of better birth than others. Solomon himself was conscious of his nothingness, and if the word “noble” occurs in the Old and New Testament, it means <!-- vol. 42, p. 246 --> nobility founded on virtues and wisdom. As vile as the life of these noble people is, so vile is the clothing that the men and women wear. In general, neither pagans nor Jews defiled the faith of Christ to such a degree as these generations founded on coats of arms and wrongfully mixed into the faith. They are not pleasing to God, and they are a harm and a burden to people. The working folk bears on itself the burden of their nobility, and they are ready to swallow it up, and everything good that there is on earth they try to seize and swallow. The great harm from them to all people comes from this, that they clothe everyone in themselves and infect others with themselves, like a corpse with an acrid stench that kills people. First of all they clothe their children and servants in themselves, teaching them vanity and all courtly ways, and then the town estate too takes over its way of life from them.

All this is written so that in these heraldic rabbles that Antichrist might be recognized of whom the apostle Paul speaks, calling him the man of lawlessness and the son of perdition.
