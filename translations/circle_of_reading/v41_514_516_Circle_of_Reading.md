<!-- vol. 41, p. 514 -->

21 July

Love is a manifestation of the divine essence, for which there is no time, and therefore love manifests itself only in the present, now, in every minute of the present.

1

To love, in general, means to do good. That is how we all understand love, and we cannot understand it otherwise.

And love is not only a word, but an activity directed toward the good of others.

If a person decides that it is better for him to refrain from the demands of present love, the very smallest, in the name of another, future manifestation of greater love, then he is deceiving either himself or others, and loves no one but himself alone.

There is no love in the future; love is only activity in the present. And a person who does not show love in the present has no love.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 515 -->

2

> Let us not delay to be just, compassionate, attentive to those we love; let us not wait until they or we are struck by illness or threatened by death. Life is short, and there cannot be too much time to gladden the hearts of our fellow travelers on this short crossing. Let us hasten, then, to be kind.

> *Amiel.*

3

> Hide yourself from the unfortunate one whom you help: let him enjoy the benefaction without knowing the name of his benefactor.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

4

> Help the poor man without trying to learn the causes of his poverty, so as not to discover what may weaken your sympathy.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

5

> *Be good, even though the world condemns you. This is better than when you are praised and you go on being bad.*

> *Lodi.*

6

> The teaching of the Gospel contains a simple faith, namely faith in God and reverence for him, or, what is the same thing, obedience to his law. And his whole law is in one thing only: to love one's neighbor. To love one's neighbor as oneself means to submit to the law and to be happy in fulfilling the law, and, conversely, to despise and hate one's neighbor—this means to fall into rebellion and obstinacy.

> *Spinoza.*

7

> There are two kinds of love.

> In the one, I simply love people, not knowing love for the spiritual principle that is one in all people.

> In the other, I love in all people only one thing—the spiritual principle that is one in all people.

> In the second case, when I love in people their essence, which is one in all of us, I will love people even when they are unpleasant to me.

> In the first case we will keep changing the objects of our love, changing wives, friends, husbands, and so on, since the people we love are constantly changing, and our feelings toward them change too.

> In the second case, as our own moral growth proceeds, we will love more and more that divine spiritual principle which we will come to know more and more clearly in all people.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 516 --> The difference between the one love and the other is that in the first case I will love people only as long as they are pleasant to me.

It is agonizingly hard to remember how you could have done a deed of mercy and did not do it, how you irreparably, forever, deprived of help the one who was waiting for it from you, and yourself of the joyful consciousness of having done what you ought.
