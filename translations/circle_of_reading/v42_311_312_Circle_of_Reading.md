<!-- vol. 42, p. 311 -->

5 December

The longer humanity lives, the more it frees itself from superstitions and the simpler the laws of life appear to it.

1

> Our age is the true age of criticism.

> Religion and legislation usually think they will escape criticism. Religion—by means of its holiness, legislation—by means of its outward majesty.

> But by this very thing they arouse suspicion against themselves and cannot count on unfeigned respect, because reason shows respect only to what is able to withstand its free and public examination.

> *Kant.*

2

> Missionaries zealously spread Christianity in India. But can church Christianity give India a more enviable lot than the one she had in the past? Can it give her more intellectual and spiritual strength than she has and has had since time immemorial? Does church Christianity have a higher conception of the omnipresent, almighty, and all-knowing deity than the followers of the Brahmin teaching have? Can it really be that the thought of a God who walked in the garden with Adam and Eve and did not hear what they were saying because he had gone some distance away from them, who was frightened by an attack on his heavenly stronghold by the builders of some tower and ate roasted lambs with old men, who flew into a rage over various trifles and every now and then cursed the unhappy people he had created for their mistakes—can the thought of such a God be anything higher in comparison with the conception of an invisible, all-knowing, almighty being who manifests his will everywhere in the universe? And faith in the divinity of Christ, in his incarnation, resurrection, and redeeming sacrifice? Is it not blasphemy to entangle the highest and greatest being in the affairs of mortals? If the Hindus must believe <!-- vol. 42, p. 312 --> in incarnation, then why should they not believe in the incarnation of Krishna or Rama; why must they believe not in them, but in Christ? But God, as it is said in the truly sacred scripture of mankind, has no body and was not born (John 4) and cannot become incarnate. The teaching of the resurrection is nothing more than a fairy tale: the grave has never given up its dead, if they really were dead. As for the redemption, this teaching contradicts even the most primitive notions of justice.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

3

> *Examine everything, give reason the first place.*

> *Pythagoras.*

4

Life consists in acquiring more and more truth about one's purpose and living more and more in accordance with this truth. All false religions, however, say that in their scripture or tradition there is a ready-made, complete, perfect truth (the Vedas, the Bible, the Quran) and that there is a means of living in accordance with this truth (faith, sacrifices, prayer, grace). So that there is no need either to seek the truth or to labor at improving one's life. This is terrible.

One should not fear the destructions that reason works in the traditions established by people. Reason cannot destroy anything without replacing it with truth. Such is its property.
