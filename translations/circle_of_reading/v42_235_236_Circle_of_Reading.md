8 November

The consciousness of our life in relation to God is the same as our senses in relation to the world, to things. If there were no senses, we would know nothing about the world, about things; if there were no consciousness of our life in us, we would know nothing about God.

1

> There is only one way to honor God. This way consists in fulfilling one's duties and acting according to the laws given by reason. God exists—this, in my opinion, can mean nothing else than that I, while keeping my free will, feel myself compelled to act rightly. That is God. In general it is our heart that knows God, and to make this knowledge comprehensible to reason is undoubtedly difficult, if not altogether impossible. It is still a question whether reason alone, without the heart, could ever have arrived at God. After the heart came to know God, reason too began to seek him.

> *Lichtenberg.*

2

> The idea of God, for all its grandeur, is the idea of our spiritual nature, only purified and exalted to infinity.

> The basis of the concept of divinity is found in us.

> *Channing.*

3

> To fear God is good, but it is better still to love him. And best of all is to resurrect him in oneself.

> *Angelus Silesius.*

4

One can find God only in oneself.

*Angelus Silesius.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 236 -->

5

A good workman certainly does not know all the details of the master's life, and only a lazy workman tries, doing nothing, to find out about the master's life and tastes in order to please him. The same is a person's relation to God. What is important is to acknowledge him as master and to know what he requires of me; but what he himself is and how he lives I will never know, because I am no match for him: I am the workman, and not the master.

*Each person understands God in the way natural to him, but all fulfill his will alike.*
