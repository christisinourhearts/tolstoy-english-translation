<!-- vol. 41, p. 537 -->

29 July

The more necessary a thing is, the more ruinous the abuse of it. The greater part of people's calamities come from the abuse of the most precious tool of life—reason.

1

*God gave his spirit, reason, so that we could understand his will and fulfill it; but we use this spirit to fulfill our own will.*

2

> Reason, becoming the servant of vice, the tool of the passions, the defender of lies, is not only perverted but becomes sick, losing the capacity to distinguish truth and falsehood, good and evil, the righteous and the unrighteous.

> *Channing.*

3

When a person uses his reason to solve the question of why the world exists and why he himself lives in this world, something like faintness, dizziness, comes over him. A person's mind cannot devise answers to these questions. What does this mean? It means that reason is not given to man in order to answer these questions, that the very asking of such questions signifies an error of reason. Reason solves only the question: “how to live?” And the answer is clear: to live so that it is <!-- vol. 41, p. 538 --> good both for me and for all people. This is necessary for everything living, and for me. And the possibility of this is given to everything living, and to me. And this solution excludes the questions: *why* and *what for*.

4

> Those people who use their reason on unnecessary subjects are like night birds, which see in the dark but go blind in the light of the sun. Their mind is very penetrating when they use it on scientific trifles, but it ceases to see when the true light strikes it.

> *Pittacus.*

5

Long is the night for one who does not sleep. Long is the verst to one who is tired; long is life for the foolish.

The purpose of reason is the discovery of truth, and therefore a great and ruinous error is the use of reason to conceal or distort truth.
