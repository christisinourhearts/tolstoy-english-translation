<!-- vol. 42, p. 312 -->

6 December

We go astray not because we cannot think correctly, but because we live badly.

1

> Ignorance never does evil; only error is ruinous. And people err not because they do not know, but because they imagine that they know.

> *Rousseau.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 313 -->

2

> Every error is poison, and therefore there can be no harmless errors; still less can there be beautiful and sacrosanct errors.

> What use to me are such consolations, over which the Damocles sword of disappointment hangs unceasingly? Only truth is safe. Only truth is firm, and on it alone can one rely. In it alone is true consolation; it alone is an indestructible diamond.—To free a man from a lie means not to take something away, but to give. The knowledge that untruth is untruth is truth. Error always does harm. Sooner or later it will come back upon the man who holds to it.

> *Schopenhauer.*

3

> We see the world by means of our thoughts; we see it not as it is, but in the light our thoughts give it. Hatred makes it for us—as if we had put on black spectacles—foggy and gloomy.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> There are errors that cannot be refuted. One must impart to the erring mind such knowledge as will enlighten it. Then the error will vanish of itself.

> *Kant.*

5

> One of the evil properties of man consists in this, that he loves and respects himself, wishes good for himself. But woe to him if he loves only himself: he will want to be great, and will see that he is small; will want to be happy, and will see himself unhappy; will want to be perfect, and will see himself full of imperfection; will want love and respect from people, and will see that his failings turn people away from him and inspire them with contempt for him. Seeing his desires unfulfilled, such a man falls into the most criminal deed: he begins to hate the truth that goes against him; he wants <!-- vol. 42, p. 314 --> to destroy this truth, and since he cannot do this, he tries, whenever he can, to distort the truth in his own soul and in the thoughts of others; and in this way he hopes to hide his failings both from others and from himself.

> *Pascal.*

6

The struggle of the spiritual and the bodily nature is one and the same in all people, and therefore people fall into one and the same errors. And being in one and the same errors, they become still more confirmed in them and take them for undoubted truth, because a greater number of people share them.

*To feed the hungry, clothe the naked, visit the sick—all these are good deeds, but a good deed incomparable with all of these is freeing a brother from error.*
