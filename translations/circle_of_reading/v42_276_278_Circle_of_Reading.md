<!-- vol. 42, p. 276 -->

24 November

Mercy consists not so much in material help as in spiritual support of one's neighbor. And spiritual support consists first of all in not condemning one's neighbor and in respect for his human dignity.

1

> Be compassionate toward those poor people who are impatient and embittered. Think how hard it is for an unfortunate man to endure misfortunes of every kind in a wretched dwelling, while a few steps from him people pass by who are sated and luxuriously dressed.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

2

> Do not consider yourselves merciful when you share with a poor man not only from your abundance but even sacrificing what is necessary for <!-- vol. 42, p. 277 --> life. True love requires of you that, beyond this, you give him also a place in your heart.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

3

He is truly merciful who pays no attention to slanders and evil-speaking.

4

> Do not believe without proof anything bad about your neighbor, and do not pass on to anyone anything bad about another.

> *Penn.*

5

> A good man, — says Theophrastus, — will inevitably have to be angry at the wicked. But if so, then the better a man is, the angrier he should be; and yet he, on the contrary, becomes all the gentler and freer from passions and hates no one. A sensible man will not hate those who have gone astray; otherwise he would have to hate himself. He has only to recall how often he himself has sinned against virtue, and how many of his actions are in need of a lenient judgment, and then he will become angry with himself as well; for a just judge judges himself by the same judgment as his neighbor. No one is able to justify himself, and one can call oneself innocent only before people, but not before one's own conscience. It is far more humane to meet one who is going astray meekly, with love, and not to persecute him, but to try to bring him back onto the right path. After all, if someone has lost his way on our land, we will not drive him away, but will lead him out onto the proper road.

> To correct one who is going astray is our duty, and we must correct him, for his own sake and for the sake of others, with serious admonitions, but without anger. What doctor is angry with his patient?

> *Seneca.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 278 -->

6

> Be truthful, do not serve anger, give to him who asks—for he asks you for little; you will draw near to the saints, walking these three paths.

> *Dhammapada.*

7

> If anyone is troubled and tempted at the sight of his neighbor falling into sin, and under the pretext of love for good nurses malice, he does not have true mercy, founded on love for God, for everything that flows from it bears the seal of calm and humility and moves us to turn our thoughts to our own shortcomings.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

8

> *By mercy and meekness, by renunciation of self, you will disarm every enemy. For lack of firewood every fire goes out.*

> *Sinhalese Buddhist.*

Try not to hide away in dark corners the shameful memories of your sins, but, on the contrary, try to keep them always at the ready, so as to make use of them when you have to judge your neighbor.
