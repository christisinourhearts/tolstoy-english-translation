<!-- vol. 41, p. 596 -->

26 August

*Justice is realized not by striving for justice, but by love.*

1

Just as, in order to hit the target, one must shoot beyond it, so, in order to be just, one must be self-sacrificing, that is, unjust to oneself. But if you wish to be only just, you will be partial to yourself and unjust to others.

2

No man can be wholly just in all his affairs. But a just man differs from an unjust one by his effort to be just, as a truthful man differs from a liar by his effort to speak only the truth.

3

There is something worse than injustice: this is the hypocrisy of virtue, love, and service of God, so often met with in the pseudo-Christian world. People, imagining or pretending <!-- vol. 41, p. 597 --> that they are fulfilling the law of love, free themselves from the demands of justice and carry their injustice to the point of complacent villainy. People give to churches, to the poor, engage in charity, while what they give is the price of the tears and blood of their brothers.

4

A judge may be guided by justice, examining only one side of a question; but in life every question has many equally just but different solutions, depending on the side from which the question is examined.

5

> Only one thing in life is precious: constantly coming up against human falsehood and injustice, not to grow weary of being meek oneself.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

6

You suffer from injustices—take comfort: truly unhappy are not those who endure them, but those who act unjustly.

It is impossible to be exactly just. You will either do too little or do too much. To avoid sinning against justice there is one means: *always do too much*.
