<!-- vol. 41, p. 203 -->

31 March

To repent means to see the whole extent of one's viciousness, of one's weakness. Repentance is the censure of everything bad in oneself; it is the cleansing of the soul, its preparation for receiving good.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 204 -->

1

If a kind person does not acknowledge his mistakes, but always tries to justify himself, then very soon he will turn from kind into very unkind.

2

*If there is anything in you that is worthy of censure, hasten to acknowledge it yourself.*

3

> Nothing so softens the heart as the consciousness of one's guilt, and nothing so turns it to stone as the desire always to be right.

> *After the Talmud.*

4

If a person does feel in his soul that he is guilty before God, but does not admit his guilt either to other people or to himself, then such a person will always readily accuse other people, and especially those before whom he is guilty.

5

> A good person is one who remembers his sins and forgets the good he has done, and an evil one, on the contrary, is one who remembers the good he has done and forgets his sins.

> Do not forgive yourself, and then you will easily forgive others.

> *The Talmud.*

6

> He who has covered his former evil deeds with good ones shines in this gloomy world like the moon on a cloudy night.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

7

> It is good to repent of one's sins while one still has the strength.

> To repent means to cleanse one's soul and to prepare oneself for a good life, and therefore it is good to repent while the strength of life has not yet left a person. One must add oil while the wick has not yet gone out.

> *After the Talmud.*

Man's consciousness of his finiteness amid the infinite world and of his sinfulness, that is, of his not having done all that he could and should have done but did not do, has always been and always will be, as long as man is man.
