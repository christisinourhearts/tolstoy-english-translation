## FROM THE TESTAMENT OF A MEXICAN KING

Everything on earth has its limit, and the most mighty and joyful fall in their greatness and joy and are cast down into the dust. The whole globe is only a great grave, and there is nothing on its surface that would not hide itself in a grave under the earth. The waters, rivers, and streams rush toward their destination and do not return to their happy source. All hurry forward to bury themselves in the depths of the boundless ocean.

What was yesterday is no more today; and what is today will be no more tomorrow. The cemetery is full of the dust of those who were once animated by life, were kings, ruled peoples, presided over assemblies, led armies, conquered new countries, demanded worship for themselves, swelled with vanity, pomp, and power.

But glory has passed like the black smoke that comes out of a volcano, and has left nothing but a mention on the chronicler's page.

The great, the wise, the brave, the beautiful—alas!—where are they now? They are all mixed with the clay, and what befell them will befall us too; it will befall those also who will come after us.

But take courage, all of you—both famous leaders, and true friends, and faithful subjects—let us strive toward that heaven where everything is eternal and where there is neither decay nor destruction.

Darkness is the cradle of the sun, and the brilliance of the stars needs the gloom of nights.

*Tetzcuco Nezahual Coyotl.*

*(About 1460 B.C.).*
