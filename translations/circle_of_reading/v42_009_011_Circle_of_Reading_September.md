## WHY DO MEN STUPEFY THEMSELVES?

In the period of conscious life a person can often notice in himself two separate beings: one—blind, sensual, and the other—sighted, spiritual. The blind, animal being eats, drinks, rests, sleeps, propagates, and moves as a wound-up machine moves; the sighted, spiritual being, bound up with the animal one, does nothing itself, but only evaluates the activity of the animal being by coinciding with it when it approves of this activity and diverging from it when it does not approve of it.

This sighted being can be compared to the needle of a compass, which points with one end to the north and with the other to the opposite south, and which is covered by a plate along its line of attraction. And the needle cannot be seen from under the plate so long as whatever carries the compass moves in the direction of the needle. But as soon as whatever carries the compass turns aside from the direction pointed out by the needle, the needle comes out from under the plate and becomes visible.

In just the same way the sighted, spiritual being, whose manifestation we call in common speech conscience, always points with one end to good and with the other, opposite end, to evil, and cannot be seen by us so long as we do not turn aside from the direction it gives, that is, from good to evil. But one has only to commit an act contrary to the direction of conscience, and there appears a consciousness of the spiritual being, pointing out the deviation of the animal activity from the direction pointed out by conscience. And just as a <!-- vol. 42, p. 10 --> seafarer could not go on working with oars, engine, or sail, knowing that he is going not where he needs to go, until he had given his movement a direction corresponding to the compass needle or had hidden its deviation from himself, so exactly every person, having felt the split between his conscience and his animal activity, cannot continue this activity until he either brings it into agreement with conscience or hides from himself the indications of conscience about the wrongness of the animal life.

The whole of human life, one may say, consists only of these two activities: 1) bringing one's activity into agreement with conscience, and 2) hiding from oneself the indications of one's conscience so as to be able to go on living.

Some do the first, others the second. For attaining the first there is only one way: moral enlightenment—the increase of light in oneself and attention to what it illuminates; for the second—for hiding from oneself the indications of conscience—there are two ways: external and internal. The external way consists in occupations that divert attention from the indications of conscience; the internal consists in darkening conscience itself.

Just as a person can hide from his sight an object that is in front of him in two ways: by external diversion of his sight to other, more striking objects, and by fouling his eyes, so exactly a person can hide from himself the indications of his conscience in a twofold way: externally, by diverting his attention with all kinds of occupations, cares, amusements, games, and internally, by fouling the very organ of attention. For people with a dull, limited moral sense, external distractions are often quite enough for them not to see the indications of conscience about the wrongness of life. But for morally sensitive people these means are often not enough.

External ways do not entirely divert attention from the consciousness of the discord between life and the demands of conscience; this consciousness hinders living, and people, in order to be able to live, resort to the sure internal way of darkening conscience itself, which consists in poisoning the brain with stupefying substances.

Life is not what it ought to be according to the demands of conscience. To turn life in accordance with these demands—there is not the strength.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 11 --> Diversions that would distract from the consciousness of this discord are insufficient, or they have palled. And so, in order to be able to go on living in spite of the indications of conscience about the wrongness of life, people poison, stopping its activity for a time, the organ through which the indications of conscience are manifested, just as a person who had deliberately fouled his eyes would hide from himself what he did not wish to see.

*L. N. Tolstoy.*
