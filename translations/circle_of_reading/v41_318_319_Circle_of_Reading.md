<!-- vol. 41, p. 318 -->

11 May

Perfection is so far from us that, however different our lives, our distance from perfection is the same for all of us.

1

> Whoever has no idea of perfection is content with what is, does not argue with reality, which becomes for him identical with justice, with the good, with beauty. For such a person there is no movement, no life.

> *After Amiel.*

2

> The driving force in all perfecting, both of personalities and of peoples, is not knowledge of what is, but the idea of what can be.

> *Martineau.*

3

“Man is weak, one must give him a task within his strength,” people say. It is the same as saying: my hands are weak, and I cannot draw a line that would be the shortest between two points; therefore, to make it easier for myself, wishing to draw a straight line, I will take as my model a curved or a broken one.

The weaker my hand, the more perfect a model I need.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 319 -->

4

*“Be perfect, like your heavenly father.”*

*The perfection of God, that is, the perfection of the highest good of all, is that toward which all humanity strives.*

5

The Christian teaching of perfection is the one teaching that can guide humanity.

One cannot, one must not replace the idea of perfection given in Christ's teaching with external rules, but one must hold this idea firmly before oneself in all its purity and, above all, believe in it.

To someone swimming not far from the shore one can say: keep to that rise, cape, shore, and the like. But when the swimmers have moved away from the shore, only the unattainable heavenly bodies and the compass showing the direction must and can serve them as guidance. Both the one and the other are given to us.

However low a person may fall, he can always see that perfection toward which he can go.
