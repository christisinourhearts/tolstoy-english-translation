<!-- vol. 42, p. 286 -->

26 November

As one candle lights another and thousands are lit from one candle, so one heart lights another, and thousands are lit.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 287 -->

1

> Beware of people who dissuade you from striving toward good on the ground that perfection is unattainable.

> Never consider it useless to submit to an influence that can awaken noble feelings in you.

> *John Ruskin.*

2

It is better to believe in the most remote and impossible good than to believe that even the smallest evil is inherent in people.

3

The reading of good books is a good suggestion; good art is the same kind of suggestion of good; prayer is self-suggestion of good; but the most powerful suggestion of good is the example of a good life. It is because of this that the good life of people becomes a good—not only for those who live it, but for all those who see, know, and later learn about such a life.

4

How often one has occasion to see that a person who is kind, intelligent, truthful, knowing the unlawfulness, the criminality of the deed he is doing—as, for example, war, meat-eating, owning land on which he does not work, the criminal court, and others—calmly goes on doing the deed he recognizes as bad. What is the cause of this astonishing phenomenon? It comes from the fact that such a person acts under the influence of suggestion, which is stronger than the demands of his conscience and reason. One can often see either how suggestion takes possession of a person more and more and he begins to do deeds contrary to his conscience, or how such suggestion gradually weakens, the demands of reason grow stronger, wavering begins, and, finally, reason gains the victory.

5

> If you want to convince a person that he lives badly, live well; but do not convince him with words. People believe what they see.

> *Thoreau.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 288 -->

6

> A person does not err alone. In erring, everyone spreads his error among those around him.

> *Seneca.*

7

*It is hard to lead to good by moral teachings, easy by example.*

*Seneca.*

Not only fear society that is harmful to the soul and avoid it, but also value good companionship and seek it.
