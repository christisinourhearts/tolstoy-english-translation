<!-- vol. 41, p. 583 -->

20 August

All people occupied with a truly important work are always simple, because they have no time to invent anything superfluous.

1

> Every need subsides, and every vice grows, from being satisfied.

> *Amiel.*

2

> Every new desire is the beginning of a new want, the germ of a new sorrow.

> *Voltaire.*

3

> The slave of his passions is the lowest of slaves.

> *The Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 584 -->

4

> The more you surround yourself with needs, the more you subject yourself to slavery: for the more things you need, the more you will curtail your freedom. Perfect freedom consists in needing nothing at all, and the next after it, in needing little.

> *John Chrysostom.*

5

> Enjoyment, luxury—that is what you call happiness; but I think that to desire nothing is the bliss of the gods, and therefore to need only a little is an approach to this highest happiness.

> *Socrates.*

6

> We must live not for our body, but reckoning with it as if against our will. Epicurus says: “If you live according to nature, you will never be poor; but if you live according to generally accepted conventional customs, you will never be rich. Nature requires little; the prevailing customs require excess.”

> *Seneca.*

7

> Limit yourselves to plants if you want to eat satisfyingly and healthily.

> *John Chrysostom.*

*Moderation has this great merit, that only with a moderate life could all people live without want and envy.*
