<!-- vol. 41, p. 461 -->

1 July

The soul of man is divine.

1

> *Every truth has God as its source. When it manifests itself in a man, this does not show that it proceeds from the man, but only that the man has the property of such transparency that he can manifest it.*

> *Pascal.*

2

> When rainwater flows along gutters, it seems to us that it flows out of them, whereas in reality it falls from the sky. It is the same with the holy teachings that divine people utter to us. It seems that they proceed from them, but in reality they proceed from God.

> *Ramakrishna.*

3

To place one's spiritual powers outside of dependence on the divine power is, according to the teaching of Laozi, the same as believing that a bellows is not an instrument that merely lets air pass through itself, but an independent source that produces it out of itself, and that a bellows could blow even in airless space.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 462 -->

4

> I feel with particular force that man, in all that he does or can do that is beautiful, great, and good, is only the instrument of something or someone higher than himself. This feeling is faith. The believing man is present with the trembling of sacred joy at these miracles that take place in him, accomplished *through him, and not from him*. He places his will at their disposal, trying reverently to efface himself, so as to distort as little as possible the higher work of this power, which for a time makes use of him to accomplish its work. He becomes impersonal and is annihilated; he feels that his “I” must disappear when the holy spirit speaks, when God acts. So the prophet hears the call, so the young mother feels the fruit moving in her womb. As long as we feel our “I,” we are limited, selfish, captives: but when, in harmony with the life of the world, we respond to the voice of God, our “I” disappears.

> *Amiel.*

5

> If only for a single moment we renounce our little “I,” do not plot evil, are only a clean glass reflecting the rays—what will we not reflect! The whole universe will unfold in radiant splendor around us.

> *Thoreau.*

6

> True wisdom teaches us that the foundations of every person's thoughts live in his humbler brothers, and that the qualities the learned man displays in his deepest discoveries are exactly the same as those the ordinary person uses in his everyday labors of life.

> The true understanding of great men consists in this, that they are only examples and manifestations of our common nature, showing what is proper to all souls, although it is as yet disclosed only in a few. The light that proceeds from them is nothing other than a faint revelation of the power that lies hidden <!-- vol. 41, p. 463 --> in every human being. They are not a marvel, not a miracle, but the natural development of the human soul.

> *From “The World's Advance Thought.”*

7

> The true work of man on earth is to keep his being in harmony with the eternal; only then can the almighty power of love and reason pour out through him, as through a clean channel.

> *From “The World's Advance Thought.”*

8

Life is given to us as a child is given to a nurse, to bring it up. The parable of the talents says this very thing (Matthew 24:14–30).

Keep yourself clean of evil, so that the power of God can pass through you. In this passing through of the power of God is the great good.
