<!-- vol. 41, p. 531 -->

27 July

Knowledge is a tool, not a goal.

1

People know little because they strive to know either what is not given to their understanding, what is inaccessible to them: God, eternity, spirit; or what is not worth thinking about: how water freezes, the theory of numbers, what bacteria are in what disease, and the like.

The path of true knowledge is one: one needs to know only how to live.

2

> To take out a splinter that has stuck into the foot, people take another and with its help take out the splinter; and when this is done, they throw away both the one and the other. In just the same way the mind is needed only in order to remove the madness that darkens the sight of the divine *I*, but the mind in itself does not constitute merit. It is only a tool.

> *Brahmanic wisdom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 532 -->

3

> — Oh, how unhappy I am! I wanted to occupy myself with reading a beautiful and most useful book, and now, instead of that, I must, if you please, carry out the requests of this tiresome person.

> — But does your duty, — I will answer you, — consist precisely in reading books at a time when help is asked of you? You must know and remember one thing: what God wants you to do now, and what he does not want. Not long ago he arranged things so that you were in solitude, so that you conversed with yourself, read, wrote, prepared yourself for good deeds. And today he has sent you people who ask you to help them in deed. By this very thing God, as it were, says:

> “Come out of your solitude and show in deed what you have learned, because the time has come for both you and people to see the use of what you have read and thought about.”

> So do not fall flat on your face; do not complain of people for having interrupted your occupation: for if there were no people, whom would you serve, and what use would it be to read books about how better to serve people?

> *Epictetus.*

4

> Science should be used for strengthening religion, and not for acquiring wealth.

> *The “Gulistan” of Saadi.*

5

> Whoever acquires knowledge but does not make use of it is like one who plows but does not sow.

> *The “Gulistan” of Saadi.*

6

People who think that the main business in life lies in knowledge are like those moths that fly to candles—they perish themselves and darken the light.

7

> The word *learned man* contains only the notion that someone has studied a great deal of something, but this does not yet mean that he has learned anything.

> *Lichtenberg.*

*The aim of life is the fulfillment of the law of God, and not the acquisition of knowledge.*
