<!-- vol. 41, p. 436 -->

24 June

Remembering death teaches a person to choose, from the works before him, those that are always finished. And these works are the most necessary.

1

> It is said that in man the desire to preserve his life is especially strong. This is true. But a large part of this desire has been fostered by people. Man by his nature cares for the preservation of his life only to the extent that he has the means for it. As soon as he feels himself deprived of these means, he grows calm and ceases to torment himself uselessly. The means of submission is given to us by nature itself. Savages, like animals, do not fight off death and bear it without complaint. But when this means has been lost, another is established, which comes from reason, but few make use of it.

> *Rousseau.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 437 -->

2

> How soon you will have to die! And still you cannot free yourself from pretense and passions, cannot give up the prejudice of thinking that worldly external things can harm a person, cannot become meek with everyone.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> *A reasonable man thinks more of life than of death.*

> *Spinoza.*

4

For the spirit there is no death, and therefore a person who lives a spiritual life is free from death.

5

> If you want to accustom yourself to think of death without fear, then try to look closely at and vividly enter into the position of those people who clung to life with all their strength. It seemed to them that death had overtaken them before their time. Meanwhile the longest-lived, who had buried many, nevertheless died at last. How short this interval of time is, how much grief and evil it holds, and how fragile is the vessel of life!

> Is it worth talking about this instant! Think—behind you is eternity, ahead too is eternity. Between these two abysses, what difference can it make to you—whether you live three days or three centuries.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

6

> Clutter hinders freedom, and clutter comes from putting things off. To know how to be ready means to know how to finish. Nothing is done that is not finished. The tasks we leave behind us will afterward rise up before us again and obstruct our way. Let each of our days deal with what concerns it, clear up its tasks, let it spare the following day, and then we will always be ready. To know how to be ready means, in essence, to know how to die.

> *Amiel.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 438 -->

7

People often say: “It's of no use to me now; it's time for me to die.” Everything that is of no use because it is time to die was of no use to do at any time. But there is a work that is always needed, and the nearer to death, the more needed—the work of the soul: to grow, to bring up the soul.

8

Whenever you are deciding the question: should I act this way or that?—ask yourself how you would act if you knew that you would die by evening, and that, moreover, no one would ever learn how you had acted.

Death teaches people the skill of finishing their works. And of all works there is only one kind that is always completely finished—works of love that seeks no reward.
