<!-- vol. 42, p. 139 -->

12 October

*Every departure from accepted customs requires great effort. And yet the first step toward perfecting is always connected with such a departure.*

1

> I must act as I think, and not as people think. This rule is equally necessary in practical and in spiritual life. This rule is hard, because you will always find people who think that they know your duties better than you yourself. In the world it is easy to live according to the world's opinion, and in solitude it is easy to follow one's own; but great is the man who in the midst of the crowd keeps the independence of his solitude.

> *Emerson.*

2

> Accommodating yourself to customs that in essence are no concern of yours—this is what your powers are spent on, what deprives you of leisure and wipes out all the originality of your nature. When you are occupied with such business, how hard it is to recognize what you really are, not to speak of wasting your best abilities on trifles. Such a life ruins soul and body.

> *Emerson.*

3

> Society says to a man: “Think as we think; believe as we believe; eat and drink as we eat and drink; dress as we dress—or be damned.” And if anyone will not submit to it, it will turn his life into hell with its mockery, gossip, abuse, boycott, and ostracism. But take courage.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

A person who, at the demand of his conscience, departs from the established customs of the milieu in which he lives must <!-- vol. 42, p. 140 --> be very strict and attentive toward himself: his every mistake, every weakness will be held against him, and above all his departure from a decision he has once made.

5

> When we, living virtuously, are subjected to persecution by evil people, and for our attachment to virtue endure ridicule from them, let us not be sad and grieve. Such is the property of virtue, that it usually arouses hatred toward itself in evil people. Envying those who want to live righteously, and thinking to prepare a justification for themselves if they blacken the glory of others, evil people hate the good as those who go the opposite way, and try in every way to bring dishonor on their life. But let us not be distressed, because the hatred of the evil serves as a sign of virtue.

> *John Chrysostom.*

It is bad to irritate people by departing from the customs they have accepted, but it is still worse to depart from the demands of conscience and reason, humoring people's customs.
