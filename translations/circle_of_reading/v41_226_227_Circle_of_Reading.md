<!-- vol. 41, p. 226 -->

4 April

Life must and can be an unceasing joy.

1

Life here is not a vale of tears, not a place of trial, but something than which we can imagine nothing better. The joy of this life is endless, if only we use it for that for which it is given to us.

2

A person's unkind disposition toward other people makes him unhappy and poisons life for others. But a loving mood, like oil, greases the wheels of life and makes it easy and pleasant both for him and for all those with whom he associates.

3

> People for the most part treat their pleasures in such a way that they are grieved if they lose them. But only that person is right who knows how to rejoice and at the same time not to grieve when the cause of his joy passes.

> *Pascal.*

4

> *Try: perhaps you will manage to live as a person content with his fate, who has gained inner peace by love and good deeds.*

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

5

> The chief secret of preserving cheerfulness is not to allow trifles to disturb us and at the same time to value those small pleasures that fall to our lot.

> *Smiles.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 227 -->

6

> Never seek pleasure, but always be ready to find pleasure in everything. If your hands are busy and your heart is free, then the most insignificant thing will give you pleasure, and you will find a share of what is interesting and pleasant in everything you hear. But if you make pleasure the aim of your life, a day will come when the most comic scenes will not call forth true laughter from you.

> *John Ruskin.*

7

A truly wise person is always cheerful.

The chief means of living joyfully is to believe that life is given for joy. If joy comes to an end, look for where you went wrong.
