<!-- vol. 42, p. 44 -->

15 September

The chief obstacle to the knowledge of truth is not falsehood but the semblance of truth.

1

> If in real life an illusion distorts reality only for a moment, in the abstract realm error can reign for whole millennia, can lay its iron yoke on whole nations, stifle the noblest impulses of humanity, and, with the help of its slaves whom it has deceived, put in chains the one it could not deceive. It is the enemy with which <!-- vol. 42, p. 45 --> the wisest minds of all times have waged an unequal struggle, and only what they won from it has become the possession of humanity. If it is said that truth must be sought even where no use is foreseen from it, because its use may turn up and reveal itself where it was not expected, then it must be added that with the same zeal one must search out and root out every error even where no harm is foreseen from it, because the harm of errors can easily turn up and someday reveal itself where it was not expected—for every error hides a poison in itself. If truth and knowledge have made man the master of the earth, then there are no harmless errors, still less—honorable and sacred ones.

> As a consolation to those who devote their life and powers to the noble and hard struggle with errors of whatever kind, it can boldly be said that, although until truth appears error will do its work, like owls and bats—at night, yet sooner will owls and bats frighten the sun and drive it back to where it rose from than the old error will displace a truth that has been recognized and clearly and fully expressed and occupy its free place unhindered. Such is the power of truth; its victory is difficult and hard, but then, once it is won, it can no longer be turned back.

> *Schopenhauer.*

2

*An exposed lie is as important an acquisition for the good of humanity as a clearly expressed truth.*

3

> To free a person from errors—that means to give him something, not to take something away. Liberation from a lie is the preaching of truth; the knowledge that what is passed off as truth is a lie—is truth. Error always does harm. Sooner or later it will do harm to the one who acknowledges it as truth.

> *Schopenhauer.*

The forward movement of humanity in the field of knowledge consists in removing the veils that cover the truth.
