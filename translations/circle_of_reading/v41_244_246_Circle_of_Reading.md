<!-- vol. 41, p. 244 -->

14 April

There cannot be good order in a society divided into the rich—who rule—and the poor—who obey.

1

> It must be confessed that we, with our worship of Mammon, have arrived at strange conclusions. We say that we live in society, and meanwhile we openly preach the most complete separation and extreme isolation. Our life presents not a picture of mutual help but an arena of mutual hostility, masked by strictly exact laws of war under the name of fair competition and the like. We have quite forgotten that relations between people do not all come down to payment in cash. “What do I care about the workers dying of hunger? — says the rich manufacturer. — Did I not openly hire them in the market and pay them to the last penny all that was due to them by agreement? What more have I to do with them?” Yes, the worship of Mammon is a very sad faith. When Cain killed his brother Abel for his own profit and he was asked “Where is your brother?”—he too answered: “Am I my brother's keeper?” The manufacturer says the same: “Did I not give my brother his pay—all that he earned?”

> *Carlyle.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 245 -->

2

> Since a person can live only from the land and on the land, then, by making the land on which he must exist the property of another, we enslave him just as fully as by making his flesh and blood the property of another. And in the end, at a certain stage of social development, the slavery that flows from the seizure of land, by virtue of the fact that the relations between master and slave are less direct and obvious under it, becomes more cruel and more corrupting than the slavery that makes the bodies of people property.

> *Henry George.*

3

> How many means of being happy, how many comforts of which our ancestors had no idea! And what then, are we happy? If a small number are happier, then the majority are all the more unhappy. By increasing the means of life for a small number of the rich, we have forced the majority to be and to consider themselves unhappy. What kind of happiness can there be that is acquired to the detriment of the happiness of others!

> *Rousseau.*

4

> Suppose that I have saved a drowning man, but before that I bargained out of him the greater part of his own property. Here, obviously, is service for service. He values his life more than his property. But what is to be said of such an agreement? And yet this is how the property of people is taken away, because millions of people own either the very least or next to nothing, and for their labors, that is, for their property, they are given the means of subsistence.

> *Salter.*

5

> The tramp is a necessary complement to the millionaire.

> *Henry George.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 246 -->

6

You cannot found the brotherhood of Christ where, on the one hand, ignorance, poverty, slavery, and depravity, and on the other hand, culture, wealth, and power hinder mutual respect and love for one another.

*Giuseppe Mazzini.*

To be an oppressor-master is worse than to be a submissive slave. Do not be burdened by poverty, be burdened by excess.

*If you receive an income without having earned it, then someone else earns it without receiving it.*

*Maimonides.*
