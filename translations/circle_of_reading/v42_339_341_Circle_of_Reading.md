<!-- vol. 42, p. 339 -->

13 December

Faith is faith only when the deeds of life agree with it and in no case contradict it.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 340 -->

1

> What good is it, my brothers, if someone says that he has faith but has no deeds? Can this faith save him? If a brother or sister is naked and has no daily food, and one of you says to them: go in peace, be warm and be fed, but does not give them what the body needs: what good is it? So also faith, if it has no deeds, is dead in itself. But someone will say: you have faith, and I have deeds; show me your faith without your deeds, and I will show you my faith by my deeds. For as the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without deeds is dead.

> *James 2:14–18, 26.*

2

> Whoever loves Christianity more than truth will very soon love his church or sect more than Christianity and will inevitably end by loving only himself, more than anything in the world.

> *Coleridge.*

3

> *In essence, there is only one means of honoring God—it is the fulfillment of one's duties and conduct in accordance with the laws of reason.*

> *Lichtenberg.*

4

> Religious exercises for human glory or for an outward appearance of holiness have no value and come from the lower demands of the soul. Repentance and self-torment or the torment of others come from false teachings. Repentance for the body is chastity. Repentance for speech is always to speak the truth, and with kindness. Repentance for thought is to master oneself, to purify the soul, and to be disposed to good.

> *Mahabharata.*

5

> Act in the daytime so that at night your sleep may be peaceful, and in youth so that your old age may be peaceful.

> *Indian proverb.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 341 -->

6

He whose faith is weak cannot awaken faith in others either.

*Laozi.*

7

> He who has religion in second place has none at all. God is compatible with much in a person's heart, but one thing is incompatible: that he should be in the heart in second place. He who assigns him a secondary place assigns him none.

> *John Ruskin.*

8

The final aim of the life of man and of the whole world cannot be understood by him, just as neither the form nor the purpose of the building being erected can be understood by a workman who delivers the building materials for its construction. But a person can know, and does know, that what he takes part in creating is something reasonable, beautiful, and needed by him and by the whole world. In this is faith.

Do not believe words, either your own or other people's; believe only deeds, both your own and other people's.
