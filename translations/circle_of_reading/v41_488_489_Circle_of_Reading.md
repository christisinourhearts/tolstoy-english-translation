<!-- vol. 41, p. 488 -->

10 July

In our world true faith has for the most part been replaced by public opinion: people believe not in God, but in what people teach.

1

> The chief and most usual way of denying the existence of God consists in always recognizing public opinion as unconditionally right and attaching no significance to one's own consciousness of God.

> *John Ruskin.*

2

> God offers every soul a choice between truth and peace. Choose one of the two. You cannot have both. Like a pendulum, a man swings between the one and the other. He in whom love of peace prevails will accept the first faith, philosophy, political party that he comes upon—most often the one his father professed—and he will receive peace, comfort, and public respect, but he will shut the door on truth.

> *Emerson.*

3

The unhappiness and evil of people come not so much from people's not knowing their duties as from their recognizing as their duties what is not their duty.

4

> The church, the state, society have certain typical forms into which the thought of the young is cast. And when the time comes in which the particular features of the new generation ought to have shown themselves, it turns out that its thought has already ossified in these forms and cannot take in anything new.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 489 -->

5

*Faith is not established by a majority of votes. He who sees in a majority of votes a sign of the truth of a faith does not know what faith is.*

6

> Every society that starts from the proposition that “there is no God” arrives at certain unexpected results. Since the world order appears to such a society as a series of accidents and endless deceptions, then any individual accidents or deceptions cannot, of course, surprise anyone any longer. And therefore all those horrors that are committed in our life no longer strike or surprise anyone. All this is quite in the order of things.

> *Carlyle.*

7

The cause of the calamitous situation into which our society has fallen lies in the fact that people of the higher classes live without any faith, trying to make up for the absence of faith, some—with hypocrisy, pretending that they still believe in external religious forms, others—with a bold proclamation of their unbelief, still others—with refined skepticism, a fourth group—with the recognition of the legitimacy of egoism and its elevation into a religious teaching.

The cause of the sickness is the non-acceptance of Christ's teaching in its true, that is, full, meaning. Healing from the sickness lies in one thing only—in the recognition of this teaching in all its meaning. And this recognition in our time is not only possible but also necessary.

The fundamental cause of the evil from which people now suffer is that most people of our time have no faith at all.
