<!-- vol. 41, p. 48 -->

21 January

The more reason grows stronger in a person and the passions grow quiet, the more the spiritual life of love for God and one's neighbor is freed within him. Happy is the person who consciously helps this happen.

1

If we saw a person who, instead of covering the roof of his house and putting in windows, every time rain and wind came would go outside and, standing in the wind and rain, become angry at the clouds and shout at them, ordering one to go right and another left, then surely, seeing such a person, we would say that he was mad. And yet we all do exactly this when we become angry at the evil people do, scolding people instead of taking care to root out the evil in ourselves. And yet to rid ourselves of evil—to cover our own roof and put in our own windows—is in our power, while to root evil out of the world is as little in our power as to command the clouds. If people, instead of teaching others, would at least sometimes occupy themselves with teaching themselves, there would be less and less evil in the world and it would become easier and easier for people to live.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 49 -->

2

> Do not let mistakes and false methods trouble you. Nothing teaches so much as awareness of one's own mistake. This is one of the chief means of self-education.
>
> *Carlyle.*

3

> Guard your heart from concerns that are foreign to you; do not interfere in what does not concern you; try instead to correct yourself and advance along the path toward perfection.
>
> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

4

> Our lives form for us a moral tradition, just as the lives of our ancestors form a tradition for the family. A great deed we have performed appears to us as an incentive for our whole life to correspond to that deed.
>
> *George Eliot.*

5

Never think that the small bad deed you have done is unimportant. “I did it today; I will not do it again.” That is not true: it will be hard for you to refrain from doing another time what you have done once.

Never say about a good deed: it is not worth making the effort; it is so easy that whenever I want to I can always do it. Do not think or say this. Even the smallest good deed always gives strength for a good life, just as a bad deed diminishes that strength.

6

A ripe apple fell from an old tree beside a young apple tree. And the young apple tree said to the apple: “Hello, little apple. I wish you would rot as soon as possible so that you may become like me.” — “Rot yourself, if you like it, you ignoramus,” said the apple. “Can't you see how rosy, fine, firm, and juicy I am? I don't want to rot; I want to enjoy myself.” — “But all your beauty and your whole body are only a covering for a time; there is no life in all that. Life is only in the seed that is in you and that you yourself do not know.” — “There is no seed; it is all nonsense,” said the apple, and stopped talking.

So too think people who are not conscious that there is a spiritual life in them and live only the life of animals. But whether a person wants it or not, just as with the apple, the more he lives, the more what he regarded as his life weakens and disappears in him, and the more clearly the true, growing, undying life is revealed. Is it not better, then, from the very beginning to live not by the life that dies, but by the life that grows without ceasing and is not destroyed?

<!-- vol. 41, p. 50 -->

*It seems to us that the most important work in the world*—*work on something visible: building a house*, *plowing a field*, *feeding livestock*, *gathering fruit*—*is the real work*, *while work on one's soul, on something invisible*—*is an unimportant matter*, *something one may do or may not do. Yet this one work alone*, *work on the soul*, *on becoming better and kinder with every day*, *this work alone is real*, *and all other visible kinds of work are useful only when this chief work on the soul is being done.*
