23 May

The less we have accustomed ourselves to, the fewer privations threaten us.

1

> Abstinence does not mean the suppression of strength, does not mean a halt in good, in the manifestation of love or faith, but, on the contrary, a manifestation of spiritual strength that hinders doing what a person considers bad.

> *John Ruskin.*

2

> As smoke drives bees out of a hive, so gluttony drives out spiritual gifts and the perfections of the mind.

> *Basil the Great.*

3

> It is a great good to have what we desire; a still greater good is to desire nothing but what we have.

> *Menedemus.*

4

> The moth flies into the flame of the lamp, not knowing the pain of the burn; and the fish swallows the worm on the hook, not knowing the danger; but we do not part with sensual pleasures, although we know perfectly <!-- vol. 41, p. 342 --> well that they are entangled in a net of calamities. Such is the bottomless depth of folly.

> *Hindu proverb.*

5

> Our desires are just like little children, who are always restless and always ask their mother for now this, now that, and are never satisfied with anything. The more one gives in to them, the more importunate they become.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

6

> Who is wise?—He who learns something from everyone.

> Who is strong?—He who restrains himself.

> Who is rich?—He who is content with his lot.

> *The Talmud.*

7

> What a person has renounced cannot cause him suffering. He who has destroyed in himself the pride that says “I,” “mine,” has passed into the higher world.

> *Indian Kural.*

8

The more you hurry, the less you get done.

9

No one has ever regretted having eaten too little.

10

*Nature requires little, imagination—much.*

11

> From pleasure arises sorrow, from pleasure arises fear; whoever is free from pleasures, for him there is no longer either sorrow or fear.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 343 -->

12

> More glorious than dominion over the earth, more beautiful than ascent to the heavens, more glorious than lordship over the worlds—is the holy joy of the first steps of liberation.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

The increase of needs is not improvement, as is often thought; on the contrary: the more a person has limited his needs, the more consciousness of his human dignity there is in him, and the freer, the more courageous, and, above all, the more capable he is of serving God and people.
