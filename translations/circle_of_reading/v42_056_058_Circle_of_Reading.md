<!-- vol. 42, p. 56 -->

19 September

The harm that false faith has done and does can be neither weighed nor measured.

Faith is the establishing of a person's relation to God and the world, and the determination, flowing from this relation, of his purpose and his activity. What, then, must a person's life be if this relation, and the determination of purpose flowing from it, are false?

1

> *However true it is that religious unbelief and contempt for the divine are a great evil, superstition is a still worse one.*

> *Plutarch.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 57 -->

2

> Ask most Christians what is the chief evil from which Christ delivered humanity, and they will say: from hell, from eternal fire, from punishment in the future. In accordance with this they think that salvation is something that another can accomplish for them. People flee from an outward hell, when in reality they carry within themselves the hell that they ought most of all to fear. The salvation most needed by a person, the one that gives a person liberation, is salvation from the evil in his own soul. There is something much worse than outward punishment. It is the state of a soul that has rebelled against God, the state of a soul endowed with divine power but giving itself up to the power of animal lusts—a soul that, living in the sight of God, fears the threat or the anger of man and prefers human glory to its own calm consciousness of virtue. There is no perdition worse than this.

> It is this very state of the soul, which the unrepentant person carries away with him into the grave, it is this that must be feared.

> To be saved, in the real meaning of this word, means to raise up the fallen spirit, to heal the sick soul, to restore to it freedom of thought, of conscience, of love. Only in this state is that salvation which Christ taught.

> All the true teaching of Christianity is directed toward such salvation.

> *Channing.*

3

> “To lose one's soul” does not mean to be doomed to an endless church hell, but means to get lost in the thicket of the passions and, having lost one's way, to turn round and round in the narrow circle of selfish thoughts, as one who has lost his way in a forest circles in one place.

> *From “The World's Advance Thought.”*

4

> Definite relations had been established, thank God, with churchliness. A dividing wall had been put up between it and philosophy, so that churchliness and philosophy could each go its own way without hindering each other. And what are they doing <!-- vol. 42, p. 58 --> now? They are breaking through the wall that divides them and, under the pretext of making us rational Christians, are making us the most irrational philosophers.

> *Lessing.*

5

People live badly only because they believe in a lie and not in the truth.

6

> The government of churches by a hierarchy may be monarchical, aristocratic, or democratic; this concerns only the internal structure. The church itself, under all forms, always remains despotic. Where the statutes of faith are considered the fundamental law, there the clergy reigns, which considers itself entitled to need neither reason nor science, because it is the sole guardian, holding power, and interpreter of the will of the invisible lawgiver, and, having power, it can, instead of persuading, prescribe.

> *Kant.*

It is not enough to throw off false faith, that is, a false relation to the world. One must also establish the true one.
