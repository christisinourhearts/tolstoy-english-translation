<!-- vol. 41, p. 126 -->

25 February

*To pray means to acknowledge and remember the laws of the eternal and infinite being of God and to measure one's past and future actions against them. It is useful to do this as often as possible.*

1

> Before setting about prayer, test yourself whether you are able to collect your thoughts; otherwise do not pray.

> Whoever makes a habit of his prayer, his prayer is not sincere.

> *The Talmud.*

2

> Why deprive ourselves of prayer, this remedy against our weakness? All the strivings of the soul that bring us near to God free us from the thought of ourselves. Asking God for help, we learn to find this help in ourselves. It is not he who changes us, but we change ourselves, drawing near to him. All that we ask of him as our due, we give to ourselves.

> *Rousseau.*

3

> And when you pray, do not be like the hypocrites, who love to stop and pray in the synagogues and on the corners of the streets, in order to show themselves before people. Truly I say to you that they are already receiving their reward.

> But you, when you pray, go into your room and, having shut your door, pray to your father, who is in secret; and your father, who sees what is secret, will repay you.

> And when praying, do not say anything superfluous, like the pagans, for they think that in their many words they will be heard.

> Do not be like them, for your father knows what you have need of before you ask him.

> *Matthew 6:5–8.*

4

Since ancient times it has been recognized that prayer is necessary for a person.

For people of former times prayer was—and for most people it still remains—an appeal, under certain <!-- vol. 41, p. 127 --> conditions, in certain places, with certain actions and words—to God or to gods, in order to propitiate them.

The Christian teaching knows no such prayers, but teaches that prayer is necessary not as a means of deliverance from worldly calamities and of acquiring worldly goods, but as a means of strengthening a person in the struggle with sins.

5

Prayer consists in this: having renounced everything worldly, everything that can distract my senses (the Muslims do excellently when, entering a mosque or beginning to pray, they close their eyes and ears with their fingers), to call forth in oneself the divine source. Best of all for this is what Christ teaches: to go alone into the storeroom and shut oneself in, that is, to pray in complete solitude, whether it be in a storeroom, in the forest, or in the field. Prayer is this: having renounced everything worldly, external, to call forth in oneself the divine part of one's soul, to carry oneself over into it, by means of it to enter into communion with him of whom it is a particle, to become conscious of oneself as a slave of God, and to check one's soul, one's actions, one's desires by the demands not of the external conditions of the world, but of this divine part of the soul.

And such prayer is not the idle tender emotion and excitement produced by public prayers with their singing, pictures, lights, and sermons, but such prayer is help, strengthening, an uplifting of the soul. Such prayer is a confession, a check on past actions and an indication of the direction of future ones.

It is good to renew one's prayer, that is, the expression of one's relation to God. A person constantly grows, changes, and therefore his relation to God too must change and become clearer. Prayer too must change.
