15 October

The purpose of man is the preserving of his soul. And the preserving of one's soul is its growth, its enlargement. This enlargement is accomplished by love.

1

“For I came down from heaven not to do my own will, but the will of the father who sent me; and the will of the father who sent me is this, that of what he has given me I should lose nothing,” it is said in John (6:38–39), that is, to preserve, to grow in oneself, to bring to the highest possible degree of divinity that spark of spiritual life which has been given, entrusted to me, as a child to a nanny. What, then, is needed in order to fulfill this? Not the satisfaction of lust, not human glory, but labor, struggle, deprivations, sufferings, humiliations, persecutions, the very thing that is spoken of many times in the Gospel. And this very thing, what we need, is sent to us in the most varied forms, both in small and in large measure. If only we knew how to accept it as we should, as work that we need and therefore joyful work, and not as something vexing that disturbs our animal existence, which we consider life.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 152 -->

2

> The deeds of the righteous are seeds that sometimes lie motionless for a long time in the soil of history, but, having received warmth and moisture, having absorbed new, healthy juices, fresh strength, these seeds begin to sprout, to bloom, and to bear fruit; while what is sown by violence and untruth rots, withers, and disappears without a trace.

> *The Talmud.*

3

> *Man is born to be a remaker of what man has made, an exposer of deceptions, a restorer of truth and good, imitating that great nature which embraces us all and which does not sleep for a minute on the old past, but every hour corrects itself, giving us every morning a new day and with every hour a new life.*

> *Emerson.*

4

The purpose of a person's life is both personal perfecting and service to that work which is accomplished by the whole life of the world.

As long as there is life in a person, he can perfect himself and serve the world. But he can serve the world only by perfecting himself, and perfect himself only by serving the world.

5

To perfect oneself means to transfer one's *I* more and more from the bodily life into the spiritual life, for which there is no time, no death, and for which everything is good.

6

From a five-year-old child to me is only a step. From a newborn to a five-year-old is a terrible distance. From an embryo to a newborn—an abyss. And what separates nonexistence from the embryo is no longer an abyss but incomprehensibility.

From childhood until death, whenever it may come, the soul of man grows; more and more it becomes conscious of its spirituality, it draws nearer to God, to perfection. Whether you know it or do not know it, whether you want it or do not want it, this movement goes on. But if you know and want what God wants, life becomes free and joyful.
