<!-- vol. 42, p. 170 -->

24 October

*If the basis of the life of all of us were not one and the same, nothing could explain the feeling of compassion that we experience.*

1

> Nothing can soften our anger, even just anger, so quickly as saying to the one who is angry: “but he is unfortunate!” For what rain is to fire, compassion is to anger. Let every person who has flared up in anger against another person and has planned to cause him suffering vividly imagine that he has already done it, and now sees him suffering in spirit or in body or struggling with need and poverty, and can say to himself: this is the work of my hands. If nothing else, this can put out his anger.

> *Schopenhauer.*

2

> The straight path, or rule of conduct, that one must follow is not far from people. What is far from people, that is, not in accord with their nature, must not be taken as a rule of conduct. A carpenter hewing an ax handle has before him a model of what he is making. Taking in his hands the handle of the ax with which he is hewing, he looks at it from one side and the other, and after he has made the new handle, he examines them both to see how alike they are; so too the wise man, who has toward others the same feelings that he has <!-- vol. 42, p. 171 --> toward himself, finds the right rule of conduct. He does not do to others what he does not wish done to himself.

> *Confucius.*

3

> When you scold a person and are at enmity with him, you forget that people are your brothers, and you become an enemy to them instead of being their friend. By this you harm yourself, because when you have ceased to be the kind and sociable being that God created you, and instead have become a wild beast that creeps up on its prey, tears it apart, and destroys it—then you have lost your most precious possession. You feel the loss of a purse with money; why then do you not feel your loss when you have lost your best wealth: kindness of soul?

> *Epictetus.*

4

> *There are many people more unfortunate than you.* This saying, it is true, does not serve as a roof under which one could live, but it is enough to take shelter under from a downpour.

> *Lichtenberg.*

5

> You groan over your misfortunes. If you remembered what others experience, you would complain less of your sufferings.

> *Solon.*

True compassion begins only when, having put yourself in imagination in the place of the one who suffers, you experience real suffering.
