<!-- vol. 42, p. 185 -->

30 October

Self-love that passes certain limits is a disease of the soul. Carried to the highest degree, it shows itself in that disease of the soul which is called megalomania.

1

> People think that self-renunciation violates freedom. Such people do not know that only self-renunciation gives us true freedom, freeing us from ourselves, from the slavery of our depravity. Our passions are the cruelest tyrants; only yield to them, and you will be in the cruelest slavery, without the strength to breathe freely. Only self-renunciation delivers from this slavery.

> *Fénelon.*

2

Self-love is needed only for the preservation of the separate life of the body, and when it shows itself within these limits, it is natural and legitimate. But when reason, by its very property destined for the destruction of all separateness, is used to affirm this separateness, then self-love becomes harmful and tormenting.

3

*Complete renunciation of self is the divine life; self-love disturbed by nothing is a life lower than the animal's. The reasonable life of man is a gradual transition from animal life to divine life.*

4

> Impartiality is as rare as justice. Personal interest is an inexhaustible source of self-deceptions for self-justification. The number of people who wish to see the truth is extremely small. People are ruled by fear of the truth, unless this truth is useful to them. People of worldly philosophy <!-- vol. 42, p. 186 --> regard truth as something that may be admitted, or may not be admitted, into life. And so, in this way, the prejudice of self-love protects all the prejudices of thought that flow from this trick of egoism. The only progress desired by mankind is the increase of pleasures. Self-sacrifice—that pleasure of great souls—has never been a law of society.

> *Amiel.*

5

There are no thinkers and artists who are enjoying themselves and self-satisfied. The only indubitable sign of the presence of a calling is self-denial, is sacrifice of oneself for the manifestation of the power placed in a person for the benefit of other people. Spiritual fruit, too, is not born without torment.

To teach how many bugs there are in the world, to examine spots on the sun, to write novels and an opera—this can be done with personal aims; but to teach people their good, which is not only in renunciation of self and in service to others, and to express this teaching powerfully, is impossible without self-renunciation.

Not for nothing did Christ die on the cross; not for nothing does the sacrifice of suffering conquer everything.

The difficulty of the necessary freeing of oneself from self-love lies in the fact that self-love is a necessary condition of life. It is necessary and natural in childhood, but it must weaken and be destroyed as reason grows clear and, above all, as true love manifests itself. A child does not feel reproaches of conscience for his self-love, but as reason grows clear and love manifests itself, self-love weakens more and more, and as death approaches it must be destroyed completely.
