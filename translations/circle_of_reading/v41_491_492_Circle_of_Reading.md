<!-- vol. 41, p. 491 -->

12 July

The foundation of love is each person's consciousness of the unity of the spiritual principle that lives in all people.

1

Everything that brings union among people is good and beauty; everything that disunites them is evil and ugliness. All people know this truth. It is imprinted in our heart.

2

What terrible suffering it is to know that I am suffering and losing my life not from a mountain's collapse, not from a bacterium, but from people, from brothers who ought to love and who now hate me, if they make me suffer! This feeling is like the one a suicide must experience.

3

> There is no bad deed for which only the one who did it would be punished. We cannot isolate ourselves so that the evil that is in us does not spread. Our deeds are like our children: they live and act independently of our will.

> *George Eliot.*

4

> I am convinced to such a degree that a person does everything for his own advantage (understanding this word properly) that I believe this is as necessary for the life of the world as sensitivity is for the preservation of the life of the body. Our “first cause” has so wisely managed to bind the interests of one part with the interests of others that we cannot do ourselves true good otherwise than by doing it to our neighbor.

> *Lichtenberg.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 492 -->

5

No one alone can reach the truth; only stone by stone, with the participation of all, through millions of generations, from our forefather Adam to our time, is that temple being raised which must be a worthy dwelling for the great God.

6

> *The life of man is a self-moving circle which, from the infinitely small, spreads out on all sides into new and ever larger circles that have no end.*

> *Emerson.*

7

> Every genuine benefaction, every completely and truly disinterested help, which therefore has in view exclusively another's need, turns out, strictly speaking, when the matter is investigated to its ultimate foundations, to be a mysterious and inexplicable act, because it flows from the mysterious consciousness of the unity of all that exists and admits of no other explanation. Indeed, even to give alms, having in view, even in remote considerations, nothing other than to lessen the need that oppresses another, is possible only because the giver recognizes that what appears to him now in the guise of that wretched beggar is he himself, because he recognizes his own being as it is in itself in this other, alien phenomenon.

> *Schopenhauer.*

We are outwardly separated from and inwardly bound up with all living beings.

Some of the vibrations of the spiritual world we feel, some have not yet reached us, but they are coming, as the vibrations of light come from stars still invisible to our eye.
