<!-- vol. 42, p. 109 -->

1 October

Wisdom does not fear ignorance, does not fear doubts, does not fear labor, investigation; it fears one thing: the assertion that it knows what it does not know.

1

> One must study much in order to realize that one knows little.

> *Montaigne.*

2

> Never be ashamed to ask about what you do not know.

> Always speak the truth, even though you know that it will be unpleasant.

> He who is learned but does not apply his learning to practice is like a man who would plow but not sow.

> *Arabic (Albitis).*

3

> Whoever investigates the history of philosophy and natural science will find that the greatest discoveries were made by people who considered only probable what was accepted by everyone as certain.

> *Lichtenberg.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 110 -->

4

> *Test everything; hold to what is good.*

> *1 Thessalonians 5:21.*

5

> There is no lack of spiritual food; what is lacking is only the capacity to take it in. It is not for lack of air that a dying person cannot breathe, but for lack of the capacity to assimilate it. All the elements—physical, mental, and spiritual, which have been or ever will be in the world—are in it now as well. The business of wisdom is to understand how to master them.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

6

True wisdom is not in *knowing* what is good and what one should do, but in knowing what is best, what is less good, and therefore what must be done first and what after.

The content of wisdom is more negative than positive: to know what is unreasonable, what is unlawful, what ought not to be.
