<!-- vol. 41, p. 505 -->

16 July

Nothing so encourages idleness as empty talk. If people kept silent and did not say the trifles with which they drive away from themselves the boredom of idleness, they could not bear it.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 506 -->

1

> He who talks much seldom puts his words into action. But the wise man is always afraid that his words may exceed his deeds.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

2

> The wise do not speak empty words, fearing that their deeds may not correspond to their words.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

3

> First think, then speak! Stop before you are told: “enough.” Man is above the animal by the faculty of speech, but he is below it if he makes improper use of it.

> *The “Gulistan” of Saadi.*

4

Do not answer a madman in the spirit of his madness, so as not to be like him.

5

> He who abides in silence easily rises up to God; distraction and empty talk bring boredom and irritability in their train.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

6

*Out of a thousand times when we repent of what we have said, it will hardly happen once that we repent of having kept silent.*

7

He who has nothing to say talks more than anyone.

8

> If you wish to keep a person from some action, get him talking on the subject: the more people talk, the less inclination they have to act.

> *Carlyle.*

The less you talk, the more you will work.
