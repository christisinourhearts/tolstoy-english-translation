<!-- vol. 41, p. 298 -->

3 May

Whatever people may take their purpose and their good to be, science will be the teaching about this purpose and good.

1

> *Intelligent people study in order to know; worthless ones—in order to be known*.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

2

What is called science and art among us is the product of idle mind and feeling, having as its aim to tickle minds and feelings just as idle. Our sciences and arts are incomprehensible and say nothing to the people, because they do not have the people's good in view.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 299 -->

3

> A person lives only in order, as far as his strength and his position allow, to further his own good and that of his neighbors. To reach his final goal the sooner in this, he makes use of the experience of his predecessors. He learns.

> To study apart from this goal, simply in order to be able to *retell* everything that others have *done*, means to occupy oneself with the last of the sciences. Such a man can no more be called really learned than a catalog can be called a book. To be a man means not only to know, but also to do for future generations the same as those who went before did for us. Must I really spend my life studying the *history of the learned* only so as not to discover again what was already discovered before? People do repeat one and the same thought twice on purpose, and there is no harm in it—if only it is expressed from a new side. If you have thought for yourself, then your discovery of what was discovered earlier will still be needed.

> *Lichtenberg.*

4

> To attain moral perfection, one must first of all care for purity of soul. And purity of soul is attained only when the heart seeks truth and the will strives toward holiness. And all this depends on *true* knowledge.

> *Confucius.*

5

> If they ask you how to recognize a prophet, answer: it is he who gives me knowledge of my own heart.

> *Persian Desatir.*

6

> When people engage in learning for themselves, this learning is useful to them; but when people do it for others, in order to seem learned, this learnedness is not only useless but harmful.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 300 -->

7

People are often closer to essential truth in their superstitions than in their science.

*Thoreau*

The purpose of life of every individual person is one: perfecting in good. And therefore only that knowledge is needed which leads to this.
