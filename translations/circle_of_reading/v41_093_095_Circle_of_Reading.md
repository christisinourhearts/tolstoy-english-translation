<!-- vol. 41, p. 93 -->

10 February

The higher a person rises in his own opinion, the less secure his position; the lower he descends, the firmer his position.

1

> To be strong, one must be like water. No obstacles—it flows; a dam—it stops; the dam breaks—it flows again; in a four-cornered vessel it is four-cornered; <!-- vol. 41, p. 94 --> in a round one—it is round. Because it is so yielding, it is the most necessary of all and the strongest of all.

> *After Laozi.*

2

Humility consists in recognizing oneself as a sinner and in not counting one's good deeds to oneself as a merit.

3

> The more a person goes deep into himself, the more insignificant he seems to himself. This is the first lesson of wisdom. Let us, then, be humble in order to be wise. Let us know our weakness, and this will give us strength.

> *Channing.*

4

> Just as water does not stay on the heights but runs off into low places, so virtue is not retained by people who exalt themselves, but is retained only in humble people.

> *After the Talmud.*

5

> A wise man is grieved by his powerlessness to do the good that he desires, but is not grieved that people do not know him or judge him falsely.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

6

> In spite of the little attention to their own failings that is common to most people, there is no person who does not know something worse about himself than what he knows about his neighbor.

> *Wolsley.*

7

> The first distinguishing trait of a good and wise person consists in the awareness that he knows very little, that there are many people far more intelligent than he, and with this he always wishes to find out, to learn, and not to teach.

> Those, however, who wish to instruct or to govern can neither teach nor govern well.

> *John Ruskin*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 95 -->

8

*He who knows himself best of all is the one who respects himself least of all.*

Try to find out your strengths. And in finding them out, do not be afraid of belittling them, but be afraid of exaggerating them.
