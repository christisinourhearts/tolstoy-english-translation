<!-- vol. 41, p. 331 -->

18 May

One cannot say that the consciousness of the divinity of one's soul gives power. This consciousness lifts a person into that realm in which there is no longer any concept of strength and weakness, consequently—of power.

1

> Heaven is nearer than earth to people who have purified their souls and are free from doubt.

> Even those who have all the knowledge that can be obtained from the five senses will find no profit in it if they do not know the true nature of things.

> True knowledge regarding any thing is the understanding of what in that thing is the true essence.

> *Indian Kural.*

2

> *Let them not think that there is any other birth for the soul besides the knowledge of the true essence.*

> Those who have entered on this path no longer return.

> *Indian Kural.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 332 -->

3

> Man is a powerful being, and he who is conscious of the power of his soul and knows that he grows weak when he seeks strength outside himself—he, having bridled body and spirit, becomes a true ruler, walks straight, and works miracles. He is like a man who, standing on his own feet, is naturally stronger than one fallen to the ground.

> *Emerson.*

4

> You will be asked: how do you know God? Answer: because he is in my heart. If this were untrue, people would be completely helpless. Look at the self-existent not with these eyes of yours, but with the eyes of your heart. Can he know God who does not know himself? True self-consciousness is knowledge of God.

> *Persian wisdom.*

5

Who can do you any harm, and who can be more powerful than you, when you unite with God? And you can do this.

6

> One thing we know or can know if we wish, namely, that the heart and conscience of man are divine, that in denying evil and acknowledging good man himself is an embodied deity, that his joy in love, his suffering in anger, his indignation at the sight of injustice, his glory in self-sacrifice are eternal, indisputable proofs of his unity with the supreme ruler.

> *John Ruskin.*

A person who recognizes the divinity of his soul and lives by it has all that he can wish for his good.
