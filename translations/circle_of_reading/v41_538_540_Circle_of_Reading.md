<!-- vol. 41, p. 538 -->

30 July

Only one who knows all his own weaknesses can be just to the weaknesses of his neighbor.

1

> My children! If anyone offends you by a word, do not attach importance to it, but look on it as a matter not deserving attention. But if an insulting word about another has escaped you, do not strike a bargain with your conscience, saying: “What did we say, after all; it is a trifle, what does it matter.” No, do not reason so, but look on your act as a matter of great importance, and do not rest until, by your own entreaties or through the mediation of friends, you succeed in inclining the offended person to complete reconciliation.

> *The Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 539 -->

2

If we were to put ourselves in the place of other persons, we would often be freed from the feelings of hatred that we nurse toward them; and if we were to put others in our place, we would often lessen our pride.

3

> Whoever does not know how to forgive destroys the bridge over which he himself will have to pass, for every person stands in need of forgiveness.

> *Lord Herbert.*

4

> The best answer to a fool is silence. Every word of answer will bounce off the fool onto you. To pay insult for insult is the same as throwing wood into the flame, but whoever meets his accuser with calm has by this very thing already conquered him.

> Muhammad and Ali once met a man who, considering Ali to have wronged him, began to abuse him. Ali bore this with patience and in silence for quite a long time, but then he could not restrain himself and began answering abuse with abuse. Then Muhammad walked on, leaving these two to finish their quarrel. When Ali came up to Muhammad again, he said to him resentfully: why did you leave me alone to bear the abuse of this insolent man? Muhammad answered: when this man was railing at you and you were silent, I saw around you ten angels, who answered him. But when you began to answer him with railing, the angels all left you, and I went away too.

> *Muslim tradition.*

5

> *Almost always, if we search in ourselves, we will find the same sin that we condemn in another. And if we do not know of that very same sin in ourselves, we have only to search, and we will find worse ones.*

> A deep river is not troubled when a stone is thrown into it; a religious man who is troubled by insults <!-- vol. 41, p. 540 --> is not a river but a puddle. If calamity has befallen you, bear it so that, by forgiving others, you yourself may deserve forgiveness. Let us remember that we shall all return to the earth, and let us be humble. Let us sprinkle our heads with ashes before we turn into dust.

> *Saadi.*

One has only to think a little, and we will always find some guilt of ours before humanity (even if it is only the guilt that, owing to the civil inequality of people, we enjoy certain advantages for the sake of which others must endure still greater privations); and this will prevent us from driving away the thought of duty by means of self-loving notions of our own merits.

*Kant.*
