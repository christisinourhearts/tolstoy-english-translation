<!-- vol. 41, p. 604 -->

31 August

Every work of false art praised by the critics is a door through which the hypocrites of art at once burst in.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 605 -->

1

Terrible as it is to say it, with the art of our time and circle there has happened what happens with a woman who sells her attractive womanly qualities, intended for motherhood, for the pleasure of those who are tempted by such pleasures.

The art of our time and of our circle has become a harlot. And this comparison is true down to the smallest details. Like her, it is always decked out, like her always for sale, like her alluring and ruinous, and always ready.

A real work of art can arise in the soul of the artist only rarely, as the fruit of the preceding life, just as the conception of a child by a mother does.

Counterfeit art, on the other hand, is produced by masters, by craftsmen, without stopping, as long as there are consumers.

Real art does not need adornment, like the wife of a loving husband. Counterfeit art, like a prostitute, must be adorned.

The cause of the appearance of real art is the inner need to express an accumulated feeling, as for a mother the cause of sexual conception is love.

The cause of counterfeit art is greed, just as it is of prostitution.

The consequence of true art is a new feeling brought into the everyday round of life, as the consequence of a wife's love is the birth of a new person into life. The consequence of counterfeit art is the corruption of man, insatiability in pleasures, the weakening of man's spiritual powers.

This is what the people of our time and circle must understand, in order to rid themselves of the filthy stream of this depraved, harlot art that is flooding us.

2

> All attempts to obtain one's means of living by art are among the worst and most harmful paths chosen by people. In every generation there are few, very few people whose words are worth listening to and whose works are worth attention. And these few will preach, sing, and draw in spite of the existing demand: they <!-- vol. 41, p. 606 --> would sooner starve, like the dragonfly of the fable, than stop singing; and even if you do not want to listen to them, you must, out of mercy, throw them a few crumbs to sustain their life. But people who set about writing or drawing in order to obtain their means of existence this way consider themselves nobler and less contemptible than ordinary beggars, whereas they are in essence only noisy and harmful cadgers. I am gladly ready to pay for the keep of our poor vagrants in workhouses, but not for their buzzing in my ears with their barrel organs and caricatures, seducing young girls with their worthless stories, or leading the whole people to ruin with thousands of square miles of dirtily printed lies, on which people get drunk. If people are unable to obtain their bread by honest labor, then let them not shout in the streets, but hold their tongues and modestly stretch out their idle hands, and they will be mercifully fed.

> *John Ruskin.*

3

> *You must not sell your talent. By selling it, you become guilty at once of simony and of prostitution. You may sell your labor, but not your soul.*

> *John Ruskin.*

Until the traders are expelled from the temple, the temple of art will not be a temple. The art of the future will drive them out.
