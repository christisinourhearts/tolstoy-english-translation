<!-- vol. 42, p. 354 -->

18 December

Mankind is unceasingly perfecting itself, and it perfects itself not of its own accord but by the efforts that people make for their own personal perfecting. The kingdom of God is established by efforts.

1

> Herod is a man of power, of arbitrary rule, a man of a special nature, who owes nothing to others but to whom all are obliged, the king of the past, whom the king of the future will cast down from the throne. Already in the first tidings of the birth of this king of the future he hears a threat. What, then, does he do? First he uses cunning and pretends. Lies are followed by murder. He commits a multitude of murders without discrimination; he kills children still sucking their mothers' milk, for he fears a child, an unknown child. To destroy him for certain, he has no other means. Therefore let all die, so long as he perishes. But he does not perish. The king of what is to come will live to struggle with the king of the past. It will be a long struggle; it will go on from age to age, from Herod to Herod, amid sufferings, weeping, and blood, amid the blood <!-- vol. 42, p. 355 --> of children and fathers, amid the weeping of mothers and the sufferings of all. But let these calamities not trouble you; do not lose heart, take courage, struggle constantly, unflaggingly, without fear and without doubt, for the king of what is to come will triumph.

> *Lamennais.*

2

One often hears arguments that all efforts to change life, to root out evil, and to establish a just life are useless, that all this will come about of itself, progress will do everything. People were traveling by oars, but the rowers arrived and stepped out onto the shore, while the travelers who remained in the boat do not take up the oars, supposing that as the boat moved before, it will move now too.

3

> Here, on earth, there is no rest, and there must not be. Life is a striving toward a goal that can be approached but that it is unthinkable to reach; that is why there is no rest here. Rest is immoral. I do not venture to point out what this goal consists in; but whatever it may be, it exists or must exist. Without it life is an absurdity; to admit this means to deny God; more than that, it would mean recognizing life as an evil and stupid joke.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

4

> *All history confirms the incontestable truth that God can be comprehended not by reasonings but by obedience, that the presence of an eternal order in the world becomes evident only in submission to this order, and that only by this path can we on earth know his will.*

> *John Ruskin.*

5

> We alone can bring justice into the life of the world. The forces of nature can do nothing without us. If mankind, as the totality of conscious beings, does not do this, no one will.

> *Gizycki.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 356 -->

6

> If we recognize that things cannot be other than they are, we become participants in that force which holds the world in its former state.

> But if we do not submit, we become part of those forces that change things.

> *Salter.*

7

> Most people live without thinking; most people so squander their strength in the struggle for existence that they have no time left to think; they simply accept what is for what ought to be. That is why the task of the social reformer is so difficult and his path so hard. That is why the mockery of the upper classes and the curses of the mob come down on those who first raise their voices in defense of some great truth, why they are persecuted and tormented, dressed in hair shirts and adorned with crowns of thorns.

> *Henry George.*

However unnoticeable and insignificant your part in the general change for the better in the life of the world may be, it is necessary, because out of such insignificant, unnoticeable efforts of a multitude is made up all that movement toward the good which you enjoy. And therefore do not shirk, but pull your traces taut, even though no one sees you or drives you on.
