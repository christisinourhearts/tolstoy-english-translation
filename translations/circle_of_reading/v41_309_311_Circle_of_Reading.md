<!-- vol. 41, p. 309 -->

7 May

A person is mistaken who hopes to find the good outside himself, whether in this life or in the future one.

1

> I went around the whole earth seeking a guiding light. Without rest I sought it both day and night, and at last I heard a preacher who revealed the truth to me. This preacher was <!-- vol. 41, p. 310 --> in my soul, and the light that I had sought throughout the world was in me.

> *Sufi wisdom.*

2

> We are both our own saviors and our own destroyers. Nothing outside a person can do him evil. If he lives according to the laws of his being, no evil will touch him even amid the destruction of matter and the ruin of worlds.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

3

> Christ seeks the transformation of the inner man, contrary to what the Pharisees do, who are occupied only with the outward. He reproaches them for having destroyed the commandment of God by their traditions. When the spirit of life leaves those who take upon themselves the teaching of people, when institutions lose their first strength and grow weak, two things happen: the customs of outward worship of God multiply and grow complicated, and an imaginary reality is ascribed to them, people being assured that outward worship of God replaces real virtue and frees them from fulfilling the true law. And then among societies subjected to such a disastrous teaching there forms, as it were, a *false conscience.* Whole peoples, often with great zeal, observe an abstract faith and at the same time calmly stagnate in the neglect of the most sacred duties and in a depravity that takes hold of their whole life. They wash their hands before eating bodily bread, they clean copper vessels, without caring about the cleansing of the soul. The heart is neglected, and out of the heart come in a heap those terrible sins that Jesus enumerates. Jesus, on the contrary, says: “Go down into your heart, to tear out of it the roots of everything bad. The outward is not important. Both good and evil are in the inward.” That is what Christ teaches. Whoever teaches otherwise does not teach as Christ, he is not a disciple of Christ, he misuses the name of Christ to deceive people; he is one of those false prophets of whom Christ himself said: “Beware of those who will come to you in sheep's skin, but inwardly are ravenous wolves.” And again: “All those who <!-- vol. 41, p. 311 --> say: Lord, Lord, who pray with their tongue but in their desires abide in evil, will not enter the kingdom of heaven.”

> *Lamennais.*

4

> *There are no accidents in fate; a man creates, and does not meet, his fate.*

> *Villemain.*

5

> You yourself commit sin, you yourself devise evil, you yourself flee from sin, you yourself purify your thoughts, by yourself you are corrupt or pure, another cannot save you.

> *Dhammapada.*

6

> Your body is a city full of good and evil. You are the sultan, and reason is your grand vizier.

> *Saif Muluk.*

7

> A man's happiness and unhappiness are not in property and not in gold; happiness and unhappiness are in his soul.

> Not he is good who does no injustice, but he who feels not the slightest desire for it.

> The wise man feels at home in every country; the whole universe serves as a fatherland for a noble soul.

> *Democritus of Abdera.*

Nothing so weakens a person's strength as the hope of finding salvation and the good in anything other than his own effort.
