## UPBRINGING

Every person possesses a special gift and is capable of carrying out a definite task. One must try to discover this special gift in the child and direct his education accordingly. After general instruction in those branches of knowledge that are good for all people, one must direct education toward the development of those special gifts that have been found in him. Upbringing means the nourishing of a child's abilities, and not the creation of new abilities that are not in him. The latter is impossible.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 304 --> But one thing is necessary for all children. It is necessary to give them a correct conception of life and of what that world is into which they have been sent to carry out their human task.

Life is a duty, a task, a mission. For the sake of all that is holy, do not preach to them teachings of happiness, either personal or general. Faith in personal happiness will make the child selfish; faith in general happiness will sooner or later lead him to the same thing. He will dream of what he cannot bring about, and in his youth will struggle for this unrealizable thing; then, when he finds that he cannot soon realize the dreams of his soul, he will concentrate on himself and try to win personal happiness, and so will sink into selfishness.

Teach him that life has meaning only as a task or a duty; that happiness, the sun of happiness shining on the traveler, may smile on him too, and then he must rejoice and bless God for it; but that the search for happiness leads a person only to ruin and, most likely, to losing the possibility of ever enjoying happiness. Teach him that to perfect himself morally and mentally for the sake of the perfecting of his fellow men is his direct obligation; that he must strive for truth and then serve it in word and deed, embodying it in life fearlessly and unceasingly; that for determining the truth he has been given two guides: his own consciousness, his conscience, and tradition, or the consciousness of all humanity.

*Giuseppe Mazzini.*
