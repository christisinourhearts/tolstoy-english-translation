## GOOD

In the world of external nature, in plants, in animals, there is neither good nor evil; nor is there any of this in the living, unreasoning human body. This boundary separating good from evil begins in the soul of man with his capacity to be conscious and to understand. In the soul of man, from early years, an unceasing struggle with evil goes on. And there, <!-- vol. 41, p. 232 --> and only there, in his own soul, is the struggle with evil both natural to man and fruitful. But the struggle with evil outside this domain is both unnatural to man and unfruitful. This very thing is what Christ's commandment of non-resistance to evil by violence says. The commandment of non-resistance to evil by evil defines exactly and clearly the place for the struggle with evil. This place is within oneself.

The limits of violence for every rational person are confined to his own body, his own flesh, because in this violence of the spirit over the flesh consists the work and nourishment of the soul. But another person, another's flesh, has its own master of the same kind, and violence directed at it has no rational justification; it is not needed. The teaching of non-resistance to evil has precisely this very aim—the aim of revealing the needlessness of violence directed at another.

Who will undertake to assert that a person could not himself, by his own will, manage within himself, that he could not understand and do everything that is required of him for the life of the world in which he lives? To assert this is to deny the freedom of life given to man by God, the freedom to save oneself or to ruin oneself, to deny one's own rational being—to assert this is to deny man. A person's will can extend beyond the limits of his being, but who will venture to assert that it is necessary there? Who will venture to assert that harm could come to the life of the world from his, man's, personal non-interference? To assert this is to speak of the insufficiency of the will of God, it is to deny God. Worldly evil consists precisely in this, that people extend their will beyond the limits of their being, that is, put their will in the place of the will of God. This sacrilege is exposed by the commandment of non-resistance to evil.

Success justifies the deed: he won, so he is right; truth lies in victory. Such is the carnal, animal, pagan conception of truth—a notion according to which the word truth is only an empty sound. — What is truth? Just you wait, they will hang you with your truth! — said Pilate. But Christ sees the truth, and sees it at the opposite end: the vanquished is right. If you personally, in a struggle with a person, have conquered by force, then know that it is certainly you who are wrong, that the truth is not on your side. The truth is in the vanquished, God is in the vanquished, the vanquished embodies in himself the notion of the dominion of God—the first and fundamental notion of a rational <!-- vol. 41, p. 233 --> being. Such is the position of man on earth, and the only way out of this position, sad at first glance, is not to resist evil, not to struggle with man, to acknowledge oneself forever vanquished in advance, to be forever vanquished by God—a way that is illuminated and exalted by true religion, by the understanding of the life of man.

Non-resistance, removing struggle, sets free the likelihood and possibility of calm, sets free the field for another, spiritual interaction, in which there are now other forces and other interests. But the calling of man on earth, as this is shown in the Gospel by the temptation of Jesus by the devil and by the conversation with Nicodemus, consists precisely in setting free and exalting above everything, in the world of people, man's divine capacity to be conscious and to understand—setting free and exalting this rational consciousness, this son of man, this son of God in man. Not to resist means: to awaken, to resurrect the son of God, to resurrect Christ; to resist means to oppress, to crucify him. Man is a rational being. The good of a rational being is in the triumph and dominion of reason. And for the triumph and dominion of reason, what is needed first of all is the calming of the passions of the flesh. And as in the life of one person, so also in the social life of peoples, the dominion of reason can never be established on the soil of passions, pride, judgment, power, violence. By the commandment of non-resistance to evil the realization in life of this axiom of wisdom is attained.

Understanding and consciousness have been laid by God in the souls of all people, and the Gospel teaches us to value this more dearly than anything. “Whoever says to his brother, ‘raca,’ is liable to judgment, and whoever says, ‘fool,’ is liable to the fiery Gehenna.” Only human souls find unity and love in consciousness and understanding; but in the external world each being loves itself more than all the rest of the world. The commandment of non-resistance to evil, defining the place for the struggle with evil, destroys the question of the eternal contradiction between the external world of discord and enmity and the spiritual world of unity and love, and unites them into one kingdom of God, as Jesus, inspired, said of this to Nathanael: “From now on the boundary between heaven and earth is abolished, and the forces of heaven will serve man just as the forces of the earth serve him” (John 1:51).

*Buka.*
