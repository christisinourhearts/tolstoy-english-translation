<!-- vol. 41, p. 559 -->

10 August

People say: a person is not free, because everything he does has its cause preceding it in time. But a person always acts only in the present, and the present is outside time; it is only the point of contact of two times, the past and the future, and therefore in the moment of the present a person is always free.

1

A sage was asked what time in life is the most important, what person is the most important, and what deed is the most important.

And the sage answered: “The most important time is the present alone, because in it alone a person has power over himself.

And the most important deed is to live in love with this person, because a person is sent into life only in order to live in love with all people.”

<!-- vol. 41, p. 560 --> The most important person is the one you are dealing with at this present minute, because no one can know whether he will ever again have dealings with any other person.

2

> *You will never bring back lost time, you will never correct evil that has been done.*

> *John Ruskin.*

3

> Only then shall we be able to make use of every minute, when we are sure of our eternity.

> Only when we bring into it the consciousness of our spirituality will the most insignificant duty not seem low to us.

> *Martineau.*

4

The first and most common temptation that seizes a person is the temptation of preparing for life instead of life itself.

“Now I may for a time depart from what I ought to do and what my spiritual nature requires, because I am not ready,” a person says to himself. “But I will get ready, the time will come, and then I will begin to live fully in accordance with my conscience.”

The lie of this temptation lies in this, that a person withdraws from life in the present, the one real life, and transfers it to the future, whereas the future does not belong to man.

In order not to fall into this temptation, a person must understand and remember that there is no time to get ready, that he must live in the best way now, such as he is, that the perfecting he needs is only the one perfecting in love, and this perfecting takes place only in the present. And therefore he must, without putting it off, live every minute with all his strength in the present, for God, that is, for all those who make demands on his life, <!-- vol. 41, p. 561 --> knowing that at any minute he may be deprived of the possibility of this service and that it is for this very hourly service that he came into the world.

The present is that state in which alone our divine essence shows itself. Let us then revere the present—in it is God.
