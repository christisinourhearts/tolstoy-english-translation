<!-- vol. 41, p. 354 -->

29 May

Life is the consciousness of one's divine essence, enclosed within limits.

1

> *The only immediate certainty is the certainty of consciousness.*

> *Descartes.*

2

> Berkeley and Fichte are right, and Emerson too: the world is only a likeness of something; fairy tales and legends are as true as natural history—even more so, since they are more comprehensible likenesses. Only the soul truly exists. What, then, is all the rest? Shadow, pretext, image, likeness, and dream. Only what we are conscious of is spiritual. The world is some kind of game, whose aim is the formation and strengthening of the soul. Truly there is only consciousness and its sun—love.

> *After Amiel.*

3

Underfoot is the frosty, hard ground, all around are huge trees, overhead is the overcast sky, I feel my body, I am occupied with thoughts—and meanwhile I know, I feel with my whole being, that the firm, frosty ground, and the trees, and the sky, and my body, and my thoughts—are accidental, that all this is only the product of my five senses, my representation, a world constructed by me, that all this is such only because I constitute this and not another part of the world, that such is my separation from the world. I know that it is enough <!-- vol. 41, p. 355 --> for me to die—and all this will not vanish, but will change its form, as there are transformations in theaters: out of bushes, stones there will come palaces, towers, and the like. Death will produce such a transformation in me, if only I am not entirely annihilated but pass into another being, separated from the world in another way. Now I consider myself, my body with its senses, to be myself; then something will be set apart as me in quite another way. And then the whole world, remaining the same for those who live in it, will become different for me. For the world is such and not otherwise only because I consider that, and not another being separated from the world, to be myself. And there can be a countless number of beings separated from the world.

4

> Seek God in your own heart; you will not find him anywhere else.

> *Almanzor dar Gafed.*

5

Our life is our consciousness of ourselves as an eternal, infinite, that is, timeless and spaceless spirit, limited by the conditions of temporal and spatial phenomena.

Human consciousness is the consciousness of divinity.
