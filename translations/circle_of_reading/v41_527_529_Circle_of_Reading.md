<!-- vol. 41, p. 527 -->

25 July

Even if the connection between our sufferings and our sins is not visible to us, this connection exists all the same.

1

“I have been repaid with evil for my good.”

But if you love the one to whom you do good, then you have already received your recompense in the good that you received from loving.

So the good that you do in loving, you always do to yourself.

2

> The reward of virtue lies in the very consciousness of a good deed.

> *Cicero.*

3

> In proclaiming the future salvation, Jesus declares to the people what the conditions necessary for it are on their part. It will be the fruit of love, self-sacrifice, mercy, forgiveness of all.

> And so, if deliverance has not yet come, if it is still the time of hunger, and the time of weeping, and the time of oppression, blame only yourselves for it.

> Have you fulfilled the commands of Christ? Have you done what you were to do? More than once you have tried to win back your right, to break your old chains, to come out of the dark and wretched refuges into which a lawless force had driven you, and to build yourselves a better dwelling. What did your efforts lead to? Why was what you had built with such labor always so soon found destroyed? Why, if not <!-- vol. 41, p. 528 --> because you were like the man who built his house on sand? The river rushed upon the house, and the house did not withstand this shock and collapsed, and the ruin of the house was great.

> *Lamennais.*

4

When a man finds the causes of his personal suffering in his personal error and directs his activity to destroying the error, he does not rebel against the suffering, and bears it easily and often joyfully. But when such a man is overtaken by a suffering that goes beyond the connection between suffering and error that he can see, it seems to him that what is overtaking him is something that ought not to be, and he asks himself: why, for what?—and, finding no object to which he could direct his activity, he rebels against the suffering, and his suffering becomes a terrible torment.

When a man does not see the connection between the sufferings he experiences and his life, he can do one of two things: either go on bearing such sufferings as torments that have no meaning at all, or acknowledge that his sufferings are indications of the sins he has committed, indications also of the means of delivering himself and other people from these sins.

On the first view, sufferings have no explanation and call forth no activity other than a constantly growing despair and bitterness that nothing can resolve. On the second, sufferings call forth that very activity which makes up the movement of true life: consciousness of sin, liberation from errors, and submission to the law of reason.

5

The legend of the fall of man—of sin and of redemption from suffering and death—is only a figurative affirmation of the connection between sufferings and sins.

6

> Only after experiencing sufferings did I learn the close kinship of human souls with one another. One need only suffer thoroughly oneself, and all who suffer become understandable to you, <!-- vol. 41, p. 529 --> and you almost know what needs to be said to them. More than that, the mind itself grows clear: the situations and walks of life of people, hidden until then, become known to you, and it becomes visible who needs what. Great is God, who makes us wise. And by what does he make us wise? By that very grief from which we flee and want to hide. It is appointed to us to obtain by sufferings and grief the grains of wisdom that are not acquired in books.

> *Gogol.*

7

For a person living a spiritual life, suffering is always an encouragement to perfecting, to enlightenment, to drawing near to God. For such people suffering can always be transformed into the work of life.

*Look for the cause of the evil from which you suffer in yourself. Sometimes this evil is the direct consequence of your activity, sometimes it has returned to you by a complicated transmission, but its source is always in you, and salvation from it lies in changing your activity.*
