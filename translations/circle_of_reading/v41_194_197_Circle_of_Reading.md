<!-- vol. 41, p. 194 -->

26 March

The main change in the life of peoples is the change in their beliefs.

1

> Jesus, as he approaches the end of his life, is occupied chiefly with two questions: the question of the abuse that will be made of his name, and that of the final establishment of his law after deep <!-- vol. 41, p. 195 --> and destructive upheavals. Before his death Christ told his disciples and all people that after him false Christs and false prophets would appear, and that one must beware of them, however much they might astonish people. He says that they will be powerful and that their power will tempt people.

> He speaks of how it will be possible to know that their teaching is false. It can be known by the same thing by which we know a good tree from a bad one—by its fruits. If there is no contempt in the teaching for the charms of this world, no renunciation of self, no mercy and love for all without any distinction, then wherever this is lacking there will be no true Christian teaching, but there will be false Christs and false prophets. And Christ says that there will be many of them. They will appear one after another until that day and hour comes which only the heavenly father knows.

> But this hour will come; the time will come when everything in human societies will begin to totter, when some peoples will be hurled upon others, when authorities and powers will begin to fall and there will be universal turmoil. Then, says Christ, will come the end of the old world and the beginning of the new world and of the establishment of the kingdom of God. And the time of this new world is not far off, says Christ, because it is already evident that the old world, the world of false Christs, false prophets, is passing away. And the peoples are already raising their heads with joy and looking around on all sides to greet the coming of the kingdom of God.

> *After Lamennais.*

2

> When in ancient times a people was unhappy, the prophets said to it: “You have forgotten God, you have turned away from God's ways—otherwise misfortune would not have overtaken you. You have lived not by the eternal laws and have not been guided by them, but have followed the laws of lies, of deceit, consciously not respecting reality, and now you see that the long-suffering of nature is exhausted.”

> All this is entirely understandable to simple, uncorrupted people. But lately people have appeared who consider nature dead, something like a clock that runs for a week, made many thousands of years ago; it still ticks, but it is good for nothing. No exhortations or rebukes can have any effect on such people.

> *After Carlyle.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 196 --> But fortunately not all people are like that, and there are people who understand that if their life is bad, only they themselves are to blame for it.

3

> As a drunken man standing on the edge of a precipice answers with foolish laughter and indecent talk the warnings of people who wish to keep him from falling, so our world too, intoxicated with impure lusts of every kind, mocks the prophets who wish to save it from the fate that threatens it. As in ancient times, one may say now: “O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, you who kill the prophets and stone those who are sent to you! How many times I wanted to gather your children, as a bird gathers its nestlings under its wings, but you did not want this.”

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> Humanity is a man who is eternally learning. Individual people die, but the truth they have thought their way to, the truth they have spoken, does not perish with them. Humanity preserves all this, and man, from the graves of the dead, makes use of what was won by the people who lived before him. Each of us is born into the world of beliefs of the humanity that lived before us; and each unconsciously brings something more or less valuable to the life of the humanity that will inherit from him. The education of humanity is accomplished like those Eastern pyramids for which each person passing by laid a stone. We, momentary lodgers, called away to another place to complete our education, depart; but the education of humanity, though slowly, is accomplished without interruption.

> *After Mazzini.*

*It is a great mistake to think that there can be one faith for all times. The longer people live, the more understandable and simple, the firmer their faith becomes.*

*And the more understandable, simple, and firm the faith, the more harmonious and the better the life of people.*

To believe that one and the same faith is fit for all times and must not change is the same as believing that those <!-- vol. 41, p. 197 --> fairy tales and jingles your mother told you when you were an infant are the real truth and that you may not stop believing in them.
