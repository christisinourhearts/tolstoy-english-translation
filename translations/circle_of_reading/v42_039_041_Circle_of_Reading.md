<!-- vol. 42, p. 39 -->

12 September

*One cannot serve God and mammon. Care for the increase of wealth is incompatible with the demands of true, spiritual life.*

1

> And behold, someone, having come up, said to him: Good Teacher! what good must I do to have eternal life? Jesus said <!-- vol. 42, p. 40 --> to him: if you want to be perfect, go, sell your possessions and give to the poor, and you will have treasures in heaven; and come and follow me.

> *Matthew 19:16, 21.*

2

> And Jesus said to his disciples: truly I say to you that it is hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven. And again I say to you: it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God.

> *Matthew 19:23–24.*

3

It seems to me that the old superstition that wealth gives happiness is beginning to crumble.

4

> Paul called the love of money idolatry, because many, having wealth, do not dare to use it, but regard it as a holy thing and, not daring to touch it, hand it on intact to their grandchildren and their descendants. And if ever they are forced to touch it, they are in such a state as if they had done something forbidden. On the other hand, just as a heathen guards his idol, so you fence in gold with doors and bolts, instead of a temple you build an ark for it and lay it in silver vessels. A heathen will sooner give up his eyes and his soul than his idol, and just so do those who love gold. If you do not worship gold, then you worship the demon that breaks into your soul from looking at gold and from passion for it. The passion of love of money is worse than a demon, and many submit to it more than others do to idols. Idols are disobeyed in many things, but love of money is obeyed in everything, and everything it orders to be done is carried out. What then does it say?—Be, it says, an enemy and a foe to all, forget nature, despise God, sacrifice yourself to me. And in all this it is obeyed. To graven images oxen and sheep are offered in sacrifice, but love of money says: offer me your soul in sacrifice—and this is carried out.

> *John Chrysostom.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 41 -->

5

> Excessive thickness of clothing hampers the movement of the body; wealth hinders the movement of the soul.

> *Demophilus.*

6

> The thirst of the desire for wealth is never quenched or satisfied. Those who possess it are tormented not only by the desire to acquire still more, but also by the fear of losing what they have.

> *Cicero.*

7

Be afraid not of poverty, but of wealth.

People seek wealth. But if they only saw clearly all that they are deprived of through it, they would use the same efforts to free themselves from it as they now use to acquire it.
