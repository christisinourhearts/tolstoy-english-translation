<!-- vol. 42, p. 167 -->

22 October

That one must not condemn one's brother one can be convinced if only by the fact that hundreds of times one has had, and still has, to repent of having condemned one's brother, and never of not having condemned him.

1

> When you see that a person is in error, do not be angry with him: understand that one cannot err on purpose. No one can want his judgment to be darkened. The one in error is he who sincerely takes falsehood for truth.

> But it also happens that people are not in error, but deliberately do not accept the truth even when it has been laid open before them to complete obviousness. They do not accept it not because they cannot understand it, but because it exposes their evil deeds and takes away from them the justification of their vices. These people too deserve not anger but compassion, because they are sick not in body but in soul.

> *Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 168 -->

2

*Time passes, but the spoken word remains.*

3

> If the crowd hates someone, one must, before judging, carefully examine why this is. If the crowd has become attached to someone, one must also carefully examine this before judging.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

4

> To curb one's tongue is a sign of great virtue.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

5

> Our anxieties depend chiefly on our trying to correct others while forgetting about our own correction.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

6

> The human soul turns away from truth and good not willingly but by force; the more clearly you understand this, the more meekly you will treat people.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

If you consider it necessary to condemn your neighbor, condemn him to his face. And condemn him in such a way as not to arouse an unkind feeling toward yourself.
