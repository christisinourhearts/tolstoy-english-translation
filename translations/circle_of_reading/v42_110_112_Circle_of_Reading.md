<!-- vol. 42, p. 110 -->

2 October

Religion tells a person what he is and what that world is among which he lives. Moral teaching is the indication of the activity that follows from the religious understanding of life.

1

> Therefore I tell you: do not worry for your soul, what you will eat and what you will drink, nor for your body, what you will put on. Is not the soul more than food, and the body more than clothing?

> Look at the birds of the sky: they do not sow, do not reap, do not gather into barns; and your heavenly father feeds them. Are you not much better than they?

> So do not worry and do not say: what shall we eat? or what shall we drink? or what shall we put on?

> But seek first the kingdom of God and his justice, and all this will be added to you.

> So do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own. Each day has enough of its own worry.

> *Matthew 6:25–27, 31, 33–34.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 111 --> And which of you, by worrying, can add to his stature even one cubit?

2

> Whoever has bread in his basket and asks: what will I eat tomorrow?—belongs to those of little faith.

> *The Talmud.*

3

> The best worship of God is that which is done without hope of attaining any aim; the worst, that which has a definite aim in view.

> He who adores the highest being must contemplate it in every creature.

> *Agni Purana.*

4

It is beyond doubt that to teach religious systems is violence, is that temptation of children of which Christ spoke. What right have we to teach what is disputed by the vast majority: the trinity, the miracles of the Buddha, of Muhammad, of Christ? The one thing we can and must teach children is what is common to all religions and comprehensible to everyone: the moral teaching of love and union.

5

> The Buddha said: there are things that are hard in the world—to be merciful while being poor; to be religious while being rich and noble; to suppress lust and carnal desires, to see a pleasing object and not desire to acquire it; to bear insults <!-- vol. 42, p. 112 --> without malice; to study a subject to its very foundation; not to condemn the ignorant; to avoid disputes; to free oneself completely from self-love and to be the same toward all people in one's heart and in one's life.

> *Chinese Buddhist.*

6

*God is not obeyed, but adored. It is better not to adore, but to obey.*

7

Live for an age and till evening. Work as if you will live forever, and behave toward people as if you will die right now.

8

> Religion, considered in itself, is the recognition of all our duties as commandments of God.

> *Kant.*

Moral teaching is incomplete if it is not religious, that is, not obligatory. Religion is not needed if it is not moral, does not lead to a good life.
