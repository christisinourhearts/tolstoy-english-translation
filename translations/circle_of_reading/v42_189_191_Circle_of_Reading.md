<!-- vol. 42, p. 189 -->

1 November

A person who considers himself the master of his life is not humble, because he thinks that he owes nothing to anyone. But a person who sees his calling in the service of God cannot help being humble, since he constantly feels that he has not fulfilled his duties.

1

> And the apostles said to the Lord: increase our faith.

> The Lord said: which of you, having a slave plowing or herding, will say to him on his return from the field: go quickly and sit down at the table?

> On the contrary, will he not say to him: prepare me supper, and gird yourself and serve me while I eat and drink, and afterward eat and drink yourself?

> Will he thank this slave for having carried out the order? I think not.

> So you too, when you have done all that you were commanded, say: we are worthless slaves, because we have done what we had to do.

> *Luke 17:5–10.*

2

> The modesty of truly good people is expressed in forgetfulness: they are so absorbed in what they are doing now that they lose sight of what they have already done.

> *Chinese proverb.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 190 -->

3

> A person standing on tiptoe cannot stand for long. A person who puts himself on display cannot shine. Whoever is pleased with himself cannot become renowned. Whoever boasts cannot have merit. Whoever is proud cannot rise high. Before the judgment of reason such people are like scraps of food and arouse everyone's disgust. Therefore he who has reason does not rely on himself.

> *Laozi.*

4

> *The deeper a person descends into himself and the more insignificant he seems to himself, the higher he rises toward God.*

> *Thomas à Kempis.*

5

For one who professes the Christian teaching, the attainment of every step of perfection calls forth the need to step up to a higher step, from which still higher ones open up, and so on without end. One who professes the law of Christ always feels himself imperfect, not seeing behind him the path that he has traveled, but always seeing ahead of him the path that he has not yet traveled.

6

> A person, while he lives, is tender and supple. When he dies, he becomes hard and dry.

> All things, grass as well as trees, are tender and supple while they live. When they die, they become hardened and dry. The hard and the strong—these are the companions of death. The soft and the tender are the companions of life. Therefore he who is strong of hand will not conquer. When a tree has become strong, it is doomed to death. The strong and the great are below; the tender and the soft are above them.

> *Laozi.*

7

> He who seeks learning grows with every day and rises higher and higher in the eyes of people.

> He diminishes more and more until he arrives at complete humility. And when he arrives at complete humility, then he becomes entirely free and, against his will, becomes a teacher of people.

> *Laozi.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 191 --> He who seeks virtue diminishes with every day and becomes lower and lower in the eyes of people.

Remember that you have no right to anything at all, that you are the slave of the source that gave you life, and therefore you have only duties.
