<!-- vol. 41, p. 352 -->

28 May

For the pagan world wealth constitutes glory and greatness. For a Christian wealth is an exposure of his weakness or his lie. To say “a rich Christian” is the same as to say “a legless racehorse.”

1

> People are so sunk in material interests that they look on the manifestations of the human soul, expressed in relations to people, only from the point of view of improving their own property position. Their respect is measured by the wealth of this one or that one, and not by the inner worth of the person. But a truly enlightened man is ashamed of his possessions, of his money, out of respect for his reasonable *self.*

> *Emerson.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 353 -->

2

> Listen, you rich: weep and wail over your calamities that are coming upon you. Your wealth has rotted, and your garments are eaten by moths. Your gold and silver have rusted through, and their rust will testify against you and will eat your flesh like fire: you have gathered treasures for yourselves for the last days. Behold, the pay withheld by you from the workmen who reaped your fields cries out, and the cries of the reapers have reached the hearing of the Lord Sabaoth.

> *James 5:1–4.*

3

> Everywhere I see a conspiracy of the rich, seeking their own advantage under the name and pretext of the common good.

> *Thomas More.*

4

> Poverty teaches us both wisdom and patience. For Lazarus too lived in poverty and yet was crowned; and Jacob desired to have only bread, and Joseph was in extreme poverty and was not only a slave but also a prisoner, and yet for this very reason we marvel at him all the more. We praise him not so much when he was distributing wheat as when he was in prison—not so much when he wore the royal crown as when he was in fetters—not when he sat on the throne, but when he was subjected to plots and was sold. And so, picturing all this to ourselves and thinking of the crowns woven by these feats, let us marvel not at wealth and honors, not at pleasures and dominion, but at poverty, fetters, bonds, and patience for the sake of virtue.

> *John Chrysostom.*

5

> The possession of wealth is a school of pride, cruelty, self-satisfied ignorance, and debauchery.

> *Puisieux.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 354 -->

6

> The insensibility of the rich is not as cruel as their compassion.

> *After Rousseau.*

*One should not respect the rich, but withdraw from their life and pity them. A rich man ought not to be proud of his wealth, but ashamed of it.*
