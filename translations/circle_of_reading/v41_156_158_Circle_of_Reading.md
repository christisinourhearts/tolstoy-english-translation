<!-- vol. 41, p. 156 -->

9 March

War and Christianity are incompatible.

1

A person need only say to himself about a bad deed that, although I know this deed is bad, it is impossible not to do it—a person need only <!-- vol. 41, p. 157 --> say this to himself, and he will do the most terrible deeds, and not only think that these deeds may be done, but will even be proud of them. One such terrible deed is war.

2

> *Armed peace and war, if they are ever abolished, will by no means be abolished by kings, by the mighty of the world. War is too profitable for them. War will cease only when those who suffer most from war understand that their fate is in their own hands, and for their deliverance from the calamities of war use the simplest and most natural means: stop obeying those who draw them into war, who make them soldiers.*

> *After Harduin.*

3

> To those who, not understanding our faith, want us to take weapons in our hands and kill people for the sake of the common cause, we can answer: “Your priests, appointed to your idols and your temples, keep their hands clean so that the sacrifices they offer to your gods may be performed with clean hands, not defiled by blood and killing. Whatever war may begin, you never enlist them in the army. If this custom is reasonable, then is it not much more reasonable still that we, Christians, should keep our hands clean of all defilement.”

> When by our exhortations we encourage the peoples not to break alliances and terms of peace, we are much more useful to rulers than their warriors are. We truly take part in labors whose aim is the public good when to our exhortations we also add reflections and exercises that teach people to free themselves from lusts. Yes, more than all others we wage war for the good of the emperor. True, we do not serve under his banners and will not serve even if he should compel us, but we fight for him with good deeds.

> *Origen (a writer of the first half of the 3rd century).*

4

> Jesus laid the foundation of a new society. Before him the peoples belonged to one or to many lords, as herds belong to their masters. Princes and the mighty of the world crushed the people <!-- vol. 41, p. 158 --> with the whole weight of their pride and greed. Jesus puts an end to this disorder, raises bowed heads, frees the slaves. He teaches them that, being equal before God, people are free, that no one can of himself have power over his brothers, that equality and freedom, the divine laws of the human race, are inviolable; that power cannot be a right; that in the social order it is an office, a service, a kind of slavery freely taken upon oneself with a view to the common good. Such is the society that Jesus establishes. Is it this that we see in the world? Is it this teaching that reigns on earth? Servants or lords—the princes of the peoples in our world? For 19 centuries generation after generation have been handing down to one another the teaching of Christ and saying that they believe in it, and what, then, has changed in the world? The peoples, crushed and suffering, are all waiting for the promised deliverance, and not because the word of Christ is untrue or ineffectual, but because the peoples either have not understood that the realization of the teaching must be accomplished by their own efforts, by their firm will, or, having fallen asleep in their humiliation, have not done the one thing that gives victory, are not ready to die for the truth. But they will wake. Already something is stirring among them; they hear a voice that says: salvation is near.

> *Lamennais.*

Man in general, and a Christian in particular, is bound not to take part in war or in preparations for it, either personally, or with money, or with reasonings about it.
