## PROCLAMATION OF THE PRINCIPLES ADOPTED BY THE MEMBERS OF A SOCIETY FOUNDED FOR ESTABLISHING UNIVERSAL PEACE AMONG PEOPLE

*Boston, 1838.*

We do not recognize any human government. We recognize only one King and Lawgiver, only one Judge and Ruler over mankind. We recognize the whole world as our fatherland, all mankind as our fellow countrymen. We love our native land as much as <!-- vol. 42, p. 347 --> we love other countries too. The interests and rights of our fellow citizens are no dearer to us than the interests and rights of all mankind. Therefore we do not admit that the feeling of patriotism can justify revenge for an insult or for harm done to our people.

The proposition preached by the churches that all the states on earth are established and approved by God, and that all the authorities existing in the United States, in Russia, in Turkey accord with the will of God, is as absurd as it is blasphemous. This proposition represents our creator as a partial being, one who establishes and encourages evil. No one will venture to assert that the authorities existing in any country whatsoever act toward their enemies in the spirit of the teaching and after the example of Christ. And therefore the activity of these authorities cannot be pleasing to God, and therefore these authorities could not have been established by God, and they must be overthrown—not by force, but by the spiritual regeneration of people.

We recognize as unchristian and unlawful not only wars themselves—both offensive and defensive—but also all preparations for wars: the building of any arsenals, fortifications, warships; we recognize as unchristian and unlawful the existence of any standing armies, of any military command, of any monuments erected in honor of victories or fallen enemies, of any trophies taken on the field of battle, of any celebrations of military exploits, of any appropriations made by military force; we recognize as unchristian and unlawful every governmental decree that requires military service from its subjects.

In consequence of all this, we consider it impossible for ourselves not only to serve in the army but also to hold offices that oblige us to compel people to act well under fear of prison or the death penalty. We therefore voluntarily exclude ourselves from all governmental institutions and renounce all politics, all earthly honors and offices.

Not considering ourselves entitled to occupy places in governmental institutions, we likewise do not consider ourselves entitled to elect other persons to these places. We also consider ourselves not entitled to go to law against people in order to make them return what they have taken from us. We consider that we are obliged to give our caftan also to him who <!-- vol. 42, p. 348 --> has taken our shirt, but in no way to subject him to violence (Matthew 5:40).

We believe that the criminal law of the Old Testament: an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth, has been abolished by Jesus Christ, and that by the New Testament forgiveness of enemies instead of revenge is preached to all his followers, in all cases without exception. But to extort money by violence, to lock up in prison, to exile, or to execute is obviously not forgiveness of wrongs but revenge.

The history of mankind is filled with proofs that physical violence does not promote moral regeneration, and that the sinful inclinations of man can be suppressed only by love, that evil can be destroyed only by good, that one must not rely on the strength of one's arm to defend oneself from evil, that true security for people is found in kindness, long-suffering, and mercy, that only the meek will inherit the earth, and those who have raised the sword will perish by the sword.

And therefore, both in order to secure more surely the life, property, liberty, public tranquility, and private good of people, and in order to fulfill the will of him who is the king of kings and Lord of lords, we accept with all our heart the fundamental teaching of non-resistance to evil by evil, firmly believing that this teaching, answering to all possible contingencies and expressing the will of God, must in the end triumph over all evil forces.

We do not preach a revolutionary teaching. The spirit of revolutionary teaching is a spirit of revenge, violence, and murder. It does not fear God and does not respect the personality of man. We, on the other hand, wish to be filled with the spirit of Christ. Following our fundamental rule of non-resistance to evil by evil, we cannot carry out conspiracies, disturbances, and acts of violence. We submit to all laws and to all requirements of the government except those that are contrary to the requirements of the Gospel. Our resistance is limited to humble submission to the punishments that may be imposed on us for disobedience. Intending to bear without resistance all the attacks directed at us, we at the same time, for our part, intend to attack unceasingly the evil of the world, wherever it may be, above or below, in the political, administrative, or religious sphere, striving by all the means possible for us to bring it about that the kingdoms of the earth merge into one kingdom of our Lord Jesus <!-- vol. 42, p. 349 --> Christ. We consider it an indubitable truth that everything that is contrary to the Gospel and its spirit, and is therefore subject to destruction, must be destroyed at once. And therefore, if we believe the prediction that a time will come when swords will be beaten into plowshares and spears into sickles, we must do this at once, without putting it off to a future time, as far as our strength allows.

Our task may bring upon us insults, wrongs, sufferings, and even death. Misunderstanding, false interpretation, and slander await us. A storm must rise against us. Pride and Pharisaism, ambition and cruelty, rulers and authorities—all this may unite to destroy us. So they acted toward the messiah, whom we strive to imitate as far as our strength allows. But these horrors do not frighten us. We put our hope not in people but in the almighty Lord. If we have renounced human protection, what can sustain us but faith alone, which overcomes the world? We will not be surprised at the trials to which we are subjected, but will rejoice that we shall be found worthy to share the sufferings of Christ.

In consequence of all this we commit our souls to God, believing what has been said: that whoever leaves houses, and brothers, and sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or fields for the sake of Christ will receive a hundred times more and will inherit eternal life.

And so, firmly believing, in spite of everything that may arm itself against us, in the certain triumph throughout the whole world of the principles expressed in this “Proclamation,” we here affix our signatures, trusting in the reason and conscience of mankind, but most of all in the power of God, to which we also entrust ourselves.
