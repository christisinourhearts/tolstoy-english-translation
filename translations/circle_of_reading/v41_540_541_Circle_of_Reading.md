<!-- vol. 41, p. 540 -->

31 July

*If Christians professed their law, there could be neither rich nor poor.*

1

> And behold, someone came up and said to him: Good teacher! What good thing must I do to have eternal life? Jesus said to him: if you want to be perfect, go, sell your possessions and give to the poor; and you will have treasure in heaven; and come and follow me.

> *Matthew 19:16, 21.*

2

> The rich man is insensitive and indifferent to another's grief.

> *The Talmud.*

3

> The rich and the poor are, as it were, complements of each other. A class of the rich presupposes a class of the poor, and insane luxury cannot but be connected with terrible want, which compels people who have nothing to serve insane luxury.

> *Henry George.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 541 --> The rich are robbers; the poor are the robbed. That is why Christ always expressed sympathy for the poor and aversion to wealth. According to his teaching, to be robbed is better than to be a robber. In the kingdom of truth that he preached, rich and poor would be equally impossible.

4

> Terrible, truly terrible is the love of money; it closes the eyes and ears, makes us fiercer than beasts, allows us to think neither of conscience, nor of friendship, nor of fellowship, nor of the salvation of our own soul, but, depriving us of everything at once, makes people its slaves. And what is worst of all in this bitter slavery—it makes people delight in their slavery, so that the more people give themselves up to it, the more their pleasure increases. It is chiefly for this reason that this disease is incurable, for this reason that this beast is untamable.

> *John Chrysostom.*

5

> A society made up of the very rich and the very poor easily becomes the prey of people who seize power into their hands. The very poor do not have enough spirit to offer resistance, and the very rich have too much at stake to be able to take risks.

> *Henry George.*

6

Riches, like manure, stink when they are in a heap; but when they are scattered, they fertilize the soil.

What an intensified darkening of the moral feelings there must be in a person who, in a Christian society, amid thousands in need, takes pride in his wealth.
