31 May

As a man unaccustomed to luxury, but who has happened to come into it, pretends, in order to raise himself in people's eyes, that luxury is so habitual to him that he is not only not surprised by it but disdains it, so an unreasonable man, considering disdain for the joys of life a sign of an elevated worldview, pretends that he is weary of life, that he can imagine something much better.

1

> To be happy, to have eternal life, to be in God, to be saved—all this is one and the same: it is the solution of the problem, the aim of existence. And blessedness grows, as sorrow too can grow. An invariably peaceful and eternal growth, an ever deeper entering in, an ever stronger and deeper taking possession of heavenly joy—that is what happiness is. Happiness has no bounds, because in God there is neither bottom nor shores, and happiness is the taking possession of God through love.

> *Amiel.*

2

The chief cause of our dissatisfaction with life is the assumption, founded on nothing, that we have a right to happiness disturbed by nothing, that we are born for such happiness.

We have been given the incomparable good of life, with all its joys, and we say: few joys. We are given the greatest joys of life: communication with the bodily and spiritual world; we say: why so little life, why does it end? It must be prolonged.

If only we understood and valued all that great good of life which gives us the possibility of communication through love with the bodily and spiritual world—we would never think that one could wish for anything more.

3

> *The best worship of God is grateful joy.*

> *Lessing.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 358 -->

4

> The joy of the spirit is a sign of its strength.

> *Emerson.*

5

One must believe in the possibility of happiness in order to be happy.

6

Give the greatest goods, the very ones he would wish for, to a man who violates the law of his life, the law of God, and he will be unhappy. Take away from a man who has placed his good in fulfilling the law all that people consider a good, and he will still be happy.

7

A man has ruined his stomach and complains about the dinner. The same with people who are dissatisfied with life.

8

We have no right whatever to be dissatisfied with this life. If it seems to us that we are dissatisfied with it, that only means that we have grounds to be dissatisfied with ourselves.
