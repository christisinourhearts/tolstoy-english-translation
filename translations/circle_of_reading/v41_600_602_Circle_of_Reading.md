<!-- vol. 41, p. 600 -->

29 August

*If a man is conscious of and feels God in his soul, he is conscious of and feels his unity with all the people of the world.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 601 -->

1

> All souls belong to one and the same family, all are of one origin, of one nature, all are animated by one and the same light, and all strive toward one and the same center, one and the same good. This truth, the greatest for us, lying at the foundation of every religion, is not only proved by reason, but seems to me an undoubted property of our nature.

> *Channing.*

2

In him who adores the Highest, pride vanishes from the heart, just as the light of a campfire does in the light of the sun. He whose heart is pure and in whom there is no pride, who is meek, constant, and simple, who looks on every being as his friend and loves every soul as his own, who treats everyone alike, with tenderness and love, who wishes to do good and to leave vanity behind—in the heart of that man lives the Lord of life.

As the earth is adorned with the beautiful plants that it produces, so is he adorned with virtues in whose soul the Lord of life lives.

*Vishnu Purana*.

3

> In you, in me, in every other dwells the God of life; in vain are you angry with me and cannot bear my approach: know that we are all equal, and therefore do not be proud, however high your position may be.

> *Indian Mahmud Gasha.*

4

> No traces of an earthly origin of souls can be found. In souls there is nothing composite, nothing that could arise or be formed on earth; there is not even anything akin to moisture, air, or fire. Indeed: neither in water, nor in air, nor in fire is there anything that would have the capacity to remember, understand, think, retain the past, look into the future, and grasp and comprehend the present. All this is proper <!-- vol. 41, p. 602 --> only to spiritual beings, that is, to gods, and you will never be able to show any other origin for this than the divine. And therefore every being, since it feels, thinks, lives, and acts, must be of heavenly and divine origin and therefore must be eternal. God cannot be imagined otherwise than as spiritual, free from the admixture of anything perishable. Such too is the human spirit.

> *Cicero.*

5

> One great thought has taken possession of my soul. This thought is the consciousness of the greatness of my soul, of its union with God, not by virtue of blind submission to him, but by virtue of its capacity to receive him, by virtue of its capacity for perfecting, by virtue of its destiny for inexpressible greatness, by virtue of its immortality.

> *Amiel.*

All people are children of one father, and therefore it is unnatural not to love one's brothers.
