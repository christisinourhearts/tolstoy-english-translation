## II STONES

Two women came to an elder for instruction. One considered herself a great sinner. In her youth she had been unfaithful to her husband and never ceased tormenting herself. The other, having lived all her life according to the law, did not reproach herself with any particular sin and was satisfied with herself.

The elder questioned both women about their lives. One confessed her great sin to him with tears. She considered her sin so great that she did not expect forgiveness for it; the other said that she knew of no particular sins in herself. The elder said to the first:

— Go, servant of God, beyond the fence and find me a large stone—as large as you can lift—and bring it... And you, — he said to the one who knew of no great sins in herself, — bring me stones too, as many as you can manage, but all small ones.

The women went and carried out the elder's instruction. One brought a large stone, the other a full bag of small stones.

The elder looked over the stones and said:

— Now do this: take the stones back and put them in the very places where you took them, and when you have put them back, come to me.

And the women went to carry out the elder's instruction. The first easily found the place from which she had taken the stone and put it back as it had been; but the other could not remember which stone she had taken from which place and so, without carrying out the instruction, returned to the elder with the same bag of stones.

— So there you are, — said the elder, — the same thing happens with sins. You easily put the large, heavy stone back in its former place because you remembered where you had taken it from.

— But you could not, because you did not remember where you had taken the small stones.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 537 --> — It is the same with sins.

— You remembered your sin, bore for it the reproaches of people and of your own conscience, humbled yourself, and so were freeing yourself from the consequences of sin.

— But you, — the elder said to the woman who had brought back the small stones, — committing small sins, did not remember them, did not repent of them, grew accustomed to a life in sins and, judging the sins of others, became more and more entangled in your own.

We are all sinners, and we will all perish if we do not repent.
