<!-- vol. 41, p. 91 -->

8 February

Why do people so love to condemn one another? Because every person, in condemning another, thinks that he would not have done what he condemns his neighbor for. For the same reason people also love to hear their neighbors condemned.

1

*Condemnation, not only unjust but also just, harms three at once: the one of whom ill is spoken, the one to whom ill is spoken, but most of all the one who condemns. “Hide another's sin, God will forgive two,” says the proverb. And this is true.*

2

Evil-speaking pleases people so much that it is very hard to refrain from doing something pleasant for one's interlocutors: from condemning a person.

3

When two people quarrel—both are always to blame. And therefore a quarrel can stop only when one of the two acknowledges his guilt.

4

> Do not judge, that you may not be judged; for with what judgment you judge, with such you will be judged; and with what measure you measure, with such will it be measured to you. And why do you look at the twig in your brother's eye, but do not feel the log in your eye? Or how will you say to your brother: let me take the twig out of your eye; and here, in your eye is a log? Hypocrite! first take the log out of your eye, and then you will see how to take the twig out of your brother's eye.

> *Matthew 7:1–5.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 92 -->

5

> Watch over yourself constantly, and before condemning another, think of your own correction.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

6

Much harm can be done by incautious praise and condemnation, but the chief harm is done by condemnation.

*John Ruskin.*

Stop condemning people—and you will feel what a drunkard feels when he gives up drinking, or a smoker smoking: you will feel that your soul has become lighter.
