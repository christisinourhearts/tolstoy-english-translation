<!-- vol. 42, p. 62 -->

22 September

Faith in immortality is natural to man.

1

> Everyone feels that he is not a nothing called into life at a certain moment by someone else. Hence his certainty that death can put an end to his life, but by no means to his existence.

> *Schopenhauer.*

2

> The soul does not live in the body as in a house, but lives in it as in a temporary shelter.

> *Indian Kural.*

3

> How many kingdoms know nothing of us! The eternal silence of these infinite spaces terrifies me. When I reflect on the brevity of my life amid the eternity that precedes and follows it, on the insignificance of the space that I occupy and even <!-- vol. 42, p. 63 --> of the space that I see—a space vanishing in the infinite immeasurability of other spaces which I do not know and which do not know me—I am seized with horror and wonder why I am in one place and not in another; for there is no reason for me to be here and not there, at the present moment and not earlier or later. Who put me here? By whose command and disposition have precisely this place, precisely this time, been assigned to me?

> Life is the memory of a single fleeting day spent as a guest.

> *Pascal.*

4

> Mortals, we do not live long; we are granted only a few moments. But our soul does not experience old age and will live forever.

> *Phocylides.*

5

> We see from experience that many people who are convinced of the existence of a life beyond the grave nevertheless give themselves up to vices and commit base acts, devising means of escaping more cunningly the consequences of their conduct that threaten them in the future. And at the same time we see that every truly moral person always knows in the depth of his soul that his life does not end with death. Therefore it seems to me more in keeping with human nature to ground faith in a future life on the feelings of a noble soul and a good life than, the other way around, to base a good, moral life on the hope of retribution in a future life. Such, in reality, is true moral faith, whose simplicity of heart can rise above all sorts of subtleties and sophistries, and which alone suits every condition of man, because it leads him by a direct and not a roundabout way to his true ends.

> *Kant.*

6

The fear of death comes from people taking for life one small part of it, limited by their own false conception.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 64 -->

7

Death is the destruction of the bodily organs by means of which I perceive the world as it appears to me in this life. Death is the destruction of the glass through which I looked at the world. But the destruction of the glass in no way includes the annihilation of the eye.

*The consciousness of our immortality is the voice of God living in us.*
