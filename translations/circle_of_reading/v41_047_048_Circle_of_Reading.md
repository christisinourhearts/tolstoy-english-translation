<!-- vol. 41, p. 47 -->

20 January

Death and birth are two limits. Beyond these limits there is the same something.

1

When you think about what will happen to the soul after death, you cannot help thinking also about what happened to the soul before birth. If you are going somewhere, then surely you came from somewhere. So it is in life. If you came into this life, then you came from somewhere. If you will live afterward, then you lived before as well.

2

Where do we go after death? To where we came from. There, where we came from, there was not that which we call our “I”—that is why we do not remember where we were, how long we were there, or what was there. If after death we come to where we came from, then after death too there will not be that which we call our “I.”

For this reason we can in no way understand what our life after death will be like. One thing can certainly be said: just as it was not bad for us before birth, so it cannot be bad after death either.

3

When a person lives a good life, he is happy now and does not think about what will happen after this life. If he does remember death, then, judging by how well life is arranged for us now, he believes that after death it too will continue to be good. To believe that God is good and has done and does everything that is best for us is much calmer and surer than to believe in all the bliss of paradise.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 48 -->

4

> *When we are born*, *our souls are laid in the coffin of our body. This coffin*—*our body*—*gradually decays*, *and our soul is freed more and more. When the body dies, the soul is completely freed.*
>
> *After Heraclitus.*

A person need not make guesses about what will happen after this life, but in this life should try to act according to the will of the one who sent us, which we know in our reason and heart.
