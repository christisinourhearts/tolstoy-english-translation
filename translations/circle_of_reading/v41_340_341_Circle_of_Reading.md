<!-- vol. 41, p. 340 -->

22 May

The very greatest changes, and everything in nature, are accomplished imperceptibly, by slow increase, and not by explosions.

It is the same in the spiritual life.

1

> All true thoughts are living thoughts and show their life in being able to nourish and to change. But they change like a tree, not like a cloud.

> *John Ruskin.*

2

*Everything truly great is accomplished by slow, imperceptible growth.*

3

> The perfection of individual people and of society is never attained for all times, for each time has its own perfection.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> Life must be the birth of the soul. The animal must be humanized, the flesh must be transmuted into spirit; bodily activity must be transformed into thought, into consciousness, into reason, justice, magnanimity, as a candle is into light and warmth. This highest alchemy justifies our presence on earth; in this is our calling and our dignity.

> *Amiel.*

5

> Just as one cannot break an egg in which a chick is sitting without danger to its life, so one person cannot free another without danger to his spiritual life. When the spirit reaches a certain growth, it will itself break its fetters.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 341 -->

6

> Life is an unceasing miracle. Knowing what growth is, we know the most hidden of nature's secrets.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

Nothing is so harmful to moral perfecting as the consciousness of one's own progress.

Fortunately, the path of true moral improvement goes on so imperceptibly that a person cannot even see his successes except after long intervals of time.

If you think that you are perfecting yourself, if you notice it—know that you are mistaken, have stopped, or are going backward.
