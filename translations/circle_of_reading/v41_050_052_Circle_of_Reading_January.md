> PERFECTION
>
> It is impossible for a person to attain the perfection of God, but he must constantly make efforts to draw nearer and nearer to it. This is the path along which the human race has from eternity been commanded to go. It is a thorny and difficult path because of the obstacles encountered at every step. But it is a consoling and joyful path because of the fruits it brings, the last of which is fraternal peace, the kingdom of peace and love on earth. Then the final great unity too will come. But unity is nothing other than the merging of each person's life with the life of all, and therefore for the realization of <!-- vol. 41, p. 51 --> this unity there is no other means than renunciation of oneself insofar as unity requires it—a voluntary renunciation of everything that divides and isolates. In this is the whole teaching of the Gospel. It is all in mercy, in universal love embracing God and all his creatures. And in all God's creatures everything changes above all in this direction. From self-love flow pride, covetousness, sensual desires, envy, anger, hostility; from the feeling of the common life, whose foundation is God, are born meekness, self-renunciation, inner peace—pure joys that turn earthly sufferings into an undisturbed blessedness.
>
> But remember: the farther you advance along this path, the greater the obstacles you will meet from the children of the age, the subjects of the king of the past. They will hate and persecute you—drag you before courts and cast you into prisons in order to stifle goodness in the bud, the goodness whose seeds you scatter around you—in order to continue the evil they serve. Make your hearts firm; strengthen your courage so as not to fall in this sacred struggle. Bequeath this struggle, as the most cherished part of your inheritance, to those who follow you. Rest will come after the battle; the battle itself will continue until the day when it is said: God has conquered, his kingdom is established on earth, and his children have a homeland.
>
> *Lamennais.*

The moral law—“love your neighbor as yourself,” as it is said in the Gospel—will not pass away until it is fulfilled. It is a law as inevitable as the law of gravity, the law of chemical combinations, and all other physical laws.

It may be supposed that physical laws too once wavered, were not common to all the phenomena of nature, were being worked out, but finally became a necessity. So it is with the moral law: it is being worked out by us.

To a reasonable person, the nearest aim of the life of the world appears to be the unification of all the beings of the world. At first some people, submitting more and more to the law of reason, will understand that the good of life is attained not by each being's striving for its own personal good, but by each being's striving <!-- vol. 41, p. 52 --> for the good of all the others; and afterward all other beings too will understand this more and more, or will be brought to it.
