<!-- vol. 41, p. 395 -->

12 June

Sufferings are necessary conditions of growth, both physical and spiritual.

1

> Truly, truly I say to you: you will weep and wail, but the world will rejoice; you will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will turn into joy. A woman, when she gives birth, endures grief, because her hour has come; but when she has borne the child, she no longer remembers the grief for joy, because a human being has been born into the world.

> *John 16:20–21.*

2

We complain of sufferings. But sufferings, sufferings of every kind, are always for our good. Sometimes we can see that bodily sufferings are of use to us, as happens when children grow or when an abscess comes to a head, and the like. But sometimes we do not see the use of sufferings, both bodily and spiritual, and then we complain. And we do not know that all sufferings are for our good: they help us to become better and to draw nearer to God.

3

The bitterness of sufferings is eased, first, by a vivid picture of the sufferings of other people, far worse than yours, and, second, by the consciousness that the bearing of sufferings can be bad—rebellious—or good—submissive.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 396 -->

4

> This is how we grow. In every thought of every person there already lies a higher thought; in the character that a person shows today a higher character is already being prepared. The youth casts off the illusions of childhood, the grown man casts off the ignorance and stormy passions of youth, the old man casts off the selfishness of the grown man and becomes more and more a universal soul. He rises to a higher and firmer step of life. External relations and conditions die away, and he enters more and more into God and God into him—until the last garment of selfishness falls from him, and he unites with God, merges his own will with his will, and shares in his greatness.

> *Emerson.*

5

> *As wise deeds are done, more and more life comes to a person.*

> *John Ruskin.*

6

In a heavy state of soul one must not give in, must not speak out to anyone but God. The important thing is to keep silent, to endure it. Either the sufferings will pass to others and make them suffer, or they will burn out in you and help you to rise, to draw a little nearer to perfection.

7

> Virtue and strength of soul are strengthened and perfected in misfortune, in sufferings, in illnesses. Therefore you must not fear the trials that may fall to your lot, but bear them with firmness. Every trial brings you nearer and nearer to God.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

8

> Calamity is a whetstone for human life.

> *Fletcher.*

Seek in sufferings their meaning for the growth of your soul, and the bitterness of suffering will vanish.
