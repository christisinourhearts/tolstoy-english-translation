<!-- vol. 41, p. 20 -->

7 January

Kindness in relations with people is obligatory. If you are not kind to a person, then you are not fulfilling your chief duty.

1

> One must respect every person, however wretched and ridiculous he may be. One must remember that in every person lives the same spirit that lives in us. Even when a person is repulsive both in soul and body, one must think this way: “Yes, there must be <!-- vol. 41, p. 21 --> such monstrosities in the world too, and we must tolerate them.” If we show such people our disgust, then, first, we are unjust, and second, we call such people to a war not for life but to the death. Whatever he may be, he cannot remake himself; what else can he do but fight us as a mortal enemy? Indeed, we want to be kind to him only if he ceases to be what he is. But he cannot do that. And therefore one must be kind to every person, whatever he may be, and not demand of him what he cannot do—that he become another person.

> *After Schopenhauer.*

2

> *Do not be hard-hearted toward one* *who is subjected to temptation, but try to comfort him as* *you yourself would wish to be comforted.*

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

3

> 1) Do not put off until tomorrow what you can do today.

> 2) Do not make another person do what you can do yourself.

> 3) Pride costs more than everything needed for food, drink, shelter, and clothing.

> 4) How much we have tormented ourselves over what did not happen but only could have happened.

> 5) If you become angry, before you do or say anything, count to ten. If it has not passed from your heart, count to a hundred, and if it still has not passed—count to a thousand.

> *After Jefferson.*

4

> Despise no one; suppress in your heart unkind judgments and insulting suspicions of your neighbor; always explain other people's actions and words to yourself in the best sense.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 22 -->

5

The holy man does not have an inflexible heart. He adapts his heart to the hearts of all people. He treats a virtuous person as virtuous, and a vicious person as a person capable of virtue.

*Eastern wisdom.*

6

The more intelligent and kinder a person is, the more good he notices in people.

Kindness adorns life: it resolves all contradictions, makes what is tangled clear, what is difficult easy, what is gloomy joyful.
