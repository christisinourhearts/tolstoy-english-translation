<!-- vol. 41, p. 590 -->

24 August

Humanity, imperceptibly but without ceasing, moves toward the realization of the kingdom of God, which is attained by union in love.

1

> As every person individually, so likewise all humanity collectively must be transformed, must pass from a lower state to a higher, without being held back in its growth, the limit of which is in God himself. Every state is the consequence of the preceding state. Growth takes place continuously and imperceptibly, like the growth of an embryo—it takes place in such a way that nothing breaks the chain of successive states of this continuous development. But if man and the whole human race are destined to be transformed, then both for man and for the human race this transformation must be accomplished in labor and suffering.

> Before putting on greatness, before coming out into the light, one must move in darkness, endure persecution, give up the body in order to save the soul; one must die—die on the cross, in order to be reborn into a life more mighty, more perfect. This is what Jesus teaches us by his word, by his example. And after eighteen centuries, having completed one of the circles of its development, humanity again strives to be transformed. The old systems, the old societies, everything that made up the old world, is being destroyed or has already been destroyed—and peoples now live amid ruins in horror and suffering. At the sight of these ruins, these deaths, already accomplished or yet to be accomplished—take courage. What is departing is the worn-out garment of an imperishable being; what is falling is an autumn leaf. When the sun sinks lower, winter draws near, but after winter comes spring and the stirring of the life-giving breath. And this time is coming.

> *Lamennais.*

2

> No, the word of almighty God has not yet been fully spoken, and his thought has not yet been revealed in all its fullness. He creates and will create through eternal ages, which the human mind is not able to grasp. The past ages have revealed to us only <!-- vol. 41, p. 591 --> fragments of his creation. Our work will never be finished. We hardly know its source and are wholly ignorant of its final goal: time, knowledge, and discoveries only widen its bounds. From age to age it rises toward destinies unknown to us, seeking its law, a few lines of which we are able to make out.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

3

> Always add, always move forward; never stand still, and do not go back, and do not turn aside. He who stands still does not move forward; he who does not continue goes back; he who rebels turns aside.

> Always be dissatisfied with what you are, if you want to attain to being other than you are, because where you stop, there you will remain. But if you say: I have enough—you are lost.

> *Augustine.*

4

> Love your work, but do not love what you have done.

> *S. Makovsky.*

5

> I have come to bring down fire on the earth, and how I would wish that it were already kindled! Do you think that I have come to give peace to the earth? No, I tell you, but division.

> *Luke 12:49, 51.*

6

*The life both of the individual person and of all humanity is an unceasing struggle of the flesh and the spirit. In this struggle the spirit always remains the victor, but this victory is never final; this struggle is endless, and it is this very struggle that constitutes the essence of life.*

7

The purpose of life is the permeation of all its phenomena by love; it is the slow, gradual turning of an evil life into a good one, <!-- vol. 41, p. 592 --> it is the creation of true life (because true life is only a loving life), it is the birth of a true, that is, a loving life.

8

> Between reason and the passions a civil war goes on in man. Man could have at least some peace if there were only reason in him, without passions, or only passions, without reason. But since he has both, he cannot avoid struggle; he cannot be at peace with the one except by being at war with the other. He is always divided and in contradiction with himself.

> *Pascal.*

The human world is constantly perfecting itself, and the consciousness of this perfecting constitutes one of man's best joys, and this joy is further increased for every person by the possibility of taking part in this perfecting.
