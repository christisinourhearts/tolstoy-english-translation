<!-- vol. 42, p. 133 -->

9 October

He who has transferred the consciousness of his life to his spiritual “I” can experience evil neither in life nor in death.

1

That material form in which the awakening of our consciousness of true life finds us in this world represents, as it were, the limits that bound our spiritual being.

Matter is the limit of the spirit. True life is the gradual destruction of this limit, ending in complete destruction, complete liberation—death. Such an understanding of life gives a person complete peace both in life and in death.

2

> Wherever fate may cast you, everywhere your essence is with you, your spirit, the center of life, freedom, and strength, when it is true to the law of its being. There are no external goods or greatness in the world for the sake of which it would be worth a man's while to break his unity with the spirit, to dissolve his union with it, and to undermine the wholeness of his soul by inner discord with himself.

> Point out what you could buy at the price of such a sacrifice?

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

Man is sometimes tormented and gladdened by the terrible contrast, vividly realized by him, between something infinitely great and almighty, of which he is conscious in himself, and something narrow and weak, which he feels in that same self.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 134 -->

4

> *What is born of the flesh is flesh, and what is born of the spirit is spirit.*

> Do not be surprised at what I said to you: you must be born from above.

> The spirit breathes where it wants, and you hear its voice, but do not know where it comes from and where it goes: so it is with everyone born of the spirit.

> *John 3:6–8.*

5

> For many reasons the soul of good people seems to me divine and eternal, but chiefly because the soul of the best and wisest people is so carried over into the future state that it seems as if all its thoughts are concentrated on eternity.

> *Cicero.*

6

> Only an active, moral, spiritual, deep, and religious consciousness gives life all its dignity and energy. It makes one invulnerable and invincible. The earth can be conquered only in the name of heaven. All goods are given, besides, to him who seeks only wisdom. You are strongest of all only when you are wholly disinterested, and the world is at the feet of him whom it cannot seduce. Why? Because spirit rules over matter, and the world belongs to God. “Take courage,” said the heavenly voice, “I have conquered the world.”

> God, give strength to the weak who desire good!

> *Amiel.*

7

> There is one highest reason, surpassing all human minds. It is far and near. It is above all worlds and at the same time penetrates everything that exists.

> The man who sees that all things are contained in the highest spirit, and that the highest spirit penetrates all beings, cannot regard any being with contempt.

> Those who perform rites alone are in thick gloom, but those who give themselves only to fruitless reflections on the highest reason are in still greater darkness.

> *An Upanishad from the Vedas.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 135 --> For him for whom all spiritual beings are the same as the highest, there can be no place for delusion or for sorrow.

8

God draws those who want to rise to him. And therefore it is not surprising that man strives toward God. God comes to people, enters into people. There is not a single soul that would be good without God.

*Seneca.*

Salvation from everything is the consciousness of one's spirituality. Whatever happens to a person who is conscious of his spirituality, evil cannot touch him.
