<!-- vol. 41, p. 228 -->

6 April

People occupy themselves with the most various tasks, which they consider very important, but almost never occupy themselves with that one single task which alone is appointed to them and includes <!-- vol. 41, p. 229 --> all the rest—the improvement of their soul, the freeing of the divine principle of the soul. That precisely this task is appointed to man is evident from the fact that it is the only aim in the attainment of which a person meets no obstacles.

1

In their young years people believe that the virtue which we wish for ourselves and for others is possible, and that the purpose of man lies in constant perfecting, and that it is possible and even easy to correct all mankind, to destroy all vices and misfortunes. These dreams are not ridiculous; on the contrary, there is much more truth in them than in the judgments of old people mired in temptations, when these people, who have spent their whole lives not as is natural to man, advise others to wish for nothing, to seek nothing, but to live as life happens to go. The mistake of the dreams of youth lies only in this, that young men transfer the perfecting of themselves, of their own soul, onto others, and also in this, that they want to see now what must be accomplished in the future.

2

> I consider that one cannot live better than by trying to become better, and that there is no greater pleasure than to feel that one is really becoming better. This is a happiness that I have not ceased to experience up to now, and my conscience tells me that this happiness is real.

> *Socrates.*

3

> We must thank those who point out our faults to us. Although our faults do not disappear because of this pointing out, since we have too many of them, yet when our faults are known to us, they begin to trouble our soul, they do not let conscience grow deaf, and we try to correct ourselves and free ourselves from them.

> *Pascal.*

4

> The state of our consciousness has more significance for us than the judgment of the whole external world, for we live in our consciousness continuously and eternally. Our happiness or unhappiness depends <!-- vol. 41, p. 230 --> not on how others treat us but on how we treat ourselves. Improve yourself, your soul, and by this you will do the very best that you can do both for yourself and for others.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

5

> *The very best happiness is to feel oneself better at the end of the year than at the beginning.*

> *Thoreau.*

6

“Be perfect, as your heavenly father” means: try to free within yourselves the divine principle of your life.

It is impossible to perfect oneself while living constantly in worldly bustle, and still less possible while living constantly in solitude. The most advantageous condition for perfecting is this: in solitude to work out and establish one's view of the world, and then, living in the world, to put it into practice.
