<!-- vol. 41, p. 510 -->

19 July

The truly useful, the truly good, and therefore the truly great is always simple.

1

> The language of truth is simple.

> *Seneca.*

2

*What is good is natural to man, and therefore everything good is simple and inconspicuous.*

3

> The true greatness of human life is almost always entirely invisible. It may very well be that before us, silently and secretly, the greatest heroic deed is being performed, the most generous sacrifice, the loftiest design is being conceived, and we do not even suspect it. I believe that such greatness is very common among many people whose names we do not hear and do not know. I am sure that among the so-called common people one more often meets with sufferings courageously borne, more often meets with unadorned truth, firm faith, and that true generosity which gives away what is necessary to the giver, and, above all, more often than among the rich, one meets with a right understanding of the meaning of life and death.

> *After Channing.*

4

Often the simplest, unlearned and uneducated people take in the true, reasonable teaching of life quite clearly, consciously, and easily, while often the most learned people, precisely by this learning of theirs, are deprived of the possibility of taking in what alone is needed by them and by every person.

5

> To feed oneself, clothe oneself, and have a dwelling, very little is needed; the rest is acquired in order to adapt oneself to other people's tastes or to outshine others.

> *Eastern wisdom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 511 -->

6

> Simplicity of life, of language, of habits gives strength to a people, while luxury of life, affectation of language, and softness of habits lead to weakness and ruin.

> *John Ruskin.*

7

> The clearest notions are often obscured by complicated reasonings.

> *Cicero.*

If you want to find an example to imitate, look for it among simple, humble people. Only there is true greatness, which not only does not put itself on show but is not even conscious of itself.
