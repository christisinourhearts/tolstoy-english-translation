<!-- vol. 41, p. 32 -->

12 January

There are people who take upon themselves the right to decide for others their relation to God and to the world, and there are people, the enormous majority, who give this right to others and blindly believe what they are told. Both are equally guilty.

1

> There are people who, having learned that all religious questions have already been resolved and all religious laws established, immediately give themselves into the hands of those people who undertake such resolution and establishment.
>
> Why should they concern themselves with what others so decisively recognize as their own inalienable business? Their lot is merely to pass the time pleasantly, filling the day with pleasures and amusements and thus rocking themselves for whole years in pleasant dreams.
>
> The fruit of such dull contentment is the absence among the people of any striving to evaluate what is instilled in them.
>
> I fear that the slave's imprint from the iron yoke created by blind faith will remain on our neck for a long time.

> *Milton.*

2

> From the moment a person renounced his moral independence, from the moment a person began to determine his duty not by the inner voice but by the views of a certain class or party, from the moment he shook off his personal responsibility because he is only one among millions—from that moment he lost his moral strength; he now expects from people what only God can accomplish; he puts in the place of divine power the crude prescriptions of the human mind.

> *Channing.*

3

> We are all like children, at first repeating the incontrovertible truths of our grandmothers, then of our teachers, and as we grow older, of other remarkable people who happen to cross our path.

> *Emerson.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 33 --> With what effort we try to memorize the words we have heard from them! But when we ourselves reach the step on which these teachers of ours stood, and understand the meaning of their words, our disappointment is so strong that we would gladly forget everything we heard from them.

4

> Beware of false prophets who come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly are ravening wolves: by their fruits you shall know them. Do they gather grapes from thorns, or figs from burdock? Thus every good tree bears good fruit, and a bad tree bears bad fruit: a good tree cannot bear bad fruit, nor can a bad tree bear good fruit. Therefore by their fruits you shall know them.

> *Matt. ch. 7, vv. 15–20.*

5

A person may make use of the tradition that has passed to him from wise and holy people of the past, but he himself must test by his reason what is handed down to him, reject one thing and accept another.

*Every person must himself establish his relation to the world and to God.*
