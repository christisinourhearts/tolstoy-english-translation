<!-- vol. 42, p. 292 -->

30 November

A humble person, renouncing himself, unites with God.

1

> There is nothing in the world more tender and yielding than water, and yet the hard and firm cannot stand against it. The weak conquers the strong. The tender conquers the cruel. Everyone in the world knows this, but no one wants to carry it out.

> *Laozi.*

2

> Whoever forces circumstances is forced by circumstances in turn, and whoever yields to them, they too yield to him.

> When you see that circumstances do not favor you, do not resist them, but leave them to their natural course, because whoever goes against circumstances becomes their slave, and whoever submits to them becomes their master.

> *The Talmud.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 293 -->

3

> When a sage holds to the law of virtue, he hides it from the eyes of people and does not regret that he is known to no one.

> *Confucius.*

4

In the land of the Parthians, — relates Saadi, — I was met by a man riding on a tiger. Seeing this, I was so frightened that I could neither run nor move from the spot. But this man said to me: “Do not be surprised, Saadi, at what you see. Only do not free your own neck from under the yoke of God, and nothing will have the power to free its neck from your yoke.”

5

> A man is very strong when he wants to be only what he is, and very weak when he wants to rise above humanity.

> *Rousseau.*

6

A person, perfecting himself in humility, as it were descends from the apex of a cone to its base. The more he descends, the wider the circle of his spiritual life.

7

> The weakest in the world conquers the strongest; therefore great is the advantage of humility and the benefit of silence. Only few in the world can be humble.

> *Laozi.*

*The humbler a person is, the freer and stronger he is.*
