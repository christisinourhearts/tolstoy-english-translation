<!-- vol. 42, p. 168 -->

23 October

Conscience is the consciousness of the divine source living in us.

1

> “Conscience! Childish errors, prejudices of upbringing,” I hear the concerted voices of supposed wise men. “In the mind of man there is nothing except what is given by experience,” they say.

> Of course, everyone acts for his own good, but if there is no moral, spiritual good, then only the actions of evil people can be explained by advantage. What a terrible philosophy that would be under which virtuous actions could not be explained otherwise than by searching out base motives for them.

> Conscience! Yes, conscience is the faithful judge of good and evil, conscience is what makes a person like God, only conscience constitutes the superiority of man's nature. Without conscience there is nothing in a person that would raise him above the animal, except the sad privilege of wandering without any guidance from error to error.

> *Rousseau.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 169 --> Even more, they reject the evident and universal agreement of all peoples, and against the striking unanimity of the judgment of all people about what is good and what is evil, they search out some example known to them alone (as if the qualities of all people could be destroyed by the perversion of some obscure little people described by a traveler), and they suppose that as soon as it has been proved that there are monsters, the common quality of all mankind no longer means anything. “All,” they say, “if they contribute to the common good, do so only for the sake of their own advantage.”—But why then are there people who serve the common good to their own evident disadvantage? How is that—to go to death for the sake of one's own advantage?

2

> *Do not do what your conscience condemns, and do not say what is not in accord with the truth. Act so, and you will fulfill the whole task of your life.*

> *No one can do violence to your will; against it there is neither thief nor robber; do not desire what is unreasonable, desire the common good and not your personal good, as most people do.*

> *The task of life is not to be on the side of the majority, but to live in accordance with the inner law of which you are conscious.*

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

3

> Thousands of outer voices call us somewhere off to the side, but only one inner, weak voice of conscience can be our reliable guide.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 170 -->

4

> To sin is natural to all people.

> The difference between people lies in the degree of the pangs of conscience after sin.

> *Alfieri.*

One cannot fight against the demands of conscience—they are the demands of God, and therefore it is better to submit to them at once.
