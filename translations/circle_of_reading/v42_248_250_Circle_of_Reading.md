<!-- vol. 42, p. 248 -->

12 November

The land is the common and equal possession of all people and therefore cannot be an object of ownership by separate individuals.

1

> If I was born on the earth, then where is my share? Be so kind, masters of this world, as to show me my plot of forest, where I could cut my firewood; my field, where I could sow my grain; my homestead, where I could put up my little house. But the masters of this world shout at me: just touch the forest, or the field, or the homestead, and you'll catch it; but you may come and work on our lands, and we will give you a piece of bread.

> *Emerson.*

2

> My reason teaches me that the land cannot be sold. The Great Spirit gave it to his children so that they might live on it and cultivate it, as need requires, for their sustenance, and as long as they occupy and cultivate it, they have a right to the land.

> *Black Hawk.*

3

> *The land can never be sold, because the land is mine, and you are all strangers.*

> *Leviticus 25:23.*

4

> Strictly speaking, the land belongs to two: to almighty God and to all the sons of men who have worked on it or who will work on it.

> *Carlyle.*

5

> Hear me, just spirit of creation, and judge who is the thief: he who takes from me the freedom to use the land that is given to me with my birth, or I, when I use a part of the land in order to live on it and feed myself by it.

> *Gerrard Winstanley.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 249 -->

6

> All people, from the very beginning and before any juridical act, are in possession of the land, that is, they have the right to be where nature or chance has placed them.

> *Kant.*

7

> Has God given anything to one without giving the same to another? Has the universal father EXCLUDED any of his children? You who demand the exclusive right to use his gifts, show the testament by which he has deprived the other brothers of his inheritance.

> *Lamennais.*

8

The possession of land as property is one of the most unnatural crimes. The loathsomeness of this crime is unnoticeable to us only because in our world this crime is recognized as a right.

9

> While I was gathering nuts in the wood, a forester poked his head out of the bushes and asked me what I was doing there. I answered that I was gathering nuts.

> — Gathering nuts? — he said. — How dare you do that?

> — And why not gather them? — I said. — Don't the monkeys and the squirrels take nuts too?

> — I am telling you, — he said, — that this wood is not common, but belongs to the Duke of Portland.

> — Is that so! — I said. — Then give the duke my respects and tell him that this wood knows as much about him as it does about me. And therefore, for everything that grows in the wood, the rule is this: whoever comes first takes it; so if the Duke of Portland needs nuts, tell him not to dawdle.

> *Thomas Spence.*

The injustice of recognizing a right of property in land for separate individuals is recognized by people in our time just as the injustice of slavery was recognized in the middle of the nineteenth century.
