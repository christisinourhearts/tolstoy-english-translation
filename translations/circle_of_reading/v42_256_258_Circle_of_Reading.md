<!-- vol. 42, p. 256 -->

16 November

Faith answers those questions to which reason cannot give an answer, but which reason itself cannot help putting to itself.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 257 -->

1

> Christ was a great man. He preached the true, universal religion—of love for God and for man. But I do not doubt that God has still greater people in the future. In saying this I do not diminish the greatness of Christ's character, but affirm the almightiness of God. And when such people come, the old struggle will be renewed: again they will beat the living prophet and deify the dead one.

> But be that as it may, Christ now teaches us truths entirely opposite to those that are usually taught. If he had brought his teaching into agreement with what he was told about people, if he had agreed only with flesh and blood, he would have been only a poor Jew, and the world would have lost the most precious treasure of religious life, the joyful news of the one, universal, and true religion.

> What if he, like others, had said: no one can be higher and truer than Moses? He would have been nothing, and the spirit of God would have left his soul. But he communed not with people but with God, listened to his hopes and not to his fears. He labored for people, with people, and through people, believed in God, and, pure as Truth, he feared neither the church nor the state and was not troubled when Pilate and Herod became friends only in order to crucify him. It always seems to me that I hear the voice of this exalted spirit, which says to me and to you: “Do not be afraid, poor brother, and do not despair. The goodness that was in me is possible for you too. God is as near to you as he was near to me then, and just as rich in truth and just as ready to inspire everyone who wants to serve him.”

> *Parker.*

2

> Death, silence, the abyss—terrible mysteries for a being that strives for immortality, for the good, for perfection. Where will I be tomorrow, after a little while, when I no longer breathe? Where will those I love be? Where are we going? What are we? Eternal riddles constantly stand before us in their implacable solemnity. Mysteries on all sides. Faith is the only star in this darkness of the unknown...

> *Amiel.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 258 --> Well, what of it? So long as the world is a work of the good and so long as the consciousness of duty has not deceived us. To give happiness and to do good—that is our law, our anchor of salvation, our beacon, the meaning of our life. Let all religions perish, so long as this one remains; we will have an ideal—and it is worth living.

3

> *There is only one true religion, though there may be many different faiths.*

> *Kant.*

4

> Only faith begets those firm, mighty convictions, that energy and that unity by which alone society can be healed.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

5

We have one, only one infallible guide, the universal spirit that penetrates us all together and each one as a unit, and places in each the striving toward what ought to be; that same spirit which in the tree bids it grow toward the sun, in the plant bids it bring forth and cast its seed, and in us bids us strive toward God and in this striving unite more and more with one another.

As long as a person lives, he believes. The closer his faith is to the truth, the happier his life; the farther this faith is from the truth, the unhappier the person.

Without faith, however, a person does not live; he dies a natural death or kills himself.
