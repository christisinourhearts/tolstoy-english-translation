6 June

Evil done by a person not only deprives the person of his true good by diminishing his soul, but often returns, even in this world, upon the one who did it.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 383 -->

1

> Evil in this world does not bear fruit at once, but, like the earth, little by little and in its own time. And these fruits are dreadful.

> *Indian Manu.*

2

> Not to do evil even to enemies—this is the chief virtue.

> He surely perishes who plots the destruction of another.

> Do not do evil. Poverty cannot serve as a justification for evil. If you do evil, you will become poorer still.

> People can escape the consequences of their enemies' malice, but they will never escape the consequences of their own sins. This shadow of theirs will follow at their heels until it destroys them.

> Let him who does not want sorrows to pursue him do no evil to another.

> If a person loves himself, let him not do evil, however small it may be.

> *Indian Kural.*

3

> As true as it is that a stone thrown upward does not stay there but returns to the earth, so true is it that, according to your good or evil deeds, the fulfillment of your heart's desires will be measured out to you, in whatever form and into whatever world you may enter.

> *Sinhalese Buddhist.*

4

> *An evil man is happy as long as the evil he has done has not ripened; but when it has ripened, then the evil man comes to know evil. Evil returns upon the one who did it, just like dust thrown against the wind.*

> Neither in the sky, nor in the sea, nor in the depths of the mountains, is there a place in the whole world where a person could free himself from an evil deed.

> *Dhammapada.*

5

> *He who broods on revenge keeps his wounds open. They would heal if he did not do this.*

> *Bacon.*

Doing evil is as dangerous as teasing a wild beast.

For the most part, in this world and in the crudest form, evil returns upon the one who did it.
