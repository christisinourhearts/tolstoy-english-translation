<!-- vol. 41, p. 40 -->

16 January

The chief cause of the bad organization of life is false faith.

1

Human life consists in bringing what is unreasonable in one's life to what is reasonable. And for this two things are needed:

1) to see, in all its significance, the unreasonableness of life and not turn one's attention away from it.

Conscious of all the unreasonableness of life and the misery that always flows from it, a person involuntarily turns away from it; and, on the other hand, clearly conscious of the possibility of a reasonable life, a person involuntarily strives toward it. Therefore, not to conceal the evil of unreasonableness and to present in full clarity the good of reasonable life ought to constitute the task of all teachers of humanity.

But it is precisely here that those who do not go to the light, because their deeds are evil, always sit in Moses' seat; and therefore people who set themselves up as teachers not only do not try to make clear the unreasonableness of present life and the reasonableness of the life that ought to be, but, on the contrary, try to conceal the unreasonableness of present life and undermine confidence in the reasonableness of the life that ought to be. For this purpose there exist and operate the police, armies, criminal laws, prisons, shelters for children, almshouses for the old, foundling homes, brothels, insane asylums, hospitals, insurance societies, and all compulsory educational institutions established with forcibly collected funds, institutions for juvenile offenders, and many others.

By a fatal necessity, each of these institutions, besides concealing evil, generates new evil and increases uncontrollably, like a snowball, the evil that it supposes itself to destroy.

If only one thousandth of the efforts used to establish all this, which has the aim of concealing evil and only increases it, were used to oppose that for the concealment of which all these institutions serve, this evil, having become manifest to us, would quickly be destroyed.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 41 --> 2) to recognize in all its purity the reasonableness of a possible life.

2

> We must treat our public affairs with deep attention; we must be ready to change our opinions, abandon old views, adopt new ones. We must cast off prejudices and reason with a completely free mind. A sailor who sets the same sails regardless of changes in the wind will never reach his harbor.

> *Henry George.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 42 -->

3

> Workers and capitalists, in order to improve their relations, must abandon the ancient Mosaic law “an eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth” and put into life the law of love—to act toward others as you wish others to act toward you.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

No violent reform will correct evil while people remain as they are, and therefore correction of evil can be expected not from a change in the form of our life, but only from the spread of goodness and reasonableness.

5

One need only directly and simply accept Christ's teaching for the terrible deception in which we all live and each of us lives to become clear.

*Submission to the demands of false faith* — *this is the chief cause of people's misery.*
