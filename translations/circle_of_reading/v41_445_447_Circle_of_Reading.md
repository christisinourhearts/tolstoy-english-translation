<!-- vol. 41, p. 445 -->

29 June

Despondency is a state of the soul in which a person sees no meaning either in his own life or in the whole life of the world.

1

There are people who, being in despondency or irritation, admire their state, even take pride in it. It is the same as if, having let go the reins of a horse that is running away with you downhill, you were to start lashing it with the whip besides.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 446 -->

2

Despondency and a bad frame of mind are not only tormenting for those around but also contagious, and therefore a decent person, just as he does all things unpleasant for others in solitude, so too indulges in his despondency and irritation in solitude.

3

To think that external causes influence the state of a person's soul is a harmful and very common error. The state of the body, fatigue, hunger, illness influence the state of the soul of a person who is conscious of the spiritual foundation of his life only in the sense that they weaken his activity, but they do not change its direction. Only people who live by the external life alone (children, nonreligious people) change their whole attitude toward life as a result of external causes: they fall into despondency or irritation and condemn and hate what they formerly praised and loved.

4

Do not trust yourself when everything appears to you in a gloomy light, everyone seems guilty, and you want to say and even do something evil to everyone. Look upon yourself in this state as upon a drunk man, undertaking nothing, and wait until this state passes. The less you do while you are in this state, the sooner it will pass: this restraint is as necessary as sleep is for a drunk man.

5

Most people who are called evil have become so only because they took their bad frame of mind for their rightful state and gave themselves up to it.

6

When the world seems ugly, people unpleasant and unkind, and all their doings stupid and nasty, hasten to make use of this state by turning your attention on yourself, and you will see in yourself what you had not seen before, and this acknowledgment of your own nastiness will be of benefit to you.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 447 -->

7

> There are few misfortunes without a way out; despair is more deceptive than hope.

> *Vauvenargues.*

8

Never be despondent.

9

A person is obliged to be happy; if he is unhappy, he is to blame.

10

It seems to me that a person should set himself as his first rule—to be happy and content. One must be ashamed of one's discontent as of a bad deed, and know that if something is not going right with me or in me, then I must not tell others about it and complain, but must as quickly as possible try to set right what is not going right.

11

*Help me, Lord, never ceasing to rejoice, fulfilling your will in purity, humility, and love.*

12

Both physical sufferings and periods of low spirits are the lot of life here, and one must wait until the one and the other pass, or this life itself.

When you feel dissatisfaction with everything around you and with your situation, withdraw, like a snail into its shell, into the consciousness of your purpose in the world, and wait for the time when the conditions that brought you into this state pass, and you will again have the strength to do your work of life.
