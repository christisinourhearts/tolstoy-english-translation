<!-- vol. 41, p. 283 -->

26 April

Consciousness of God is simple and accessible to everyone. Knowledge of him is accessible to no person.

1

> A reasonable and modest person, with a developed but limited mind, feels his limits and does not go beyond them, and within these limits finds the concept of his soul and of his creator, conscious of the impossibility of bringing these concepts to complete clarity and of contemplating them as only a pure spirit could contemplate them. He stops before them with submission and does not touch the veil, satisfied with the consciousness that he stands before a lofty being. Only up to this limit is philosophy useful and necessary. What lies beyond this is idle abstractions, not proper to man, from which the reasonable person abstains and which are foreign to the man of the crowd.

> All the peoples of the world know and honor God; although each clothes him in its own way, under all these garments it is still the same God.

> *Rousseau.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 284 --> A select minority with higher demands of learning, not satisfied with the data of simple common sense, seeks a more abstract God. I do not condemn these people. But it is wrong if, putting itself in the place of all humanity, this minority asserts that God is hidden from people because it does not see him. I admit that it may happen that the cunning tricks of people may for a time convince the majority that there is no God, but this fashion cannot last. And one way or another, man will always need God. If, contrary to the law of nature, the deity were to manifest itself to us with still greater obviousness, I am sure that people opposed to God would invent new subtleties in order to deny him. Reason always submits to what the heart demands.

2

*The most indubitable thing for me in all the world is my consciousness of myself in the present.*

3

> Faith in God is as proper to man's nature as his ability to walk on two legs; in some people this faith may be modified and even die out altogether, but, as a general rule, it exists and is necessary for a reasonable life.

> *After Lichtenberg.*

4

> Equally incomprehensible are the propositions that there is a God and that there is none, that there is a soul in the body and that there is no soul in us, that the world was created and that it was not created.

> *Pascal.*

5

> Religion is from God, and theology is from people.

> *D'Escherny.*

*Live in God, live with God, conscious of him in yourself, and do not try to define him in words*.
