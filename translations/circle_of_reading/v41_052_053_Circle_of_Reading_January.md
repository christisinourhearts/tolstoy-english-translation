22 January

No conditions can make killing cease to be the crudest and most obvious violation of the law of God, expressed both in all religious teachings and in people's conscience.

1

> Where is Christ? Where is his teaching? Where can it be found among Christian peoples? In institutions—it is not there; in laws permeated by the spirit of unjust inequality—it is not there either; in customs permeated by egoism—it is not there. Where, then, is the teaching of Christ? It is in the future, being prepared by work in the depths of human nature; it is in the movement stirring peoples from one end of the earth to the other; it is in the aspirations of pure souls and righteous hearts; it is in the consciousness of all, for all are conscious that what exists cannot continue, because it is evil, the denial of mercy and brotherhood, the inheritance of the race of Cain, something already rejected, destined to be scattered by the breath of God.
>
> *Lamennais.*

2

What is military service? This is what it is. As soon as a young man has grown up and become strong and can help his parents, he is brought to the recruiting office, ordered to undress, examined, and then ordered, on the cross and the Gospel, to swear that he will obey his superiors in everything and will kill all those whom he is ordered to kill. When he has carried out this command, contrary to reason, conscience, and even the letter of the law of Christ expressed in the Gospel, they dress him in a uniform, give him a rifle, train him to shoot, and send him to kill people—his brothers. The people he is ordered to kill have done him no harm; he has never seen them, but he shoots them because he swore to do so on the Gospel—on the very Gospel in which it is said that one must not swear and must not only not kill but not even be angry with one's brother.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 53 -->

3

Military service in general corrupts people by placing those who enter it in conditions of complete idleness, that is, absence of reasonable and useful work, and freeing them from common human duties, in place of which it puts only the conventional honor of regiment, uniform, and banner, and, on the one hand, unlimited power over other people, and on the other, slavish submission to superiors above them.

Their idle, dissolute life acts especially corruptingly upon military men because if a civilian leads such a life, in the depths of his soul he cannot help being ashamed of it. Military men, however, consider that it is as it should be and boast of and take pride in such a life, especially in wartime. “We are ready to sacrifice our lives in war, and therefore such a carefree, merry life is not only forgivable but necessary for us. And so we lead it.”

4

> One person must not kill. If he has killed, he is a criminal, a murderer. Two, ten, a hundred people, if they do it, are murderers. But a state or a people may kill as many as it likes, and this will not be murder but a good, worthy deed. Only gather more people together, and the slaughter of tens of thousands becomes an innocent affair. But exactly how many people are needed for this? That is the question. One person may not steal or rob, but a whole people may. But exactly how many are needed for this? Why must one, ten, a hundred people not violate the laws of God, while very many may?
>
> *A. Ballou.*

*In every human body lives one and the same divine principle*, *and therefore neither an individual person nor gatherings of people can have the right to break this established union of the divine principle with the human body*, *that is, to deprive a person of life.*
