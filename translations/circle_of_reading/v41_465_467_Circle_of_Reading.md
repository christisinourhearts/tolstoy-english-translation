<!-- vol. 41, p. 465 -->

3 July

A person is unfree only to the extent that he places his life in his animal self.

1

> They say that the greatest good for a person is his freedom. If freedom is a good, then a free person cannot be unhappy. So if you see that a person is unhappy, suffers, whines—know that this person is not free: he is certainly enslaved by someone or something.

> If freedom is a good, then a free person cannot be a voluntary slave. And therefore, if you see that a person humbles himself before others, flatters them—know that this person too is not free. He is a slave who is seeking either a dinner, or a profitable post, or something else; in general he is seeking to control what does not belong to him.

> A free person controls only what can be controlled without hindrance. And one can control entirely without hindrance only oneself. And therefore, if you see that a person wants to control not himself but others, then know that he is not free: he has become the slave of his desire to rule over people.

> *Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 466 -->

2

> Without inner freedom outward freedom is worth nothing. What use is it to me if I am not crushed by external violence, but, owing to ignorance, vice, egoism, fear, I do not govern my soul. I will call free only the person who is not shut up in himself or his sect, who overcomes pride, anger, sloth, and is ready to give himself as a sacrifice for the good of humanity.

> *Channing.*

3

> Nothing is done without faith. Doubt kills a person, kills peoples. Why is the liberation of peoples so difficult, so hard, so long? Because they have no faith in their right, in the invincible power of their right. Why do the oppressed classes everywhere groan in expectation of a relief that never comes? Because they have no faith either in themselves or in God, who is always ready to save them, but not without their participation, because the privilege of free beings is to be what they want to be, just as their punishment is to be what others want to make them when they submissively bow under the yoke of injustice and oppression. But even then God does not abandon them. To awaken them he sends the messengers of his mercy, puts his word into them, clothes them with his might—and the world suddenly sways, crowds come running to listen, peoples are stirred, they rise like fermenting dough; a dim notion of a better future appears to their imagination. At the sight of it they tremble, they are filled with a feeling of overflowing life.

> But here come the oppressors too, the Pharisees and the scribes. Confused, alarmed, trembling for their shaken power, they either strangle the envoys of the heavenly father or, if they can, slander both their teaching and their deeds; in the very good done by these envoys they find pretexts for condemning them. They say: “True, we cannot deny this—these men cast out demons, but they cast them out by the power of the prince of demons.” Lift up, lift up your head, and above the darkness that these hypocrites are trying to thicken around you, you will see in the east the sun already rising, which will soon light you with its full light and warm you with its rays.

> *Lamennais.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 467 -->

4

> To become truly free, you must always be ready to give back to God what you have received from him. You must unite your will with the will of God. Only in what is contrary to the will of God can a person be unfree. But desiring only what God desires: truth, love, you cannot be unfree. There is no situation in which you could not manifest truth and love, and therefore there is no situation in which you could be deprived of freedom. But if you do not want this, then all your life you will remain a slave among slaves, even if you had every possible worldly honor, even if you became the king himself.

> *After Epictetus.*

5

> Where there is no freedom, life comes down to the life of an animal.

> *Giuseppe Mazzini.*

6

> *Do not be the slave of one or of a few. By making yourself the slave of all, you make yourself the friend of all.*

> *After Cicero.*

7

> Human dignity and freedom are proper to us. Let us then preserve them, or die with dignity.

> *Cicero.*

If you feel yourself unfree, look for the cause in yourself.
