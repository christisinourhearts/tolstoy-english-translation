<!-- vol. 41, p. 345 -->

25 May

A person's morality can be seen in his attitude toward words.

1

> If anyone among you thinks that he is pious, and does not bridle his tongue, but deceives his own heart, that man's piety is empty.

> *James 1:26.*

2

> Attention to the faults of others comes from neglect of oneself. Often, in condemning our neighbor, we fall into the same errors that we have just censured. Whoever is not occupied with the salvation of his soul and does not try to correct himself easily falls into temptation and is carried away by a bad example.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 346 -->

3

If you have learned of your neighbor's weaknesses, do not tell anyone about them.

4

> Do not repeat hurtful and insulting words, do not speak either to a friend or to an enemy about your neighbor's faults, and do not disclose what you know that is bad in his conduct. When you hear your neighbor condemned, try to stifle it.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

5

There is no more evident harm for a person of quick mental abilities than the temptation of witty condemnation and mockery of one's neighbor.

6

Witty condemnation is a corpse under a sauce. Without the sauce you would turn away in disgust, but under the sauce you swallow it without noticing.

7

*Never listen to those who speak ill of others and well of you.*

Not to think ahead about what you will say is possible only when you feel calm, kind, and loving. But the less calm, the less good-natured, and the more irritated you are, the more you must beware of sinning in words.
