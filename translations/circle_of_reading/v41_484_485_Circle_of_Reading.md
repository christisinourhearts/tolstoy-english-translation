<!-- vol. 41, p. 484 -->

8 July

The feeling that resolves all the contradictions of human life and gives man the greatest good is known to all people. This feeling is love.

1

> How is one to conquer a bad mood? First of all by *humility:* when you know your weakness, why be irritated when others point it out? It is unkind on their part, but they are right. Then by *reasoning:* in the end you will still remain what you were, and if you respected yourself too much, then you only have to change your opinion of yourself; a neighbor's discourtesy leaves us just as we were. But above all by *forgiveness:* there is only one means of not hating those who do us evil and wrongs—it is to do them good, to conquer one's anger with kindness; you will not change them by this victory over your feelings, but you will curb yourself.

> *Amiel.*

2

> What is the worth of eyes in which there is no kindness? Kindness is true wealth. Both the good and the wicked own property. Stand on the true path, reflect, and be kind; even if you have studied the rules of all religious teachings, only kindness will give you the good. He in whose soul kindness lives will never enter the realm of darkness and sorrow. No evil will befall him who is kind and serves all beings.

> *Indian Kural.*

3

Love destroys death and turns it into an empty phantom; it also turns life from senselessness into something meaningful and makes happiness out of unhappiness.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 485 -->

4

> Nothing can draw a poisoned sting out of a wound except the balm of silent and attentive mercy. Why allow oneself to be irritated at human malice, at ingratitude, at envy, even at treachery? There will never be an end to bickering, complaints, punishments. The simplest thing is to wipe everything away. Offenses, reproaches, outbursts trouble the soul. One must have a means of healing from these evils. Fire purifies everything in the material world; love—in the spiritual world.

> *Amiel.*

5

> *If you are not consciously kind to all, you will often be unconsciously cruel to many.*

> *John Ruskin.*

6

Love takes a man out of himself, out of his personality, and therefore, if the personality suffers, love delivers from suffering.

7

The less love, the more a man is subject to the torment of suffering; the more love, the less the torment of suffering; and a fully reasonable life, all of whose activity is manifested only in love, excludes the possibility of any suffering. The torment of suffering is only that pain which people feel in their attempts to break the chain of love that unites human life with the life of the world.

When things are hard for you, when you are afraid of people and of yourself, when you have become entangled in reasonings and affairs, say to yourself: I will love those with whom life brings me together, and try to do this, and you will see how everything will pass, grow lighter, come untangled, and you will have nothing either to desire or to fear.
