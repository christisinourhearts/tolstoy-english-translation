## III LETTER OF THE PEASANT OLKHOVIK, WHO REFUSED MILITARY SERVICE

“On the 15th day of October of the year 1895 I was called up for military duty. When my turn came to draw a lot, I said that I would not draw a lot. The officials looked at me, then talked among themselves and asked me why I would not draw.

I answered that it was because I would neither swear the oath nor take a rifle.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 502 --> They said that that business would come later, but the lot had to be drawn.

I refused again. Then they told the elder to draw the lot. The elder drew; it came out No. 674. They wrote it down.

The military commander comes in, calls me into the office, and asks: ‘Who taught you all this, that you don't want to swear the oath?’

I answered: ‘I learned it myself, reading the Gospel.’

He says: ‘I don't think you understood the Gospel that way yourself; why, everything in it is hard to understand; to understand it you have to study a lot.’

To that I said that Christ did not teach wisdom, because the simplest unlettered people, even they understood his teaching.

Then he told a soldier to take me to the detachment. The soldier and I went to the kitchen and had dinner there.

After dinner they started asking me why I didn't swear the oath.

I said: ‘Because in the Gospel it says: do not swear at all.’

They were surprised; then they asked: ‘Is that really in the Gospel? Go on, find it.’

I found it, read it; they listened.

‘Even if it's there, still you can't not swear the oath, because they'll torment you to death.’

I said to that: ‘Whoever loses the earthly life, he will inherit eternal life.’

On the 20th they put me in a line with other young soldiers and told us the soldiers' rules. I told them that I would not do any of that. They asked: ‘Why?’

I said: ‘Because, as a Christian, I will not bear arms and defend myself from enemies, because Christ commanded that enemies too be loved.’

They said: ‘What, are you the only Christian? Why, here we are, Christians too.’

I said: ‘I don't know anything about others; I only know about myself, that Christ said to do what I am doing.’

He said again: ‘If you won't do the drill, I'll let you rot in prison.’

To that I said: ‘Do whatever you like with me, but I will not serve.’

Today the commission inspected. The general said to the officers: ‘What convictions this milksop has found, that he refuses <!-- vol. 41, p. 503 --> service! Some millions serve, and he alone refuses. Give him a good flogging with the birch, then he'll drop his convictions.’”

Olkhovik was arrested and exiled to the Yakutsk region.
