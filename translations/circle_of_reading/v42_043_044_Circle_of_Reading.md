<!-- vol. 42, p. 43 -->

14 September

Violence is especially harmful in that it always clothes itself in outward grandeur and thereby inspires respect for what ought to arouse only revulsion.

1

> He who compels us by force seems to deprive us of our rights, and we therefore hate him. We love, as our benefactors, those who know how to persuade us. It is not the wise but the coarse, unenlightened man who resorts to violence. To use force, one needs many accomplices; to persuade, one needs none. He who feels enough strength in himself to govern minds will not resort to violence: why should he do away with a man of other views, when it is in his own interest to win him over to his side by friendly persuasion.

> *Conversations of Socrates.*

2

People who have power are convinced that only violence moves and guides people, and therefore they boldly use violence to maintain the existing order. But the existing order is held together not by violence but by public opinion, whose action is disrupted by violence. And therefore the activity of violence weakens, disrupts the very thing it wants to maintain.

3

> Man is created as little for compelling as for obeying. People are corrupted by each other through these two habits: here stupefaction, there insolence, and nowhere true human dignity.

> *Considerant.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 44 -->

4

> Our life would become beautiful if only we saw all its baseness.

> *Thoreau.*

5

> From the fact that it is possible to subject people to justice by violence, it by no means follows that it is just to subject people by violence.

> *Pascal.*

6

> Unjust is the man who does anything by means of violence; no, only he who distinguishes both paths—of truth and of untruth—who teaches others and guides them not by violence but by justice, who is faithful to truth and reason—only he will be called truly righteous.

> Not he is a sage who makes good and beautiful speeches, but he who is patient, free from hatred, and free from fear—only he is a truly wise man.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

*All violence is contrary to reason and love. Take no part in it.*
