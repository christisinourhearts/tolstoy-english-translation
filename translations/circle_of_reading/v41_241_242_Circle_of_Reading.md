<!-- vol. 41, p. 241 -->

12 April

At a certain degree of going deep into himself, a person becomes conscious of something superhuman in himself.

1

> God exists for the very reason that we exist. Call it God or whatever you like, but it is beyond dispute that there is life in us that was not created by us but given to us, and call its source God or whatever you like.

> *Mazzini.*

2

> Imagination creates phantoms and fears them—this is forgivable in it because it is imagination. But for the mind to submit to and fear the reasonings that it generates is unforgivable in it, because it cannot and must not be deceived. And yet the superstition of magnitude is a deception of the mind, which creates the concept of space. But the created cannot be greater than the creator, the son is not greater than the father. Here a correction is needed. The mind must free itself from space, which gives it a false concept of itself. But this liberation is possible only when we learn to see space in the mind instead of seeing the mind in space. How so?—By returning space to its basic property. Space is a condition of the mind's activity.

> Therefore God is everywhere, without occupying billions of cubic versts, neither a hundred times smaller nor a hundred times larger.

> In consciousness the world has no space, but in reasoning about it heavens within heavens are needed.

> Time and number, too, are not needed for consciousness, but are found only in the mind; therefore man is not less but greater than the most enormous space and infinite time and number.

> *Amiel.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 242 -->

3

> When I, standing in the forest, look at a bug that is crawling on the ground, trying to hide from me among the spruce needles, and ask myself why it is so timid and hides its head from me, when I am perhaps ready to be its benefactor and to impart to it and to all its kind perhaps very joyful news—I involuntarily recall that great benefactor who stands over me, a human bug.

> *Thoreau.*

4

*There is no God only for the one who does not seek him. Only begin to seek him—and he is in you and you are in him.*

5

> To seek God is the same as to scoop up water with a net. While you are scooping—the water is in the net. As soon as you have pulled it out—you have caught nothing.

> While you seek God with thought and deed—God is in you. As soon as you have decided that you have found God, and have settled down—you have lost him.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

6

It is astonishing how I could fail to see before that indubitable truth that behind this world and our life in it there is someone, something that knows what this world exists for, and we in it, like bubbles in boiling water, leap up, burst, and vanish.

7

> In this great union of beings, where everything quietly speaks of God, the unbeliever sees only eternal silence.

> *Rousseau.*

If a person is not conscious of God, he has no right whatever to conclude from this that he does not exist.
