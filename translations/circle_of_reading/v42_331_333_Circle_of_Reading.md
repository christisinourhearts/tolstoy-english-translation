<!-- vol. 42, p. 331 -->

9 December

A person's calling is service to all, to all people, and not such service to some in which the doing of evil to others is inevitable.

1

For a Christian, love for the fatherland stands as a barrier to love for one's neighbor. And as in the ancient world love for the family had to be sacrificed to love for the fatherland, so in the Christian world love for the fatherland must give way to love for one's neighbor.

2

> If the blindness of people who do not try to learn the meaning of their life is unnatural, still more terrible is the blindness of those who believe <!-- vol. 42, p. 332 --> in God and live badly. Almost all people are in one blindness or another.

> *Pascal.*

3

> If a person has lost true nature—anything at all becomes his nature; in just the same way, if the true good is lost—anything at all becomes his good.

> *Pascal.*

4

> The last refuge of a scoundrel is patriotism.

> *Johnson.*

5

> Patriotism is not a virtue: to sacrifice one's life for the sake of the outlived superstition of the state cannot be our duty.

> *Theodorus.*

6

> Patriotism in our time is put forward as a pretext for justifying both every social evil and personal baseness. It is instilled into a person that for the good of his country he should renounce everything that makes his country worthy of respect: for the sake of patriotism a person must submit to every shameful deed which, corrupting private persons, leads the whole people to ruin.

> *Beecher.*

7

People do much evil for the sake of self-love; they do still more evil for the sake of the family; but the most terrible evil deeds—espionage, exactions from the people, and terrible killings, wars—are committed by people for the sake of patriotism, and those who commit them pride themselves on these evil deeds.

8

To preach, in our time of worldwide interaction among peoples, exclusive love for one's own people and constant readiness <!-- vol. 42, p. 333 --> for war with another people is the same as to preach, in our time, among peaceful people, exclusive love for one's own village, and in every village to gather troops and build fortresses. Love for one's own exclusive fatherland, which formerly united the people of one country, in our time, when people are already united by means of communication, trade, industry, science, art, and above all by moral consciousness, no longer unites but divides people.

9

Love for one's fatherland, like love for one's family, is a natural property, but, like love for the family, it can in no way be a virtue, but it can be a vice when it crosses those limits at which love for one's neighbor is violated.

Patriotism is so unnatural to the people of our time that it can be aroused only by suggestion.

This is just what is done by governments and by those to whom patriotism is profitable: they instill it in those who no longer feel it and to whom it is unprofitable. One must be on guard against this deception.
