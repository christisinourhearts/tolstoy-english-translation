<!-- vol. 42, p. 343 -->

15 December

Truth in itself is not a virtue, but it is a necessary condition of everything good.

1

There is the conscious lie, when a person knows that he is saying what is untrue, but says it because it is profitable to him, and there is the involuntary lie, when a person would like to tell the truth but does not know how.

2

*Only error needs artificial support. Truth can stand alone.*

3

> All goods are nothing before the good of truth; all sweetness is nothing before the sweetness of truth; the bliss of truth immeasurably surpasses all joys.

> *Buddhist wisdom.*

4

A person cannot be wholly truthful, because the most varied and opposite strivings are always struggling in him, growing stronger and growing weaker, and a person is often powerless to express them exactly.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 344 -->

5

Errors hold only for a time, but truth always remains what it was, even after all the attacks, obscurings, tricks, sophisms, evasions, and every kind of lie.

6

One must constantly learn to do, to speak, and to think the truth. Only he who begins to learn this will understand how far we are from the truth.

7

A lie is harmful in all the affairs of everyday life: to sell old for fresh, spoiled for sound, to promise to repay a debt and know that you will not, and the like; but every such lie is nothing before the lie in spiritual matters: to pass off as God what is not God, to assure people that what gives no good to the soul is saving for the soul, to pass off as sin and bad what is righteous and good, and the like. In such matters is the chief evil of untruth.

There is no sinless person, and there is no wholly truthful person. The difference between people is not that one is wholly sinless and truthful and another wholly sinful and false, but that one strives for the greatest sinlessness and truthfulness, and another does not strive for it.
