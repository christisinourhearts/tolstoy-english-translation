<!-- vol. 41, p. 119 -->

21 February

There was a time when people ate one another; a time came when they stopped doing this, but they still go on eating animals. Now a time has come when people are more and more giving up this terrible habit too.

1

> How strange that the various societies for the protection of children and of animals are entirely indifferent to vegetarianism, whereas it is precisely the eating of meat that in most cases is the cause of the cruelty they want to fight by means of punishment. The fulfillment of the law of love can restrain cruelty more strongly than the fear of criminal liability. There is hardly any difference between the cruelty that is committed in torturing and killing in order to satisfy one's feeling of anger and the cruelty that is committed in torturing and killing in order to make use of the meat of animals, by feeding on which people kindle in themselves the chief hearth of cruelty.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

2

> The great trinity of curses: tobacco, wine, and the meat of animals. From this terrible trinity come both great calamities and great ruin. Falling into the power of this trinity, people come close to animals and lose both the human likeness and the best good of human life: a clear understanding and a good heart.

> *After Hills.*

3

> *In the delusion that our actions toward animals have no moral significance, or, to speak in the language of generally accepted morality, that there are no duties toward animals, in this delusion an outrageous coarseness and barbarity show themselves*.

> *Schopenhauer.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 120 -->

4

> A traveler came up to African cannibals at a time when they were eating some meat. He asked them what they were eating. They answered that this meat was human.

> “Can you really eat this?” cried the traveler. “Why not? With salt it's very tasty,” the Africans answered him. They were so used to what they were doing that they could not even understand what the traveler's exclamation referred to.

> In the same way meat-eaters do not understand the indignation that vegetarians feel at the sight of pigs, lambs, and oxen eaten only because this meat is tasty with salt.

> *After Lucy Mallory.*

5

The killing and eating of animals comes about chiefly because people have been assured that animals are destined by God for people's use and that there is nothing bad in killing animals. But this is untrue. In whatever books it may be written that it is not a sin to kill animals, it is written more clearly than in books, in the hearts of all of us, that an animal must be pitied just as a person must, and we all know this if we do not stifle conscience in ourselves.

Do not be troubled that when you give up meat food all those close to you at home will attack you, condemn you, laugh at you. If meat-eating were a matter of indifference, meat-eaters would not attack vegetarianism; they are irritated because in our time they are already conscious of their sin but do not yet have the strength to free themselves from it.
