<!-- vol. 42, p. 314 -->

7 December

The whole bodily life of man is a series of changes imperceptible to him but open to observation. But the beginning of these changes, accomplished in earliest childhood, and their end—in death—are inaccessible to human observation.

1

> Truly, truly I say to you: if a grain of wheat, having fallen into the earth, does not die, it will remain alone; but if it dies, it will bear much fruit; he who loves his soul will destroy it, and he who hates his soul in this world will keep it for life eternal.

> *John 12:24–25.*

2

> Life constantly changes its outward appearance. Only an ignoramus, who sees no deeper than the surface of things, thinks that life is annihilated when it disappears in some one form. For if it disappears in some one form, it is only <!-- vol. 42, p. 315 --> in order to appear again in another. The caterpillar disappears and appears again in the form of a butterfly, the child disappears, but in its place appears a youth; the animal man disappears and appears again as a spiritual man.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

3

> What is an acorn but an oak deprived of its branches, leaves, trunk, and roots, that is, of all forms, all particularities, but concentrated in its essence, in its productive power, which can again win back all that it has cast off? This impoverishment, then, is only an outward contraction. To return to one's eternity—that is just what it means to die. To die means not to be annihilated, but to return to one's potentiality.

> *Amiel.*

4

> Have we not already risen once from a state in which we knew less about the present than we now know about the future. As our preceding state is to the present one, so the present one is to the future.

> *Lichtenberg.*

5

> Does change really frighten you? Why, nothing in the world is done without change. You cannot heat water without a transformation taking place in the firewood; nourishment is impossible without a change in the food. The whole life of the world is nothing other than change. Understand that the transformation awaiting you has exactly the same meaning, that it is necessary by the very nature of things. One must take care of one thing only: not to do anything contrary to true human nature, and to act in all things as it directs.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

6

Everything in the world grows, blossoms, and returns to its root. Return to one's root means calm in accordance <!-- vol. 42, p. 316 --> with nature. In accordance with nature means eternal; therefore the destruction of the body holds no danger at all.

*Laozi.*

*Death is a change of the form with which our spirit is joined. One must not confuse the form with what is joined to it.*
