<!-- vol. 42, p. 309 -->

4 December

It is not by chance that it is said that the whole law is to love God and one's neighbor. Love of one's neighbor is a particular case. A neighbor may be there and may not be; God always is. And therefore a person alone in a desert, in prison, can fulfill the law by loving God and all his manifestations accessible to him, if only in memories, in imaginings, in thoughts.

1

Remember that in every person lives the spirit of God—the same one that gives you life, and therefore not only love, but revere as a holy thing the soul of every person.

2

> A horse saves itself from an enemy by its swift running, and it is unhappy not when it cannot crow like a cock, but when it has lost what was given to it—its swift running.

> In just the same way a person becomes unhappy not when he cannot overpower a bear, or a lion, or wicked people, but when he loses what was given to him—kindness and good sense. It is such a person who is truly unhappy and deserving of pity.

> What is pitiable is not that a person has died, that he has lost his money, his house, his estate: all this does not belong to a person. What is pitiable is when a person loses his true property—his human dignity.

> *Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 310 --> A dog has scent; when it is deprived of what was given to it—its scent—it is unhappy, and it is not unhappy when it cannot fly.

3

*Do nothing contrary to your conscience, either before people or alone.*

4

> In our time people forget that before all else they must reverently honor the human being in themselves. And the highest property of a person consists in this, that, with balance of soul, his consciousness is able, entering into communion with the source of understanding, to merge with boundless spiritual life. And meanwhile people, instead of drawing spiritual food directly from this source, prefer, like beggars, to beg from one another a mug apiece of stagnant water.

> *Emerson.*

5

> The most wretched of us still possesses some gift or other, and however ordinary this gift may apparently be, yet, constituting our particularity, it can, if rightly applied, become a gift for all humanity.

> *John Ruskin.*

Besides duties to one's neighbor, every person has a duty to himself, as to a son of God.
