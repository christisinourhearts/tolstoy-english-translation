<!-- vol. 41, p. 523 -->

23 July

Effort is a necessary condition of moral perfecting.

1

> Virtue lies in fulfilling what you consider your duty; but this fulfilling must never turn into <!-- vol. 41, p. 524 --> a habit, and when you have grown used to fulfilling what you consider your duty, a new demand of duty must arise from the soul of man.

> *After Kant.*

2

> As sentries watchfully guard a fortress, guard it both around the wall and inside it, so a man must keep alert guard over himself, never for a single moment losing sight of himself, especially in his relations with people; whoever lets the decisive minute in life slip out of sight will inevitably set out on the path to the underworld.

> *Dhammapada.*

3

> Hopeless is the condition of the one who in his misfortunes reproaches not himself but fate, thereby asserting his own self-satisfaction.

> “We would be kind and meek if we were not irritated; we would be devout if we were not so busy. I would be patient if I were healthy; I would astonish the world if I had fame.”

> If we cannot make good and holy the situation in which we find ourselves, then we will make no situation good and holy.

> The difficulties of our situation are given to us so that we may smooth them away, destroy them by our kindness and firmness; the gloom of our situation is given to us so that we may light it up with the divine light of inner, spiritual work; sorrows—so that we may bear them patiently and trustingly; dangers—so that we may show our courage; temptations—so that we may overcome them by our faith.

> *Martineau.*

4

> *How cruelly mistaken are those who think that they can live a lofty spiritual life while their bodies stagnate in idleness and luxury. The body is always the soul's first pupil.*

> *Thoreau.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 525 -->

5

> Nothing will be counted to a man but his effort. Only in his effort does a man appear in his true light.

> *The Quran.*

We are angry at circumstances, we are grieved, we want to change them; and yet all possible circumstances are nothing other than indications of how one must act in which situations. You are healthy—make an effort to use your strength in serving people; you are sick—make an effort not to trouble people with your illness. You are rich—make an effort to rid yourself of wealth; you are poor—make an effort to demand nothing from people. You are wronged—make an effort to love those who wronged you. You have wronged—make an effort to undo the evil you have done.
