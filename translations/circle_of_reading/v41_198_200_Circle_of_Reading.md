<!-- vol. 41, p. 198 -->

28 March

Wisdom is attained by inner work in solitude and by the same kind of work on oneself in dealings with people.

1

> Listen, be attentive, but speak little.

> Never speak if you are not asked, but if you are asked, answer at once and briefly, and do not be ashamed if you must confess that you do not know what you are asked about.

> Do not argue for the sake of arguing.

> Do not boast.

> Do not seek the higher place, and do not accept it even if it is offered to you.

> In indifferent matters, that is, those in which you do not have to betray your duty, conform to the habits and wishes of those with whom you live.

> Do not try to do anything that is not part of your obligation and does not contribute to the comfort of those who live with you, or else this habit will become an idol. And every person must smash his idols.

> *Sufi wisdom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 199 -->

2

> Only with the eyes of others can one see one's own faults.

> *Chinese proverb.*

3

> Every person has in another a mirror in which he can clearly make out his own vices, faults, and bad sides of every kind; but for the most part we act in this like a dog that barks at a mirror on the supposition that it sees there not itself but another dog.

> *Schopenhauer.*

4

> “Know yourself” is the fundamental rule. But do you really think that one can know oneself by peering into oneself? No. You can know yourself only by looking closely at what is outside you. Compare your powers with the powers of others, your interests with their interests; try to think of your own interests as something secondary; bow before the merits of others, proceeding from the conviction that there is probably nothing special in you.

> *John Ruskin.*

5

> If three of us come together, I will surely find two teachers. A good person I will try to imitate, and looking at a bad person, I will try to correct myself.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

6

> I have learned much from my mentors, still more from my comrades, but most of all from my pupils.

> *The Talmud.*

7

> When you see a holy person, think about yourself: can you not be the same too? When you see a debauched person, think about yourself: are not the same vices in you too?

> *Chinese wisdom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 200 -->

8

“Be careful, when you want to strike at the devil in a person, not to hit the God in him.” This means that, when condemning a person, do not forget that the spirit of God is in him.

9

“Quarrel with the sin, make peace with the sinner.” Hate what is bad in a person, but love the person.

10

True love, not in words but in deed, not only cannot be foolish, but love alone gives true insight and wisdom.

*Living with people, do not forget what you have learned in solitude. And in solitude think over what you have learned from your dealings with people.*
