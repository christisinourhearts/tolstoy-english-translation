<!-- vol. 41, p. 143 -->

3 March

What further reward is there for a good deed? The reward has already been received in the joy that a person experienced in doing the good deed. Every reward diminishes this joy.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 144 -->

1

> He who does good to another does the most good of all to himself—not in the sense that he will be rewarded for it; but in that the consciousness of the good done already gives great joy.

> *Seneca.*

2

> A man of holy life prayed to God for people like this: “O God! Be merciful to the wicked, because to the good you have already been merciful: it is well with them because they are good.”

> *Saadi.*

3

> To do good and demand a reward means to destroy the action and the power of good.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

4

> Very often there remains for us no trace of the favors done to us by others, but the services done by us to others will never remain without a trace.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

5

> *Let your left hand not know what your right is doing.*

> *Matthew 6:3.*

6

> Some people, if they do someone a service, expect a reward or gratitude for it for themselves; others, although they do not expect a reward and gratitude, still do not forget what they have done, and consider those to whom they have done good their debtors. But true good is good only when it is done not for another but for oneself, and the person who has done it does not seek a reward, but does good as a fruit tree does when it grows its fruits and is fully content that these fruits are used by those who need them.

> *After Marcus Aurelius.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 145 -->

7

> Treat people well in the expectation of gaining an advantage from their gratitude, and you will not receive the slightest return for your kindness; but treat them well without any self-interested considerations—and you will attain both gratitude and benefit. And so in everything: “Whoever wants to save his soul—will lose it, but whoever loses his soul for my sake will find it.”

> *John Ruskin.*

8

> Practice every virtue and avoid every vice. One virtue draws others after it, one vice—other vices. The reward of virtue is virtue, the retribution for vice is vice.

> *Benchasai.*

It is joyful to do good. The joy increases when you know that no one knows about the good you have done.
