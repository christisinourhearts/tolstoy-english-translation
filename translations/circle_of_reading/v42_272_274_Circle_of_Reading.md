<!-- vol. 42, p. 272 -->

22 November

The less a person is satisfied with himself and occupied with improving his inner life, the more he shows himself in outward social life.

1

We are so used to reasonings about how it is proper for some people to arrange the life of other people—of people in general—that such reasonings do not seem strange to us. And yet <!-- vol. 42, p. 273 --> such reasonings could never exist among religious and therefore free people. Such reasonings are the consequence of recognizing the lawfulness of the governing of people by one man or by several.

So reason both the governors themselves and the people who submit to them.

This error is not only entirely senseless, because there is no reason why some people—and the majority—should submit to the will of others—of a minority of the least moral people: it is also especially harmful in that it weakens in all people the consciousness of the necessity of correcting themselves, whereas this is the only real means of acting for good upon other people.

2

The aim of representative government is not that greater justice should be achieved, but that people, submitting to bad government, should not have the right to complain of it.

3

> If the elders (people made wise by experience) tell you: “destroy,” and the young: “build,” then destroy and do not build, for the destroying of the elders is building, and the building of the young is destroying.

> *The Talmud.*

4

> All constitutional charters lead to nothing; they are contracts between a master and slaves: the task is not that the slaves should be better off, but that there should be no slaves.

> *Herzen.*

5

> *Not only does one person have no right to dispose of many, but many have no right to dispose of one.*

> *Vladimir Chertkov.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 274 -->

6

> What is truth?—Truth for most people means plausibility—something that can be measured by the number of votes received in its favor.

> *Thomas Carlyle.*

7

> The measure of justice cannot be a majority of votes.

> *Schiller.*

8

> Not only the saber and the rifle do we assign to that shelf in the museum where the instruments of torture lie; but we cannot fail to know that soon the police system and the ballot box too will follow them.

> *Ernest Crosby.*

9

> When I sit here by the sea and listen to the waves breaking with a splash against this shore, I feel free of all obligations, and the peoples of the whole world can revise their constitutions without me.

> *Henry David Thoreau.*

Never build but always plant, because in the first case nature will hinder you, destroying the works of your labor, while in the second it will help your work, granting growth to all that you have planted. So too in the spiritual realm: do what is in accordance with the eternal laws of the nature of man, and not what is in accordance with the temporary institutions of people or only with your own desires.
