<!-- vol. 41, p. 44 -->

18 January

An enlightened person is one who knows his purpose in life.

1

A learned person is one who knows much from books; an educated person is one who has assimilated all the knowledge and methods most widespread in his time; an enlightened person is one who understands the meaning of *his* life.

2

Since humanity has existed, there have always appeared among all peoples teachers who formed the science of what a person most needs to know. This science has always taught what a person's purpose consists in, and therefore what the true good of every person and of all people consists in. Only by this science can one judge the significance of all other knowledge.

The subjects of the sciences are *countless*; and without knowing what the purpose and good of all people consist in, there is no possibility of choosing among this infinite number of subjects, and therefore without this knowledge all other knowledge and arts become, as they have become in our time in the Christian world, an idle and harmful amusement.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 45 -->

3

The only explanation of that insane life, contrary to the consciousness of the best people of all times, which people of our time lead, lies in the fact that the younger generations are taught many of the most difficult subjects: the position of the heavenly bodies, the state of the earth millions of years ago, the origin of organisms, and so on. They are not taught only the one thing that everyone always needs: what the meaning of human life is, how one ought to live it, and what the wisest people of antiquity thought about this question and how they resolved it. Not only are the younger generations of our world not taught this, but instead, under the name of the law of God, they are taught the most obvious absurdities, in which even those who teach them do not themselves believe. Under the whole building of our life, instead of stone, bubbles inflated with air have been placed. How could this building not collapse?

4

The most ordinary phenomenon of our time is to see people who consider themselves learned, educated, and enlightened, knowing a countless number of useless things, stagnate in the deepest ignorance, not only not knowing the meaning of their life, but being proud of this ignorance. And conversely, it is no less ordinary to meet among semiliterate and illiterate people, who know nothing about the chemical table, parallaxes, and the properties of radium, people who are truly enlightened, who know the meaning of their life and do not pride themselves on it, but only pity those supposedly enlightened people who, through boundless self-confidence, make their ignorance indestructible.

*The one thing needful in science* — *knowledge of* *how a person should live. And this knowledge is accessible to everyone*.
