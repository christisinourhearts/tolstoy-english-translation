13 September

A wise man does not seek a change in his situation, because he knows that the fulfillment of the law of God—the law of love—is possible in any situation.

1

> *The wise man seeks everything in himself, the fool—everything in others.*

> *Confucius.*

2

> I did not complain of fate and did not murmur. But once, when I was without shoes and had nothing to buy footwear with, I murmured. I then went with a heavy heart into the great mosque in Kufa, and there in the mosque I saw a man without legs. And I gave thanks to God that I had both legs and only lacked shoes to put on them.

> *Madi.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 42 -->

3

> Without going out of the gates and without looking out of the window, the reasonable man knows what he needs to know, being conscious of the heavenly reason in himself. The farther you go, the less you know. Therefore the reasonable man, without traveling, has knowledge, without seeing things, defines them, and, without working, accomplishes what is great.

> *Laozi.*

4

> A person should never be grieved by two things: by what he can help, and by what he cannot help.

> *“Every body's Book of Proverbs and Quotations.”*

5

If a person is dissatisfied with his situation, he can change it by two means: either improve the conditions of his life, or improve the state of his soul. The first is not always in his power, the second always is.

6

> Treat your thoughts as you would guests, and your desires—as children.

> *Chinese proverb.*

7

> Man is unhappy because there lives in him the infinite, which, in spite of all his efforts, he cannot bury under the temporal.

> *Carlyle.*

8

> Try to establish in yourself an inner quiet and that perfect silence both of the lips and of the heart, when we are no longer occupied with our imperfect thoughts and vain judgments, but when God himself speaks in us and we, in simplicity of heart, listen for the expression of his will, so that, in our silence, we might fulfill his will alone.

> *Longfellow.*

The more a person is dissatisfied with people and circumstances, and the more satisfied with himself, the farther he is from wisdom.
