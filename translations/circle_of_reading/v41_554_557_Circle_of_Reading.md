7 August

A vain man wants to be praised. In order to be praised, he needs to be what people consider good. But people consider good what pleases them. And what pleases them is to be considered good. And therefore there is no occupation more vain than the satisfaction of vanity.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 555 -->

1

> Whoever is ashamed of what is not shameful and is not ashamed of what is shameless, following false opinion, enters upon the evil path of ruin.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

2

> The vain man is so full of himself that there is no room in him for anything else.

> *Penn.*

3

> You will wear yourself to death and still not impress people.

> *Proverb.*

4

> “One must act as others act.” This rule almost always means that one must act badly.

> *La Bruyère.*

5

> One man asked another why he did work he had no sympathy with.

> — Because everyone does so, — the other answered.

> — Well, suppose not everyone, — said the first, — because here I don't do it, and some others too, whom I can point out to you.

> — Well, if not everyone, then very many, the greater part of people.

> — And tell me, please, — the first asked again, — which people are there more of in the world, intelligent or foolish?

> — Foolish, of course!

> — And if so, then it follows that in imitating the majority you are imitating the foolish.

> *K.*

6

> To make intelligent people take us for something other than what we really are is in most cases more difficult than actually to become what we want to appear.

> *Lichtenberg.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 556 -->

7

> A man is as presumptuous as he is limited in understanding.

> *Pope.*

8

> Both now and of old they ridicule the one who is silent, and the one who speaks much, and the one who speaks little—there is no one on earth who is not censured.

> There never has been, there never will be, and there is not anyone who is always blamed, as there is also no one who is always praised.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

9

There is no falser guide in life than people's opinion.

10

> *It is very difficult not to agree with our self-love and not to find pleasant those who approve of us.*

> *Amiel.*

11

> Human pride is a strange quality, one that does not so easily let itself be suppressed; you have just darned one hole, and before you can look around, the same pride in another form is peeping out of another hole, and if you stop up that one, it is already looking out of a third hole, and so on.

> *Lichtenberg.*

12

> We approve of others insofar as we find them like ourselves; so that often to respect someone means only to make him equal to oneself.

> *La Bruyère.*

13

> True virtue never looks back at its shadow—fame.

> *Goethe.*

To care about human fame, about people's approval, is unreasonable if only because not only do people not all consider one and the same thing good, but often some consider bad the very thing that others consider best.
