<!-- vol. 42, p. 114 -->

4 October

Love gives not only spiritual, inner joy to the one who experiences it, but it is also the chief condition of a joyful worldly life.

1

True love is not love for one exclusive person but a state of the soul, a readiness to love all—it is that state in which alone we are conscious of the divine source of our soul.

2

Do not think that your good disposition toward people is a gift you are making to your neighbors: you are making this gift to yourself.

3

> *Do not try to draw the love of others to yourself: love, and you will be loved.*

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

4

> To turn the bitterness of life's experience into good-heartedness, ingratitude into benefaction, insults into forgiveness—this is the holy alchemy of lofty souls. And this transformation must <!-- vol. 42, p. 115 --> become so habitual, so easy, that it seems natural to people and that we need no approval from people for it.

> *Amiel.*

5

To love means to live the life of the one you love.

6

> The holy man has no feelings of his own. The feelings of the people become his feelings. The good he meets with goodness, and with goodness he also meets the evil. Believers he meets with faith, and with the same faith he meets unbelievers too.

> The holy man, living in the world, is concerned with his relations to people. He feels for all people, and all people turn their ears and eyes to him.

> *Laozi.*

7

Love reveals to us a beneficent secret: to live in harmony with oneself and with all people.

*From “Pious Thoughts.”*

8

> Without love no deed brings benefit, and every deed inspired by love, however small and insignificant it may seem, brings abundant fruit.

> *From “Pious Thoughts.”*

9

> Religion is the highest form of love.

> *Parker.*

The more love a person shows, the more people love him. And the more he is loved, the easier it is for him to love others; that is why love is infinite.
