<!-- vol. 41, p. 381 -->

5 June

The whole external world, as we see it, is such only for us. To say that this world really is such as we see it is the same as saying that there cannot be beings with external senses other than ours.

1

People find strange the thought that everything material is only our representation. “All the same, the table exists, and always... And if I leave the room, it exists, and for everyone it is just the same as it is for me,” people usually say. Well, but when you cross two fingers and roll one little ball, don't you unmistakably feel two? After all, in just the same way, every time I take a ball like that there will be two, and for anyone who takes a ball like that there will be two, and yet there are not two balls. In just the same way the table, too, is a table only for the crossed fingers of my senses, while it is perhaps half a table, one hundredth of a table, perhaps not a table at all, but something altogether different.

2

I look, and I fit the visible lines to a form that lives in my representation. I see something white on the horizon and involuntarily give this white the form of a church. Does not everything we see in this world likewise take on the form that already lives in our representation, carried over from a former life?

<!-- vol. 41, p. 382 -->

3

> I think that the question whether objects outside us have an independent existence is truly devoid of reasonable sense. By our nature we are compelled to say of certain objects of our perception: they are outside us; we cannot do otherwise. The question whether what we recognize as existing really exists is just as absurd as this one, for example: is blue paint really *blue.* We cannot get out of this question. I say that things are outside me, since I am compelled to regard them so; however, this *being outside me* may have any constitution whatever; of this we are not in a position to judge.

> *Lichtenberg.*

4

> The law of life is that the invisible produces the visible. The cause is hidden, the consequences are visible. The cause is infinite, the consequences are finite. To believe in the invisible means to believe in the cause of all power; to acknowledge only the visible means to be useless, unfruitful, transitory, mortal.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

5

> In two ways do we conceive of objects as actually existing: either insofar as we observe them in their relation to a given place and time, or insofar as we think that they are contained in God and flow from the necessity of the divine nature. As such objects we recognize everything spiritual.

> *After Spinoza.*

*The external world in reality, in itself, is not such as we know it. And therefore everything that is material in this world is unimportant. What, then, is important? That which is surely the same everywhere, always, and one and the same for all beings: the spiritual principle of our life.*
