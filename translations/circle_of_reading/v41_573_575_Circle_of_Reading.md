15 August

The joy of life is proper to animals, children, and saints: to animals because they have no reason which, being falsely directed, would deprive them of this joy; to children because their reason has not yet had time to be perverted; to saints because life gives them the very thing they desire: the possibility of perfecting, of drawing near to God.

1

> Past sorrow becomes pleasant in memory, along with past, future, and present pleasures. So only future and present sorrows torment us—a remarkable preponderance in our world on the side of pleasures, increased still further by the fact that we constantly try to procure pleasures for ourselves and in many cases can foresee the enjoyment of them with sufficient certainty, whereas the sorrow still ahead of us can be foretold far more rarely.

> *Lichtenberg.*

2

> You should rejoice, thinking that God has created immeasurably more of the beautiful than the human eye is able to take in, but grieve at the thought that man has done <!-- vol. 41, p. 574 --> far more evil than his soul is able to comprehend and his hand to correct.

> *John Ruskin.*

3

*Happiness is pleasure without remorse.*

4

> Oh, how happy we are, living without hatred for those who hate us; how happy we are if among those who hate we live!..

> Oh, how happy we are, free from greed among the greedy! Among people consumed by greed we live, free from it!..

> Oh, how happy we are, calling nothing our own! We are like the bright gods, steeped in holiness!..

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada.*

5

Hear another parable. There was a certain householder who planted a vineyard, put a fence around it, dug a winepress in it, built a tower, and, having let it out to vinedressers, went away. And when the time of the fruits drew near, he sent his servants to the vinedressers to receive his fruits; the vinedressers, seizing his servants, beat one, killed another, and stoned another. Again he sent other servants, more than the first; and they did the same to them. At last he sent his son to them, saying: they will be ashamed before my son. But the vinedressers, seeing the son, said to one another: this is the heir; come, let us kill him and take possession of his inheritance. And seizing him, they took him out of the vineyard and killed him. So when the master of the vineyard comes, what will he do to these vinedressers? They say to him: he will put these evildoers to an evil death, and will give the vineyard to other vinedressers, who will render him the fruits in their seasons (Matthew 21:33–41).

People have been given a garden which they did not plant, and in order to rejoice in life they need only fulfill the condition on which the garden was handed over to them. They do not fulfill it and say that it is not they who are to blame, but the master of the garden.

6

You seek paradise, you want to be where there is no suffering and no enmity—free your heart, make it pure and bright, and even here you will have the paradise you desire.

If life does not seem to you a great, undeserved joy, that is only because your reason is falsely directed.
