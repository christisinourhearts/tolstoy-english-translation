<!-- vol. 41, p. 346 -->

26 May

We call death both the annihilation of life itself and the minutes or hours of dying. The first is outside our power; the second, dying, is the last and enormously important work of life.

1

> *Death can be an assent, and therefore a moral act. An animal expires; a man must commit his soul to its creator.*

> *Amiel*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 347 -->

2

The great word attributed to Christ is his prayer before death for those who do not know what they do.

3

The words and deeds of a dying person have great power over people, and therefore, however important it is to live well, it is perhaps more important than anything to die well. A bad, unsubmissive death weakens the influence of a good life; a good, submissive, firm death redeems a bad life.

4

When the scenery is carried from one scene to another, it becomes evident that what we took for reality is only a performance. *So too at the moment of death it must become evident to a person what was reality and what was scenery.*

5

A dying person understands everything living with difficulty, but at the same time one feels that he does not understand the living not because he is deprived of the power of understanding, but because he understands something else, something that the living do not and cannot understand and that absorbs him entirely.

6

At the moment of a person's death, the candle by which he was reading the book filled with anxieties, deceptions, grief, and evil flares up with a brighter light than ever before, lights up for him everything that was formerly in darkness, crackles, dims, and goes out forever.

7

That which is dying already partakes in part of eternity. It seems that the dying person speaks to us from beyond the grave. What he says to us seems to us a command. We imagine him almost <!-- vol. 41, p. 348 --> a prophet. Evidently, for one who feels his life departing and the grave opening, the time for significant words has come. The essence of his nature must show itself. The divine that is in him can no longer hide.

*Amiel.*

Prepare for death not in the sense in which preparation is usually understood, when this readiness is taken to lie in performing rites or in caring for worldly affairs, but prepare to die in the best way, that is, to make use of those solemn minutes of death during which a person is already, as it were, in another world, and his words and deeds acquire a special power over those who remain.
