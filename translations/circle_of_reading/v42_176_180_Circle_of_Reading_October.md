## REVELATION AND REASON

*(From the “Profession of Faith of the Savoyard Vicar” by Rousseau)*

What is passed off as the revelation of God only degrades God, ascribing human passions to him. Instead of clarifying the concept of the Great Being, particular dogmas only confuse it; instead of ennobling our concept of God, they degrade it; to the incomprehensible mysteries that surround him they add senseless contradictions, which make man proud, intolerant, cruel; instead of establishing peace on earth, they bring strife. I ask myself why this is, and I do not know what to answer. I see in it only the crime of people and the misfortune of mankind.

I am told that revelation was necessary in order to teach people to serve God; as proof of this they point to the difference of beliefs that are established in the world, and they do not want to see that this difference comes precisely from revelations. As soon as peoples took it into their heads to make God speak, each made him speak in its own way, made him say what <!-- vol. 42, p. 177 --> it itself wanted. If we listened only to what God says in the heart of man, there would be only one religion on earth.

They say: a uniform worship of God is needed; but the worship that God requires for himself is the worship of the heart; it is always uniform if it is sincere. It is mad to imagine that the priest's vestment is so important to God, and the sequence of the words he utters, and the movements he makes before the altar, and his genuflections. No, my friend, stand at your full height, and you will still be close enough to the earth. God wants to be worshiped in spirit and truth, and in this is the duty of all religions, all countries, and all people.

Examining the development of the sects that reign on earth and that mutually reproach one another with falsehood and error, I asked: *which of them, then, is the true one?* and all answered me: mine. Each said: “only I alone and my supporters think rightly; all the rest are in error.” But how do you know that your sect is the true one? — “Because God said so.” — And who told you that God said so? — “My priest; he knows well. My priest tells me to believe as he says—and so I believe; he assures me that all who do not agree with him lie, and I do not listen to them.”

What! — I thought: can it be that truth is not one? And what is just with us may be unjust with you? If the means of conviction of the one who goes by the right path and of the one who is in error is one and the same, then how does the one differ from the other? The choice, then, is a matter of chance; and to blame people for it means to blame them for having been born in this country and not in another.

Either all religions are good and pleasing to God, or there is one that he himself prescribed to people and for the non-acknowledgment of which he punishes; in that case he has surely given unmistakable and evident signs by which this true religion can be recognized. These signs must be equally accessible to all people, great and small, learned and ignorant, Europeans, Indians, Africans, savages.

If there were such a religion on earth, for not professing which eternal torments are imposed, and if anywhere at all there is even one mortal sincerely seeking the truth who was not convinced by its obviousness, then the God of such a religion is the most cruel and unjust tyrant.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 178 --> I am told: “submit your reason.” But only someone who is deceiving me can say this to me. I need proof in order to submit my reason.

Since all people are of one kind with me, everything that a person can comprehend by natural means I too can comprehend, and every person can be mistaken just as I can: if I believe what I am told, I believe not because this or that person says it, but because he proves to me what he says. And therefore the testimonies of people are in essence only the testimonies of my own reason and add nothing to those natural means that God has given me for knowing the truth. Apostles of truth, what can you tell me of which I would not be the judge? — “God himself said it: obey his revelation. This is how God spoke.” — That is a great word, but to whom did he say it? — “He said it to people.” — Why then have I heard nothing about it? — “He charged other people to pass on his words to you.” — Very well: so people will tell me what God said? Would it not have been better if God had told me directly? It would have been no harder for him, and I would have been spared the possibility of deception. — “But he attests the truth of his words by confirming the mission of his apostles.” — In what way? — “By miracles.” — Where are these miracles? — “In books.” — And who made these books? — “People.” — And who saw these miracles? — “People who affirm them.” — What, human testimonies again! It is all people telling me what other people tell. How many people between God and me! Still, let us examine, let us compare. Ah, if God had spared me all this labor, would I have served him with any less zeal!

And note into what a terrible line of reasoning we are now being drawn, what learning is needed in order to distinguish all these antiquities, to examine, weigh, collate prophecies, revelations, facts, all the monuments of faith put forward in all the countries of the world, in order to determine the time, place, authors, and circumstances. What critical precision one must have to sort out genuine monuments from supposed ones, to collate objections with replies, translations with originals; to judge the impartiality of the witnesses, their common sense, their enlightenment, to decide whether anything has been left out, added, or transposed; <!-- vol. 42, p. 179 --> to remove the contradictions that remain, in order to judge the significance of the silence of opponents, of what was said against them, to find out whether what was said against them was known to them, and so on and so on.

Having at last acknowledged these monuments as indubitable, we shall have to pass on to the proofs of the reality of their authors' mission. One must know the probabilities of the possibility of a prediction's being fulfilled without the intervention of the miraculous, one must know the spirit of languages in order to be able to distinguish what in these languages is prediction and what is only an oratorical form, which events are natural and which supernatural, decide to what degree a clever man could deceive the eyes of simple people, astonish even educated people, find the signs of a genuine miracle and that degree of reality at which it must be acknowledged and for not acknowledging it one may be punished, compare the proofs of true and false miracles, find sure rules for distinguishing them, decide, finally, why God, to confirm his word, uses means that themselves need confirmation, exactly as if he were deliberately amusing people and purposely avoiding the means that could convince them.

Let us suppose that the majesty of God can descend so far as to make one man the organ of his sacred will; is it reasonable, is it just to demand that the whole human race obey the voice of this chosen one without having made his calling manifest? Is it just, by way of confirming his calling, to perform a few special signs before a small number of benighted people, while all the rest of people learn about it only by hearsay? If one were to acknowledge as true all the miracles that the common people and benighted people said they had seen, then every sect would be the one true one, and there would be more miracles than natural events. The unchanging order of things confirms me most of all in acknowledging the wisdom of God. If this order allowed so many exceptions, I would not know what to think of it, and I believe in God too firmly to believe in such a number of miracles unworthy of him. And the miracles you speak of were performed in dark corners, in deserts, where it is easy to astonish spectators already prepared to believe everything. Who will tell me how many eyewitnesses are needed to make a miracle credible? If your miracles, <!-- vol. 42, p. 180 --> which are performed to prove your teaching, themselves still need proof, then what are they for? One might as well not have performed them at all.

It now remains to consider the most important question in the teaching proclaimed, namely, that if those who say that God performs miracles also say that the devil often imitates them, then even the best-attested miracles do not decide the matter; and since Pharaoh's magicians, in the very presence of Moses, produced the same miracles that he performed by the will of God, nothing prevented them from asserting in Moses' absence that their miracles were done in the name of God. So that, having proved the teaching by means of miracles, one has, in order not to confuse the work of the devil with the work of God, to prove the miracles by means of the teaching.

A teaching that comes from God must have a sacred, divine character. It must not only clarify our vague notions of the deity, but it must offer us a moral teaching and rules corresponding to the attributes that we ascribe to the deity.

So that if the teaching presented to us only senseless propositions, if it aroused in us a feeling of aversion toward our neighbors, if it presented God to us as angry, jealous, vengeful, partial, hating people, a God of war and battles, always ready to destroy and crush, always speaking of torments, punishments, and boasting that he punishes the innocent, my heart would not be drawn to such a terrible God. Your God is not my God, I would say to these sectarians. A God who begins by choosing for himself one people and excluding the rest of the human race cannot be the common father of people; he who destines the majority of his creatures to eternal torment is not the merciful and good God whom my reason has revealed to me.

As to dogmas, reason tells me that they must be clear, transparent, and striking in their obviousness. Faith is established by understanding; the best of all religions is the clearest; and the one that fills with mysteries and contradictions the worship it preaches by this very fact makes me wary of it. The God I adore is not a God of darkness; he did not give me reason in order to forbid me the use of it. When I am told to submit my reason, I see in this an insult to its creator.
