<!-- vol. 42, p. 267 -->

19 November

Material evil done by a person may in this world not come back upon the one who did it, but the evil feeling that called forth the bad act will surely leave its trace in the person's soul and, one way or another, make him suffer.

1

> The resolve of the sinless is not to cause sorrow to others, even though he could gain great profit by it.

> The resolve of the sinless is not to do evil to those who have done him evil.

> If a person makes even those who hate him without cause suffer, he will in the end have an ineradicable sorrow.

> The punishment of those who do evil consists in making them ashamed of their deeds by the great good done to them.

> What use is the learning of one who does not try to deliver his neighbor from suffering as much as himself.

> If a person wishes in the morning to do evil to another, in the evening evil will visit him.

> *Indian Kural.*

2

> Just as the seasons of themselves arrive at the signs proper to each season, so the acts of all beings of themselves bring these beings into the conditions proper to them.

> The one who is wronged can sleep sweetly and wake joyfully and live joyfully, but the wrongdoer perishes.

> Let no one be wrathful, even if he suffers, let him not offend anyone either by deed or by thought, let him not utter <!-- vol. 42, p. 268 --> a word that may be unpleasant to anyone, because all this hinders the attainment of the good.

> *Indian Manu.*

3

> We must not flee from this LIFE because evil turns out to be bound up with it. Evil is our own doing, the consequence of our ignorance of the true law. Ignorance of the true law makes us unhappy in this life, and it will make us unhappy everywhere. Let us begin, then, by freeing ourselves from our ignorance, and our misfortunes will cease of themselves.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

4

> *An evil person harms himself before he harms others.*

> *Augustine.*

5

> A person can escape the misfortunes sent down by heaven, but from those misfortunes which he brings upon himself there is no escape.

> *Eastern proverb.*

6

There are people who deliberately put themselves in the gloomiest conditions of life in order to have the right to be gloomy. For the same purpose they are always hurriedly and stubbornly busy. The chief pleasure and need of these people consists in this: on meeting the animation of life, to fling their gloomy, stubborn activity in the face of this animation. These people are very unhappy, but they must understand that they alone are to blame for their unhappiness.

7

> He who does not do good when he has the opportunity to do it will suffer.

> *Saadi.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 269 -->

8

> Let every person make himself such as he teaches others to be. He who has conquered himself will conquer others too. Hardest of all is to conquer oneself.

> Each has power only over himself. Evil done by oneself, nurtured by oneself, destroys a person as a diamond breaks a stone. You do evil yourself—you suffer from yourself; you destroy evil yourself—you cleanse yourself of evil.

> Let no one forget his duty in order to please another, whoever he may be.

> *Dhammapada.*

No material good can make up for that damage to the soul which evil committed produces.
