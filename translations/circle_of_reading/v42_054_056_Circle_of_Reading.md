<!-- vol. 42, p. 54 -->

18 September

The essence of life is not in the body but in consciousness.

1

> It is undoubtedly true that if I did not have bones and muscles and other such things, I could not do what I consider just; but it would be completely wrong to assert that the cause of what I do is bones and muscles, and not love of the good. To speak so means not knowing how to distinguish the cause from what is inseparably connected with the cause. Yet most people, groping as in the dark, do this and call the cause what only accompanies the cause.

> *Socrates.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 55 -->

2

> To explain human life not by the force of the spirit but by material force, or even by the joint action of both these forces, only because the spiritual life cannot manifest itself without the material support of the body (by means of food, drink, and air), is just as wrong as it would be to explain the movement of a locomotive not by the pressure of steam but by the movement of the slide valve, which governs the timely admission of steam into the cylinder.

> Indeed, steam could not get from the boiler into the cylinder at the right time if the slide valve did not govern this. But then the slide valve, in its turn, could not move if this movement were not transmitted to it from the very driving axle that rotates as a result of the pressure of the same steam.

> Such too is the seemingly vicious circle into which people often fall who discuss superficially the relation of the soul to the body. The way out of this seemingly vicious circle is very often either not seen at all, and people fall into dualism, or else is found in recognizing matter as the single basis of life.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

3

> The divine lives in us and unceasingly strives toward its source.

> *Seneca.*

4

True rational life consists in recognizing as the cause of one's actions the spiritual principle, which has no cause, and in being guided in life by this principle.

But people who do not recognize the spiritual principle take as the guide of their actions physical causal connection, the very connection that is so complex that we can never know it, since every effect is the effect of an effect.

And therefore such people can never have firm grounds for their actions.

5

> *I call spirit in man that principle which has an independent life in itself and arouses man to conscious life.*

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 56 -->

6

> Having understood the perishability of what is created, you will behold the eternally unchanging.

> *Buddhist wisdom. Dhammapada*

7

> The soul is invisible, but it, it alone, sees.

> *The Talmud.*

8

> Man lives by understanding. Never ascribe the properties of life to the body—the vessel that encloses this inner force. The whole envelope of man is alive only by this force of understanding; without it, it is like a shuttle without a weaver, a pen without a writer.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

The spiritual guides the bodily, and not the other way around. And therefore, to change his state, a person must work on himself in the spiritual realm, not the fleshly.
