<!-- vol. 41, p. 343 -->

24 May

God is not love. Love is only one of the manifestations of God in man.

1

> That we love the children of God, we know by this, when we love God and keep his commandments. For this is love for God, that we keep his commandments. And his commandments are not heavy.

> *1 John 5:2–3.*

2

> One of the scribes, hearing their dispute and seeing that Jesus had answered them well, came up and asked him: which is the first of all the commandments?

> Jesus answered him: the first of all the commandments is: hear, Israel! The Lord our God is the one Lord. And love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your understanding, and with all your strength: this is the first commandment.

> The second is like it: love your neighbor as yourself. There is no other commandment greater than these.

> *Mark 12:28–31.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 344 -->

3

> Epicureanism leads to despair. The philosophy of duty is less cheerless. But salvation lies in the harmony of duty and happiness, in the union of the personal will with the divine will, in the faith that this higher will is governed by love.

> *Amiel.*

4

> Love of man includes justice.

> *Vauvenargues.*

5

> The sage said: my teaching is simple, and its meaning is easy to grasp. It consists wholly in loving one's neighbor as oneself.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

6

The aim of life is the permeation of all its phenomena by love, is the slow, gradual transmutation of an evil life into a good one—is the creation of true life (because true life is only a loving life), is the birth of true, that is, loving life.

7

> Goodness is something self-existent and real. As much goodness as there is in a person, so much life is there in him. The consciousness of this law of laws awakens in the soul a feeling that we call religious and that constitutes our highest happiness.

> *Emerson.*

8

To be happy, one thing is needed—to love, and to love with self-renunciation, to love everyone and everything, to cast a cobweb of love out in all directions: whoever gets caught, take him.

9

What living person does not know that blissful feeling, experienced at least once, and most often in earliest childhood, in which one wants to love everyone: those close to one, and father, and mother, and brothers, <!-- vol. 41, p. 345 --> and wicked people, and enemies, and the dog, and the horse, and the grass; one wants one thing: that all should be well, that all should be happy, and still more one wants oneself to make it so that all should be well, oneself to give oneself, one's whole life, so that all may always be well and joyful. This is it, and this alone is that love in which a person's life lies.

10

If there is strength for activity in you, then let your activity be loving; if there is no strength and you are weak, then let your weakness be loving.

11

The virtue of love of man is not far from us: one has only to wish to have love of man, and it will come to you of itself.

Cleanse your soul of everything that clogs it, and love alone will remain. And this love, seeking an object for itself, will not be content with you, but will choose as its object everything living and that which gives life to the living—God.
