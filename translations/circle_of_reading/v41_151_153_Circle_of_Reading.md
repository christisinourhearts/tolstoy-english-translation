6 March

Love for God is love for itself—love for love. This love is the highest good. Such love does not admit the possibility of not loving any being whatever. As soon as you do not love even one person, you have already lost love for God and the good of this love.

1

> And one of them, a lawyer, tempting him, asked, saying: Teacher! which is the greatest commandment in the law? Jesus said to him: love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your understanding: this is the first and greatest commandment; and the second is like it: love your neighbor as yourself; on these two commandments rests the whole law and the prophets.

> *Matthew 22:35–40.*

2

> Where do all calamities and torments of the soul come from? Only from the attachment of the soul to such things as cannot be constantly possessed, since they are subject to endless changes. People, after all, fear and suffer only for the objects they love, and all offenses, suspicions, enmities—all come only from this love for objects that a person can never fully possess.

> Only love for an eternal and infinite object gives our soul pure joy; and it is precisely toward this good that we must strive with all our strength.

> <!-- vol. 41, p. 152 --> And therefore the highest good of man not only depends on the knowledge of God, but is wholly contained in it. That this is so is clear from the fact that the perfection of man increases according to the degree of perfection of the object that he loves more than all others, and conversely. And therefore it is clear that a man will be the more perfect and the more a partaker of the highest blessedness, the more he loves the most perfect being, that is, God, and the more he gives himself up to this love. Therefore both our highest good and the foundation of our blessedness lie only in the knowledge of God and in love for him.

> Once this is acknowledged, it is obvious that the means of attaining this final goal of human strivings can and must be acknowledged as commandments of God, since the use of these means is prescribed to us by God himself, insofar as he exists in our soul. And therefore the rules of conduct that lead to this goal can also be called divine commandments or the divine law. The divine law, for its part, is wholly and entirely contained in one highest commandment: to love God as the highest good, that is, not from fear of punishment and not from love for some other object, but so that love for him should be the final goal toward which all our actions would be directed.

> The carnal man does not understand this; this rule seems empty to him, because he has only an imperfect notion of God and sees in that highest good which is offered to him nothing tangible, nothing pleasant to the senses, nothing that satisfies the body, the source of his pleasures, since the good offered to him consists only in abstract thought, in reason. But those people who are able to understand that there is nothing in man higher than reason and more perfect than a pure soul without doubt cannot think so.

> If, however, we examine attentively the essence of this divine law, we shall see, first, that this law is universal, that is, common to all people, since it is derived from the nature of all people; second, that this law does not need support by means of any historical narratives whatever; since, deriving this law exclusively from the nature of man, we shall find it in the soul of every person, whether he lives in solitude or among those like himself. Third, we shall see also that this natural divine law of love for God does not require of us any rites of worship, that is, actions of the kind which, being indifferent in themselves, <!-- vol. 41, p. 153 --> are considered good only by virtue of a tradition acknowledged by all; since that natural light of reason which lives in us requires nothing of us that we would not be able to understand and clearly picture to ourselves as good in itself and as a means of attaining blessedness. Fourth, we shall see, finally, also that the reward for fulfilling the divine law will be the law itself, that is, the knowledge of God and a pure, free, and abiding love for him. The punishment for those who break the law, for its part, will be only the deprivation of these goods, the slavery of the body and of a soul always changeable and always troubled.

> *Spinoza.*

3

*Love for one's neighbor without love for God is the same as a plant without roots. Love for a person without love for God is love for those who love us, who are pleasant to us, who are beautiful and cheerful. Such love often turns from love into enmity. But when you love your neighbor because you love God, then you love those too who do not love us, who are unpleasant to us, who are ugly, loathsome in body. This love is real and firm, and such love does not weaken, but the further it goes, the more it is strengthened and the more and more good it gives to the one who experiences it.*

4

People say: I do not understand what it means to love God. But who, then, understands what it means to love anything or anyone whatever? Only he who loves understands this.

If a person does not know what it means to love art, science, how can one explain this to him, if he does not know what art, science, is?

How, then, can one explain to a person what it means to love God, when he not only does not know what God is, but is proud of not knowing it.

They say one must fear God. This is not true. God must be loved. And how can you love someone you fear. Besides, you cannot fear God for this reason too, that God is love. How can you fear love? You must not fear God, but love him. And if you love God and do not fear him, then you will not fear anything in the world.
