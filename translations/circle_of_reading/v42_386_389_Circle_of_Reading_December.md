> ON THE SECT OF THE NAZARENES, WHICH HAS SPREAD IN HUNGARY, SERBIA, AND CROATIA

> The essence of the Nazarene teaching consists in following the teaching of the New Testament, chiefly the Sermon on the Mount. They recognize no hierarchy, no written doctrine, and no organization at all; their teaching is not settled, it changes, it differs in dogmatic respects in different communities—even in one and the same community there are members who believe in their own way. But the moral teaching is one and the same for all. They all lead a strictly moral, abstinent life. They consider the chief rules of life to be: industriousness, meekness in dealing with people, humble bearing of injuries, and abstention from taking part in violence. They do not recognize the courts, do not pay taxes voluntarily, do not swear oaths, and refuse military service, and in general they regard the state as an institution they have no need of.

> Into their communities, which consist chiefly of working people, the Nazarenes accept only those “risen in spirit,” who have repented and are living a new life. Therefore the children of Nazarenes are not considered Nazarenes until they reach the age of discretion and themselves wish to enter the community of believers.

> <!-- vol. 42, p. 387 --> The Nazarenes' refusal of military duty brings on them persecution by the Austrian government. But the Nazarenes hold firmly to their conviction that military service is incompatible with Christianity, and submissively bear the punishments laid on them, without betraying the law of Christ.

> The Nazarenes base their refusals of military duty on the words of Christ: “But I say to you: do not resist evil” (Matthew 5:39) and “love your enemies, bless those who curse you, and pray for those who wrong you” (Matthew 5:44).

> Simple peasant lads, the Nazarenes often astonish their persecutors by the firmness with which they bear every kind of torment. And it is not only recruits who act so, but reservists too, that is, those who became Nazarenes after they had already completed their active service. When they are called up for maneuvers, they refuse to take arms in their hands. Knowing that for this they may be sentenced to life imprisonment, they arrange their household affairs beforehand so that the wife can manage alone, and they take leave of their families as if forever. Their families for the most part sympathize with their martyrdom.

> Thus, several years ago Ioga Radovanov (a Serb) from Vechbas (Bačka), having been enrolled in Pest in the 6th regiment, 6th company, refused to take up arms, saying that his faith did not permit him to. The court sentenced him to imprisonment for 2 years. His elder brother, sentenced to imprisonment in 1894, was already in his 10th year in prison. The mother of these two brothers came to visit the younger one. The authorities did not permit her a visit. She stood in the prison yard and wept. And just then she saw her son's face in one of the windows and at once cried out to him: “Syne moy zlati, ne moy za boga uzeti pushku! (My golden son, for God's sake don't you take a rifle!)”

> At the end of August 1895 the reservists of the Szeged reserve regiment were called up. When rifles were being handed out to the reservists, two of them would not accept the rifles, because, as they said, the Nazarene faith did not permit them this. Captain Olchvari began telling them that God loves the army, that after all they were not going to war now but only to maneuvers, where no one would shed blood. To this the Nazarenes answered: “But we are being led to maneuvers in order to be taught to kill people.”

> <!-- vol. 42, p. 388 --> The captain tried to work on them through fear. He told them that last autumn one Nazarene had behaved in the same way, and he had been punished several times and finally confined for 17 years in a fortress prison.

> — Let them shoot us, — the Nazarenes answered calmly, — but we cannot go against the laws of God.

> Other reservists went to the families of these Nazarenes, and their wives, who were not yet in the sect, begged their husbands with tears to submit to the authorities, but they would not agree. The captain first put them under 10 days of heavy arrest. When they were being led away, they parted from their families weeping.

> — Stay with God, — they said, — we will be buried alive for the sake of the Lord God, for the sake of holy innocence and purity of soul, because people must be like the lambs of God.

> Franko Novak was to serve his military service in Temesvár. When he was led for the first time with the other recruits to the drill ground, he refused to accept arms. Noticing the commotion around Novak, a general who was on the drill ground rode up to the spot and asked what had happened. It was reported to him. The general kindly asked Novak why he did not want to take arms. Novak took a small Gospel out of his pocket and said: “The higher authorities permit this little book to be printed, and they also do not forbid living by the precepts set forth in it. And in this book it is said: ‘Love your neighbor as yourself.’ I do not accept arms because I want to follow the precepts of the savior.”

> The general calmly heard Novak out to the end, then said to him: “Yet in this same little book it is said: Caesar's—to Caesar, God's—to God.”

> Novak was at first confused and silent, but then, having thought it over, took off his military cap, his weapon, his uniform and, having laid all this down, said: “Here, all this is his majesty Caesar's, so I am giving back to him all that is his.”

> In 1897 a decrepit old man came to the town notary of Velika Kikinda. In his hands was a sheet of paper: a certificate of the right to a pension as a disabled soldier of '48.

> — Kindly write down, Mr. Notary, — said the old man, — that I renounce my pension.

> The astonished notary asked the old man:

> “What's this, Vanda, have you found a treasure?”

> <!-- vol. 42, p. 389 --> — True, quite true, Mr. Notary, — answered the old man: — I have found a treasure. I have found, Mr. Notary, my Lord, who is dearer to me than all the treasures of this world and who does not like his slave to be fed on bread procured for him by arms.

> In spite of the strict measures used against them by the government, the Nazarenes do not change their faith.

> *V. Olkhovsky (From the book “The Nazarenes in Hungary.” A “Posrednik” publication).*
