<!-- vol. 41, p. 78 -->

2 February

*Life in forgetfulness of death and life in the consciousness of drawing hourly nearer to death are two completely different states.*

1

The more life has been transferred from the bodily realm into the spiritual realm, the less terrible death is. For a person living a wholly spiritual life, this fear cannot exist.

2

> When you are firmly convinced and remember that any hour now you are to cast off your outer shell, your body, that is, to die, it is easier for you to observe justice and act according to truth, easier to submit to your fate. Think only of how not to depart from truth in each task before you today and how to bear submissively what lies before you now. Live so—and you will not only meet calmly all human talk, gossip, attacks—you will not even think about them, but all the calamities that may befall you will seem unimportant to you, because, living so, all your desires will merge into one—to fulfill the will of God. And this you can always do.

> *After Marcus Aurelius.*

3

Think more often of death and live as though you knew that you must soon die.

However much you may be in doubt about how to act, imagine that you will die by evening, and the doubt is at once resolved; at once it is clear what is a matter of duty and what are personal desires.

4

The thought of the nearness of death distributes all our actions according to the degree of their true importance for our life. A man condemned to immediate execution will not trouble himself about increasing, preserving his fortune, nor about establishing for himself <!-- vol. 41, p. 79 --> a good name, nor about the triumph of his people over others, nor about the discovery of a new planet, and the like, but a minute before death he will try to comfort someone grieved, will lift up a fallen old man, bind up a wound, mend a child's toy...

5

I love my garden, I love reading a book, I love caressing children. In dying, I am deprived of this, and therefore I do not want to die, and I am afraid of death.

It may be that my whole life is made up of such temporary, worldly desires and their satisfaction. If so, I cannot help fearing that which puts an end to these desires. But if these desires and their satisfaction have changed in me and have been replaced by another desire—to fulfill the will of God, to give myself to him in the form in which I am now and in all the possible forms in which I shall be, then the more my will has been replaced by the will of God, the less is death not only terrible to me, but the less does death exist for me. And if my desires for the good of my personality are wholly replaced by the desire to fulfill the will of God, then there will be nothing for me but life.

To replace the worldly, the temporary, with the eternal—this is the path of life, and it is along this path that one must go. And how?—Each of us knows this in his own soul.

To recall death means to live without the thought of it. One must not recall death, but live calmly, joyfully, with the consciousness of its constant approach.
