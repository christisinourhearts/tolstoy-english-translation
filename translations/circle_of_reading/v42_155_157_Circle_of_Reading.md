<!-- vol. 42, p. 155 -->

18 October

The past is no more, the future has not come; there is only the present. In it alone the divine, free essence of the soul of man is manifested.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 156 -->

1

> Then Jesus said to them: For a little while longer the light is in you; walk while there is light, so that the darkness may not envelop you, and he who walks in darkness does not know where he is going.

> *John 12:35.*

2

> Everyone knows that every habit is strengthened and made firm by exercise. For example, to become a good walker, one must walk often and much; to become a good runner, one must run much; to learn to read well, one must read much, and so on. Conversely, if you stop doing what you are used to, the habit itself will little by little disappear. If you, for example, lie for 10 days without getting up and then begin to walk, you will see how weak your legs have become. So if you want to accustom yourself to some activity, you need to do that activity often and much; and, conversely, if you wish to break yourself of something, then do not do it. The very same thing happens with the faculties of our soul: when you are angry, know that you are doing not only this one evil, but that at the same time you are strengthening in yourself the habit of anger—you are putting wood on the fire. When you have given in to a carnal temptation, do not think that you are guilty only of this—and of nothing more: no, at the same time you have also strengthened the habit of lustful acts. Every reasonable person will tell you that our ailments of the soul, our evil thoughts and desires, grow stronger in just this way. And therefore, if you do not want to accustom yourself to anger, restrain your anger in every way and do not let the habit grow. But by what path is such strength in the struggle with one's thoughts acquired?

> In the struggle with tempting thoughts it is useful at times to seek the company of people more virtuous than yourself, or to recall and read the teachings of wise people who lived before you. The true fighter is he who fights with his vicious thoughts. This struggle is holy and brings you closer to God. On its success depend the peace and happiness of your life. Always remember two times: one, the present time, in which, yielding to vicious thoughts, you will enjoy lust, and the other, that in which, having had your fill of it, you will repent and reproach yourself. Take into consideration also the pleasure <!-- vol. 42, p. 157 --> that you will feel if you abstain. Remember also that it will be hard to abstain if once you have overstepped the measure. But if you yield to your vicious thoughts and assure yourself that you will win tomorrow, and tomorrow say the same thing, you will thereby bring yourself to such weakness that in the future you will stop even noticing your mistakes, and if you do notice them, you will always have a ready justification for all your vicious acts.

> *Epictetus.*

3

*Remember that if you can do a good deed, you must do it now, because the opportunity will pass and will not return.*

All repentance is useful, because it is always regret that one did not use the present as was proper to that power which manifests itself in the present. Repentance is a reminder of how one ought to act in the present.
