<!-- vol. 41, p. 148 -->

4 March

Gluttony is the most ordinary vice. We take little notice of it only because almost everyone is subject to it.

1

There are sins against people and there are sins against oneself. Sins against people come from our not respecting the spirit of God in another person. Sins against oneself—from our not respecting the spirit of God in ourselves. One of the most ordinary sins against oneself is overeating.

2

> If it were not for greed, not one bird would fall into the net. With this same bait people too are caught. The belly is a chain on the hands and fetters on the feet. A slave of the belly is always a slave. If you want to be free—first of all free yourself from the belly. Eat in order to satisfy hunger, and not in order to get pleasure.

> *After Saadi.*

3

A person who overeats is unable to struggle against laziness, and for a person who overeats and is idle it is still harder to struggle against sexual lust. And therefore in all teachings the striving for abstinence begins with the struggle against the lust of gluttony, begins with fasting.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 149 -->

4

> Every person is like a tamer of wild beasts, and these beasts are his passions. To tear out their fangs and claws, to bridle them, to tame them, to make them into domestic animals, servants, even if growling, yet still submissive—in this lies the task of self-education.

> *Amiel.*

5

*God sent people food, and the devil—cooks.*

6

The sage Socrates himself abstained from everything superfluous, from such things as are eaten not to satisfy hunger but for taste, and urged his pupils to do the same. He said that there is great harm both for the body and for the soul from superfluous food and drink, and advised never to eat one's fill, but to get up from the table while one still wants to eat. He reminded his pupils of the tale of the wise Ulysses: how the sorceress Circe could not bewitch him only because he would not overeat, but his companions she turned, every one, into pigs as soon as they threw themselves on her sweet dishes.

7

> It is no misfortune if the body suffers from the work of the soul, but it is shameful when the most precious thing in a person—the soul—suffers from the body.

> *After the Talmud.*

8

Watch over your mouth: through it diseases enter. Act so that you would still like to eat when you get up from dinner.

Intemperance in food is not considered a sin only because it does not cause noticeable harm to others. But there are sins against a person's consciousness of his own dignity. Intemperance in food is one of them.
