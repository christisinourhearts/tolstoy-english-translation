<!-- vol. 42, p. 336 -->

11 December

Of all kinds of labor the most joyful is agricultural labor.

1

> All peoples will in the end acknowledge the truth long since grasped by those people who were their intellectual guides, namely, that the first virtue of humanity consists in acknowledging its imperfection and in submitting to the laws of a higher being. “Dust you are, and to dust you will return” is the first truth that we come to know about ourselves; and the second consists in tilling the earth from which we were taken, which also constitutes our chief duty. In this work and in those relations which it establishes between us, the animals, and the plants lie the basic conditions for the development of our higher faculties and of our greatest well-being. And without this work neither peace nor the development of his mind and spiritual powers is conceivable for man.

> *John Ruskin.*

2

> He who buys bread at the market may be likened to an orphaned nursing infant: many wet nurses feed him, <!-- vol. 42, p. 337 --> but the child still goes hungry; but he who eats his own bread is like a child nursed at its mother's breast.

> *The Talmud.*

3

> All workmen and craftsmen will in time return to agriculture, as scripture says.

> “And they will come down from their ships, all who wield the oar, all who sail the sea... they will put in to the land” (Ezekiel 27:29).

> *The Talmud.*

4

> *Truly, the best food is that which you yourselves or your children have earned.*

> *Muhammad.*

5

> “In the sweat of the face shalt thou eat thy bread.” This is an unchanging physical law. To woman is given the law to bear children, to man, to work. Woman cannot free herself from her law. *If she adopts a child not born of her, it will still be someone else's child, and she will be deprived of the joy of motherhood.* It is the same with the labors of men. If a man eats bread not worked for by himself, he is deprived of all the joy of labor.

> *Bondarev.*

6

> More respect is deserved by one who lives by the labor of his own hands than by one who prides himself only on his fear of God.

> It is shameful for a person when he is advised to imitate the ant in industriousness; doubly shameful if he does not follow this advice.

> *The Talmud.*

Agriculture is not one of the occupations natural to people. Agriculture is the one occupation natural to all people and giving the greatest independence and good.
