<!-- vol. 42, p. 149 -->

14 October

Art is a human activity that unites people in one and the same feeling. If these feelings are good, then the activity of art is beneficial; and vice versa.

1

Always, at every time and in every human society, there is a religious consciousness, common to all the people of that society, of what is good and what is bad, and it is precisely this religious consciousness that determines the worth of the feelings transmitted by art.

2

Christian art must bring it about that the feelings of brotherhood and love for one's neighbors, now accessible only to the best people of society, become habitual feelings, an instinct, of all people. By evoking in the imagination of all people feelings of brotherhood and love, Christian art will accustom people to experience the same feelings in reality. It lays <!-- vol. 42, p. 150 --> in the souls of people those rails along which the actions of people brought up by such art will naturally run.

3

The essence of the Christian consciousness consists in the recognition by each man of his sonship to God and of the union of people with God and with one another that flows from it, as is said in the Gospel (John 17:21), and therefore the content of Christian art is such feelings as further the union of people with God and with one another.

4

Works of Christian art can only be those works that unite all people without exception, either by evoking in people the consciousness of the sameness of their position in relation to God and their neighbor, or by evoking in people one and the same feeling, even if the simplest, but not contrary to Christianity and proper to all people without exception.

5

Christian teaching so changed the ideal of people that, as is said in the Gospel, what was great before people became an abomination before God. The ideal became not the greatness of Pharaoh and of the Roman emperor, not the beauty of the Greek or the riches of Phoenicia, but humility, chastity, compassion, love.

The hero became not the rich man, but the beggar Lazarus; Mary of Egypt not in the time of her beauty, but in the time of her repentance; not those who acquire wealth, but those who renounce it, who live not in palaces, but in catacombs and huts.

6

The purpose of art in our time is to transfer from the realm of reasoning into the realm of feeling the truth that the good of people lies in their union with one another, and to establish, in place of the violence that now reigns, that kingdom of God, that is, of love, which appears to all of us as the highest aim of the life of mankind.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 151 -->

7

The variety of feelings flowing from religious consciousness is infinite, and they are all new, because religious consciousness is nothing other than the indication of a new relation of man to the world, whereas the feelings flowing from the desire for enjoyment are not only limited, but were long, long ago explored and expressed. And therefore the unbelief of the upper European classes has led them also to the art that is poorest in content.

Perhaps in the future new ideals, still higher than the present ones, will open before art, and art will realize them; but in our time the purpose of art is clear and definite.

*The task of Christian art is the evoking in people of the feeling of brotherly union.*
