4 September

*The true good is acquired not all at once but by constant efforts, because the true good lies only in ever greater and greater perfecting.*

1

> When we are taught literacy, we learn how to read and write. But literacy will not teach us whether we need to write our friend a letter or not. In just the same way, music teaches us to sing or to play an instrument, but it will not teach us when we may sing and play.

> In endowing us with reason, God put at our disposal what is most needful to us and what we are able to manage.

> In creating me such as I am, God as it were said this to me: “Epictetus! I could have bestowed far more on your paltry body and your little lot. But do not reproach me for not having done so. I did not wish to bestow on you complete freedom to do everything that comes into your head, but I placed in you a divine particle of myself. I bestowed on you the ability to strive toward good and to avoid evil; I placed in you free understanding. If you apply your reason to everything that happens to you, then nothing in the world will serve you as an obstacle or a constraint on the path that I have appointed for you; you will never complain either of your lot or of people; you will not condemn them or curry favor with them. Do not think that this is little for you. Is it really little for you to live your whole life reasonably, calmly, and joyfully? Then be content with this!”

> *Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 15 --> Reason alone shows us what we ought to do and what we ought not to do.

2

> On the bathtub of King Ching-Chang the following words were carved: “Renew yourself completely each day; do this again, again, and once again.”

> *Chinese wisdom.*

3

> The virtue of the wise resembles a journey to a distant land and an ascent to a height: those going to a distant land begin their walking with the first step; those ascending a height begin at the foot of the mountain.

> *Confucius.*

4

> In order to do any task rightly and well, one must learn to do it. Everyone understands this. In just the same way, in order to live rightly and well, one must learn to live rightly and well.

> *Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 16 -->

5

> No one who has put his hand to the plow and is looking back is fit for the kingdom of God.

> *Luke 9:62.*

6

> A person is happy only when he can say that he has carried out his own work, that he has put his soul into his labor and brought it to the end as best he could. But if he acts otherwise, then even when he has finished with his labor, he will feel neither gladness nor relief.

> *Emerson.*

Do not expect, not only quick, but any success visible to you from your efforts toward good. You will not see the fruits of your efforts, because however far you have moved forward, just so far has the perfection toward which you strive moved forward ahead of you. Efforts are not a means of attaining the good, but the efforts themselves give the good.
