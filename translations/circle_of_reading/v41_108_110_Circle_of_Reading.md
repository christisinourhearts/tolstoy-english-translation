<!-- vol. 41, p. 108 -->

17 February

All the people of the world have equal rights to the use of the natural goods of the world and equal rights to respect.

1

We wonder at how Christianity has been perverted, how little, even not at all, it has been realized in life, and yet could it have been otherwise with a teaching that by its requirement established the true equality of people: all are sons <!-- vol. 41, p. 109 --> of God, all are brothers, the life of all is equally sacred. True equality requires not only the abolition of castes, ranks, privileges, but the abolition of the chief instrument of inequality—violence. Equality cannot be realized, as is thought, by civil measures; it is realized only by love for God and people. And love for God and people is instilled not by civil measures, but by true religious teaching.

That people could fall into the crude error that freedom, brotherhood, and equality can be introduced by executions, threats of executions, violence, does not show that what people were striving for was wrong, but only that the path by which erring people tried to realize freedom, brotherhood, and equality was wrong.

2

*They say equality is impossible because some people will always be stronger, more intelligent than others. Precisely for this reason, because some people are stronger, more intelligent than others, says Lichtenberg, equality of people's rights is especially needed. The oppression of the weak by the strong is so terrible now precisely because, besides the inequality of intelligence and strength, there is also inequality of rights.*

3

One has only to look at the life of the Christian peoples, divided into people who spend their whole life in stupefying, killing labor unnecessary to them, and others satiated with idleness and every kind of pleasure, to be struck by the terrible degree of inequality to which people professing the law of Christianity have come, and especially by that lie of a preaching of equality, amid an ordering of life, that appalls by the most cruel and obvious inequality.

4

No one realizes true equality in life as children do. And how criminal adults are, violating this holy feeling in them, teaching them that there are emperors, kings, rich men, <!-- vol. 41, p. 110 --> celebrities, whom one must treat with respect, and there are servants, workers, beggars, whom one may treat with disdain! “And whoever tempts one of these little ones...”

Christ revealed to people what they had always known: that all people are equal to one another, equal because one and the same spirit lives in all of them. But people have long since so divided themselves into kings, grandees, rich men, workers, and beggars that, although they know that they are all equal, they live as if not knowing it, and say that in practice the equality of people cannot be. Do not believe this. Learn from little children. Do as they do, come together with all people with love and affection, and with all alike. If you say the familiar “you” to some people, then say the familiar “you” to all; if the polite “you,” then say the polite “you” to all. If people exalt themselves, do not respect them more than others. But if people are humiliated, then try especially to respect these very humiliated ones, so as not to give in to a bad example.
