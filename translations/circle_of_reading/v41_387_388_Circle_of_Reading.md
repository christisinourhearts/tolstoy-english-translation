<!-- vol. 41, p. 387 -->

9 June

The existing order of Christian societies is contrary to the Christian law in its true meaning.

1

Almost all the efforts of the human mind are directed not toward easing the labor of those who labor, but only toward easing and adorning the idleness of those who idle.

2

The people of our time have arranged for themselves a life contrary both to the moral and to the physical nature of man, and they strain all the powers of their mind to assure man that this, precisely, is the most real life. Everything that is called culture in our time: our sciences, arts, improvements—all this is only attempts to deceive the moral demands of man.

3

> If anyone were to look at our world, how much folly he would see, how many tears he would shed, what laughter he would laugh, how much hatred he would feel! We truly act in such a way that our deeds are laughable, and foolish, and pitiful, and hateful. Here one feeds dogs to catch wild beasts, and himself falls into beastliness; another—oxen and asses to haul stones, yet despises people dying of hunger; spends an abyss of gold to erect men of stone, yet leaves in neglect the real ones, who have turned to stone from calamities. Another collects precious little stones and with great labor adorns walls with them, yet, seeing the naked limbs of beggars, is not in the least moved. Some devise still more clothes for their clothes, while others have nothing to cover even their naked body.

> Another squanders everything on harlots and parasites, another on comedians and dancers, another on splendid buildings, on buying villages and houses. One calculates interest, another interest on interest, another composes papers filled with many murders and has no rest even at night, sleeplessly keeping watch for the ruin of others. Scarcely has day come when one runs to unrighteous <!-- vol. 41, p. 388 --> gain, another to dissolute spending, and others to theft from the state. In general there is much care for what is superfluous and criminal, but of what is necessary there is not even a thought.

> *John Chrysostom.*

4

> Just as it is contrary to the law of nature that a child should govern grown men or a madman a wise man, so it is also contrary to the law of nature that a handful of people should be surfeited with excesses while the hungry crowd does not have what is necessary.

> *Rousseau.*

5

> In the ages of cannibalism the strong devoured the weak, simply ate their bodies. But, in spite of all the laws established by people, in spite of the successes of the sciences, strong, heartless people still go on living at the expense of the weak, the unfortunate, and the foolish. True, they do not eat their flesh, do not drink their blood, but all the same they live off their privations and want. The poor, who cripple themselves with work and spend their whole life only in cares about feeding themselves and their families, are in essence devoured by their fellow men. Seeing the disintegration of the civilized world, its anxiety and tears, broken hopes and pitiful reality, famines and crimes, humiliation and disgrace, one involuntarily comes to the conclusion that cannibalism was not a crueler form of existence at another's expense.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

*There is, was, and always will be only one work for a person to which it is worth devoting his whole life. This work is loving communion with people and the destruction of the barriers that people have raised between themselves.*
