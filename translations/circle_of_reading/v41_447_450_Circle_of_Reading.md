<!-- vol. 41, p. 447 -->

30 June

It is enough for a person to turn away from resolving external questions and to set himself the one inner question truly proper to man: how is he best to live his life?—for all the external questions to receive the best resolution.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 448 -->

1

We do not know, cannot know, what the common good consists in, but we know firmly that the attainment of this common good is possible only through the fulfillment by each person, and consequently by me too, of that law of good which is revealed to every person.

2

True life takes place not where great external changes are accomplished, where people move about, collide, fight, and kill one another; it takes place only where the very slightest, imperceptible changes are accomplished: in the spiritual consciousness of people.

3

> Martha! Martha! You are anxious and bustle about many things; but only one thing is needful. And Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her.

> *Luke 10:41–42.*

4

> The peoples of the earth tremble and shudder. Everywhere one feels some working of forces, as if preparing an earthquake. Never yet has man had upon himself such an enormous responsibility. Every moment brings with it more and more important cares. One feels that something great must come to pass. But before the appearance of Christ the world awaited great events and yet did not receive him when he came. So now too the world may be undergoing the *pangs of birth* before his *new coming* and still not understand what is happening.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

5

> There exist two kinds of socialism. Both pursue the greatest well-being of all.

> One strives to attain universal happiness; the other—to give everyone the possibility of being happy in his own way.

> One recognizes the authority of the state; the other recognizes no authority at all.

> One wishes the governed class to become the ruling one; the other wishes the abolition of classes.

> One believes in social war; the other believes only in works of peace.

> There exist only these two socialisms. One is in the age of childhood; the other—of manhood. One is already the past; the other—the future. One must give place to the other. And each of us must choose between these two socialisms or else not acknowledge himself a socialist at all.

> *Ernest Lesigne.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 449 --> One demands monopoly for the state; the other wishes the abolition of all monopolies.

6

“When among 100 people one rules over 99—that is unjust, that is despotism; when 10 rule over 90—that too is unjust, that is oligarchy; but when 51 rule over 49 (and that only in imagination—in reality, again, 10 or 11 of these 51)—then it is entirely just, that is freedom!”

Can there be anything more ridiculous, in its obvious absurdity, than such reasoning? And yet this very reasoning serves as the foundation for the activity of all the improvers of the state order.

7

> It is hard to make out the voice of truth amid the cries of excited parties.

> *Schiller.*

8

> A person who wishes to serve truth and justice must be ready to remain alone.

> *Bersier.*

9

> There is no political alchemy by means of which one could get golden conduct out of leaden instincts.

> *Herbert Spencer.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 450 -->

10

> *If people wished, instead of saving the world, to save themselves; instead of liberating mankind, to liberate themselves—how much they would do for the saving of the world and for the liberation of mankind!*

> *Herzen.*

The more people believe that they can be led to a change and improvement of their lives by something external, acting of itself, apart from their will, the harder it will be for this change and improvement to come about.
