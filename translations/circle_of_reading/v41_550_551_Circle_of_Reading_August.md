> THE SPARROW

> I was returning from hunting and walking along the garden path. The dog was running ahead of me.

> Suddenly it shortened its steps and began to creep, as if scenting game ahead of it. I looked down the length of the path and saw a young sparrow, with yellow around its beak and down on its <!-- vol. 41, p. 551 --> head. It had fallen from the nest (the wind was strongly rocking the birches) and sat motionless, helplessly spreading its barely sprouting little wings.

> My dog was slowly approaching it, when suddenly, darting down from a nearby tree, an old black-breasted sparrow dropped like a stone right in front of its muzzle and, all ruffled, distorted, with a desperate and pitiful cheep, hopped once or twice toward the open, toothy jaws.

> He rushed to save, he shielded his offspring with himself, but his whole small body was trembling with terror, his little voice had grown wild and hoarse, he was swooning, he was sacrificing himself! What a huge monster the dog must have seemed to him! And yet he could not stay on his high, safe branch... A force stronger than his will hurled him down from there.

> The dog stopped, backed away... Evidently it too recognized this force.

> I hurried to call off the abashed dog and withdrew, in awe.

> Yes, I stood in awe of that small, magnanimous bird, of its loving impulse.

> Love, — I thought, — is stronger than death, and the fear of death. Only by it, only by love, does life hold together and move.

> *I. S. Turgenev.*
