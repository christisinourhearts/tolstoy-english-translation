<!-- vol. 41, p. 181 -->

22 March

If the truth exposes our life, it is still better to acknowledge the truth than to hide it: our life can change, but the truth will always remain the truth and will not cease to expose us.

1

> We ought to live as though we were in view of all. We ought to think as though the most hidden corners of our soul were open to someone's gaze. Why hide anything <!-- vol. 41, p. 182 --> from people? From God it is impossible to hide anything. All divine and human teaching comes down to one truth: that we are all members of one great body. Nature has joined us into one family, and we are all made so as to live our life in common, helping one another.

> *After Seneca.*

2

> The Christian revelation was a teaching of the equality of people, that God is the father and people are brothers. It struck at the very root of that terrible violence which was strangling the civilized world; it broke the chains of slaves and destroyed that great wrong which enabled a handful of people to live in luxury at the expense of the mass and deprived working people of the products of their labor. That is why early Christianity was persecuted, and that is why, when it became clear that it could not be destroyed, the ruling classes accepted it and perverted it. In its triumph it ceased to be the true Christianity of early times and became the servant of rich people.

> *After Henry George.*

3

> Your brother by birth—I mean spiritual birth—is perishing of hunger, while you are exhausted with surfeit. The brother goes about with a naked body, while you make garments even for your garments, to protect them from worms by such coverings. And would it not be much better to cover the bodies of the poor with them? Then the garments would remain whole, and you would be free of needless care. For if you do not want your garments to be eaten by the moth—give them to the poor: they know how to shake out garments well. If people intoxicated with wealth stop their ears to my words, those who live in poverty will take note of them instead. But how, you will say, does this apply to the poor? They have neither gold nor many garments, after all. But they have bread and cold water and feet to visit the sick: they have a tongue and words to comfort the unhappy; a house and a roof to receive the wanderer.

> *John Chrysostom.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 183 -->

4

> The mistake of all good people in our time is that, politely holding out a hand to the evil, supporting them in their evil and often even assisting them, they hope to avert the consequences of evil.

> In the morning, to satisfy a need of the heart, they help the want of two or three ruined families, and in the evenings they dine with the ruiners of these families, envy them, and prepare to follow the example of the rich speculator who has ruined two or three thousand people. In this way they destroy in a few hours more than they have the strength to repair in the course of decades. They are, at best, like people who, while trying to feed a starving population in the rear of an all-destroying army, try to increase ever more the numbers of this army and the speed of its movement.

> *John Ruskin.*

5

> You push a man into a pit and then tell him that he must be content with the position in which providence has placed him. Such is our present-day Christianity. You say: “We did not push him.” Yes, of course, we shall not be conscious of all that we do and do not do until every morning we put to ourselves the question how, in the course of the day, to do not what is profitable but what is proper to man.

> *John Ruskin.*

*If the truth does not point out to us what we must do, then it will always point out to us what we must not do or must stop doing.*
