10 November

From the hour when the first members of the councils said, “it hath pleased us and the holy spirit,” that is, raised external authority above the inner, recognized the result of pitiful human reasonings <!-- vol. 42, p. 238 --> at councils as more important and holier than the one truly holy thing that is in man—his reason and conscience—from that hour began that lie, lulling both the bodies and the souls of people, which has destroyed millions of human beings and continues its terrible work to this day.

1

Strange as this may seem, it is beyond doubt that only in those teachings which were called heresies did Christianity show itself and move, that is, become clear and be realized. Heresies could contain errors, but they could also contain true Christianity; but the teachings recognized as state teachings, upheld by power, by violence, could not be Christianity, since their foundation, violence, was anti-Christian. Catholicism, Orthodoxy, Lutheranism, Anglicanism could not be Christian teachings, because they denied one of the fundamental requirements of Christianity—loving admonition—and in its place used the most anti-Christian methods of violence, extending to the greatest tortures, executions, burnings. All these churches that have united with state power, which the sectarians not without reason call the apocalyptic harlot, not only were never Christian but were always the bitterest enemies of Christianity, and they continue even now, not repenting of their crimes but acknowledging all their past as sacred, though in milder forms, to struggle in exactly the same way against true Christianity, constituting the chief obstacle to the peoples' acceptance of the truth revealed to them.

2

> Anglicanism was from the very beginning the most servile and zealous servant of oppression, striving with the help of secular power and by means of pompous solemnity to attain the same position that Catholicism had attained in Europe. In every difficult situation it turned to the help of state power.

> *Lecky.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 239 -->

3

> In 1682 in England Doctor Leighton, a respected man who had written a book against episcopacy, was tried and sentenced to the following punishments, which were carried out on him. He was cruelly flogged, then one ear was cut off and one side of his nose slit, then the letters SS: sower of sedition, were burned onto his cheek with a hot iron. After seven days he was flogged again, although the welts on his back had not yet healed, and the other side of his nose was slit, and the other ear cut off, and a brand burned on the other cheek. All this was done in the name of Christianity.

> *Morrison Davidson.*

4

> Christ founded no church, established no state, gave no laws, no government, nor any external authority, but he tried to write the law of God in the hearts of people so as to make them self-governing.

> *Heber Newton.*

5

In 1415 John Hus, for his exposure of the godless deeds of the pope, was declared a heretic, tried, and sentenced to death without the shedding of blood, that is, to burning.

The place of execution was outside the city gates, among the gardens, near the Rhine. When Hus was brought to the place of execution, he fell on his knees and began to pray. When the executioner ordered him to step onto the pyre, Hus drew himself up and said loudly:

— Jesus Christ! This terrible, shameful death I endure for the preaching of your word; I will endure it submissively and with humility!

The executioners stripped Hus and tied his hands behind him to a post; Hus's feet stood on a bench. Around him they laid firewood and straw. The pyre reached up to Hus's chin. For the last time the imperial marshal von Poppenheim offered Hus to save his life by renouncing the heresy.

— No, — said Hus, — I know of no guilt in myself.

Hus began to sing a hymn: “Christ, son of the living God, have mercy on me!” The flame, fanned by the wind, rose high, and Hus soon fell silent.

<!-- vol. 42, p. 240 --> Then the executioners lit the pyre.

6

It is said that true believers make up the church. Whether these true believers exist or not, we cannot know. Each of us would naturally wish to be such a true believer, and each tries to be one; but no one can say either of himself or of those who believe as he does that they alone are the true believers. Whoever can say this thereby renounces true Christianity.

*If there is a church, it cannot be visible to those who are in it.*
