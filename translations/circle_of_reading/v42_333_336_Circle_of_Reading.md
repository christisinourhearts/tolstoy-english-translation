<!-- vol. 42, p. 333 -->

10 December

One of the commonest temptations, and one leading to the greatest calamities, is the temptation expressed in the words: “everyone does so.”

1

> Woe to the world because of temptations, for temptations must come; but woe to that man through whom the temptation comes. If your hand or your foot tempts you, cut them off and throw them from you: it is better for you to enter into life without a hand or without a foot than with two hands and two feet to be cast into the eternal fire; and if your eye tempts you, tear it out and throw it from you: it is better for you to enter into life with one eye than with two to be cast into the fiery Gehenna.

> *Matthew 18:7–9.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 334 -->

9

> The chief obstacle to every useful undertaking that lies before us is always the consideration that “we cannot give up our position in the world.”

> For most people who use this excuse, to keep the position in which they have been placed by life or by “Providence” means to keep all their carriages, all their footmen, all those spacious houses which they are able to pay for. But I think that if Providence placed them in a position of this kind, which is rather doubtful, then it also requires of them that they leave this position.

> The position of Levi consisted in collecting taxes; Peter's—in being a fisherman on the Lake of Galilee; Paul's—in being in the high priest's anteroom. And all these positions they left, because they considered this their duty.

> *John Ruskin.*

3

> If a person's hand is not wounded, he can touch a snake's venom—venom is not dangerous to a healthy hand. Evil is harmless only to him who does no evil himself.

> *Buddhist wisdom.*

4

> And no one puts a patch of unbleached cloth on an old garment; for what is newly sewn on will tear away from the old, and the hole will be still worse.

> Nor do they pour young wine into old wineskins; otherwise the wineskins burst, and the wine runs out, and the wineskins are lost; but young wine is poured into new wineskins, and both are preserved.

> *Matthew 9:16–17.*

5

> A person is in great danger when he has bound himself to his sins by such obligations that freeing himself from them is tormenting. At first a person is ashamed to confess his sin, then it is hard to get loose from it; then it comes to this, <!-- vol. 42, p. 335 --> that to give up his sins means to ruin himself in the opinion of the world. He who does not stop at the first step of sin will reach the last.

> *Baxter.*

6

> Where things present themselves as worthy of our special respect, one must strip them bare, remove from them all those words with which they are glorified, because outward brilliance is capable of perverting reason. Because it is precisely when you are fully convinced that you are occupied with matters deserving your respect that you are deceived worst of all.

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

7

> An elder had a temptation: he was tormented by thoughts of why God permits evil in the world. And he reproached God for this.

> And he had a dream: he sees an angel of God coming down from heaven, holding in his hand a bright wreath, and looking about and seeking someone on whom to place it. And the elder's heart was kindled. And he says to the angel of God: “How can one earn the bright wreath? I will do everything to receive this reward.”

> And the angel says: “Look here.” And, turning, the angel pointed with his finger to the north side. And the elder looked round and saw a great black cloud. The cloud covered half the sky and came down to the earth. And now the cloud parted, and there came into view a great horde of black Ethiopians advancing on the elder; and behind them all stood one Ethiopian so great and terrible that with his huge feet he stood on the earth, and with his shaggy head, with terrible eyes and red lips, he propped himself against the sky.

> “Fight with them, defeat them, and I will place the wreath on you.”

> And the elder was horrified and said: “I can and will fight with all of them, but the great Ethiopian, standing with his feet on the earth and propping his head against the sky, is beyond a person's strength. I cannot fight with him.”

> “Fool!” said the angel of God. “All the little Ethiopians with whom you do not want to fight for fear of the great Ethiopian, all these little Ethiopians are the small human passions, and they can be overcome. But the great Ethiopian is the evil of the world that has arisen out of these same small <!-- vol. 42, p. 336 --> human passions, that very evil for which you reproached God, and there is no need to fight with him—he is entirely empty. Overcome the passions, and evil will vanish from the world of itself.”

> *Legend.*

8

> *False shame is the devil's favorite tool. He achieves more by it than even by false pride. By false pride he only encourages evil, but by false shame he stops good.*

> *John Ruskin.*

There is no evil in the world. All evil is in our soul and can be destroyed.
