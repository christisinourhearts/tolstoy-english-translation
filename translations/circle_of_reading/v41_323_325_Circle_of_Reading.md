<!-- vol. 41, p. 323 -->

13 May

Each person must decide for himself the questions of the meaning of life and death.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 324 -->

1

> A wise man demands everything only of himself; a petty man demands everything of others.

> *Chinese wisdom.*

2

> The soul does not learn; it only remembers what it has always known.

> *Daud el Gaffir.*

3

> A wise man always finds help for himself in everything, because his gift consists in extracting good from everything.

> *John Ruskin.*

4

> A political victory, a rise in income, the recovery of your sick, the return of absent friends, or something else favorable of the same kind raises your spirit, and you think that better days will come for you. Do not believe it. Nothing will bring you peace but yourself.

> *Emerson.*

5

> In vain will you seek in the external world answers to the questions concerning the purpose of life. The answers to all your questions are within you, but only in germ. You must grow your answers by a good life. This is the only path to wisdom.

> *Lucy Mallory.*

6

> *Woe to him who seeks a companion, because the faithful companion is only he himself, and he who seeks cannot be a faithful companion to himself.*

> *Thoreau.*

7

Truth learned by rote only sticks to us like an artificial limb, a false tooth, a wax nose, and at most—like a nose made of someone else's skin. But truth acquired by one's own <!-- vol. 41, p. 325 --> thinking is like a real limb of the body; it alone truly belongs to us.

*Schopenhauer.*

If on the questions of life and death a person accepts the answers of other wise people who lived before, still the choice and recognition of these answers depend on him himself.
