<!-- vol. 41, p. 401 -->

16 June

Only the moral perfecting of people can improve the structure of social life.

1

> If the state achieves its aim, it will establish the same condition as if complete justice in the cast of mind reigned everywhere. But the inner essence and origin of these two conditions—the semblance of justice and complete justice—are directly opposite. Namely: in the latter case the condition would be such that no one would want to *do* injustices; in the former, such that no one would want to *suffer* injustices, and the means chosen would fully answer this aim. Thus an external aim can be reached by two opposite means. Thus a beast of prey in a muzzle is as harmless as a grass-eating animal. But the state cannot lead beyond this limit: consequently, it cannot unfold before us the picture that would take shape with mutual goodwill and love among all.

> *Schopenhauer.*

2

> Opposite the window at which I am writing, a huge bull is tied by a ring put through his nostrils. Nibbling the grass, he has wound his rope around a post and, like a prisoner, now stands, <!-- vol. 41, p. 402 --> tormented by hunger in sight of luxuriant grass, deprived of the possibility even of tossing his head to drive off the flies that bite his shoulders. He has many times made vain efforts to free himself, but each time, after lowing plaintively, he has quieted down to suffer in silence.

> This bull, possessing mighty strength but not having enough sense to understand how he could free himself, suffering from hunger amid plenty and becoming the helpless victim of the weakest creatures—appears to me as it were an emblem of the working classes.

> In all countries the people by whose labor an abundance of wealth is created struggle in poverty. While advancing civilization widens the horizon of thought and awakens new desires, these people sink, for the sake of satisfying their animal needs, to the level of a brutish existence. Conscious of all the bitterness of injustice, feeling in the recesses of their souls that they were not at all created for so wretched a life, these people too at times struggle and protest. But until they learn to connect effect with cause; until they understand how they are entangled and how they can free themselves—until then their efforts and protests will be as vain as the efforts and plaintive lowing of the entangled bull—will be even more vain. I will go out of the room and drive the bull off, guiding him so that the rope unwinds, whereas no one will guide people to freedom. Until they begin to use the reason with which they are endowed, no one will help them, no one will make them free.

> True power, under every possible form of government, is in reality always in the hands of the mass. And in reality it is not at all kings or aristocrats, not at all landowners or capitalists who everywhere enslave the people. The people are enslaved by their ignorance.

> *Henry George.*

3

> One cannot fight against bad organization not only with violence but even with good organization.

> Why not organize labor!

> But the well-being of humanity can be attained only by an independent moral-religious path.

> For what is vexing and outrageous is not so much that a bad social structure exists, as that man has engendered it, tolerates it, and even uses it for his own selfish ends.

> And what outrages more is what one must begin by fighting against.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 403 --> One may take up this too. But one must not forget that in organizing labor we attain not the well-being of humanity but only the success and productivity of labor itself.

4

> We live in an age of discipline, culture, and civilization, but still far from an age of morality. In the present condition of people one may say that the happiness of states grows together with the unhappiness of people. And it is still a question: would we not be happier in a primitive state, when we would not have this culture, than in our present state?

> For how can one make people happy when they are not made moral and wise!

> *Kant.*

*The common evil of life can be overcome by only one means: by the moral perfecting of one's own life.*
