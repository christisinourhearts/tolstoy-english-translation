<!-- vol. 42, p. 174 -->

27 October

True religion cannot be contrary to reason.

1

> Do not believe that in the matter of religion it could be possible not to trust reason. Faith in the power of reason lies at the foundation of every other faith. One cannot believe in God if we belittle the significance of that faculty by means of which we know God. If, after a conscientious and impartial use of our best faculties, a certain doctrine seems to us contradictory or not in agreement with the chief truths of which we have no doubt, we must undoubtedly refrain from <!-- vol. 42, p. 175 --> belief in this teaching. I am more convinced that my reasonable nature is from God than that any book whatever is the expression of his will.

> *Channing.*

2

> If God, as the object of our faith, is above our understanding and we cannot embrace him with reason, it still does not follow from this that we must neglect the activity of reason, considering it harmful.

> Although the objects of faith are, without any doubt, outside the circle of our understanding, above it, nevertheless reason, in relation to them too, has such important significance that we can in no way do without it.

> *Fyodor Strakhov.*

3

> Still naive, like a grown-up child, is he who can seriously believe that at some time some nonhuman beings explained to our human race its existence and the purpose of it and of the whole world. There is no other revelation than the thoughts of sages, although their thoughts too are subject to error—as is the lot of everything human—and often clothe themselves in the form of wonderful allegories and myths, taking on then the name of revelations. And therefore, it would seem, it is all the same whether one relies on one's own thoughts or on other people's, because the thoughts that are handed down to us as revelations are, after all, only human thoughts. And yet—people are usually more inclined to rely on other people's heads, supposedly possessing superhuman sources of thought, than on their own. However, if one takes into account the whole intellectual inequality of people, then perhaps the thoughts of one may indeed seem a supernatural revelation to another.

> *Schopenhauer*

4

> They try to make impossible not only the uttering and communicating of truth, but even the very thinking about it and discovering of it, by handing the head over from earliest childhood to be worked on by the clergy, who press so deep the rut in which the fundamental thoughts will henceforth <!-- vol. 42, p. 176 --> move that in most cases they will take shape and settle for the whole of life.

> *Schopenhauer.*

5

Light remains light, although a blind man does not see it.

6

> While the light is in you, believe in the light, that you may be sons of light.

> *John 12:36.*

*What is needed in order to know the truth is not to suppress one's reason, as false teachers teach, but on the contrary to purify it, to exert it, to test by it everything that is offered.*
