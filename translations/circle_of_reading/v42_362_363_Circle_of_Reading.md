<!-- vol. 42, p. 362 -->

21 December

At the highest point of his consciousness a person is alone. This solitude is strange, unaccustomed, and seems hard. Unreasonable people save themselves from the weight of the consciousness of this solitude by distractions and at once come down from this highest point to a lower one; reasonable people hold themselves at this height by prayer.

1

The relation to God, the one that he wants from us, is the constant fulfillment of his will in life. But the interests of life, our passions, constantly, every minute, distract us from this. And so, having realized this, we resort to an outward, verbal expression of our relation to God, to prayer, trying to call up in ourselves a living consciousness of our dependence on God. Such prayer reminds us of our sins, our duties, and saves us from temptations, if in moments of temptation we manage to call up in ourselves a prayerful mood.

2

Personality is a limitation, and therefore God, however he may be understood, is not a personality. Prayer, though, is an address to God.

How, then, is one to address the impersonal?

Astronomers know that what moves in their field of view is not the stars of the heavenly vault, but the earth on which they stand with their observatory and telescope, and yet they record not the movement of the earth but the movement of the stars. There is no other way. It is exactly the same in prayer. God is not a personality. But I am a personality, and therefore I cannot express my relation to God otherwise than as to a personal God, although I know that he cannot be a personality.

3

A person hopelessly buried in a mine, freezing in the ice, dying of hunger at sea or in solitary confinement, or simply dying, gone deaf and blind, what would such a person live by for the rest of his life if there were no prayer?

<!-- vol. 42, p. 363 -->

4

> *How good it is for a person when he has worn himself out in vain searches for the good in worldly life and, weary, stretches out his hands to God.*

> *Pascal.*

One can live without prayer only when either the passions have wholly taken possession of a person, or when his whole life is a service to God. But for a person struggling with the passions and still far from fulfilling what he considers his duty, prayer is a necessary condition of life.
