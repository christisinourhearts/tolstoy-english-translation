<!-- vol. 41, p. 124 -->

24 February

In order for truth to be heard, it must be spoken with kindness. However clever and true what is said in anger may be, it is not passed on to another. And therefore know that if what you say to a person is not taken in by him, then it is one of two things: either what you consider truth is not truth, or you convey it without kindness, or both together.

1

> The only means of conveying truth is to speak lovingly. Only the words of a loving person are heard.

> *Thoreau.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 125 -->

2

Speaking the truth is the same as sewing well, mowing deftly, writing beautifully. It is given only to him who has sewn much, mowed much, written much. However hard you try, you will not do well what you have not done many and many times. And therefore, in order to speak the truth, one must accustom oneself to it. And in order to accustom oneself to it, one must in every matter, even a small one, speak only the truth.

3

> *We are so used to pretending before others that we often pretend before ourselves*.

> *La Rochefoucauld.*

4

> In essence, only one's own fundamental thoughts possess truth and life; only them do you understand in their real sense. Other people's thoughts, read out of books, are scraps from another's table, clothes off a foreigner's shoulder.

> *Schopenhauer.*

5

If a person loses his nerve before the truth and, having seen it, does not acknowledge it, but goes on stifling in himself the consciousness that what he considered truth is a lie, he will never know what he is to do.

6

> The best minds, loving truth for truth's sake, do not care about appropriating truth as their property. They receive it with gratitude wherever they meet it and do not put on it the brand of anyone's name, because this truth from of old, in eternity, already belonged to them.

> *Emerson.*

Truth cannot make a person unkind or self-assured. The manifestations of truth are always meek, humble, and simple.
