## I UNION

“Each personality is a being completely separate from all the rest. My true being is only in myself, and all the rest is not I and is alien to me.” Here is the knowledge whose truth is attested by flesh and bones, which lies at the basis of all self-love, and whose real expression is every unloving, unjust, or malicious act.

“My true inner being lives in all that lives as immediately as in my self-consciousness it reveals itself only to myself.” This knowledge, which is expressed in Sanskrit by the unchanging formula tat-twam-asi, that is, “all this is you,” shows itself in the form of compassion, on which therefore all true, that is, unselfish, virtue is based, and whose real expression is every good deed. It is on this very knowledge that every appeal to meekness, love of humanity, and mercy counts in the end: for an appeal of this kind is a reminder of that point of view from which we are all one and the same being. On the contrary, self-love, envy, hatred, persecution, callousness, vengeance, malicious joy, and cruelty are based on that first knowledge and hold to it. The tender emotion and rapture that we feel on hearing of, still more on seeing, and most of all on doing a noble deed has its deepest ground in the fact that it gives us the assurance that beneath the multiplicity and diversity of personalities is hidden their unity, which really exists and is accessible to us, since it has revealed itself in deed.

The manifestation of one or the other of these two kinds of knowledge shows itself not only in separate acts but also in the whole character of people's consciousness and state of spirit. In a person of good character this consciousness is quite different from that in a person of evil character. A person of evil character everywhere feels a solid partition between himself and everything that is outside him. The world for him is not-I, and his relation to it is hostile from the very start; therefore his fundamental mood is always unfriendliness, suspicion, envy, malicious joy. But a person <!-- vol. 41, p. 161 --> of good character lives not in himself alone but in the external world, which he is conscious of as one in essence with himself; others for him are not not-I but “still I myself, and I.” And therefore his relation to everyone is always friendly: he feels his kinship with all beings, takes an immediate part in their well-being and misfortune, and trustingly supposes the same sympathy in them. And in him are firmly established peace and that assured, calm, contented state of spirit from which everyone near him feels good.

*Schopenhauer.*
