15 November

The joys of wealth are deceptive.

1

> Do not gather treasures for yourselves on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves dig under and steal; but gather treasures for yourselves in heaven, where neither moth nor rust destroys and where thieves <!-- vol. 42, p. 255 --> do not dig under and do not steal; for where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.

> *Matthew 6:19–21.*

2

> *“Where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”*

> *In what terrible filth, then, is the heart of a man whose treasure is wealth.*

3

> People, having seen bright houses, a multitude of fields, crowds of servants, silver vessels, a great collection of garments, try in every way to have still more, so that the richest are the cause of this evil for the less rich, and these for those of still smaller means. Yet if the rich did not amass riches and squander them, they would not be teachers of the love of money for the less rich and the poor. And attachment to wealth is worse than any tyranny: it gives birth to cares, envy, treachery, hatred, slanders, and countless hindrances to virtue—carelessness, debauchery, covetousness, drunkenness. And this makes even free men slaves, and even worse than slaves: slaves not of people but of the most terrible of the passions and diseases of the soul. Such a person ventures on much that is contrary both to God and to people, for fear that someone may tear him out from under this dominion. Bitter, slavish, devilish dominion! What is especially ruinous here is that, being in so unhappy a state, we kiss our fetters and, dwelling in a prison full of darkness, do not wish to come out into the light, but cling to evil and delight in our disease. That is why we cannot be freed, and are in a worse position than those who work in the mines, since we are subjected to labors and calamities but do not enjoy the fruits. And worst of all is that if someone did wish to deliver us from this heavy captivity, we not only do not allow it but are even angry and indignant, and in this way prove no better than madmen and even far more unhappy than all of them, because we do not want to part with our madness. Were you really born a man for this, only to gather gold? It was not for this that the Lord created you after his own pattern: he created you so that you might do his will.

> *John Chrysostom.*

<!-- vol. 42, p. 256 -->

4

And wealth, and power, and everything that people arrange and guard with such diligence—all this, if it is worth anything at all, is worth it only for the pleasure with which all this can be thrown away.

5

> People take a thousand times more trouble over acquiring wealth for themselves than over the cultivation of their mind and heart; although for our happiness what is in a person is undoubtedly more important than what a person has.

> *Schopenhauer.*

6

> When you have bought a fashionable ornament for yourself, you have to buy ten more things so that everything on you goes with everything else.

> *Emerson.*

7

> Why should a man be rich? Why does he need expensive horses, fine clothes, splendid rooms, the right of entry to public places of amusement? All this comes from a lack of thoughts.

> Give this man the inner work of thought—and he will be happier than the richest people.

> *Emerson.*

8

> A poor man laughs more often and more carefreely than a rich one.

> *Seneca.*

For those who live a spiritual life, wealth is not only unnecessary but constraining: it hinders true life.
