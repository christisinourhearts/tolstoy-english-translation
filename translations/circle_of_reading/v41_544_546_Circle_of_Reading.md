<!-- vol. 41, p. 544 -->

2 August

If man is only a bodily being, then death is the end of everything. But if man is a spiritual being and the body is only a limitation of the spiritual being, then death is only a change.

1

Our body limits that divine, spiritual principle which we call the soul. And it is this limitation—as a vessel gives form to the liquid or gas enclosed in it—that <!-- vol. 41, p. 545 --> gives form to this divine principle. When the vessel is broken, what was enclosed in it ceases to have the form that it had, and spills out. Does it unite with other substances? Does it receive a new form?—we know nothing of this, but we know for certain that it loses the form that it had in its limitation, because what limited it has been destroyed; but we can know nothing about what will happen to what was limited. The soul after death becomes something else—something about which we cannot judge.

2

Emerson, who affirmed his immortality, was asked: “Well, and what about when the world ends?” He answered: “In order not to die, I have no need of the world.”

3

Humility is needed not only for life, but is needed for death too. One must become small before people, before oneself, in order to enter the house of God. Renounce yourself, and you will merge with him. The more renunciation, the nearer to him, the easier death.

4

> Death is only one step in our uninterrupted development. Our birth was also such a step, with only this difference: that birth is death for one form of being, and death is birth into another form of being.

> Death is happiness for the dying man. In dying, you will cease to be mortal. I cannot look on this change with horror, as some people do. In my opinion, death is a change for the better. Are we not mad when we talk of preparing for death? Our business is to live. He who knows how to live will know how to die too. I want to live; our soul never tells us that we will die. The senses die, and it is the senses that created death. So is it worth reasonable people's while to worry about it?

> *Theodore Parker.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 546 -->

5

> The last day brings us not annihilation but only change.

> *Cicero.*

6

Life is the liberation of the soul (a spiritual, independently living essence) from those conditions of the bodily personality in which it has been placed.

*For a person who lives a spiritual life there is no death.*
