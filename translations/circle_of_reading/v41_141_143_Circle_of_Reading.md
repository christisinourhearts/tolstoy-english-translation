<!-- vol. 41, p. 141 -->

2 March

The more a man has merged his will with the will of God, the firmer the man is in his actions.

<!-- vol. 41, p. 142 -->

1

We do not know and cannot know why we live, what work we are doing for the life of the world, but we know that if we submissively fulfill the will of him who sent us, then we are doing what we ought, and all is well with us. A horse harnessed to a cart cannot know where, why, and what it is hauling; but if it is meek and docile and hauls, then it knows that it is working for its master, and all is well with it. So too with people. “My yoke is good and my burden is easy,” said Christ. What God wants from us is easy and good for us, if only we do what he wants from us.

2

> Do the will of God as your own, then he will do your will as his own; give up your desire to please his desire, then he will make others give up their desires to please your desires.

> *The Talmud.*

3

> What strength there is in a man who always acts according to the will of God and submits to him in everything!

> *Marcus Aurelius.*

4

> When robbers are plundering on the highway, a traveler does not set out alone: he waits to see whether someone will be going with a guard, joins him, and then is no longer afraid of the robbers.

> A reasonable man acts the same way in his life. He says to himself: “There are many troubles of every kind in life. Where can I find protection, how can I keep safe from all this? What traveling companion should I wait for, to get through in safety? Whom should I follow—this one or another? A rich man, an important grandee, or the king himself? But will they keep me safe? Why, they too are robbed and killed, and they suffer misfortune just as other people do. It may even be that the one I go with will himself attack me and rob me. Where then can I find myself such a strong and faithful traveling companion, who would be a protection to me and would not himself attack me? Whom should I follow?”

> *After Epictetus.*

<!-- vol. 41, p. 143 --> There is only one such faithful companion. This companion is God. One must follow him so as not to fall into trouble. And what does it mean to follow God? It means to desire what he wants and not to desire what he does not want. And how is this to be attained? To understand his laws and follow them.

5

A workman will do his work well only when he understands his position. The teaching of Christ takes possession of a man only when the man clearly understands that his life is not his, but belongs to the one who gave it, and that the aim of life is not in man but in the will of the one who gave it, and that therefore one must come to know this will and do it.

6

> You desire nothing; do not think that this is what is needed: one must desire what God wants.

> *Amiel.*

7

> *Do not think that your situation is such that you cannot do in it what is destined for man. At every point on earth we are equally near to heaven and to the infinite.*

> *Amiel.*

The path of a good life is narrow. But it is easy to recognize. We recognize it just as easily as we recognize a path laid with boards across a bog. As soon as one has stepped off it to one side or the other, one has fallen into the bog of unreason and evil. A reasonable man, having slipped into the bog, at once returns to the boards, but the senseless man wedges himself farther and farther into the bog, and it becomes harder and harder for him to get out of it.
