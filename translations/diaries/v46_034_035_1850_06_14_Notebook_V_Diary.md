[14 June 1850. Y. P.] 14 June 1850. Again I have taken up the diary, and again [^1] with new zeal and a new aim. How many times is this now? I do not remember.—All the same, perhaps I will give it up again; but it is a pleasant occupation, and it will be pleasant to reread it, just as it was pleasant to reread the old ones.—So many thoughts occur in one's head that seem very remarkable; but when you examine them, they turn out to be empty; some, though, really are sound—that is what a diary is needed for. It is very convenient to judge oneself by one's diary.—Then, [^2] since I find it necessary to determine all my activities in advance, a diary is also necessary for that.—I would like to get used to determining my way of life in advance, not for one day, but for a year, for several years, even for my whole life; it is too difficult, almost impossible; nevertheless, I will try, first for a day, then [^3] for two days—as many days as I remain faithful to the determinations, for that many days I will set them for myself in advance.—By these determinations I mean not moral rules independent of either time or place, rules that never change and that I formulate separately, but specifically determinations of time and place: where and how long to stay, when and what to do.—

Cases arise in which these determinations may be changed; but I allow deviations of this sort only in cases when they are determined by rules; therefore, in cases of deviation I will explain their reasons in the diary.—

<!-- vol. 46, p. 35 -->

For 14 [^4] June. From 9 to 10 bathe and walk, 10 to 12 music,—6 to 8 letters, 8 to 10 estate management and office.— — —

[^5] The last three years, which I spent so dissolutely, sometimes seem to me very interesting, poetic, and partly useful; I will try to recall and write them down more frankly and in greater detail. Here is [^6] a third purpose for the diary.—
