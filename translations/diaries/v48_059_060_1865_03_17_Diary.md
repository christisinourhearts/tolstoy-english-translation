<!-- vol. 48, p. 59 -->

17 March.

Was in Tula. At the funeral at Seryozha’s. Even for grief a person must have rails laid down along which to go—wailing, a memorial service, etc. Yesterday I saw in the snow, on a person’s footprint that had not sunk in, a dog’s footprint that had sunk in. Why is its point of support small? So that it will not eat all the hares, but exactly as many as needed. This is the wisdom of God; but this is not wisdom, not intelligence. This is the instinct of the Deity. This instinct is in us. And our <!-- vol. 48, p. 60 --> intelligence is the ability to deviate from instinct and to comprehend these deviations. These thoughts came to me with terrible clarity, force, and delight. Today I was at the Pashkovs’. The children are ill, and Sonya too. Had not written for about four days. Wrote today. Once got angry with the German and for a long time could not forgive him. I am reading *Ragusa’s Memoirs*. Very useful to me. —
