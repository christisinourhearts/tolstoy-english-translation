17 January. 1851.

— Since the 14th I have behaved unsatisfactorily. — I did not go to the Stolypins’ ball; I lent money and therefore am sitting without a kopek; and all because I grew weak in character. — Rule. Do not play Yeralash for stakes of less than 25 silver kopeks. — I have no money at all; many promissory notes are already past due for payment; I am also beginning to notice that in no respect does my stay in Moscow do me any good, while I am living far beyond my <!-- vol. 46, p. 45 --> income. Rule. Call things by their names. With people who speak superficially about money matters, conceal the state of my affairs and, on the contrary, try to stop them and bring them onto this subject. — To put my affairs right, of the three means that presented themselves to me I have let almost all slip, namely: 1) Get into a circle of gamblers and, when I have money, play. 2) Get into high society and, under certain conditions, marry. 3) Find a position advantageous for service. Now a 4th means presents itself, namely — borrow money from Kireevsky. — None of all 4 things contradicts another, and I must act.[^1] — Write to the country so that they send 150 silver rubles as soon as possible,[^2] go to Ozerov and offer the horse, have it printed in the newspapers again. Visit the Countess and wait, find out about invitations to the Zakrevskys’ ball, order a new tailcoat. — Before the ball think and write a great deal. — Go to Prince Sergei Dmitrievich and talk about a position, to Prince Andrei Ivanovich and ask for a position. — Pawn the watch.

Find out from Evreinov where Kireevsky lives and go to him. At 1 <sup>1</sup> / <sub>2</sub> to Evreinov, and from there, whatever kind of answer there may be, go to Nikolai Vasilyevich. —
