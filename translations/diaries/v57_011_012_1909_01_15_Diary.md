15 Jan. 1909. Y. P.

Yesterday Landowska arrived. Weak impression. Very tired. Walked well. Wrote nothing. Somehow ashamed, ashamed, ashamed of everything. Slept badly. A letter from God's Regiment.

To Ivan Ivanovich. Birth can be looked at as falling asleep, and then life is sleep, and death is awakening. And birth can be looked at as awakening from sleep, and death as falling asleep. One can also look at it this way: birth is both a falling asleep into this bodily life of ours and at the same time an awakening from the former sleep, and death likewise as an awakening from this life and a falling asleep into a new life.

<!-- vol. 57, p. 12 --> The first, the second, and the third are all true. Awakening and sleep, sleep and awakening, is the truest conception of our life in time.
