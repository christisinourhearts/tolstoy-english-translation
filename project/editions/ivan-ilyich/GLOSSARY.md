## 2. Why these choices

**легко, приятно и прилично** (*legko, priyatno i prilichno*). This refrain is how Ivan Ilyich wants life to run, and its exact repetition is the satire. *Decorously* is the key word: прилично means "fitting, what society approves," not "morally good." "Properly" or "decently" would suggest real virtue, which is exactly what Ivan Ilyich's life lacks. *Decorous* is slightly literary but precise.

**не то** (*ne to*, literally "not that"). In chapter XII Ivan realizes his whole life was не то, then asks what то (*to*, "that") would be. The English must work both as a phrase and as a bare noun: "It was all not the right thing … But what is the right thing?" "Not it" is closer to the Russian, but "What is *it*?" becomes confusing next to the *It* used for death. Hence **the right thing**.

**Она** (*Ona*). In chapter VI Tolstoy refers to something with a capitalized feminine pronoun. Both смерть (*smert'*, "death") and боль (*bol'*, "pain") are feminine, so the Russian leaves room for both. "She" would personify more strongly in English than in Russian, and "Death" would settle an ambiguity Tolstoy leaves open (false explicitness, E4). ***It*** keeps the ambiguity; the loss of gender is explained in the unit note.

**Кончено** (*Koncheno*, "finished/over") — **adopted provisionally: "finished" (2026-10-06).** Someone at the deathbed says «Кончено!»; Ivan repeats it inwardly as «Кончена смерть» (*Konchena smert'*, "death is finished"). Rendered **"It is finished!" … "Death is finished."** The same word must appear in both places.
- Reason: "finished" keeps the Russian's participle form ("death [has been] ended") and the link between the two lines.
- Cost (P2): English readers will hear the King James echo of John 19:30, which the Russian does not contain verbally. The Russian Bible there has совершилось (*sovershilos'*, "it is accomplished"), from a different root. Mark the unit **CHOICE**.
- Alternative kept on record: *It is over / Death is over* (plainer, adds no echo).
- Unit note should explain: кончено (*koncheno*) ← кончить (*konchit'*, "to finish") ← конец (*konets*, "end") ← Proto-Slavic \*konъ, probably the same root as начать (*nachat'*, "to begin") and искони (*iskoni*, "from the beginning"); and that "finished" adds a Gospel echo the Russian leaves out.
