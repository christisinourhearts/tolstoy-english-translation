All seven units passed the finite workflow and mechanical validation. The pilot revealed real category differences that should be modeled before unattended scaling: source-layer anomalies; files composed primarily of editorial variant apparatus; body language differing from front-matter language; deliberate multilingual code-switching; rough notebook fragments; and `Krug chteniya` quotations that Tolstoy compiled, translated, adapted, or shortened. See `qa/reports/P001_REVIEW.md` for the post-pilot assessment.

## 1. Two Brothers and Gold — PASS

Source: `corpus/works/v25_028_030_Dva_brata_i_zoloto.md`

Difficulties encountered:
- The audited Markdown reads `жили в горè` in the opening sentence. A cross-check against another Russian collected-text witness gives `жили на горе`, which also agrees with the later geography of the story. The English therefore uses “lived on a hill.” This is a possible source-layer transcription/conversion defect and should be reported upstream rather than silently changing the Russian repository.
- `беремени на два` is archaic; rendered conservatively as “enough for two loads.”

Substantive fidelity corrections during audit:
- Changed “mountain” to “hill” throughout for consistency of the repeated `гора` imagery and the opening witness noted above.
- Removed an unnecessary possessive from “only with one's labor”; the Russian says simply `только трудом`.

English edit:
- Kept Tolstoy's repeated “Athanasius” and repeated sentence structures rather than varying them decoratively.
- Retained the deliberately plain moral-fable register.

Unresolved textual questions: none blocking acceptance. The opening Russian-source discrepancy is an upstream QA item.

Apparatus: not applicable.

Final source audit: PASS.
Mechanical validation: PASS.

## 2. Childhood — Sovremennik textual variants — PASS

Source: `corpus/works/v01_097_099_Detstvo_Varianty_teksta_Sovremennika_1852_g_No_9.md`

Difficulties encountered:
- This `works` file is not a continuous Tolstoy work. It is overwhelmingly scholarly/editorial variant apparatus containing small snippets of Tolstoy text. The current manifest's simple body/apparatus distinction does not adequately model this case.
- Several entries exist specifically to record very small wording differences. A smooth translation can accidentally erase the difference being documented.
- French epigraphs belong to the textual witness and therefore remain in French; the second source footnote is translated, while the first remains blank because the source note is blank.

Substantive fidelity corrections during audit:
- `образок моего ангела` revised from “icon of my guardian angel” to “image of my patron saint,” avoiding an incorrect identification of the Orthodox name-saint image with a guardian angel.
- `Ежели судить по настоящему` / `как по настоящему` was initially flattened into “properly”; revised so the contrast around reality/realism remains visible.
- `повалился` / `пал` initially collapsed to the same English verb; revised to “collapsed” / “fell” so the variant remains meaningful.
- `всматривались` / `рассматривались` revised to preserve the distinction between looking intently and examining.
- `надо бы приготовить` / `надо было приготовить` revised to “ought to be prepared” / “had to be prepared,” preserving modality and passive structure.

English edit:
- Editorial formulae were standardized in clear English while retaining references to pages, lines, editions, manuscript redactions, and `Sovremennik`.
- Intentionally awkward micro-variants were not normalized away.

Unresolved textual questions:
- The foreign epigraph `Honny soit qui mal у pense` contains nonstandard spelling/character forms in the source. It is preserved rather than silently emended.

Apparatus: translated, but this unit demonstrates that `apparatus_translation_status` needs a richer model for files whose entire content is editorial apparatus.

Final source audit: PASS.
Mechanical validation: PASS.

## 3. Letter to T. A. Yergolskaya and Countess E. A. Tolstaya, 27 October 1848 — PASS

Source: `corpus/letters/v59_008_T_A_Ergolskojigr_E_A_Tolstoj.md`

Difficulties encountered:
- The Markdown front matter declares `language: ru`, but Tolstoy's entire letter body is French. Russian appears only in the editorial apparatus. The English file records `body_source_language: fr`.
- Tolstoy's French contains youthful grammatical/spelling irregularities. They were not imitated as bad English; meaning and informal register were preserved.
- Editorial notes 1–2 are the Soviet editors' Russian rendering of the same French letter. Translating the apparatus therefore produces an intentionally redundant English rendering, which must remain clearly editorial.

Substantive fidelity corrections during audit:
- “the questions are all exhausted” was revised to the natural but equivalent “I've exhausted them all,” preserving the referent of French `elles` while avoiding a mechanical calque.
- The final postscript sentence follows the editors' clarification `повторяю` (“I repeat”), which resolves the awkward French `Je fais ... tout ce que j’avais fais` without adding new content.

English edit:
- Kept the light, slightly self-mocking tone of `un genre de vie assez bête et inactif` without making the letter artificially elegant.

Unresolved textual questions:
- `ATB` is preserved as an archival acronym because the source does not expand it here.

Apparatus: translated and clearly separated.

Pilot schema finding:
- A single `source_language` field is insufficient for mixed/document-layer cases. The manifest should distinguish declared file language, Tolstoy body language, and apparatus language.

Final source audit: PASS.
Mechanical validation: PASS.

## 4. Diary, 4/16 April 1861 — PASS

Source: `corpus/diaries/v48_033_034_1861_04_04.md`

Difficulties encountered:
- The entry is deliberately telegraphic and switches repeatedly between Russian and German. Polishing it into ordinary prose would falsify the diary's texture.
- `Цвецен` was identified from the volume's own scholarly commentary as Zwätzen, the village near Jena with the Karl Friedrich agricultural school. The English uses that identified place-name rather than mechanically transliterating `Цвецен`.
- Several visual/mnemonic fragments (`Пруссак ездок и цветочки`; the woman in the plowed field; `Келер как будто напрасно`) remain intrinsically compressed. They were not expanded into explanations.

Substantive fidelity corrections during audit:
- Kept the German pedagogical terms in the body and translated the source footnotes, preserving the fact of Tolstoy's code-switching.
- Revised the pedagogical fragment around `ограничиваясь` to retain the reflexive/limiting idea rather than turning it into a polished list of abstract “limits.”
- Moved footnote [^3] to the corresponding point between “only” and “instruction,” matching the deleted-word location more closely.
- Kept `определения определенного` as the intentionally awkward “defining the defined” rather than supplying an interpretation not present in the source.

English edit:
- Fragments, abrupt judgments, and noun phrases were retained as fragments.
- “Situations” is used for `положенья` in the art-theory sentence, where Tolstoy contrasts situations with characters.

Unresolved textual questions:
- `Келер как будто напрасно` remains semantically compressed; the translation “Kähler, it seems, needlessly” deliberately does not invent the omitted action.

Apparatus: all four source notes translated.

Final source audit: PASS.
Mechanical validation: PASS.

## 5. Notes Nos. 2 and 3, 1870 — PASS

Source: `corpus/notes/v48_342_346_Zapisi_No_2_i_3_1870.md`

Difficulties encountered:
- No. 2 is a set of extremely compressed historical-novel research and scene notes. Several tokens (`Кардим`, the “10 years” attached to the tsaritsa's stolniks, the isolated numbers 1660/82) cannot safely be expanded without reconstructing an unwritten context.
- The volume commentary identifies several historical references (including the source of the court-life notes and `Портомои` as an old term for washerwomen), but the English body remains a translation of Tolstoy's note rather than an editorial reconstruction.
- No. 3 abruptly shifts among self-criticism, a cook-and-dogs allegory, dramatic theory, Pushkin, philosophical systems, Russian drama, and fashion. The discontinuity is original.
- The Pushkin lines are explicitly an inexact recollection in the scholarly commentary. They are therefore translated from Tolstoy's quoted wording rather than silently replaced by a canonical Pushkin text.

Substantive fidelity corrections during audit:
- `Сапела` was initially mistaken for a proper name (“Sapela”). Corrected to the verb “She panted,” while leaving the following obscure fragment `след неприятен` unexpanded.
- Restored the past tense in the dogs' judgment of the cook (`он хорошо готовил обед` → “he cooked dinner well”).
- Simplified the overly literal “the hero himself cannot be expressed in words by himself” to “the hero cannot express himself in words,” which preserves the intended claim without retaining accidental Russian syntactic awkwardness.
- Kept every manuscript deletion in the numbered notes, including incomplete material such as `драм[а]` and `Он дурно`.

English edit:
- Historical names and technical terms were minimally normalized (`stolniks`, tsaritsa, tsarevna) rather than paraphrased into speculative offices/relationships.
- Titles of well-known plays use conventional English titles while the surrounding argument remains close to Tolstoy's wording.

Unresolved textual questions:
- `Кардим брат` is left as “Kardim brother”; there is insufficient evidence in this unit to identify or reinterpret `Кардим`.
- Tolstoy's parenthesis about Schopenhauer—`он говорит свод`—is rendered literally as “he says a vault.” A later philosophy-specific audit may be able to identify the exact Schopenhauer passage and improve this without guesswork.
- The marginal pencil note in footnote [^12] is kept as an unpunctuated fragment because the source itself supplies no syntactic resolution.

Apparatus: all twelve source notes/deletions translated or represented.

Final source audit: PASS.
Mechanical validation: PASS.

## 7. Circle of Reading, 1 January — PASS

Source: `corpus/krug_chtenija/v41_011_013_Krug_chtenija_daily_jan_1_1.md`

Difficulties encountered:
- The entry is a compilation of attributed thoughts by Emerson, Locke, Seneca, Thoreau, and Schopenhauer, framed by Tolstoy's own opening and closing aphorisms. The chief danger is to replace Tolstoy's Russian compilation with familiar or canonical English/German originals.
- The volume commentary states that Tolstoy translated material for *Krug chteniya*, sometimes departed from foreign-language originals, and also altered or shortened selected texts. Therefore the Russian compiled wording is the translation object for this corpus.
- This category blurs authorship roles: Tolstoy is compiler/editor and, in many cases, translator or adapter, while the attributed thought belongs to another author.

Substantive fidelity corrections during audit:
- Retained the Russian compilation's imagery and argumentative sequence even where a known English source might invite restoration to different wording.
- Kept Schopenhauer's deliberately harsh images (`летние мухи`, `литературные плевелы`, `нравственный яд`, `не вылезает из собственной грязи`) rather than softening them into modern critical prose.
- Preserved the ruminant metaphor in Locke and the source's abrupt injunction to “unlearn the habit of reading,” rather than smoothing either into paraphrase.
- Kept the final contrast as “material” versus “mental” poisons, matching Tolstoy's compiled conceptual opposition.

English edit:
- Quotations read as coherent English but remain translations of the Russian wording, not reconstructed quotations from external editions.
- Author attributions are rendered in conventional English forms: Emerson, Locke, Seneca, Thoreau, Schopenhauer.

Unresolved textual questions:
- No passage blocks acceptance of this unit. Exact provenance of each foreign quotation may be worth a later scholarly provenance layer, but provenance reconstruction should not overwrite Tolstoy's compiled Russian text.

Apparatus: not applicable in this source file.

Pilot policy finding:
- `Krug chteniya` needs an explicit quotation/adaptation policy. The metadata should distinguish source author, compiler/editor, and translation basis, and should permit optional provenance records without treating an external-language original as automatically authoritative over Tolstoy's Russian wording.

Final source audit: PASS.
Mechanical validation: PASS.
